Buffy the Vampire Slayer. Season six, episode 10. Mein Warren.

CHAPTER 1
The Automated Alibi

That Monday morning in the Trio’s basement lair, the fluorescent tubes flickered with a low electrical hum above a concrete floor cluttered with cables and junk. A bank of computer monitors glowed against the wall and threw the only real light in the room, a pipe dripped in the corner, and across the street a lawnmower droned on in perfect suburban normalcy.

Warren was slumped in a beat-up office chair with his legs splayed out in front of him, in a wrinkled navy-blue Star Trek: The Next Generation t-shirt with an energy drink stain down the front, his dark hair a mess and his eyes half closed.

Jonathan lay curled on the sagging couch in an oversized grey hoodie with the hood pulled up over his flat, greasy hair, and Andrew was sprawled across a beanbag in an oversized beige Jedi robe, his thick black-rimmed glasses sitting crooked on his nose, sleepy but somehow still eager-looking, even at rest.

A black Casio alarm clock sat on a milk crate, and without warning it went off with a piercing electronic wail.

Warren groaned loudly and dragged one hand down his unshaven face, his eyes narrowing as a short, sharp huff escaped through his nose.

Warren muttered in a raspy voice, "Turn it off, Andrew."

Andrew’s eyes went wide behind his crooked glasses, and a quick, defensive flush rose in his cheeks while the alarm kept shrieking like the krayt dragon howl out of Star Wars. He scrambled up off the beanbag with the Jedi robe tangling around his legs and slapped frantically at the Casio until it died, then pushed his glasses up with both hands.

Andrew said defensively, "Hey, that sound worked perfectly for Obi-Wan to scare off the Tusken Raiders. It’s a completely valid acoustic deterrent."

Jonathan rubbed his eyes with the heels of both hands and pushed himself up off the couch with a groan, his mouth twisting into a tired grimace as he let out a long, weary sigh.

Jonathan said, "Yeah, well, we’re not Sand People, Andrew. We’re criminal masterminds on a schedule."

Andrew ignored the jab and hurried over to the monitors, typing fast, until a blinking green box popped up on the screen.

Auto-payment cleared for Sunnydale residential. Rent, twelve hundred dollars. Utility overage, eight hundred fifty dollars, for high kilowatt drain detected. Total debit, two thousand fifty dollars.

Andrew’s face turned smug as he tapped the screen with one finger.

Andrew said proudly, "Auto-monthly rent and utilities are paid up. The landlord doesn’t care that we’re pulling enough electricity to power a small casino as long as the direct deposit clears. We’re officially off the grid."

Warren tipped his head to one side and stretched his neck until it cracked loudly, looking satisfied.

Warren said, "Exactly. Automated invisibility. As long as the money transfers, they could forget about this house for a year. Nobody is going to come looking."

Andrew turned away from the monitors, opened the mini-fridge, and pulled out three cans of Jolt Cola, tossing one to Warren and one to Jonathan, and all three of them popped the tabs at the same time.

Warren’s smirk spread wide, and he sat up straighter and raised the can.

Warren said, "Breakfast of champions and super villains everywhere!"

They lifted the cans together in a circle and drank.

Jonathan wiped his mouth with the back of his hand and walked over to the worktable, where he picked up the big crystal-tipped Confusion Ray and hefted it in both hands, feeling the weight of it. A dark kind of excitement spread across his face as his fingers tightened around the grip.

Jonathan said, "Let’s do this. It’s going to be one hell of a Monday for Sunnydale!"

He grabbed the barrel with his left hand and racked it like a shotgun, and the loud mechanical clack-clack echoed off the concrete walls.

Warren stood up out of his chair, full of purpose.

Warren said, "Load the van."

CHAPTER 2
The Commute and the Code Eight

Outside Sunnydale High that same morning, the bright California sun laid long shadows across the sidewalk and the school steps. The new building stood where the old one had burned down on graduation day, and students flooded up the front stairs as if nothing had ever happened there.

Xander’s beat-up pickup truck pulled up to the curb. He sat behind the wheel in his plaid flannel, sleeves rolled to the elbows, his yellow hard hat on the seat between them.

Dawn sat in the passenger seat in her chaotic oversized sweater, her hair pulled up in a messy ponytail.

Dawn grabbed her backpack off the floorboard and threw it over one shoulder, and Xander leaned across the console toward her, his voice suddenly serious and protective.

Xander said, "Hey. Take care in there today."

Dawn stopped with her hand on the door handle and looked back at him with a small, fond eye-roll.

Xander kept going, earnest, and said, "I know they rebuilt the place, but this school is still full of… well, you know. The past. Just keep your head on a swivel. If anyone offers you a suspicious bake-sale brownie or a teacher starts shedding their skin, you run."

Dawn rolled her eyes again, all the way this time, but she was smiling as she hitched the backpack higher on her shoulder.

Dawn said, "I know, Xander. Avoid the weirdos, don’t go into the basement, and skip the mystery meat in the cafeteria. Got it. See ya!"

She hopped out and slammed the passenger door with a metallic thunk. Xander watched her disappear into the crowd until she was safely through the front doors, then threw the truck into drive and pulled away from the curb.

A little later, Xander turned down Elm Street and then right onto Maple, where the traffic sat bumper to bumper, horns honking and a crossing signal ticking at the intersection ahead.

He sighed and took a long sip of black coffee from the dented thermos in the cup holder, then glanced in the rearview mirror, and his eyes narrowed.

A beat-up black panel van from the eighties, with heavily blacked-out windows, had pulled out hard from a residential side street and cut off a minivan.

Xander’s hands tightened on the wheel, and every muscle in his body went on alert.

Xander said under his breath, "What in the fresh hell are they up to? Not this early."

He grabbed the heavy plastic CB microphone off the dash, thumbed the button, and slipped into his professional construction-crew voice.

Xander said, "Unit 4 to dispatcher. Got something coming up. May be a code eight. Need to pull off the road here in a minute. Over."

He worked the truck through the lanes until he was exactly three cars back from the black van, and kept a steady, paranoid watch on it.

The line of cars rolled up to a large intersection as the light turned red, one of those agonizingly slow Sunnydale signals, and its red glow flooded the cab while Xander stared at the van’s back doors.

A sudden sharp ringing started in his ears, and the air inside the cab turned heavy and close, while the light stayed red.

Then the back doors of the black van flew open. Jonathan stood inside with his feet planted wide, in a black graphic t-shirt and cargo shorts, grinning with maniacal triumph, and he brought up the Confusion Ray and screamed over the traffic.

Jonathan said, "Have a nice Monday, construction man!"

He pulled the trigger, and a crackling blue beam shot across the intersection, blasted through Xander’s windshield, and flooded the cab with blue light. Xander froze in pure terror as it washed over him.

A pulsing red glow spread across his whole body and sank into his skin and his eyes. The terror was gone at once, and his face locked into a mask of cold, homicidal rage.

With a deep, guttural yell, Xander lifted his foot off the brake and stomped his heavy work boot all the way down on the gas. The truck roared forward with its tires screeching, launching straight at the back of the van.

CHAPTER 3
The Berserker and the Sledgehammer

At the intersection, the red of the long traffic light hung over the street as Xander’s pickup roared at the rear of the panel van.

Behind the wheel his eyes were wide and glassy, the muscles standing out along his clenched jaw, sweat beading on his brow and his breath coming animal-hard, while the red glow pulsed under his skin, around his eyes and down his neck.

The truck smashed into the back of the van with a deafening crash of metal, and the impact lifted the van’s back wheels clean off the ground.

Inside, Jonathan was thrown forward and slammed into the passenger seat, crushing Warren against the door, while Andrew let out a high-pitched, electronic-sounding scream, like C-3PO.

Jonathan yelled in terror, "Uh oh!"

Warren shoved at the seat with both hands, panicking.

Warren shouted, "He’s trying to kill us! We need to move!"

The three of them tore back a rug and yanked open a hidden hatch in the van’s floor, and beneath it an open manhole gaped in the asphalt with the dark mouth of the sewer tunnel waiting below, and they started spilling down into it.

Outside, Xander kicked open the driver’s door and stepped down onto the asphalt, the white tee under his flannel torn at the collar from the crash, his lips pulled back in a snarl.

He reached behind the driver’s seat and yanked out a long-handled sledgehammer whose head caught the red of the traffic light, and he marched at the crumpled van like a machine, growling low in his throat.

Xander said, "Come out…"

He swung the sledgehammer in a huge overhead arc, and it crashed into the side of the van with a deafening metallic dong that caved the panel inward, and he swung again, another brutal dong, and another.

Inside, the walls buckled with every hit while the Trio scrambled halfway down the escape hatch.

Warren screamed at the other two, "Go go go!"

Outside, Xander kept at it, breathing like a wild animal, driving the dents in deeper and deeper.

A Sunnydale police cruiser screeched to a stop nearby with its lights flashing, and a weary but professional officer in her mid-forties stepped out with her hand already on the taser at her hip.

The officer said, "Sir! Drop the weapon and get on your knees! NOW!"

Xander pivoted toward her with the sledgehammer raised high over his head, and he didn’t see a police officer, only another thing in his way.

She drew the taser with steady hands and fired without hesitating, and two small probes shot out on thin copper wires and sank into his chest with a loud electric crackle.

Xander’s whole body spasmed. The sledgehammer flew out of his hands and clattered onto the asphalt as the red glow around him shattered like glass, and the rage left his face, replaced by sheer confusion and pain. He collapsed onto the pavement beside his truck, unconscious.

Beneath the street, the Trio splashed away through the dark sewer tunnel, the Confusion Ray clutched tight in Warren’s arms.

CHAPTER 4
The Bus Escape and the Phone Call

Back at the intersection, the red light still glowed over the wreck. A block away, a manhole cover scraped aside, and the Trio hauled themselves up out of the sewer, smeared with muck, and sprinted across the street just as a bus pulled in to the stop.

They tumbled onto the bus breathing hard, paid their fare in a hurry, and slumped down into the back seats, trying to look like ordinary stressed commuters with sweat on their faces and their hair plastered down.

Warren laid the Confusion Ray across his lap and stroked it like a beloved pet. The few other passengers glanced at the crystal-tipped rifle, rolled their eyes, and went back to their newspapers, because to them it looked like three nerds carrying a cheap prop home from a sci-fi convention.

The bus pulled away, and for a moment the three of them sat in tense silence, Warren’s fingers digging into the weapon in his lap, until a frustrated breath burst out of him.

Warren said in a low, angry voice, "We just lost the van. Impounded. This is a disaster."

Jonathan’s face flushed, and his hands flapped nervously in front of him as his voice cracked.

Jonathan said, "You told me to zap him!"

Andrew blinked fast behind his glasses and fumbled to straighten them.

Andrew said in a small voice, "We can fix this… right?"

The bus drove on while the three of them stared out the window, already working out their next move, until Warren's face changed with a slow, pleased idea.

Warren said, mostly to himself and very pleased with the idea, "Do you know what this means? We can zap her with it and just stand back and watch the fun."

Andrew waved both hands weakly in front of him.

Andrew said, "No, no way, Warren. Are you crazy?"

Jonathan hunched down in his seat and chewed at his lip.

Jonathan said nervously, "He has a point, Warren. She is super strong, and we are not. If she’s homicidal, she will kill people, tear them apart. No. That is too much!"

Warren glared at the two of them, annoyed by their lack of vision, but he kept the weapon lowered in his lap.

Back at the Trio’s house, the basement door banged open, and the three of them stumbled down into the lair, covered in sweat, bus grime and sewer muck, their sneakers slapping wetly on the concrete.

Warren dropped the Confusion Ray onto the table in annoyance and started pacing.

Warren said, "Fine! We don't use it on the Slayer yet. But the field test proved the output is completely unpredictable. It turned the carpenter into a berserker instead of just scrambling his brain."

Jonathan bent over with his hands on his knees, still catching his breath.

Jonathan said, "So what do we do? The police have the van."

Warren sneered. He said, "We adapt. We just need to adjust it is all, find out the proper frequency."

Then he stopped pacing and looked over at Andrew with a cold, calculating gleam in his eye.

Warren said, "We can test it on your mom's cat."

Andrew swallowed hard and pushed his glasses up his nose, realizing he was completely out of his depth.

That afternoon at the Summers house, the warm sun poured in through the front windows and gave the living room a cozy golden glow over the floral couch and the coffee table.

It was a lazy Monday afternoon, and neither Tara nor Willow had left the house since morning, and they were curled up together on the couch, watching Maury.

Tara wore a soft, earthy long-sleeved blouse in muted greens and browns, her golden dirty-blonde hair hanging loose, and Willow wore a heavy, drab brown, oversized knit cardigan over a plain white tee, and for the first time in days they were peaceful.

Their fingers were laced together on the cushion between them. Willow’s thumb stroked slowly over the back of Tara’s hand, and every so often Tara gave Willow’s hand a little squeeze.

Tara let out a small laugh at the screen and shook her head.

Tara said, "I can’t believe people actually go on TV for this…"

Willow smiled warmly without taking her eyes off the television.

Willow said softly, "It’s strangely comforting. Makes our problems feel normal."

They shared a quiet, loving glance, and Tara leaned her head against Willow’s shoulder for a moment, their laced hands holding on to the warmth of the afternoon.

Then the yellow wall phone in the kitchen rang, loud and shrill and old-fashioned, and Tara lifted her head and looked at Willow with her eyebrows raised in mild surprise.

Tara said, "Who would be calling the house phone?"

Willow gave Tara’s hand one last gentle squeeze, got up off the couch, and walked across the living room into the kitchen, her bare feet padding softly on the hardwood, and picked up the receiver.

Willow said cheerfully, "Hello? Summers residence."

Anya’s voice came through the phone, frantic and loud enough that Willow had to pull the receiver away from her ear.

Anya said, "Willow! It’s Anya! We’re at the police station! Xander got arrested — something about attacking a van with a sledgehammer! We need help bailing him out!"

Willow’s eyes widened, and her voice went urgent.

Willow said, "We’re on our way. Stay calm."

She hung up quickly and hurried back into the living room, where Tara was still on the couch, looking up at her with worry already pulling at her face, and she stumbled a little over the words.

Tara asked, "W-W-Willow? What’s wrong?"

Willow went to the bottom of the staircase, cupped both hands around her mouth, and yelled up the stairs in a voice that could have commanded an army.

Willow said, "BUFFY! GET DOWN HERE… NOW!"

Footsteps thundered overhead, and Buffy appeared at the top of the stairs in a fitted slate-blue ribbed top and black cargo pants, her hair in a messy ponytail, alert and slightly annoyed and more present than she had been in weeks. She came down, folded her arms, and blew out a sigh.

Buffy said, "This better be good. I was trying to have five minutes where the world wasn’t falling apart for once."

Tara got up off the couch while Willow quickly filled Buffy in, her words coming fast and serious.

Willow said, "Anya just called. Xander’s at the police station. He attacked the Trio’s van with a sledgehammer. We need to go bail him out."

Buffy sighed again and rubbed her temple with two fingers, exasperated but already moving toward the door.

Buffy said, "Of course he did. Because nothing says ‘normal Monday’ like Xander going full berserker on a panel van. Let’s go."

Tara looked between Buffy and Willow and folded her hands in front of her.

Tara said, "I have to stay here. Dawn will be out of school soon. It’s almost four p.m."

Buffy and Willow both nodded, and Willow leaned in and kissed Tara gently on the lips, pulling away slowly.

Buffy rolled her eyes and said in a dry voice, "Come on, Will — time for that later. Let's go."

Buffy grabbed Willow by the arm and pulled her toward the front door, and the two of them left together while Tara stood in the living room and gave them a little wave with her fingers.

CHAPTER 5
The Station & The Erase

Later that afternoon, the Sunnydale police station echoed with noise, telephones ringing, boots thudding on the linoleum, and voices talking over one another under the hard white lights.

Anya had been at the station since late morning, when the police finally let Xander make his one phone call, and she had spent most of the day trying to shout him free on her own before she gave up and called the house. Now she stood at the scuffed booking counter in a tailored burgundy blazer, waving her arms at a very tired desk sergeant in a tan uniform.

Anya said, "This is a gross misallocation of municipal resources! You cannot detain a taxpayer simply for executing necessary blunt-force trauma on an illegally parked, structurally unsound van! I demand his immediate release, full restitution for his emotional distress, and compensation for my lost retail hours!"

The desk sergeant pinched the bridge of his nose and closed his eyes.

A few feet away, on a row of hard plastic chairs, Willow sat very quietly. Buffy sat next to her, arms crossed, keeping an eye on Xander, who was slouched in the next chair, looking dazed.

Willow was staring straight at the glowing computer monitor behind the booking desk, her eyes narrowed in hard concentration.

A faint, almost transparent purple bubble of energy slowly swelled outward from her body like a heat mirage. It washed over the entire room without choosing anyone, passing through the desk, the computers and the officers, and straight over Buffy, Xander and Anya.

On the screen behind the desk, Xander’s digital arrest record highlighted itself, grim mugshot and felony charges and all, though nobody touched the keyboard, and then the file deleted itself and left an empty desktop behind.

Willow took a slow, deep breath, and her shoulders dropped as she stopped feeding power into the bubble.

Willow whispered, her voice tight and strained, "Okay. It's done. Let's go."

She stood up quickly, and Buffy blinked hard and shook her head a little before she took Xander by the arm and pulled him to his feet.

Over at the counter, Anya stopped her tirade mid-sentence and went blank for a fraction of a second, then turned on her heel and marched toward the glass doors, leading all of them out of the precinct.

Outside, in the bright, harsh afternoon sun, the magic settled fully into their heads as they crossed out into the fresh air.

Xander stopped short on the concrete steps, blinking fast and shaking his head from side to side as if he were trying to clear a thick fog out of it. He rubbed his temples with both hands, unsteady on his feet, his brow furrowed deep in confusion.

Xander said, "Wait... why were we just in there? Did I get a parking ticket? I don't even remember parking."

Buffy paused on the step beside him and pressed the heel of her hand to the center of her forehead, wincing, because with her Slayer constitution the memory wipe hit her like a sudden, sharp hangover. She squinted hard against the sun.

Buffy said groggily, "My head is buzzing... I don't know, Xand. Did someone steal your truck? What are we doing here?"

Anya nodded confidently, her ego paving right over the gap in her memory, and brushed a speck off the lapel of her blazer.

Anya said, "Yes. It was an unjust municipal fine regarding the vehicle, and I successfully intimidated the authorities into dropping it. You're welcome."

Willow walked ahead of them to the driver’s side of Joyce’s compact sedan, and her face showed no confusion at all, only a deep physical strain.

She stopped at the car door with her back turned firmly to the others, and a single dark, thick drop of blood slid out of her left nostril in the sunlight, and she gasped softly, her eyes widening in fear at what the magic had cost her.

Before Buffy or Xander could notice, she lifted her left arm and dragged the sleeve of her brown cardigan across her nose. It wiped the blood away and left a faint rust-brown smudge on the cuff that would stay there for the rest of the day.

She took a quick breath and squared herself, then pasted on a bright, entirely fake smile and turned back to the group, her voice pitched a little too high.

Willow said, "Who wants pizza? My treat!"

CHAPTER 6
Pixels, Sardines, and Partings

Later, in the arcade corner at the back of the pizza place, sixteen-bit game noise mixed with teenage chatter and the smell of greasy pepperoni, and the flickering screens threw blue and red across the dim back of the restaurant.

Xander stood in front of a shooting game with a plastic gun in each hand, one red and one blue, mashing the triggers, his flannel wrinkled now, a little asphalt dust still ground into the cuffs.

Anya stood next to him with her arms crossed, aiming a look of intense fiscal disapproval at the game.

Xander hunched toward the screen and yelled at it, "Cover me, Mac! The aliens are breaching the perimeter!"

Anya frowned at him without uncrossing her arms.

Anya said, "Xander, you inserted four quarters for a cooperative experience. By monopolizing both plastic firearms, you are severely decreasing our return on investment."

Xander didn’t look away from the screen. He said, "Tactical superiority, Ahn! It’s all about the volume of—"

He pulled the plastic trigger, and the gun kicked back against his palm with its simulated, vibrating recoil.

A blinding white flash went off behind his eyes, and a high, deafening electronic whine cut out all the noise of the arcade, and for a fraction of a second the game was gone and Xander was standing in the middle of the sunlit street again, watching the officer raise her taser, while the fifty-thousand-volt shock rippled across his chest in a crackle of blue, his body remembering it all at once.

Then the arcade rushed back in. Xander dropped the red plastic gun, which swung from its metal cord, and stumbled back a step, gasping, rubbing the center of his chest where the phantom pain still burned.

Willow’s voice went sharp with sudden panic. She said, "Xander? Are you okay?"

Xander blinked hard and shook his head as the noise of the pizza place came flooding back in. He looked at the arcade cabinet, shaken and confused, one hand still rubbing at his chest, and forced a weak smile onto his pale face.

Xander said, "Yeah. Yeah, I’m fine. Just... weird brain freeze. I didn't even eat any ice cream. Must be the jalapeños fighting back."

Willow’s face tightened with guilt she couldn’t show, and she kept her eyes off his.

Willow said, "Let’s get out of here. We should stop by the Magic Box and check in on Giles."

Out in the parking lot, the four of them walked through the bright afternoon sun toward Joyce’s compact sedan and tried to fit four grown adults inside it.

Willow took the wheel and Anya claimed the passenger seat, which left Xander and Buffy to squeeze into the back. Xander’s broad shoulders took up more than half the seat and pinned Buffy against the door, the two of them packed in like sardines while the suspension creaked and a horn honked somewhere outside.

Buffy grimaced, her cheek squished against the window, and twisted awkwardly, trying to make some room.

Buffy said, "Xander, your elbow is literally inside my ribcage."

Xander’s face flushed, and he braced one hand on the seat and tried to make himself smaller.

Xander said, "I can't help it, Buffy! My skeletal structure requires a certain square footage!"

Buffy shoved at his arm with her elbow. She said, "Well, shrink! You're squishing my bladder, and I just drank a gallon of root beer. Step on it, Will, before I drown in the backseat."

At the Magic Box, the front door chimed as the gang spilled into the shop, stretching their cramped arms and legs, and Buffy headed straight for the back hallway without breaking stride.

Buffy said, "Bathroom. Now. Talk amongst yourselves."

She disappeared down the dark back hall, and the others turned toward the counter.

Giles stood behind the register in tweed trousers and a waistcoat, without his usual jacket, and beside the register sat the small cardboard box holding his journal and his old brass magnifying glass.

He looked at Willow, Anya and Xander and took a steadying breath, then removed his glasses to clean them, and his voice came out low.

Giles said, "I’m glad you’re all here. There is... something I need to tell you."

The easy mood in the room was gone at once, and Xander and Willow stepped up to the counter and traded a worried glance.

Willow frowned and said, "Giles? What's going on?"

Giles looked down at the counter for a moment, then lifted his eyes and met theirs.

Giles said, "I spoke with Anya last week. The paperwork is already in motion. I am moving back to England, permanently."

Xander’s jaw dropped open in shock, and Willow looked as though someone had punched her in the stomach. Anya stood a little apart with her eyes on the glass of the counter, because she had known since last Wednesday and kept it to herself.

Down the hall, the bathroom door clicked open, and Buffy walked out wiping her hands dry on the legs of her pants, her posture loose and relaxed for the first time all day.

Buffy said lightly, "Okay, crisis averted. What did I—"

She stopped dead. She looked at Xander’s shocked face, at Willow’s teary eyes, and at Giles standing beside a packed cardboard box, and the reality of it hit her all at once.

For a long, painful moment Buffy just stared at the three of them while the old numbness tried to rise up and swallow her, and nobody in the shop moved or made a sound.

But then she took a breath, and she didn’t retreat. She walked straight around the counter, stepped right into Giles’s space, put both hands firmly on his shoulders, and pulled him into a tight, desperate hug, and her voice came out soft and clear and full of raw feeling.

Buffy said, "I love you, Giles."

Giles closed his eyes, disarmed, and wrapped his arms around her and hugged her back tightly with his chin resting near her shoulder, and his voice was thick.

Giles said, "I love you too, Buffy."

They slowly pulled apart while the rest of them stood silent, stunned and deeply moved by Buffy’s sudden warmth.

Xander cleared his throat, his voice cracking a little as he tried to lighten the air.

Xander said, "So... no goodbye party? No streamers? We could get the good chips."

Giles smiled, a tired and fond look crossing his face, and slid his glasses back on, and then almost at once he took them off again and pointed them at Xander, perfectly deadpan.

Giles said, "Xander, the last time we gathered for a purely social occasion, I ended up unconscious behind my own sofa. I would like to make it to the airport with a functioning temporal lobe, if it's all the same to you."

CHAPTER 7
The Departure and The Void

In the brick alleyway behind the Magic Box, the late afternoon sun laid long golden-orange shadows across the bricks, and the far-off sound of Sunnydale traffic ran under the quiet.

Giles closed the trunk of his rental car with a solid, final thud, a dark green Jaguar S-Type hired for the drive to the airport and packed tight with boxes and luggage.

Buffy, Willow, Xander and Anya stood around him in a loose half circle, and Anya jingled the ring of store keys without noticing, her nerves colliding with her brand-new ownership of the shop.

Giles looked at each of them in turn, and his eyes lingered longest on Buffy.

Giles said, "Right. Well. Try to keep the apocalypse to a minimum while I’m sorting out my flat in Bath."

Buffy held his gaze and answered softly but steadily.

Buffy said, "We’ve got it covered, Giles. Safe travels."

He nodded and gave them one last tight, brave smile, then slipped into the driver’s seat and started the engine.

They watched the Jaguar pull slowly out of the alley, tires crunching over the gravel, until it turned the corner and disappeared. None of them spoke, and the four of them stood in the empty alley looking at the corner where the car had gone.

Xander shoved his hands deep into his pockets and kicked a loose stone across the gravel with a resigned sigh.

Xander said, "Man. It’s actually real. He’s gone."

Willow was staring at the empty street with her face eerily calm and her eyes a little glazed, almost entirely detached from the sadness the rest of them were feeling. One slow, measured breath was the only sign of the emptiness she was hiding.

Willow said flatly, "He’ll be fine."

Buffy looked over at her, surprised at how cold Willow sounded, and leaned in a little toward her.

Buffy said, "You sure, Will? It’s a long flight, and starting over—"

Willow turned smoothly on her heel and started walking toward the car before Buffy could finish, cutting her off without looking back.

Willow said, "I’m sure. I already looked ahead. His flight lands at eight-fourteen a.m., local time."

Xander and Buffy traded a deeply unsettled look, because Willow had just casually admitted to peering into the future to check a flight itinerary.

CHAPTER 8
The Empty House

By dusk the sun had already set, and the Summers living room sat in deep blue twilight with only one warm table lamp turned on.

Anya had headed home to the apartment once the Jaguar was gone, so it was only Buffy, Willow and Xander who walked into a house that was deafeningly quiet. The living room felt big and dark and empty without the Watcher there to hold it steady.

Tara stepped out of the kitchen, drying her hands on a dish towel, and her face fell into sympathetic concern the moment she saw theirs.

Buffy walked slowly over to the antique weapons chest against the wall and ran her hand across the lid, staring at it blankly: a Slayer without a Watcher. Her hand went still on the wood.

Willow was pacing fast and jerky beside the floral couch, her fingers twitching at her sides, because the day had gone so far out of her control that she couldn’t hold still.

Willow said, the words tumbling out slightly too fast, "We should patrol. Or research. We should check the grids, make sure the Hellmouth isn't reacting to a shift in Watcher energy—"

Tara walked forward and gently caught Willow by the arm, stopping her pacing, her eyes steady and warm as she took one slow breath.

Tara said in a soft but firm voice, "Will. It's okay. The grids are fine."

Willow looked up at her with her green eyes wide and frantic, desperate for something to hold on to, and Tara smiled gently and brushed a strand of red hair back from Willow’s face with her fingertips.

Tara said, "How about I draw us a hot bath? We can just... breathe for a minute. Just us."

Willow’s stiff shoulders loosened a little, and she let out a shaky breath and nodded, letting Tara take her hand, and the two of them turned and went slowly up the dark wooden stairs, their soft footsteps fading away and leaving Buffy and Xander alone in the quiet, darkening living room.

CHAPTER 9
The Goddess, The Execution, and The Reset

That night, in the upstairs bathroom across the hall from the master bedroom, four amber candles flickered along the edges of the porcelain sink, and the air was thick with warm steam from the bath while somewhere a faucet dripped with a faint echo.

Tara and Willow had changed into soft, oversized terry-cloth bathrobes, with Willow’s brown cardigan hanging on the hook behind the door, and they sat cross-legged on the cool white tile facing each other, their fingers tightly linked across the space between them and Tara’s wet dirty-blonde hair clinging to her neck, both of them with their eyes closed in deep, matched meditation.

Willow’s brow furrowed in frustration, and she spoke without opening her eyes.

Willow whispered, "I'm trying to see them, Tara. Warren and the others. Where they are hiding. But it's… it’s just a void. Like a black shadow is pulled over the entire grid."

Tara squeezed Willow’s hands and kept her own voice low and steady, grounding her.

Tara said, "I know, sweetie. They must have a cloaking hex up. It’s too shielded. We can’t push too hard together, Willow. I can feel your power trying to surge, and it... it would hurt me if you went full power while we're connected."

Willow blew out a sharp, frustrated breath, and Tara let out a tired sigh of her own as the magic wore on her, then opened her eyes and gave Willow a gentle, tired smile.

Tara said, "Let's take a break, okay? I need to brush out my hair."

Tara unlinked their hands and rose smoothly, her robe shifting around her, and because the mirror over the sink was steamed over, she picked up her wooden hairbrush from the counter and stepped out through the open doorway into the bedroom, where she stood at the dresser mirror with her back to the bathroom.

Willow stayed on the floor and glanced up at Tara’s back through the doorway, her eyes narrowing slightly to make sure Tara wasn’t watching, and then she closed her eyes.

Without Tara there as a grounding wire, Willow dropped every barrier she had and dove hard and deep into the magical current.

Everything went dead silent, and time stopped, so that inside the frosted window a single drop of condensation hung frozen in the air where it had fallen from the glass, and the only sound left was the caw, caw, caw of ravens from somewhere impossibly far away.

Willow wasn’t in the bathroom anymore, and the cold of another place closed around her.

She was standing on a great, barren, rocky hill in torchlight under a huge silver moon. At the top of the hill a pale woman with long, flowing black hair sat on a throne of black iron, ravens perched along its back. Her dark green and purple gown was knotted with Celtic patterns and lined across the shoulders with raven feathers.

It was the Goddess Badb, and when she spoke her voice was ancient and layered and impossibly powerful.

Badb said, "Child of light, and child of darkness. You seek what is hidden, but you are blind to your own path."

Willow stood frozen before the throne, her breath showing white in the cold air as the ancient voice resonated through her bones.

Badb went on, "You must find your path to balance. Know this: life is struggle. And from that struggle, true strength is forged. You cannot alter the reality of those around you to spare them pain, for you strip away the very essence of their humanity. Cease your meddling with their minds."

Badb leaned forward, and her completely black eyes pierced straight through Willow.

Badb said, "In exchange for this pact, I offer a boon. I will show you the cost of the path you are currently walking. Behold your future."

The vision tore through Willow, and it wasn’t like watching a memory but like living something that hadn’t happened yet.

It was two mornings from now, in the Summers master bedroom, with the morning sun bright and golden and beautiful, and Tara stood in front of the bedroom window with her back to the glass in a soft, light-colored shirt, smiling lovingly at Willow.

A deafening gunshot cracked through the morning.

A small hole opened in Tara’s chest, just above her heart, and a fine, horribly subtle red mist of blood sprayed outward. Willow could taste and smell the blood just under her nose as the blood mist sprayed softly across her mouth. The love of her life’s blood. The entire world felt like it fell from under her all at once. She felt swallowed by darkness, rage and fear as it rose from her chest up into her face. And Tara, still smiling, her voice full of warmth, spoke.

Tara said, "Your shirt..."

Then Tara’s smile died, and she fell backward, slowly, agonizingly slowly, and the path of the bullet ran back through the window glass and across the backyard grass to Warren, standing by the fence and lowering a smoking handgun.

Then the vision ended all at once. Willow’s body jerked hard and her eyes snapped open with a violent gasp, her hands flying to her mouth, and she was back on the bathroom floor. Time started again, and the hanging drop of condensation hit the tile with a soft plink.

Out in the bedroom, Tara turned around from the dresser with the hairbrush in her hand and came to the bathroom doorway, looking in at Willow with mild concern.

Tara said, "Will? You okay?"

Willow scrambled to her feet with her hands shaking and forced out a high, frantic laugh as she tried to hide what she had just seen.

Willow said, "Yep! I'm good! Just... my stomach is suddenly doing backflips! I think Xander's pepperoni pizza is fighting back! Give me a minute!"

She practically shoved Tara back over the threshold into the bedroom, slammed the bathroom door between them, and turned the lock with a loud click.

She gripped the edges of the porcelain sink and leaned forward to stare into the steamed mirror, hyperventilating, trying to get control of the world-ending terror of what she had seen, while her reflection stared back at her, trembling, with tears welling up in its eyes.

Slowly the trembling stopped, and the fear hardened into something else entirely, into cold resolve, and in the mirror the whites of Willow’s eyes faded to pitch black while her green irises bled into a glowing, violent red.

She calmed her breathing and turned on the faucet to wash her face, then flushed the toilet loudly for cover.

In the bedroom it was dark except for the moonlight coming through the blinds, and Willow walked out and slipped under the covers beside Tara, pressing her face against Tara’s back as if everything were normal.

Then Willow opened her right eye, which had gone solid black, and dark veins of black magic began creeping out across her pale skin from around it, like cracks spreading through porcelain.

Later that night, with Tara deeply asleep, turned away from the bathroom door and breathing softly, Willow stood silently at the foot of the bed in her bathrobe.

She closed her eyes, lifted one hand, and focused on her own side of the duvet, and a faint purple glow gathered around the blanket as Willow shaped the fabric with her mind until it pulled in and settled beside Tara’s body, as though someone were still lying there with her.

Willow opened the bedroom closet and raised both hands, and a wash of dark shadow swallowed her body, and when it passed her soft bathrobe was gone, replaced by her dark magic clothes: a black Victorian corset, tight black leather pants, and heavy black boots.

She moved silently to the bedroom door and slipped out into the dark house.

Out on the front lawn, the street lay under eerie silver moonlight, and Willow looked up at the dark shapes of the neighborhood trees, then closed her eyes and parted her hands with her palms turned up, fierce determination hardening her face over a deep, unresolved pain.

She spoke in Gaelic, her voice low, raspy, and strained, "A Bhandia Bhadhbh, éist le m’achainí; tabhair dom sciatháin fiaigh le heitilt anocht."

The words meant: Goddess Badb, hear my plea. Give me raven’s wings to fly this night.

A vivid dark purple aura covered her for a moment, and she lifted silently and effortlessly into the air and rose above the treetops, tears shining on her pale cheeks against the hard, dark-veined set of her face, and she followed a flock of ravens across town toward the Trio’s house.

She came down on the Trio’s front walk without a sound and walked straight through the solid front door, phasing through the wood as a wave of purple energy rippled across its surface, and she went down the hallway, opened the basement door, and stood at the top of the stairwell in the blue-white glow of the monitors below.

Down in the lair, Warren, Jonathan and Andrew were arguing furiously, unaware of her, and Warren was pacing, jabbing a finger at Jonathan.

Warren said, "You complete idiot! The van is impounded! Do you know how hard it is to fake DMV registration for a vehicle that doesn't legally exist?!"

Jonathan backed away with both hands raised in front of him.

Jonathan said, "Me?! You told me to open the doors! You said, ‘Zap the construction guy, it'll be funny!’ How was I supposed to know he’d go full Incredible Hulk and try to cave my skull in with a sledgehammer?!"

Andrew pushed his glasses up his nose with a frantic finger and stepped between them.

Andrew said, "Guys, guys, we can fix this. We just need to break into the impound lot using a localized EMP and a grappling hook. Like Batman in Detective Comics issue 426!"

Warren rounded on him and screamed, "Shut up, Andrew! Life isn't a comic book! We have real problems now!"

Andrew shrank back from the yelling, and his gaze dropped to the bottom of the basement stairs. He blinked, because he was looking at a flat concrete wall.

Andrew said, his voice trembling with confusion, "Guys... where did the stairs go?"

All three of them looked up, and Willow was coming down invisible stairs, one step at a time, the air around her heavy black boots shimmering like the heat off a desert road.

Jonathan’s face went white, and he scrambled backward and tripped over a pile of computer cables, his hands going up as his voice shot high with panic.

Jonathan said, "Oh crap, she is here to arrest us!"

Willow looked down at them, her pitch-black eyes locked on Warren without blinking, the last trace of mercy draining out of her face, and she spoke in a cold, hollow voice.

Willow said, "No.... No one is going to arrest you... not anymore."

She raised one hand and flicked her wrist sharply.

Every thick electric cable, internet cord, and extension cord in the room twisted and snaked across the floor like living vipers, wrapping Andrew and Jonathan up from head to toe and cinching tight as the two of them toppled onto their sides, unable to move, until a sickening, heavy pop of crushing bone cut off their screams.

Willow turned slowly and stepped down onto the concrete floor, her eyes fixed on Warren.

He pressed his back against a server rack, eyes wide in animal fear, and his voice cracked as he screamed.

Warren said, "You can't do that! You're not a bad guy! Are you crazy?!"

Warren dug desperately into his pocket and pulled out a dark, jagged runed stone, holding it out flat on his palm and aiming it at Willow like a weapon.

Warren snarled, "Oh, I got something for you, bitch!"

A bright, crackling cyan light started to glow around the runed stone as Warren opened his mouth to chant, but before a single syllable could leave his lips, Willow gestured sharply with two fingers, and a sheet of smooth, solid flesh grew seamlessly over his mouth, erasing his lips entirely.

The runed stone slipped out of his hand and clattered onto the concrete, its light dying at once, and Warren’s hands flew up to claw at the smooth skin where his mouth had been, muffled screams humming under the flesh.

Willow’s eyes flicked to the spot on the floor where Katrina’s body had lain, and a ghostly, transparent after-image settled over it: Warren, holding the champagne bottle over the dead girl.

Willow’s head turned slowly back to Warren, and her voice was low, absolute, and cruel.

Willow said, "So, Warren, you like to kill girls. You think it's funny? Well, now I am going to show you what I think is funny."

She grabbed Warren by the back of his hair with her left hand and yanked his head back so he had to look into her eyes, and then, quickly and effortlessly, she drove her right hand straight into his chest.

There was no blood, only a violent surge of dark, crackling purple energy where her hand went through his sternum, and she drew her hand back out holding his small, pulsing heart, and Warren dropped dead to the floor like a puppet with its strings cut.

Willow stared at the heart in her hand with a cold, empty curiosity, while the horror of what she had done registered only somewhere far away.

Willow said in a bored voice, "So you actually had one of these after all, huh?"

She opened her fingers and let the heart fall onto Warren’s still body.

She looked around the room at the carnage, three bodies, two of them grotesquely mangled, and all of them silent.

Then the adrenaline and the dark magic dropped out of her all at once, and the drain of it hit her so hard that she collapsed to her knees with her hands flat on the cold concrete, retching, and she stayed there for a long moment, crying silently, her black eyes leaking ordinary tears.

She got up slowly, staggering, and made her way into the Trio’s cramped basement bathroom, bumping her shoulder against the wall on the way in, and caught the sink with both hands, knocking bottles of lotion and hair spray onto the floor.

She bent over the basin and vomited into it, twice, and a few strands of her bright red hair fell loose and clung to the wet porcelain.

She wouldn’t look at herself in the mirror. She turned on the faucet and rinsed out her mouth and the sink, washed her hands and face, and cupped her palms under the running water to gulp it down cold until her breathing steadied. Then she turned off the tap, pushed herself up straight, and flicked off the bathroom light, steadier than before, never noticing the red strands left behind on the porcelain.

She walked back out into the lair and raised her trembling hands, and a silent purple flame washed over the three bodies, leaving no scorch marks on the floor, and in an instant it reduced them to a fine layer of white calcium powder.

Later that night, back in the Summers master bedroom, Willow stood beside the bed in the soft silver light of the moon, still dressed in her dark magic leather, and looked down at Tara, deeply and peacefully asleep, her chest rising and falling slowly under the heavy duvet.

Willow stared down at the woman she loved, the woman she had just killed three people to protect, and silent tears spilled over her dark-veined cheeks as the guilt pressed down on her until she could hardly stand under it.

Slowly she raised her wrist and whispered in Latin, her voice breaking, "Ex mea voluntate et potestate, obliviscor."

A thick dark vine, Lethe’s Bramble, grew out of the shadows and wrapped itself tightly around her wrist, pulsing with deep, poisonous magic, and then it pulled back and sank completely into her pale skin.

In the dark of the bedroom, it was as if an X-ray had lit up the inside of Willow’s chest, a mingling of blue and purple veins reaching through her chest and back like roots, connected and alive, until a black hole opened in the middle of them, and a place that had been full of reaching, connected roots sat empty and damaged.

Tara slept on peacefully for a few quiet seconds.

On Willow’s side of the bed, the heavy black leather, the corset, and the dark veins were all gone, and Willow stood there in a cute oversized Winnie the Pooh t-shirt and cotton boxers, her face clear of guilt and fear and memory, and a small, innocent smile curved her lips as she let out a long, contented sigh.

She climbed carefully into bed beside Tara and slid in close, snuggling her face gently into the back of Tara’s hair and breathing in her scent. She had no idea at all of what she had done.

Over Tara’s shoulder, Willow’s right eye was still open, and slowly, peacefully, it fluttered shut.

After that there was no sound at all in the master bedroom but the slow, even breathing of the two of them in the dark.
