Buffy the Vampire Slayer. Season seven, episode 15. The Shallow End.

PREVIOUSLY

Giles said, "Last time on Buffy the Vampire Slayer..."

The group had moved camp to the Sunnydale Public Pool, and the Slayer Scythe was secured. Kennedy had been rescued and drafted. The high school was now sealed behind a glowing purple shield, guarded by Bringers. And the First Evil was still watching.

---

CHAPTER 1
Kennedy Wakes

It was the morning after the shield had gone up over the high school, after a long night of keeping watch and waiting for Giles and the crystal Buffy had asked him to bring, and in the office of the Sunnydale Public Pool a warm desk lamp was on, and a cool shaft of daylight cut through the half-open blinds of the side window that faced the parking lot. Cigarette smoke drifted lazily through the light. Through the open doorway behind them, the water of the main pool showed, its gentle ripples always moving. The lap and echo of it never stopped, and the fluorescent lights hummed. The office desks were a mess, supply boxes were stacked around the room, and Mr. Gordo sat on a side table in the corner.

Faith was slouched on the old couch with her legs kicked up and crossed at the ankles on the edge of the coffee table. She wore an open denim jacket over a black tank, black tight jogging bottoms, and black leather boots with bright yellow laces that caught the light. Her hair was semi-messy, a fading yellow-green bruise still lay across the bridge of her nose where the Turok-Han's skull had broken it, healing fast the way a Slayer's hurts did, and the cut above her eyebrow had closed to a thin pink line, while her purple-blue ash eyeshadow was fresh and her lips were bold with red gloss. She held a motorcycle magazine in her right hand, gripped from the bottom middle, and turned a page slowly with her left thumb.

She took a long, relaxed drag on her cigarette. The cherry glowed bright, and the smoke curled up into the beam of light. Her denim jacket rustled softly as she settled deeper into the couch. She flicked the ash onto the office floor with a casual snap of her wrist, and her eyes never left the magazine. She was calm, almost bored.

Kennedy was passed out in the large, worn green fabric chair to Faith's right. Her purple Mountain Dew t-shirt was twisted from sleep, and she wore dark brown cargo pants and black leather calf-high boots. She had no makeup on. Her long dark hair was pulled back, but sleep had pulled half of it loose into a mess. Her head had lolled heavily to the right, and her mouth hung slightly open.

Then Kennedy took a sudden, sharp breath and sat bolt upright, gasping.

Faith slowly lowered the magazine just enough to look over the top edge. She gave a small half-smirk, and her eyes were calm but protective.

Faith said, low, warm and smoky, "Welcome back, kiddo."

For a moment nobody spoke. Cigarette smoke drifted, the pool water echoed, and Kennedy's confusion settled on her face.

Tara came in from the pool doorway in a soft, oversized cardigan that swayed as she walked. She left her white-oak staff leaning against the doorframe. In both hands she held a cold can of 7-Up, beaded with condensation.

Tara said, with a soft, gentle stutter, "Here…"

Kennedy's eyes darted, still scared and startled. She tucked her knees tight under her chin, and the fabric of her cargo pants stretched. Her right hand reached out timidly, the fingers trembling a little, and she took the can. The tab popped loudly. She gulped it down fast, her throat working, and then a loud, unashamed burp echoed through the little office.

Faith said, her smirk widening, teasing but kind, "Wow… look at that. Got a little piggy over here."

Tara sat carefully on the arm of the green chair beside Kennedy. She slowly took Kennedy's right hand in both of hers and stroked it gently with her thumb.

Tara said, with a soft, warm, reassuring stutter, "You're aw aw awake… that's good. We we we we were worried about you…"

Kennedy looked back and forth between Tara and Faith, her eyes wide, confused and searching. Her knees were still tucked up, and the can was clutched in her left hand.

Kennedy said, her voice small, raw and overwhelmed, "I have so many questions…"

The three of them stayed like that for a while. Faith still held the magazine loosely, amused and protective. Tara still held Kennedy's hand, her face full of worry and care. Kennedy looked from one to the other, completely lost, while the pool water lapped somewhere beneath it all.

Outside in the parking lot, the morning sun was bright, and it threw long shadows across the lot. Warm gold light lay against the cool concrete of the pool building.

A plain white van with no windows along its sides, the one Giles had rented at the little Sunnydale airport less than an hour before, pulled in and stopped hard, its tires crunching on the gravel, and the engine cut off with a heavy tick.

Giles stepped out of the driver's seat first and straightened his tie with both hands, a precise Watcher's habit. He leaned back into the van, grabbed a leather briefcase from the passenger seat, and shut the door with his hip. Then he started toward the pool entrance with purpose. He wore his classic tweed jacket, slacks and glasses, and his tie was now perfectly straight.

The side door of the van rattled open, and Anya and Xander stepped out together. They were rumpled from travel, and the weight of the moment showed in the way they stood. Anya wore a bright yellow sundress with a red cherry print. It was wrinkled and creased from the flight, the hem a little uneven, with a small coffee stain on her left hip. She wore practical sandals, and her hair was half up in a clip. Her airsick pallor was fading into relief. Xander wore a black turtleneck stretched across a broader chest and shoulders, black cargo pants and boots. His body was stronger now, more filled out, and his eyes were steady, mature and confident.

They fell into step behind Giles, and the three of them walked toward the entrance in loose formation. Giles was in the center with his briefcase swinging. Anya fidgeted with her strap, and Xander scanned the building with a new kind of confidence. The pool building loomed over them, and the distant echo of water carried out through the doors.

CHAPTER 2
The Reunion

Out in the main pool area the steel shutters were still down. Around their edges, thin lines of morning daylight slipped in high on the walls, and the overhead fluorescent banks reflected off the deep blue water. Soft patterns of rippling light danced across the concrete walls and across everyone's faces. The group sat casually around folding tables and in chairs at the pool's edge, near the shallow end. The Scythe was propped against a table leg, and Mr. Gordo sat upright in the center of the table like a silent observer.

Buffy wore a white tank top, fitted, with a slight stretch across her chest from the pregnancy, dark grey combat pants and leather boots. Willow wore a black jacket with silver trim, a dark blue undershirt, and black leggings under a knee-length black skirt with a subtle glitter that caught the light. Spike wore his usual black leather duster, a black undershirt and boots, with a cigarette tucked behind his ear. Angel wore his classic black leather jacket, a black shirt and dark pants. Dawn wore a pink hoodie with the hood down, blue jeans and white sneakers. Riley and Sam wore tactical vests over their civilian clothes, with their rifles leaning nearby. Lucian sat near the table in a tactical vest and practical boots.

The doors from the parking lot swung open with a heavy clack of the push bar. Giles led the way with his briefcase in his right hand, and Xander and Anya were right behind him.

The seated group reacted at once.

Willow, Riley, Sam, Buffy and Dawn all stood in one fluid wave, chairs scraping on the concrete. Spike and Angel stayed seated, but they leaned forward, their eyes alert.

Riley stepped forward first. He gave Giles a firm military handshake and a quick clap on the shoulder, then did the same with Xander. Sam mirrored him with Anya and gave her a brief, warm hug.

Lucian rose from his seat near the table with steady, controlled movements. His vest rustled softly as he pushed his chair back a little with his left leg. He stepped forward with a purposeful stride, his boots firm on the wet concrete, and held out his right hand, palm open, the fingers relaxed but ready. He met Giles's hand in a firm, deliberate shake. The grip was solid, with no hesitation, their hands clasped at eye level. Their eyes met directly. Lucian's gaze was steady, focused and stoic, his jaw set with a quiet intensity, his shoulders square and his posture upright.

Giles returned the firm shake. After he let go, he placed his left hand on Lucian's right shoulder in a solid, fatherly pat. He looked first at Buffy with a knowing, approving glance, and then back at Lucian.

Giles said, "Good lad. I see my judgement of you was right…"

Lucian held his eyes for a moment and gave a single stoic nod. He accepted the compliment without flourish and without a smile, only a slight shift in his posture to show the words had landed, his eyes steady and respectful. Then he lowered his right hand slowly and let it return to his side.

Dawn rushed in and gave Anya a big squeeze of a hug that bunched up her dress, then gave Xander a quick side hug.

Willow hugged Xander tightly, her glitter skirt sparkling as she turned, and then she hugged Giles, longer and full of feeling.

Buffy waited half a second, her eyes glistening, and then she stepped into Giles's arms.

Giles dropped his briefcase with a soft thud. He wrapped both arms fully around Buffy and pressed her firmly to his chest, one hand on the back of her head and the other between her shoulder blades. He held on for a long time. Buffy buried her face in his tweed with her eyes shut tight, and for the first time in days her shoulders relaxed. They swayed gently, their clothes creasing, their boots shifting on the concrete.

Xander said, grinning, his voice warm but cocky, with classic Xander timing, "We are here. Someone ordered another apocalypse?"

Soft, relieved laughter rippled through the group. Shoulders shook, heads nodded, and hands stayed on arms and shoulders. Dawn wiped her eye quickly. Willow smiled through glassy eyes. Buffy pulled back from Giles just far enough to look at all three of them, and a small, grateful smile broke across her face.

Slowly everyone settled back into their seats or pulled up extra folding chairs. Giles kept one hand on Buffy's shoulder a moment longer. Anya smoothed her cherry-print dress self-consciously. Xander rolled his shoulders, clearly enjoying the stronger build that showed under his turtleneck. Spike lit the cigarette from behind his ear with a quiet flick and watched in silence. Buffy's rejection in the locker room was still fresh, and he laid claim to nothing. Angel gave Giles a small, respectful nod from across the pool's edge. The water rippled on in the background, and the light danced over every face.

For just a moment, without thinking, Buffy's hand came to rest low on her stomach while Giles's stayed warm on her shoulder, and the relief of the three of them being there sat heavy and warm over the whole group, with nothing under it but the slow lap of the pool water.

CHAPTER 3
The Crystal

Buffy looked straight at Giles, Xander and Anya with the calm, expectant look of a leader.

Buffy said, "Did you bring what I asked?"

Giles nodded once. He reached into the inside pocket of his tweed jacket and pulled out a cyan crystal, eight inches long and three inches wide. Its sides were smooth and polished, and it glowed faintly with a soft, off-blue aura that pulsed gently.

Willow's eyes went wide. She jumped up from her seat, her glitter skirt flashing, and practically snatched the crystal out of his hands with both of hers.

Willow said, excited and breathless, her voice cracking with relief, "You have no idea how bad we need this right now…"

Buffy watched her, and a soft, nostalgic smile spread across her face. Her eyes were warm, remembering the old days of Sunnydale magic, and she tilted her head a little.

Willow dropped straight to one knee on the concrete floor to the right of the group. She pulled a thick piece of white chalk from her jacket pocket and started drawing quick, precise symbols and circles, ignoring everyone around her. The chalk scraped loudly and puffed up little clouds of dust, and the knees of her leggings pressed into the floor.

Dawn turned to Xander, wide-eyed and honestly impressed. She stepped closer and jabbed him playfully in the chest with one finger, and it landed with a solid thump on his turtleneck.

Dawn said, "You look so strong now… we all figured being a supervisor in Texas would have made you… weaker?"

Xander said, grinning, proud, flexing a little under his shirt, "I have been training. Taking Judo and Karate, and weight lifting between work. Last three months before the wedding of Buffy? That's what I have been doing. No more clumsy Xander!"

Anya was still smoothing her cherry-print sundress. She gave a perfectly timed side-eye, deadpan and blunt.

Anya said, "Training? Please. I trained him in the bedroom for three years and he still couldn't find the right hole half the time."

Short, genuine laughter burst out of the group, and hands flew up to cover mouths as it shook through them. Dawn snorted, Buffy bit her lip to hold it in, and even Riley smirked. Anya looked pleased with herself.

Buffy glanced to her right, where Willow was still crouched on the floor, drawing intently.

Buffy said, soft and fond, "How soon until it's ready?"

Willow said, without looking up, the chalk still scraping fast, "It's almost there… give me another minute…"

Angel stood smoothly and walked over to Giles. They shook hands, firm and respectful, with a small nod of shared history. Then Angel turned and shook Xander's hand with a strong grip, and they nodded to each other with mutual respect.

Spike stayed seated exactly where he was, with his arms crossed and his cigarette lit now. He watched the whole reunion in silence from the edge of the group, and his leather duster creaked when he shifted once.

In front of them all, Willow kept drawing, with the crystal glowing on the concrete beside her, while the group chuckled softly and the pool's reflections danced across every face, and Spike sat apart at the back and watched them all with his cigarette burning down between his fingers.

---

CHAPTER 4
Barrier Price

Willow knelt and placed both palms flat on top of the cyan crystal. Her eyes closed, and her breathing steadied. The crystal began to shimmer at once with a warm cyan glow, and the glow grew stronger.

Willow said, quiet and focused, in an ancient cadence, "Dea foci et domus, te obsecramus. Da nobis spatium sacrum a malis praesentia et conspectu."

She was calling on the goddess of hearth and home for a sacred space, hidden from the presence and the sight of evil, and the crystal answered with a high-pitched, crystalline hum that kept building and building until Willow gently rolled it away from herself across the concrete with her fingertips.

There was a sudden bright cyan flash. When the light faded, a perfect burn mark in the exact shape and size of the crystal was seared into the cement floor. Its edges glowed faintly, and then they settled to a matte black.

Willow let out a breath and looked up at Buffy with a small smile, proud but tired.

Willow said, "It's done… But the barrier has a price. Same as before. Magic inside the bubble is all scattery — limited. But it cannot listen in… and it cannot see us."

Buffy looked down at her and gave a soft, concerned nod.

Buffy said, "How long?"

Willow said, "About three days. This time I'm sure."

For a moment Willow's face was quiet. Her hand trembled slightly, and she hid it by brushing her hair back. There was pride in her face, and an awareness of what it had cost. Little motes of cyan light still floated gently around the group.

---

CHAPTER 5
War Council

Buffy nodded once, decisive. With a small tilt of her head and an open palm, she motioned everyone toward the folding table near the shallow end.

Riley, Angel, Sam, Giles, Xander, Anya and Buffy all moved to the table and sat, chairs scraping on the concrete. Mr. Gordo still sat upright in the exact center of the table, facing Spike's empty chair like a tiny pink judge.

Buffy sat at the head of the table and looked slowly at each of them in turn. She made deliberate eye contact, calm and in charge, and for a moment her hand rested low on her stomach, protective.

Buffy said, "Now… for the plan."

Buffy said, "First thing, and this one isn't up for a vote. I said it yesterday and I'm saying it again, because we've got new people in the building, same rule. Nobody is ever alone. Not for a second. Not on watch, not on a snack run, not when you're sleeping, not even if you shit, shower or shave. You go in pairs, you come back in pairs, and if your buddy wanders off, you go get them and drag them back by the ear. The First loves somebody by themselves. That's when it shows up wearing a face you miss and starts talking. So we don't hand it one. Ever. And yes, I mean the bathroom. Get comfy with each other, people."

Anya lifted one hand halfway off the table, the creased cherry-print sleeve of her sundress sliding down toward her elbow, and she raised it with the perfectly reasonable expression of a businesswoman about to object to a clause in a contract.

Anya said, "I would like it noted that I have been shaving alone for over a thousand years and nothing has ever once tried to eat me during it."

Xander's shoulders shook under the black turtleneck as he ducked his head to hide the grin, Riley coughed into his fist, and Buffy kept her face perfectly straight for a full second before the corner of her mouth gave her away, and then she pointed one finger down the table at Anya with the dry, patient look of a teacher calling on the back row.

Buffy said, "Then it's overdue. Pairs."

The cyan light off the pool caught every face, and Mr. Gordo sat in the middle of it all. The water lapped softly below them, and Buffy's gaze stayed steady.

CHAPTER 6
Taste of Girls

In the pool office the lamp cast soft pools of amber light, and a cool cyan glow bled in through the open doorway from the main pool area. Somewhere out there the water lapped and echoed, and the fluorescent lights hummed.

Kennedy sat in the large green fabric chair, eating a hot dog with her left hand under the bun and her right hand over the top. She took thoughtful bites and chewed slowly. Her purple Mountain Dew t-shirt was still rumpled from sleep, and a small rainbow pride pin showed on the left side of her chest. Tara sat beside her on the wide arm of the chair, one hand resting lightly on Kennedy's forearm in quiet support. Faith leaned against the desk across from them with her arms crossed over her chest, the worn denim of her jacket bunching softly at the shoulders.

Faith said, low, smoky and cocky with that signature edge, her head tilted as she watched Kennedy eat, "So that's it, sister. You're a Potential. Me and B were too, once. Your entire life now is that. Monsters, apocalypses, the whole damn circus. No going back to normal."

Kennedy swallowed her bite and took a sip from the 7-Up can with a loud gulp. Then she leaned forward with her elbows on her knees and ran both hands through her hair from back to front. Her shoulders hunched, and then she sat back with a deep, heavy sigh. She fidgeted with the hot dog wrapper in her lap, picking at it nervously, and her left hand brushed the little rainbow pin on her shirt.

Faith's eyes flicked down, and she noticed the pin. She raised an eyebrow, and her voice turned teasing and direct, with a low chuckle.

Faith said, "That mean you like girls, huh?"

Kennedy glanced down at the pin, then back up. She took another bite, chewed, and wiped her mouth with the back of her hand. She sat up a little straighter, and her voice was direct and unapologetic, with a hint of challenge.

Kennedy said, "Yeah. I like the taste of girls. Always have."

Faith's smirk widened into a full, cocky grin. She uncrossed her arms, pushed off the desk and took a casual step forward, open and confident. One hand went to her hip, and the other gestured loosely. She tilted her head, her eyes sparkling with dark humor.

Faith said, with a low, approving laugh, her eyes meeting Kennedy's, raw, with no filter, "Kid, I like the taste of everything. Once I start eating it, it all belongs to me. Girls, boys, whatever's on the menu. Life's short in this line of work. You take what you want."

Tara watched quietly, with a soft, understanding smile. She leaned in a little closer to Kennedy and gave her forearm a gentle squeeze. She didn't make a big speech. She was just being Tara.

Tara said softly, with a small nod, open and reassuring, "It's… it's okay. You don't have to explain it or anything. Me and Willow… we've been together almost three years now."

Kennedy's face softened a little. She looked between them, and a small, wry laugh escaped her. She took another sip of her soda and set the can down. Then she leaned back, more relaxed, with one arm hanging over the arm of the chair. Her eyes were a little brighter, and her voice picked up a playful edge.

Kennedy said, lighter, with a half-smile and one eyebrow raised, "Damn. You make it sound simple."

Faith grinned. She reached over and flicked Kennedy's shoulder playfully with two fingers, and she shifted her weight with that Faith swagger. Her voice stayed cool and grounded.

Faith said, "It is. Be yourself. No one here's gonna give you shit about it. You like girls? Good for you. Own it. We got bigger monsters to worry about."

Kennedy chuckled softly and nodded. She picked up the hot dog again and took a more confident bite. Her free hand touched the pin again, this time with a little more ownership, and her shoulders dropped as some of the tension eased.

Kennedy said, thoughtful but stronger, looking at Faith with growing respect, and then at Tara, "Yeah… okay. Still feels unreal, but… thanks. Both of you."

Tara gave Kennedy's shoulder another gentle squeeze, her face full of quiet warmth. Faith nodded once, satisfied, and leaned back against the desk with her arms loosely crossed and a small, approving smirk.

Tara said softly, "You're not alone in any of it."

Faith said, firm but kind, "Damn right."

Tara said softly, "Excuse me…"

Tara stood quietly and gave Kennedy's shoulder one last reassuring squeeze. Then she walked out of the office toward the main pool area, her cardigan swaying, and left Faith and Kennedy alone.

Kennedy watched her go. Her fear was easing into a reluctant acceptance, and there was a small spark of connection in her face. She held the hot dog in both hands, and the soda can sat on the armrest. The distant echo of the pool water swelled. Faith watched Kennedy with a knowing, protective look.

CHAPTER 7
Coffee and Vision

A faint cyan glow still breathed up off the crystal's burn mark on the deck. Morning light pushed in around the edges of the steel shutters in long, hazy rays. The pool's reflections danced across faces, the table, the mugs and the Scythe's blade, and the steam from the coffee caught the light.

Tara came in from the office doorway carrying a wooden tray loaded with exactly ten steaming mugs of coffee and a small plate of sugar cookies. Steam curled up from the mugs, and the tray wobbled a little over the concrete floor. She wore her soft, oversized cream cardigan over a pale lavender top, beige linen pants and black ankle boots. Her soft, caring smile moved over the group.

She offered mugs and cookies to Riley first, who took his with a firm grip and a nod, and then to Sam, who gave the same nod back. She gave Willow a loving look and a brief brush of her hand on the sleeve of Willow's jacket. With Buffy she lingered, warm, quietly reading how she was holding herself. Giles received his with both hands, precise, his mind already elsewhere. The rest of the mugs and cookies made their way to Xander, Anya, Dawn, Lucian and Tara herself. Dawn jumped up to help pass two of the mugs and grabbed a cookie for herself, bouncing lightly on her sneakers while she set Mr. Gordo upright with her free hand. Lucian quietly unfolded a small tactical map on the edge of the table, and he steadied a folding chair with his boot so it wouldn't wobble.

Willow now held the Scythe across her lap, and her glitter skirt sparkled in the cyan light.

Giles said, looking straight at Willow with a Watcher's quiet concern, his glasses glinting, "We must be cautious going forward… Even though it cannot hear us or haunt our area, it knows we are coming soon."

Willow was breathing harder. A light sweat beaded on her forehead, and she rocked gently back and forth in her chair, gripping the Scythe tightly across her lap. Her skirt shifted with each rock, and her jacket sleeves rode up a little.

Riley said, grinning, decisive, "That is why I am bringing a small army."

Sam said, grinning right back, "Damn right!"

They high-fived with a sharp clap. Dawn cheered softly while she nibbled her cookie and slid extra sugar packets toward Giles. Lucian gave a quiet, approving nod while he kept straightening the corner of his map. Spike blew out smoke from his seat, his leather duster creaking as he shifted his weight.

Spike said, in a sarcastic drawl, one eyebrow raised, "Yeah right…"

Buffy sipped from her mug, the steam rising past her face, and her eyes stayed on Willow with growing worry. Willow's chest was rising sharply now. Then the mugs, the cups and the whole tray on the table began to vibrate. Ceramic rattled against wood, coffee sloshed, and the sugar cookies shifted on the plate.

Everyone stood up quickly from their chairs, except Angel and Spike, who stayed seated but leaned forward, alert. Buffy reached out on instinct to touch Willow's shoulder, but Tara stepped in fast, her cardigan flaring.

Tara said, with a quick, protective stutter, "No — don't touch her!"

Buffy pulled her hand back slowly, her eyes wide.

Buffy said, "Got it… live wire…"

Willow's eyes snapped open, and suddenly she was completely normal, as if nothing had happened. She blinked and looked around at everyone standing, honestly confused, and her skirt settled as she sat up straight.

Willow said, "Something wrong?"

Buffy said, "Yeah, you were acting… all possessed there for a second. You okay?"

Willow nodded, still a little dazed.

Willow said, "Yeah… that was a rush…"

Tara crossed her arms at Willow. Willow saw it and hunched her shoulders a little. Then she slowly peeled her fingers off the back hilt of the Scythe one by one, and it took real effort. The leather creaked, and her knuckles went white and then relaxed. Buffy gently took the weapon back in her right hand and held it down by the back handle, with the blade facing the floor.

Willow said, "It showed me so many things… it's all a blur really… but it showed me something interesting. I can use the weapon to activate Slayers."

Tara's brow furrowed with quiet worry. Giles took off his glasses and polished them once.

Giles said, "Isn't that dangerous? I mean more along the lines of cosmic terror?"

Willow shook her head and stood up, more excited now, her glitter skirt flashing as she rose.

Willow said, "No — it's about telling the universe that Faith has died, then Kennedy, then Dawn and so on… and each Slayer in line will be activated thinking the other has died."

Willow stood taller, her energy building.

Willow said, "It's really easy actually… well the power won't be… but you know what I mean. I can do this."

Around the table Dawn kept nibbling her cookie, Lucian folded his map away, Anya brushed crumbs off her dress and Spike let out a slow breath of smoke, while Tara's arms stayed crossed and Buffy kept a steady hold on the Scythe, and Willow's excitement hung in the air over the last faint rattle of the mugs.

CHAPTER 8
Dawn in the Line

Giles reached out and placed a concerned hand gently on Willow's shoulder.

Giles said, "I don't know about lying to the universe, Willow…"

Willow backed away a little and shrugged his hand off with a small, defensive roll of her shoulder.

Tara stepped forward at once. She placed both hands softly on Willow's shoulders and looked her straight in the eyes, her cardigan bunching gently at the elbows, warm and steady.

Tara said, soft and caring, "Sweetie… you said Dawn… was going to be one?"

Willow looked down at the ground for a moment, and then slowly back up at Tara.

Willow said, "Yeah… I said that. I've known for a while now… I just… I just didn't think it was okay to tell anyone…"

Quiet shock crossed Buffy's face, and her eyes widened slightly. Willow turned to look straight at her.

Willow said, "Since Dawn was made from your blood… it only makes sense really…"

Buffy sank back into her folding chair. The words had hit her hard, and one hand came to rest low on her stomach without her thinking about it. Beside her, Dawn stepped quickly closer, protective, tugging her hoodie sleeve down over her hand. Lucian placed a quiet, steadying hand on the edge of the table near Buffy's chair.

Giles wagged a warning finger at Willow as he spoke.

Giles said, "Activating a few may be safe… but not the entire line all at once. I will not allow that. The cosmic fracture that could potentially cause is astronomical!"

Tara nodded softly in agreement, her hands still resting gently on Willow's shoulders. Willow sighed and finally gave in.

Willow said, "Fine… we will do the two we have then… Kennedy and Dawn…"

Willow looked at Tara.

Willow said, "Where is the new girl anyways?"

Tara said, "They are in the office area still… she is recovering still…"

Willow nodded.

Willow said, "Oh…"

For a moment nobody moved much. Dawn still hovered protectively near Buffy, and Lucian quietly slid a chair back into place. Breaths overlapped, clothes shifted, and the pool water lapped beneath the weight of it all.

At the same time, across the hall in the men's locker room, which nobody had claimed, the overhead fluorescents were warm and a soft haze of steam hung in the air, cool daylight came in from the high windows, and water droplets sparkled on the tiles and on the white plastic curtains that divided its eight shower stalls, a bigger, barer room than the three-stall one the women had used the day before.

Faith was showering in the stall right next to Kennedy's. The water poured steadily over her as she soaped her arms and shoulders in slow, relaxed circles. She turned a little under the spray so the water hit her back and ran down in rivulets, and suds slid across her skin. She talked casually over the sound of the running water, and her voice carried easily between the stalls.

Faith said, "So you see it's like that… you just got to accept it, because it's really happening to you whether you want it to or not…"

Kennedy washed her hair with both hands, working the shampoo into thick suds that ran down her back and shoulders. Water streamed over her head and face as she tilted back to rinse, her hands moving through her long wet hair. Her voice was small, still taking it all in, and there was a nervous energy in the way she moved.

Kennedy said, "I get that… But it's just so unreal… all of this… the monsters… the world might end?"

Faith chuckled lightly as she reached for more soap and turned all the way around under the spray, the water hitting her front and then her back.

Faith said, "Yeah, you get used to it… or at least you get used to the fact that 'normal' is just code for 'the apocalypse hasn't hit yet this week.' Monsters are easy. It's the paperwork and the bad coffee that really wear you down."

Kennedy paused in the middle of rinsing, with one hand on the wall of the stall for balance, the water still pouring over her shoulders and back. She let out a small, surprised half-laugh, full of disbelief, and shook her head as the suds washed away.

Kennedy said, lighter but still overwhelmed, "Bad coffee? That's what you're worried about? I feel like I just got dropped into the middle of a horror movie with no script."

Faith said, teasing but warm, soaping lower and stepping side to side under the water, droplets flying, "Kid, the script is 'survive Tuesday.' Everything else is bonus. And hey, at least now you get to hang with the cool kids who actually know how to throw a punch... or a party. Perks of the gig."

Faith jerked her chin toward Kennedy without even looking at her and pointed at the second pizza with two fingers.

Faith said, "Take the kid with you. B's rule, right? Nobody solo. Go on, Ken, grab the other one and don't eat it all before you get there."

Kennedy was already scooping up the second pizza on its own square of cardboard, balancing it flat on both hands with her nose practically in the cheese, and she fell in beside Lucian as he shouldered through the swinging doors, and Tara watched them go together down the long fluorescent hallway toward the weight room, the tall man in the tactical vest and the barefoot new Slayer, close enough that their elbows nearly bumped, until the doors flapped shut behind them and the sound of their footsteps faded into the endless echo of the pool.

Then it was just Faith and Tara in the big humming kitchen, with six dressed pizzas still waiting on the steel and the ovens ticking in the heat.

Tara picked up the next two pans and slid them into the right oven, twisted its timer to twelve minutes with the same ratcheting tick, then held out the big plastic shaker of oregano to Faith without a word, the way she would have handed Dawn a dish towel, and Faith looked down at it in Tara's hand and then up at Tara's face with one eyebrow raised before she took it, turned it over in her own right hand like a weapon she hadn't been trained on yet, and shook it over the nearest pizza far too hard, so that a little green avalanche of flakes buried one whole side of it.

Tara said, with a soft laugh, "Gentle. It's not a Bringer."

Faith said, "Everything's a Bringer if you hit it hard enough."

But she eased off, and shook the next pass slower and lighter, her tongue caught in the corner of her mouth in concentration, and for a while the only sounds in the room were the patter of the oregano, the freezer droning in its corner and the soft ticking of the ovens as the heat climbed inside them. Then Faith reached across the steel with two fingers, plucked a single cold slice of pepperoni off the last pizza in the row and popped it into her mouth, and Tara swatted the back of her hand lightly without even looking up, and Faith grinned around it and chewed, unrepentant, and set the shaker down.

She didn't pick it back up again; she stood there with both hands resting on the edge of the counter and her eyes on the oregano-dusted pizza in front of her, and the swagger went out of her shoulders slowly, the way it only ever did when something was about to come out of her that she hadn't rehearsed.

Faith said, quieter, not looking at Tara, "Can I tell you something, T?"

Tara said, "Always."

Faith rubbed the back of her neck with her left hand and blew out a breath through her nose, and her eyes went to the painted-over strip of window up by the ceiling and stayed there, as though it were easier to say it to the glass.

Faith said, "Kennedy. She ain't like the ones before. And I ain't who I was with them, either. Back then it was all quick fix, you know? Get in, get what I needed, get gone before anybody got a real good look at me. And I know what it looks like out there. Shower. Weight room. The kid walking around with her neck all chewed up. Everybody's looking at us like it's the same old Faith grabbing whatever's on the menu."

She shook her head once, slowly, and her jaw set.

Faith said, "It ain't. It ain't a quick fix like it looks."

Tara didn't move and didn't speak; she stood very still beside her at the counter with her hands folded loosely in front of her and simply let Faith find the rest of it, the way she had once let her find it on the laundry-closet floor.

Faith said, and her voice caught and roughened on the words, "You remember what I told you? In the laundry closet? That glow. The warm thing in my chest that started in the backyard when you just held me."

Tara said softly, "I remember."

Faith said, "It's there when I touch her, T. When I kiss her. It's right there, same as it was with you. I didn't think it could… I figured the warm stuff went in one box and the rest of it, the hungry stuff, went in another, and you never let 'em touch. But it's all in the same place with her. I put my hand on her and it just… lights up."

She laughed under her breath, short and shaky, and wiped the corner of one eye fast with the knuckle of her thumb as though something had gotten into it, and when she finally turned her head and looked at Tara her dark eyes were wide and wet and almost frightened, younger than Tara had ever seen them.

Faith said, "Scares the hell outta me. But I ain't running from it. Not this time."

Tara's face crumpled into something soft and fierce at the same time, her eyes spilling over, her bottom lip trembling once before she pressed her lips together to steady it, and the pride that came up in her was the plain, enormous pride of a mother watching a child she had once had to teach to say please stand up on her own two feet and say something true. She reached up with her right hand and tucked a loose, damp strand of hair back behind Faith's ear, slow and careful, and then she cupped the side of Faith's face for a moment with her palm warm against Faith's cheek and her thumb brushing the last of the wet away from under her eye.

Tara said, with a soft, trembling stutter, "Oh, sweetie… I'm s so proud of you. That's not a quick fix. That's the real thing. It's supposed to be scary. That's how you know it matters."

Faith's face did something complicated, a crack and a grin and a swallow all at once, and she ducked her head and leaned into Tara's side, and Tara slid her arm around Faith's shoulders over the worn denim of her jacket and pulled her in close, and Faith let her head come to rest against Tara's shoulder, her hair smelling of pool chlorine and cheap shower soap against the sage and rosemary of Tara's.

Faith said, muffled against the cardigan, "Thanks, T."

The timer on the right oven ticked steadily down toward its bell, and neither of them moved away from the counter; they stood side by side at the long steel counter under the humming fluorescent light, the Slayer who had never in her life waited for anything she could simply take and the woman who had taught her how, with Tara's arm around her shoulders and Faith's head on Tara's shoulder, watching through the small square window of the oven door where the cheese had begun to bubble and brown in the orange glow of the burners, and the warmth of it reached out across the steel and found them both, and for once Faith Lehane was content to stand still and let something good finish on its own.
