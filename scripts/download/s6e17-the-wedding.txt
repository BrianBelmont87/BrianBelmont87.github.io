Buffy the Vampire Slayer. Season six, episode 17. The Wedding.

CHAPTER 1
The Shadows and the Roadtrip

Late morning sun stood outside Buffy’s bedroom window, thick pale gold cutting through the glass.

Buffy and Lucian were bathed in the warm morning light, their bodies soft, intimate shapes against the glow.

Lucian was lying on his back, topless, wearing only a pair of grey sweatpants, the black raven tattoo spread across his chest and left shoulder. His long jet-black hair fanned out on the pillow, his bright aqua-blue eyes half-lidded in the gold light. Buffy was straddling his waist, sitting up slightly in a delicate, dark silk bedtime top.

Their hands were raised between them, fingers deeply interlocked, palm-to-palm. They were kissing—a slow, heavy, deeply connected kiss, completely lost in their own world.

Three sharp, rapid knocks hit the bedroom door.

Buffy broke the kiss and dropped her forehead against Lucian’s chest and let out a long, heavy, exasperated sigh.

Before either of them could speak, the door handle clicked.

Willow pushed the door open, already fully dressed in a beautiful, flowing, pale-lavender bridesmaid dress.

Willow stopped short in the doorway with one hand still on the knob, took in the two of them tangled together on the bed in the late-morning light, and rolled her eyes all the way up to the ceiling, crossing her arms over the soft lavender bodice of her dress.

Willow said in an exasperated, all-business voice, "Guys, really? Again? You need to get ready right now. We are running late!"

Buffy turned her head, resting her chin on Lucian’s chest, looking at Willow with a slightly guilty, entirely unrepentant smirk.

Willow said, "Tara and I already helped Dawn with her dress and makeup. Stop with the snogging and get moving."

Willow didn’t wait for a response; she pulled the bedroom door shut with a firm, commanding click.

Buffy and Lucian looked at each other in the quiet, sunlit room. Lucian smiled, his eyes warm and completely devoted.

Lucian said, his voice low, a soft rumble in his chest, "I love you, Buffy Summers."

Buffy leaned down, pressing a soft, lingering kiss against his lips.

Buffy whispered against his mouth, "I love you, Lucian."

Buffy slid effortlessly off his waist, stood up beside the bed, and casually offered her right hand down to him.

Lucian’s thumb brushed her knuckles for one beat before he gripped her hand. With a completely casual, effortless flex of Slayer strength, Buffy pulled Lucian’s entire body weight up off the mattress, popping him onto his feet in one smooth motion.

They turned away from each other to start getting dressed. Behind him, Buffy smoothly slid the dark silk top off over her head, baring her shoulders to the sunlit room.

CHAPTER 2
The Eyeliner and the Elixir

That afternoon, warm, soft vanity lights glowed around a large mirror in Anya and Xander’s bedroom, creating a cozy but slightly chaotic atmosphere.

Anya was wearing a luxurious white silk bridal robe over her undergarments, her hair pinned up but her face bare of makeup.

Halfrek, Hallie to her friends, was lounging elegantly on the edge of the unmade bed, already wearing her bridesmaid dress, a deep, rich maroon that contrasted sharply with Willow’s lavender.

Anya said, pacing frantically with her hands fluttering, "I am perspiring. Brides are not supposed to perspire, Hallie. It ruins the aesthetic of pure, untouched radiance. What if Xander looks at my radiant glow and realizes it’s just sheer, unadulterated panic sweat?"

Halfrek sighed elegantly.

Halfrek said, "Anyanka, stop. You are wearing a trench in the mortal’s hardwood flooring."

Anya ignored her, doing another frantic lap toward the closet and back, rambling on in a hyper-fixated rush.

Anya said, "And the caterers! What if they mixed up the miniature quiches? If I ingest a mushroom, I will break out in hives, and I refuse to say my eternal vows looking like a diseased fruit!"

Halfrek slid elegantly off the bed and stepped directly into Anya’s pacing path, forcing Anya to abruptly stop.

Halfrek raised her right hand, holding a crystal rocks glass filled with a heavy, faintly glowing amber liquid.

Halfrek said firmly, with a gentle smile, "Drink. Hallie’s orders."

Anya didn’t hesitate; she grabbed the crystal glass from Halfrek’s hand, tilted her head back, and aggressively downed the entire glass in a single, sustained gulp.

She slammed the empty glass down onto the vanity table with a sharp clack.

Halfrek stared at the empty glass, her eyes widening slightly in genuine surprise, and she let out a rich, amused laugh.

Halfrek said, "Oh, well... um. That was a little demonic alcohol... but okay. Let’s get you sitting down before gravity becomes a suggestion."

Halfrek firmly grasped Anya by the shoulders and pushed her down into the cushioned chair in front of the vanity mirror.

Halfrek picked up a fine, sharp eyeliner brush and leaned in very close, her face only inches from Anya’s, adopting the intense, hyper-focused posture of a master artist.

Halfrek whispered, eyes darting between the brush and Anya’s eyelid, "Look up at the ceiling. Do not blink."

Anya tilted her chin up as the demonic alcohol hit her bloodstream with supernatural speed. Her rigid, panicked shoulders melted into a deeply relaxed, slightly tipsy slouch, and a warm, goofy smile spread across her face.

Anya said, "You know what I was just thinking about? St. Petersburg. 1881."

Halfrek carefully dragged the brush across Anya’s lash line, her hand perfectly steady.

Halfrek said, "Ah, yes. The Duke who cheated on his wife with the scullery maid."

Anya said, "We spent three weeks cursing him and his entire court to speak only in high-pitched dog whimpers. We laughed so hard in that tavern we knocked over a massive copper samovar. We were practically invincible back then, Hallie."

Halfrek pulled back just an inch, inspecting the sharp wing of the eyeliner. She picked up a soft blending brush and leaned right back in, her expression softening into genuine affection.

Halfrek said, "We were. But Anya... you don’t need to be invincible today. And you don’t need to be nervous."

Anya’s tipsy smile faltered just a fraction, and she looked away from the ceiling, meeting Halfrek’s eyes directly.

Anya said, "You don’t think he’s going to panic? Human men are incredibly fragile, Hallie. They flee at the slightest emotional pressure."

Halfrek lowered the makeup brush and looked directly into Anya’s eyes, her demeanor shifting from a sassy bridesmaid back to a centuries-old demon who read the souls of men.

Halfrek said, "Anyanka, listen to me. I have tasted the intentions of thousands of men. I know exactly what betrayal, cowardice, and rot smell like. I have looked at your Alexander. I have sensed his true intentions."

Halfrek reached out, gently brushing a stray lock of hair behind Anya’s ear.

Halfrek said, "He is a remarkably simple creature, but his devotion to you is entirely absolute. There is no rot in him. He is not going to run."

Anya stared at her old friend as the demonic alcohol buzz gave way to a profound, overwhelming wave of relief, and her eyes welled up with happy, shining tears.

Anya said, "Really?"

Halfrek said, "Really. Now, look back up at the ceiling and do not cry, or you are going to walk down that aisle looking like a raccoon who found a glitter stash."

Anya let out a wet, happy laugh and obediently tilted her head back up toward the ceiling.

CHAPTER 3
The Britney Sing-along

Bright, brilliant midday California sun hung over the coastal highway, and the Pacific Ocean stretched out endlessly to the left, sparkling deep blue.

Inside Joyce’s old sedan, the heavy, iconic bass synth and pop beat of Britney Spears’ "Oops!... I Did It Again" was blasting through the factory speakers.

Willow was behind the wheel, sitting very straight with both hands on it, the seat pulled all the way forward the way she always drove.

The physical toll of her fight with Amy was still visible. Despite a heavy layer of carefully applied concealer, the skin around her eyes was slightly red and bruised, and the thin cut across her forehead from the park showed as a faint pink line beneath the side-swept fringe Tara had pinned over it. Her lower left lip had a faint purple bruise where she had hit the dirt. As her hands gripped the steering wheel, tiny cuts and red scabs were visible across her knuckles.

Tucked neatly onto the dashboard right above the steering wheel was a pair of pristine, elbow-length lavender silk bridesmaid gloves, waiting to cover the battle damage once they parked.

Willow was not singing; she was staring at the winding coastal road, a soft, peaceful smile on her face. She bobbed her head slightly to the heavy pop beat, her scabbed thumbs tapping happily against the steering wheel.

Tara was sitting in the passenger seat, wearing her matching lavender dress.

Dawn, Lucian, and Buffy were crammed into the back seat, with Lucian pinned directly in the middle.

Lucian was wearing a sharp, dark suit for the wedding, but his body language was a disaster, because he was entirely out of his element. He was squeezed in tight, trying to take up as little space as possible, his hands clamped awkwardly together between his knees. He was not singing, only staring straight ahead, visibly overwhelmed and entirely unsure of what to do during this intense moment of female bonding.

Dawn and Buffy were completely ignoring his discomfort, out of breath and laughing.

Dawn sang the lyrics at the top of her lungs, bouncing in her seat, "Oops!... You think I’m in love! That I’m sent from above!"

Dawn aggressively pointed her finger right across Lucian’s chest at Buffy for the chorus. Lucian slowly leaned back an inch to avoid getting poked.

Buffy laughed, leaning in over Lucian to belt it right back at Dawn.

Buffy sang, "I’m not that innocent!"

Tara twisted around in the passenger seat, looking back at the Summers sisters. She threw her hands up, joining in on the pure, unadulterated pop-star drama.

Tara sang, "Oops!... I did it again to your heart! Got lost in this game, oh baby!"

Willow glanced over at Tara singing and laughing, then caught Lucian’s terrified, deer-in-the-headlights reflection in the rearview mirror. Willow’s bruised smile widened into a quiet laugh, and the heavy, suffocating dread and the near-death fight at the park felt a million miles away.

At the Northern Cliffs wedding venue, a pristine white wedding tent was set up on the manicured grass near the edge of the cliff. Rows of white folding chairs faced a beautiful floral archway with the Pacific Ocean serving as a massive, sweeping backdrop.

Joyce’s old silver sedan pulled smoothly into the paved parking lot near the estate house, well above the sandy beaches below.

Willow shifted the car into park and cut the engine. The loud pop music cut off instantly, replaced by the muffled, rhythmic sound of the ocean waves crashing against the cliffs far below.

Lucian let out a long, quiet sigh of relief and finally unfolded himself, the first easy moment he’d had since Britney trapped him in the middle seat.

Buffy said, "Okay. We have officially arrived. Nobody tell Anya we were singing Britney, she’ll think we aren’t taking the sanctity of her eternal mortal bondage seriously."

Dawn giggled, already grabbing the door handle.

Willow took a deep breath, reached forward to the dashboard, and picked up the pristine lavender silk gloves. Carefully, wincing just slightly, she worked the silk down over her scraped knuckles and scabbed fingers one at a time, until the soft lavender fabric hid every trace of the previous evening’s fight.

She turned to Tara, her hands now looking perfectly elegant.

Willow said, "Ready?"

Tara smiled, reaching out to gently squeeze Willow’s silk-covered hand.

Tara said, "Ready."

All four doors swung open at once, and Lucian waited for Buffy to climb out first.

The moment the doors opened, a heavy, fresh ocean breeze whipped through the parking lot.

The wind caught the flowing fabric of the girls’ dresses, sending the lavender and pastel materials fluttering dramatically against their legs. Buffy had to reach up and catch her hair to keep it out of her face. Lucian stepped out of the middle seat, finally able to stretch to his full height, and smoothly buttoned his suit jacket against the coastal wind.

They stood together in the bright coastal sun, looking out at the massive white tent and the ocean beyond, ready for the wedding.

CHAPTER 4
The Empath and the Ex

That evening, just after sundown, Angel pulled out of Los Angeles and pointed his black 1967 Plymouth GTX convertible north, top up, headlights cutting a long white path down the dark highway. Sunnydale was about two hours away.

Angel was behind the wheel in his signature black duster over a dark shirt, his hands gripping the steering wheel at a perfect ten-and-two and his jaw locked as he stared straight ahead at the dark highway in the sweep of the headlights, lost in a profound, unyielding, two-and-a-half-century brood.

Lorne sat in the passenger seat beside him, angled comfortably toward the window in a loud, tailored, shimmering emerald-green suit and a silver silk shirt that caught every passing streetlight.

Lorne was holding down the passenger-side sun visor, using the tiny lighted vanity mirror to aggressively reapply a layer of lip gloss.

Lorne said, "I’m just saying, Angel-face, the aura coming off this zip code is an absolute cocktail of anxiety, tulle, and unholy terror. Are we absolutely sure human weddings on top of a Hellmouth are a good idea? Because my empath horns are vibrating like a cheap pager in 1998!"

Angel didn’t blink or turn his head, and his eyes stayed fixed on the white dashes sliding toward him out of the dark.

Angel said, "It’s a wedding, Lorne. Just try to blend in."

Lorne looked down at his shimmering emerald suit, then looked at his own bright green, demonic skin. He looked back at Angel, utterly deadpan.

Lorne said, "Blend in. Right. I’ll just stand near the floral arrangements and pretend I’m a very aggressive fern."

Angel’s grip on the steering wheel tightened. He stared dead ahead down the dark highway toward Sunnydale.

Nearly two hours later, the GTX was far up the coast from Los Angeles, its headlights still carving a long white path through the dark.

Inside the car, the final thirty seconds of Britney Spears’ "Oops!... I Did It Again" was playing through the car radio.

Angel was still gripping the steering wheel as the dark coast slid past outside the windows, and he looked profoundly annoyed by the heavy, bubblegum-pop bass echoing in his car.

Angel narrowed his eyes, took his right hand off the steering wheel, and reached toward the center console to shut off the radio.

Lorne immediately shot his hand out, firmly slapping his green palm over the radio dial, physically blocking Angel.

Lorne said, "Oh, honey, don’t you dare."

Angel glared at him, but he slowly pulled his hand back, letting the empath have his moment.

Lorne leaned his head back against the leather passenger seat, closing his eyes and letting the cool night wind from his cracked window hit his green face. He took a deep, theatrical breath and sang perfectly along with the final track.

Lorne sang, with genuine soulful lounge-singer vibrato, "Oops!... I did it again to your heart... Got lost in this game, oh baby... I’m not that innocent."

The song ended, fading into the ambient sound of the highway wind.

Lorne let out a long, dramatic sigh, opening his eyes and sinking back into the seat.

Lorne said, smoothing the lapels of his emerald suit, "Thank you. After all the negative energy radiating off this Hellmouth zip code, I desperately needed that. It’s like a sonic palate cleanser."

Angel said nothing and kept his eyes locked dead ahead on the winding road.

Lorne said, "So, Angel-face. Two hours of dark highway and a brood you could park a car in. Remind me why we’re really doing this."

Angel said, "Xander’s getting married. He’s hated me ever since high school. I figured it was time to try and bury the hatchet."

Xander had called and asked Angel to find and buy the ring he couldn’t yet afford on his new job: a black opal, because a diamond was too common for Anya, and she was as rare to him as the stone. Angel had paid for it out of the money he’d tucked away over two and a half centuries, and Xander reaching out to him first was a big part of why he thought the hatchet could finally be buried.

Lorne said, "Mm-hmm. And to see her."

Angel said nothing, because there was no point lying to Lorne. The empath had seen straight through him the second he suggested this trip, and they both knew it.

Angel said, "To see her... as well."

He was quiet for a long moment, his eyes never leaving the road.

Angel said, "Buffy."

Lorne shifted slightly in his seat, turning his head to look at Angel’s rigid profile, the playful energy dropping from his face as his empathic senses tuned in to the heavy, complicated aura pouring off the vampire sitting next to him.

Lorne said, his voice softening, entirely sincere, "So. We’re about five minutes out. Have you figured out how we’re going to react to seeing her again? Are we doing the tragic, lingering stares? The stoic, respectful nod? The brooding-vampire-in-the-corner routine?"

Angel didn’t answer right away. He watched the yellow lines on the road blur past beneath the headlights, his hands steady at ten and two, and Lorne could feel him folding whatever he felt down small and tight and putting it away where nobody could reach it.

Angel said, his voice low, "I’m just going to congratulate Xander after I give him the ring. I’ll keep to the shadows, and I’ll stay completely out of her way. It’s not my life anymore."

Lorne watched Angel for another second and gave a slow, knowing, slightly pitying nod, well aware that Angel was lying to himself.

Lorne said, "Right. Keep to the shadows. Sounds like a bulletproof plan, Angel-face."

By the time Angel pulled into the Northern Cliffs parking lot, it was full night over the cliffs, and the wedding tent glowed ahead like a lantern. The heavy coastal wind immediately whipped through the open car door.

CHAPTER 5
The Sandwich and the Scent

Night had taken the cliffs. Soft warm string lights and the spill of lanterns filled the massive white tent; beyond the canvas flaps, the coast was fully dark.

The seating arrangement was an absolute, deeply awkward disaster, with the aisle dividing the two families perfectly.

On the right side sat Anya’s demonic guests: hulking, scaled creatures in ill-fitting tuxedos, horned demons holding tiny wedding programs, and older vengeance demons chatting casually.

On the left side, Xander’s mortal family was huddled together, shifting uncomfortably in their seats, staring wide-eyed across the aisle, aggressively whispering to each other in sheer terror. Notably, the seat designated for Xander’s father at the very front remained heavily empty.

Buffy, Tara, Willow, and Dawn were standing in a small cluster.

Buffy was actively eating a massive, foot-long Subway sandwich loaded with thick hot beef strips and melted cheese. To protect her delicate lavender bridesmaid dress, she had a white cloth napkin tucked tightly under her chin, holding another napkin under the sandwich to catch the grease.

Buffy said, "I’m just saying, if we have to stand up there for an hour, I need protein. Slayers run hot."

Tara smiled, gently adjusting the floral arrangement near the archway.

Tara said, "At least the sandwich does not fight back..."

A sudden, sharp gust of coastal wind cut through the heavy canvas flaps at the back entrance of the tent.

Buffy froze entirely, mid-bite.

The ambient chatter of the wedding guests slowly faded down to absolute silence.

Buffy lowered the sandwich an inch, her hazel eyes widening as her Slayer senses locked onto a specific, ancient, deeply familiar scent carried on the ocean wind.

Buffy slowly turned her head, looking over her shoulder, past the rows of terrified humans and bewildered demons, straight down the long, white carpet runner.

At the back of the tent, the heavy white canvas flaps had parted, and Angel stepped through the gap with one hand still holding the canvas aside. The cool glow of the parking lot lights behind him silhouetted his tall, broad frame for a split second before he stepped fully into the warm lantern light of the tent.

He dropped his hand from the canvas and stood at the end of the aisle in his long black duster, his dark eyes instantly cutting through the crowd and locking dead onto Buffy’s face.

Buffy was completely paralyzed, holding her half-eaten beef sandwich, staring back at him across the length of the tent.

Willow’s eyes went wide, and she quickly slapped her silk-gloved hand over her mouth in sheer shock.

Tara stopped adjusting the flowers, her mouth parting slightly as she looked back and forth between Buffy and Angel in absolute awe of the sudden, heavy tension flooding the room.

Dawn peeked out from behind Buffy’s shoulder, looked at Angel standing at the back of the tent, and then looked at her sister completely frozen in place.

Dawn said, "Uh oh..."

CHAPTER 6
The Handshake and the Choice

The warm lantern light inside the tent felt suddenly heavy and still.

Lucian was on his way over with a clear plastic cup filled with ice and bright orange soda for her to wash down the sandwich. He slowed and then stopped a step away from her, the cold cup sweating in his left hand, looked at Buffy’s frozen face, and then followed her line of sight, turning his head slowly toward the back of the tent.

Angel was walking slowly and deliberately down the white carpeted aisle toward them, his long black duster sweeping just above the floor.

Buffy snapped out of her trance, quickly set the massive Subway sandwich and napkins down onto a nearby empty folding chair, and stepped sideways, placing her body squarely between Lucian and the approaching vampire.

Angel came to a stop just a few feet away and looked down at her.

Buffy said, her voice breathless and soft, caught somewhere between a dreamy whisper and a warning, "Angel..."

Lucian did not hide behind her. He stepped smoothly around Buffy’s shoulder, out into the open in front of Angel. Lucian squared his jaw, raised his right hand, and offered it to Angel in a formal, masculine greeting.

Angel slowly tore his eyes away from Buffy and looked down at Lucian’s extended hand.

Angel reached out and took it, and the two men shook hands firmly, palm to palm, neither giving an inch. Then Angel inhaled softly through his nose, and his face went still. He inhaled a second time, a deeper, more deliberate sniff pulling in the scent of the air directly over Lucian’s shoulder.

Angel’s heightened vampiric senses immediately processed the heavy, lingering scent of Buffy’s sweat, the warm sheets, and the profound, physical intimacy from that morning.

Angel’s dark eyes instantly snapped up to meet Lucian’s, and the stoic, brooding calm vanished, replaced by a cold, hard, ancient predator’s glare.

Without breaking eye contact, Angel violently squeezed Lucian’s hand.

There was a sickening grind of knuckles crushed together under immense, supernatural pressure.

Lucian said, "Ah—!"

The plastic cup in his left hand slipped from his fingers and hit the carpet runner, spilling ice and orange soda across the white fabric and into the grass beside it.

Lucian’s knees buckled, and he dropped hard to both knees on the carpet, trapped in Angel’s crushing grip.

Lucian’s jaw locked against the pain. He did not yank away or break the handshake first, and he kept his eyes on Angel the whole time.

Buffy’s Slayer instincts instantly overrode her shock. She lunged forward and slammed her palm into Angel’s left shoulder, using her raw Slayer strength to shove him backward, and the heavy impact broke Angel’s grip on Lucian’s hand.

The shove left Buffy standing mere inches from Angel, face-to-face with him.

The air between them crackled with absolute, undeniable electricity. Buffy’s chest was heaving as she looked up into Angel’s dark eyes, and for a split second the longing was overwhelmingly obvious. Her lips were parted, and she looked like she was leaning in, turning her head to the left, completely pulled by the gravity of their history into a kiss.

Angel’s breathing hitched as he raised his left hand, his fingers hovering just an inch away from the side of her face, desperate to touch her.

Buffy blinked, and the spell broke. She looked down at Lucian kneeling on the floor, clutching his bruised hand against his chest.

Buffy’s jaw hardened. She took a deliberate, sharp step backward, pulling clean out of Angel’s orbit, and dropped down to her knees beside Lucian.

Buffy said, "Hey. Let me see. I’ve got you."

Buffy snatched up the cup Lucian had dropped, scooped a handful of the spilled ice off the carpet runner, and gently pressed it into the palm of Lucian’s swelling hand, wrapping her own hands carefully around his.

Buffy helped Lucian stand back up and deliberately turned her back on Angel, shutting him out entirely to focus on the man standing beside her.

Angel stood completely frozen, his left hand still hovering uselessly in the empty air where Buffy’s face had just been. He looked at her back, the crushing realization of her choice washing over him.

Lorne was standing a few feet away, clutching the lapels of his shimmering emerald suit. His eyes were wide as his empathic horns processed the massive, chaotic emotional explosion that had just detonated in the room.

Lorne said in his usual soothing voice, "Oh... my..."

Behind him, Willow, Tara, and Dawn stood in absolute shock, none of them daring to make a sound as the weight of what just happened settled over the wedding tent.

CHAPTER 7
The Read and the Rock

Moments later, on the demon side of the tent, the night beyond the canvas cast long, soft shadows across the folding chairs under the tent lights.

Angel and Lorne were entirely surrounded by Anya’s demonic guests. A massive, scaly demon in a too-tight tuxedo shifted uncomfortably in the chair next to Angel, and Angel ignored it.

Angel leaned forward, elbows on his knees, and dropped his head to stare at his hands, looking utterly gutted.

Across the tent, Buffy was still standing with Lucian, Tara, Dawn, and Willow, keeping her back firmly turned toward the demon section.

Angel said, his voice barely above a whisper and meant only for Lorne, "I don’t know what happened. It just... took over."

Lorne sat beside him, gracefully crossing his legs and smoothing out the crease in his emerald suit pants. He looked over at Angel with genuine sympathy.

Angel said, "I caught her scent on him, and suddenly it was just... flashes. Everything we used to be. The way she felt."

Angel slowly clenched his right hand into a fist, staring at the empty air.

Angel said, "And then it just hit me, all at once. I am never going to touch her like that again."

Lorne let out a soft, understanding sigh and turned his head to look across the aisle at the group, his eyes settling on Lucian’s broad shoulders.

Lorne said, "You want a basic read on the guy, Angel-face? I don’t even need him to hit a high C to pick up his frequency. I can tell you exactly what he’s putting out there."

Angel kept his eyes locked on the floor.

Angel said, "Go ahead."

Lorne raised his right hand, elegantly fluttering his green fingers in the air as if he was physically feeling the invisible emotional currents drifting across the wedding tent.

Lorne said, "He is like a solid rock around her ocean. Completely grounded. No dark past trying to drag him under. He is not a monster, he’s not a demon, he doesn’t have a cursed soul... as you obviously already know. He’s just a man who knows exactly what he wants."

Lorne’s empathic horns gave a tiny, almost imperceptible twitch. He raised his eyebrows, his hands doing a flamboyant, expansive wave.

Lorne said, "And boy... the sex they are having—"

Angel said, "That’s enough, Lorne!"

Lorne immediately dropped his hands into his lap, pressing his lips together in a tight, unapologetic line.

Angel let out a sharp, angry sigh and looked away, turning his face toward the canvas wall of the tent.

Lorne watched him for a moment, the playful lounge-singer persona fading entirely, and then he reached out, resting a gentle, green hand on Angel’s tense shoulder.

Lorne said, his voice softening, entirely sincere, "Angel, baby... you let her go for this reason, didn’t you, after high school? So she could find this. What you are seeing right now."

Angel froze, and the anger drained out of him, leaving nothing but an overwhelming, crushing wave of grief.

Angel slowly put his face into his hands, hiding his eyes. He stayed perfectly still for several seconds, the weight of two and a half centuries pressing down on his soul.

Then he slowly lowered his hands to his knees and sat looking at them for a long moment, while the murmur of the guests and the flap of the canvas went on around him.

Angel said, "Yes. It’s what I wanted for her."

Lorne leaned back in his white folding chair, letting out a long, dramatic, theatrical sigh. He elegantly waved his hand through the air one last time, a soft gesture toward the couple across the room.

Lorne sang, with genuine soulful lounge-singer vibrato, "The young... so fragile, yet so beautiful."

CHAPTER 8
The Altar and the Opal

Later that night, a warm golden glow from the string lights and lanterns filled the tent, and a soft, traditional string quartet began to play a gentle, sweeping melody from the corner of the tent.

The low murmuring of the divided guests—the terrified humans on the left, the restless demons on the right—died down.

From the far left side of the altar, Xander stepped out from behind a floral partition in a sharp, classic black tuxedo. He looked nervous, but his jaw was set with absolute determination.

Simultaneously, from the far right side of the altar, Anya stepped into view.

Anya looked breathtaking in a classic, elegant white bridal gown, her hair perfectly styled and the demonic eyeliner sharp and flawless.

Xander and Anya walked toward each other, meeting directly in the center beneath the floral archway. Xander let out a massive, shaky breath of pure relief the second he saw her. He reached out, and Anya took his hands.

The officiant, a very nervous-looking human man, stepped up to the podium behind them.

Before the officiant could speak, there was a sudden movement from the back row of the demon section.

Angel rose slowly from his folding chair, the scaly demon beside him leaning away to let him pass, and stepped out into the aisle.

He walked slowly and silently down the long, white carpeted aisle. Xander tensed slightly, his old, protective instincts flaring up, but Anya gently squeezed his hands.

Angel stopped directly beside Xander. He didn’t look angry; he looked calm and completely serious.

Angel reached into the pocket of his black duster, pulled out a ring box, clicked it open, and extracted the wedding band.

The ring was a masterpiece, an elegant, thick, perfectly smooth silver band studded along its center channel with tiny, glittering gems that led up to a breathtaking, rare black opal center stone that caught the golden light.

Angel held the ring out to Xander.

Xander looked at the ring, then up into the vampire’s dark eyes, and a silent, profound understanding passed between the two men who had hated each other for years.

Xander reached out and firmly shook Angel’s hand, accepting the ring.

Angel said, his voice barely above a whisper and meant only for Xander, "Take care of her, Xander."

Xander nodded once, deeply sincere.

Xander said, "I will."

Angel released his hand, turned around, and walked quietly back down the aisle.

Angel sat back down next to Lorne. He smoothly folded his right leg over his left, clasped his hands loosely together in his lap, and looked forward, his expression locked into a stoic, respectful mask.

Buffy, Tara, Willow, and Dawn were sitting together in the front row, holding their small bridesmaid bouquets.

Lucian sat on the end of the row, right next to Buffy, looking relaxed. He leaned slightly back in the folding chair with his long legs stretched out comfortably, holding a fresh plastic bag of crushed ice wrapped in a white napkin against his swollen right hand.

Lucian didn’t even glance across the aisle at Angel. Instead, he turned his head slightly, his warm eyes entirely focused on Buffy’s profile as she watched her best friend get married.

Lucian shifted the ice pack a fraction against his swelling knuckles without looking down, hiding a small, controlled wince from Buffy so the ceremony could stay hers.

Buffy felt his gaze and turned her head away from the altar for a split second, and she saw Lucian looking at her, holding the ice to the hand Angel had just bruised because of her.

Buffy gave him a small, private, soft smile and reached over to gently rest her hand on top of Lucian’s knee.

Lucian smiled back, entirely secure, solid as a rock.

At the altar, the officiant cleared his throat.

The officiant said, "Dearly beloved... and... others... we are gathered here today to witness the union of Alexander and Anyanka..."

CHAPTER 9
The Vows and the Choice

The golden lantern light bathed the altar in a warm glow, and the heavy canvas of the tent fluttered gently in the coastal night wind.

Xander and Anya stood face-to-face beneath the floral archway, oblivious to the terrified humans on the left and the scowling demons on the right, as if they were the only two people in the entire world.

The officiant stepped back, gesturing for them to begin.

Xander took a deep, shaky breath and looked down at Anya’s hands resting in his, his thumbs gently brushing over her knuckles. When he looked up into her eyes, all of his nervous, sitcom energy evaporated, and he was just a man deeply, profoundly in love.

Xander’s voice cracked slightly, but he held her gaze.

Xander said, "I remember the first time I really looked at you. It was back in high school. You were this terrifying, intensely weird, aggressive girl who didn’t seem to understand how basic human currency worked."

A quiet ripple of genuine laughter went through the front row of the human side. Anya smiled, a single tear breaking free and rolling down her cheek.

Xander said, "You practically demanded that I take you to the prom. And I was terrified of you. Honestly, I was just the guy who stood in the background, told bad jokes, and tried not to get eaten. But you didn’t care about any of that. You looked at me like I was the bravest, most important man in the room."

Xander gently squeezed her hands.

Xander said, "You gave up the universe for me, Anya. You gave up being invincible. You chose this fragile, messy, mortal life just so you could share it with me. I don’t know what I did to deserve that kind of miracle... but I promise, with everything that I am, I will spend the rest of my life making sure you never regret it. I love you, Anyanka."

Anya was openly crying now, not even caring about the demonic eyeliner Halfrek had so perfectly applied, and her chest heaved with a massive, shaky, happy breath.

Anya said, her voice thick with emotion but steady, "I have lived for over a thousand years, Alexander. I have seen empires rise and burn. I have watched continents shift. I thought I knew absolutely everything there was to know about how cruel and disappointing the universe could be."

Anya let go of his left hand just to reach up and press her palm gently against Xander’s cheek.

Anya said, "And then I met a boy in a terrible, loud button-down shirt. A boy who smelled like cheap cologne and complete, absolute bravery."

Xander let out a wet, breathless laugh, a tear finally spilling over his own eyelashes.

Anya said, "You taught me what it means to actually live. Not just exist... but to feel. To care. To be terrified of losing someone because they mean so much to you. You are my heart, Xander. I choose you. Over magic, over immortality, over everything. I choose this life, because you are in it."

The tent was dead silent. Even the hulking demons on the right side of the aisle were staring at the altar, stunned.

Xander reached into his pocket and pulled out the ring Angel had given him—the thick silver band with the stunning black opal center stone.

With a trembling hand, Xander carefully slid the heavy silver ring onto Anya’s finger, and it fit perfectly.

The officiant wiped his own eyes with a handkerchief.

The officiant said, "By the power vested in me... I now pronounce you husband and wife. You may kiss the bride."

Xander didn’t hesitate. He wrapped both of his arms securely around Anya’s waist, pulling her flush against his chest. Anya threw her arms around his neck, tangling her fingers into his hair.

They kissed, and it was not a polite, reserved wedding peck but a massive, sweeping, deeply passionate kiss fueled by years of surviving the Hellmouth together.

The string quartet instantly swelled into a triumphant, joyful crescendo.

The whole tent erupted at once, chairs scraping back on both sides of the aisle as humans and demons alike came to their feet.

On the human side, Willow, Tara, and Dawn shot up out of their folding chairs, cheering and clapping wildly. Buffy stood up next to them, beaming with pure, unadulterated joy, wiping a tear from her own eye. Lucian stood up beside her, offering a respectful, solid round of applause.

On the demon side, Halfrek stood up and whistled loudly, ignoring mortal decorum. Even Lorne was on his feet, clapping his green hands together and shouting his approval over the music.

Xander and Anya finally broke the kiss, both of them flushed, out of breath, and smiling so hard it hurt. Xander turned toward the crowd, grabbed Anya’s hand, and raised their arms together in absolute victory.

CHAPTER 10
The Black Opal and the Hairpin

Hundreds of warm, glowing fairy lights strung across the canvas ceiling lit the massive white tent for the reception, and an upbeat, late-nineties dance track was pumping through the rented DJ speakers.

A massive demon with leathery wings was carefully holding a tiny, delicate plate of hors d’oeuvres, nodding politely as Xander’s terrified but slightly drunk uncle talked his ear off.

Anya was eagerly shoving her left hand into Halfrek’s face, wiggling her fingers so the fairy lights caught the gemstone.

Anya said, practically vibrating with excitement, "Look at it, Hallie. Look at the structural integrity. The sheer aesthetic weight of it. It’s entirely superior to a traditional mortal diamond."

Halfrek leaned in, narrowing her ancient, discerning eyes as she inspected the ring.

Halfrek said, impressed, raising a manicured eyebrow, "I’ll say. That center stone is a Black Opal, Anyanka. Depending on the play of color, that can be worth significantly more than a flawless diamond."

Anya’s eyes widened to the size of saucers as she stared at her hand, awestruck by the sheer capitalistic value of her husband’s gift.

Anya pursed her lips and let out a long, impressed whistle.

Anya said, "Whew. Good job, Xander."

Willow was standing near the edge of the grass, laughing at something across the room. In her right hand, she was casually holding a small, heavy shot glass of dark whiskey. She wasn’t looking at it; she had just grabbed it off a passing tray in the high energy of the party.

She started to lift the glass to her lips.

Buffy stepped smoothly in from the left and, without breaking her stride, plucked the shot glass out of Willow’s fingers just before it reached her lips, tipping it over and pouring the dark whiskey straight into the dirt outside the tent.

Willow blinked in surprise. Before she could say a word, Buffy’s other hand came up and firmly pressed a cold, unopened can of cherry soda directly into Willow’s empty palm.

Willow winced slightly as the realization hit.

Willow said, "Oh. Right. Sorry... I got caught up in the moment. I completely forgot."

Buffy didn’t lecture her; she just gave Willow a warm, fiercely protective smile, bumped her shoulder against Willow’s, and kept walking into the crowd.

Tara stepped up right behind Willow, wrapped an arm around her waist, and gently patted her shoulder, offering silent, judgment-free support. Willow leaned back into Tara’s touch, popping the tab on the soda with a grateful sigh.

Dawn was sitting cross-legged at a drastically lowered folding table, surrounded by four small, terrifying-looking demon children with scales, tiny horns, and glowing yellow eyes, all leaning forward, captivated by the teenage girl.

Dawn said, "So, I got my blood on the magic hairpin and this wave overcame the entire school cafeteria! I barely made it out of there because they all wanted me!"

A tiny green demon with ridges on its forehead gasped, clutching its juice box in suspense.

Dawn said, "Suddenly, I look behind me, and literally all the boys from my school are sprinting down the street, screaming my name! They chased me halfway across the entire town of Sunnydale!"

The demon children stared at her in absolute awe of this terrifying mortal tale of survival.

Dawn said, "I barely made it through the front door before they literally smashed into the side of my house!"

A small, purple demon child with sharp little teeth slowly raised its hand.

The purple demon child said, "Did you eat them?"

Dawn paused and looked at the purple demon child, completely deadpan.

Dawn said, "No. But my sister threw a few of them into the bushes."

The demon children nodded slowly, deeply respecting the Summers household defense strategy.

CHAPTER 11
The Love Theme and the Drive

Outside the tent, the bright fairy lights glowed warmly through the heavy white canvas, while the rest of the world was painted in deep, cool, moonlit blues, and the faint, muffled bass of the reception dance floor carried out into the night.

Buffy pushed her way through a side flap of the tent, stepping out onto the cool grass to get away from the noise, and the coastal wind immediately caught her hair as she let out a long, exhausted, but happy breath, wrapping her arms around herself to ward off the ocean chill.

Angel was standing alone a little way off at the edge of the grass, near the rope fence above the drop, looking out at the dark, rolling ocean, his black duster billowing heavily in the wind.

The muffled dance music from the tent faded behind the wind, leaving only the slow roll of the ocean in the night air.

Buffy stopped, but she didn’t look surprised to see him. She slowly walked over, coming to a stop a few feet away from him, looking out at the exact same dark horizon.

For a long moment, the only sound was the crashing of the waves far below.

Angel said, his voice low, carrying easily over the wind, "It was a beautiful ceremony."

Buffy said softly, "It was. I’m really glad you came, Angel. Xander... he needed to see that you supported him. It meant a lot."

Angel finally turned his head to look at her profile in the moonlight. The intense, primal jealousy from earlier was gone, and all that was left was a profound, aching understanding of the centuries that separated them.

Angel said, "I didn’t just come for Xander. I needed to see it for myself."

Buffy looked down at the grass; she knew exactly what he meant.

Angel said, "He’s good for you, Buffy. Lorne read him. He’s solid. He isn’t going to pull you into the dark... he’s just going to stand next to you in the light."

Buffy finally turned her head to look at him, her hazel eyes shining with unshed tears but her expression strong.

Buffy said, "I’m tired of the dark, Angel. I fought in it for a really long time. I just... I want to build something that doesn’t feel like it’s going to fall apart the second I close my eyes, or get comfortable."

Angel nodded slowly, a tragic, beautiful acceptance settling over his features, and he took half a step closer to her.

Angel said, his voice breaking, "I will always love you. You know?"

Buffy didn’t look at him; she turned her head back to the dark ocean, the sea breeze catching a single tear as it fell down her cheek.

Buffy whispered, resolute, "I know."

The heavy crunch of gravel interrupted the quiet.

Buffy and Angel both turned their heads.

Coming around the corner of the tent were Willow, Tara, Dawn, and Lucian, Willow spinning her car keys around her finger and Dawn carrying a leftover piece of wedding cake on a napkin, while Lucian walked slightly behind them, his injured hand resting casually in his pocket.

They all stopped when they saw Buffy and Angel standing in the moonlight.

Willow and Tara exchanged a quick, protective look. Dawn froze with the cake.

Lucian didn’t tense or glare; he just stopped, standing perfectly still, offering Buffy the space to make her own choice.

Buffy looked at Angel one last time, a silent, final goodbye, and then she turned her back on the vampire.

Buffy walked smoothly across the grass toward her family, and when she reached Lucian, she didn’t say a word; she simply looped her arm securely through his uninjured left arm, leaning her weight comfortably against his side.

Lucian looked down at her, a warm, protective smile crossing his face, and he gently rested his hand over hers.

Together, the five of them walked across the parking lot toward Joyce’s old silver sedan.

The car doors opened and thumped shut one after another, and the old engine turned over on the second try.

The muffled, upbeat bass of the Britney Spears song "Stronger" suddenly blasted from the car stereo.

Through the rear window of the sedan, Dawn, Buffy, and Tara threw their hands up, happily singing along with the lyrics as Willow shifted the car into drive.

The silver sedan pulled out of the parking lot, the taillights glowing brightly as it drove away down the winding coastal highway, leaving the darkness behind.

Angel stood alone by the cliffside with the wind dragging at his duster, watching the two red taillights wind down along the coast road until the curve of the cliffs swallowed them, and he neither moved nor spoke.

Lorne stepped quietly out from the shadows of the tent.

Lorne walked up beside the brooding vampire, his emerald suit dull in the moonlight, and for once he offered neither a joke nor a dramatic sigh.

Lorne simply raised his green hand and rested it firmly on Angel’s broad shoulder, giving it a gentle, grounding squeeze.

Lorne looked down the empty highway where the car had just disappeared, and a faint, genuinely happy smile touched the empath’s face, because he knew they were safe.

Angel closed his eyes in the cool night wind.
