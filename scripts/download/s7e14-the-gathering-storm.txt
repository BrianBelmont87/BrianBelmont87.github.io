Buffy the Vampire Slayer. Season seven, episode 14. The Gathering Storm.

PREVIOUSLY

Giles said, "Last time on Buffy the Vampire Slayer..."

Buffy, Willow and Spike had secured the Slayer Scythe at the vineyard, and Angel and Faith had reunited with them and with the weapon. The house shield had been holding since the Turok-Han attack, but now it was dying. And the Bringers were waiting on the lawn.

CHAPTER 1
The Barrier Fails

Out on the front lawn of the Summers house, deep in the small hours, there were two long seconds of nothing but darkness before the night came back around the edges of it, a low wind moving somewhere far off down Revello Drive and, under it, a faint, high-pitched crackle of energy building and building with nothing else to cover it.

An orange glow stick lay on the sidewalk, spitting and sputtering, casting a harsh pool of sodium-orange light. It lay close beside the lead Bringer's boot, close enough that the glow caught the edge of its robe hem.

Seven Bringers stood in a perfect, motionless line facing the house. They wore brown robes and hoods, and their eyeless faces were hidden in shadow.

The barrier around the house flickered violently, a faint, translucent blue dome, the temporary Magna Mater shield that Willow and Tara had forced up through the crystal's jagged static after the Turok-Han attack, and the static had been chewing at it all night. Blue energy arcs spiderwebbed across its surface, sparking and dying in random places, and anyone could see the dome was failing.

For a moment nothing broke the silence except the wind and the hum of the failing barrier.

Inside, the Summers living room sat in low lamplight behind the shattered bay window, with the cold night air drifting in over the broken glass, and Tara was curled on the couch under a blanket when her head suddenly snapped up and her eyes went wide, because she felt the shield going in her bones before anyone else did.

Tara said, sharp and urgent, "The barrier— it's dying!"

She stood fast, knocking a blanket off her lap.

At the broken bay window, Riley and Sam were already moving. Riley's rifle came up in a fluid shoulder-to-cheek mount. Sam mirrored him a half second later, with both weapons trained on the line of Bringers outside. Lucian was still on the couch, watching the barrier flicker.

On the front lawn, the Bringers exploded into motion.

All seven broke from their line at the exact same instant and sprinted straight at the house. They came full speed and silent, their robes billowing behind them like dark wings. Their footwork was unnaturally precise for creatures without eyes, and they moved as one coordinated unit.

Riley said, calm and military, "Contact front!"

He fired, and his energy rifle discharged with a deep whump and a crack. A brilliant blue-white plasma bolt burst from the muzzle, traveled in a perfectly straight line, and slammed dead center into the lead Bringer's chest.

The Bringer's torso caved inward on contact, and its whole body lifted clean off the ground and flew backward in a flat, ragdoll arc, eight or nine feet. It landed hard on its back on the pavement with its limbs splayed, and its head bounced once. A small wisp of smoke rose from the charred hole in its robe. It did not move again, and the body lay there long enough for the kill to sink in.

Sam tracked the next Bringer in the line, second from the left, and fired.

The plasma bolt hit the Bringer square in the hooded face, and its head snapped violently to the side. Its whole body twisted in a grotesque half circle to the left, almost all the way around at the waist, robes flaring, and then it collapsed sideways onto the grass and lay completely still, without a twitch or a sound, and the spin that had put it there was wrong in a way that turned the stomach, too fast and too broken.

Inside, Tara's hand dove into her pocket and came out with a small, palm-sized rune stone. The Kenaz rune carved into its face ignited with a deep, pulsing blue light that reflected off her face and the walls.

She stepped right up to the broken bay window and planted her feet, then drew her arm back in a clean overhand throw and hurled the stone in a high, fast arc toward the front of the house.

The stone turned slightly in the air as it left her hand, its deep blue glow cutting through the night, and it cleared the window frame and began to fall toward the lawn.

Tara said, clear and commanding, as the stone flew, "IGNIS, INCENDE!"

The words told the fire to kindle and catch, and the rune stone hit the grass directly in front of the charging Bringers and did exactly that, detonating as it landed.

A roaring fireball burst outward in a semicircle, bright orange-yellow flames mixed with blue magical sparks. The blast wave was strong enough to make the front ranks stumble.

The Bringers scattered. Two broke hard to the left with their robes flapping, and one of them dropped into a low crouch, then sprang sideways. Two more veered right, one of them almost tripping over the dead body on the ground. The last one jumped backward several feet.

For a moment the line was blown apart.

The fire licked up from the lawn in hungry tongues and climbed the wooden siding on either side of the bay window and the lower edge of the broken glass, and for now it made a flickering wall of flame between the house and the remaining Bringers, a soft barrier of pure elemental fire.

The fire burned low but steady along the front of the house, and the remaining Bringers quickly regrouped.

They formed their line again, shoulder to shoulder, directly in front of the burning bay window, their hooded heads tilted slightly forward and their eyeless faces turned toward the people inside the house, and they waited there, patient, without moving and without making a sound, while the fire along the siding crackled and popped and slowly began to die down, and the last of the barrier's blue energy flickered and went out over all of them.

CHAPTER 2
The Drive

Lucian's black 1981 Pontiac Firebird Trans Am Special Edition was hauling ass down the freeway back from the vineyard with its dashboard lights low, Willow behind the wheel with the keys Lucian had tossed her, Spike in the back, and the Scythe lying across Buffy's lap in the passenger seat with her hand resting on the handle like it was part of her now, while miles ahead of them the temporary shield on Revello Drive was dying over the people they had left under it.

Willow had both hands tight on the wheel when her eyes suddenly went distant. Her pupils dilated, unfocused, like she was looking through the windshield at something miles away. She took a sharp breath, and her head tilted slightly, as if she were listening to something only she could hear.

Willow said, quiet and urgent, her voice tight, "The shield failed… Buffy… they're under attack. Right now."

Willow's eyes snapped back into focus. She was already speeding up, her foot pressing harder on the gas, and the car lurched forward with a growl.

In the passenger seat, Buffy's head whipped toward Willow. Her eyes narrowed and her mouth tightened into a hard line. The hand on the Scythe handle gripped harder until her knuckles went white. There was no panic in her, just pure, cold focus.

Spike was in the back seat, leaning forward between the front seats. His eyebrows shot up and his cigarette froze halfway to his mouth. He stared at the back of Willow's head for half a second. Then his face hardened into something dangerous, and a low growl started in his chest.

Buffy didn't even look at Spike. Her voice was low and deadly calm.

Buffy said, "Step on it, Will."

Willow nodded once, sharp. The Trans Am's engine roared as she floored it, and the car surged forward and wove through the late-night traffic like a bullet.

Buffy's face stayed still. A passing streetlight caught the Scythe blade, and it flashed red across her eyes.

Right behind them, Angel's black 1967 Plymouth GTX kept perfect pace with the Trans Am, its single working headlight cutting through the dark under the buckled V of its hood.

Inside the GTX, Faith rode in the passenger seat with one boot up on the dash where the windshield used to be, and the night wind poured in through the empty frame and whipped her hair across her swollen, broken nose. Ahead of them, she noticed the Trans Am suddenly pick up speed, its taillights flaring brighter as it pulled away.

Faith said, frowning, sitting up straighter, "The hell…?"

Angel didn't answer. His hands tightened on the wheel. He shifted his foot, and the GTX growled and matched the Trans Am's new speed. Both cars came off the freeway overpass with their tires singing on the curve of the off-ramp. Their headlights swept across the concrete barriers.

A cold blue light flickered across the empty back seat, and a low hum rose under the roar of the wind and stayed there, cold and steady.

The First appeared in the form of Mayor Richard Wilkins. He wore a crisp 1950s suit, and he had perfect hair and that warm, fatherly smile that never quite reached his eyes. He leaned forward between the front seats like he was just joining old friends for a drive. He had no body anyone could touch, but they were outside the house bubble now, and the same rules applied as always.

The First said, as the Mayor, smooth, folksy and affectionate, "Well now… fighting for the wrong team this time, aren't you, firecracker?"

Angel didn't even glance in the rearview mirror. His eyes stayed on the road, and he ignored it completely.

Faith froze, and her shoulders locked up. She fought the urge to turn around, her jaw tight, her eyes flicking to the side mirror instead as she breathed through her nose. One hand slowly curled into a fist on her thigh.

The Mayor leaned in closer, and his voice dropped into that familiar tone, reasonable and poisonous.

The First said, as the Mayor, "You know that red-headed witch up there? Willow? She's come a long, long way since the last time I was in town. Shy little thing back then… all stammer and sweaters. Now? She's a power. Real scary stuff. The kind that makes old demons nervous."

He chuckled softly, like he was proud of a wayward daughter.

The First said, as the Mayor, "She's also a cold-blooded murderer, Faith. Let's not pretty that up. Killed people. Good people. Powerful people. And the worst part? She liked it. Still does, deep down. That kind of power… it doesn't stay quiet. It eats. And one of these days it's gonna eat the people she claims to love."

He tilted his head, and his voice turned almost gentle.

The First said, as the Mayor, "You're a Slayer, sweetheart. It's in your blood to put down threats like that before they burn the whole damn town down. It's not personal. It's just… what you are. What you were made for. Willow Rosenberg needs to be put down. And you know it."

In the side mirror, Faith's own reflection showed the Mayor's smiling face behind her, and her breathing got tighter while she white-knuckled the urge to spin around and swing. It was the same thread the First had pulled on Willow in the Trans Am, though Faith had no way to know that, and the private truth it was tugging at, the one only Buffy, Spike and Lucian carried, stayed unspoken.

Angel reached over without looking and flipped on the stereo.

Kiss exploded out of the speakers at full volume. It was I Was Made for Lovin' You, and that famous guitar riff and disco rhythm filled the car like a middle finger to the First.

The Mayor's smile didn't falter. He just stared at Faith from the back seat for three full seconds while the music blared, his eyes calm, paternal and utterly empty. Then the cold blue light folded in on itself and he was gone, and the hum dropped away to nothing under the blaring music.

Faith's expression stayed locked as the music hit, but her eyes were burning. The cheerful, sleazy Kiss song played on, right over the heavy, poisonous shit that had just been said.

Faith exhaled hard through her nose and finally turned to look at Angel.

Faith said, low and pissed, "You gonna say something, or we just gonna pretend that didn't happen?"

Angel kept his eyes on the road, following the Trans Am's taillights as they sped toward Sunnydale.

Angel said, flat and final, "It's not real. Don't give it what it wants."

He turned the volume up another notch.

The GTX and the Trans Am raced on through the night toward Revello Drive, nose to tail, toward the fire and the waiting Bringers and whatever the First had planned next.

CHAPTER 3
Stairs and Blood

Back in the Summers living room, Riley and Sam, who had dropped down behind the couch when the fireball went up, popped up from behind it in perfect sync with their rifles already shouldered. They stepped hard to either side of the broken bay window frame, Riley to the right and Sam to the left. They pressed their bodies tight against the wall on either side of the sill.

They opened fire, and the energy bolts ripped through the open window into the night.

Out on the lawn, the remaining Bringers moved as one. They stepped sideways in unison, some to the left and some to the right, and vanished behind the edges of the wall and the window frame itself. Nothing hit. The bolts cracked into the lawn and the distant trees.

Riley said, sharp and immediate, "Cease fire! No joy!"

Sam said, confirming, her eyes still on the dark, "Confirmed!"

For a moment the room held a tense silence.

Then came muffled footsteps upstairs, heavy and many, moving fast across the second floor.

Tara said, looking up, her voice tight, "They're inside the house… they must have used the vine trellis."

Riley and Sam stood up together and set their rifles down on the couch without looking. Both drew steel batons from their belts, and the batons snapped out to full length with a sharp metallic click.

Riley said, low and focused, "Close quarters only."

Lucian rose from the couch and went to the big chair near the wall, where a longsword was leaning, and he picked it up in one hand and hefted the weight, testing the balance with a small roll of his wrist so that the blade caught the low light, and then he looked up the stairs into the darkness above with his face set and glanced back at Riley and Sam, and they nodded once.

The three of them moved to the stairs and went up one by one. Riley went first with Sam right behind him, and Lucian came last, the sword held low and ready, his eyes on the shadows above.

The only sounds were boots on wood and the distant crackle of the dying fire outside.

As Riley reached the top step, a Bringer exploded out of the darkness from the left hallway. Its dagger was raised high, its body fully committed in a leaping fall straight at him.

Riley saw it coming. He drove his left shoulder into Sam and slammed her hard against the stair rail wall on the left side, and at the same time he raised his steel baton in a sharp upward block.

The Bringer's full body weight crashed into them, and the impact was brutal. Riley, Sam and the Bringer were all knocked backward, and the three of them tumbled down the entire staircase in a chaotic, rolling pile while other Bringers came running down after them from the second floor, until they slammed into the living room floor in a tangle.

Riley was on his back with the first Bringer on top of him. He grabbed both of its forearms, lifted his legs and locked them, then twisted hard, scissoring the Bringer's neck between his legs. There was a sharp, wet crack, and the Bringer went limp. Riley shoved the body off him.

At the same moment, a second Bringer lunged at Riley from behind, its knife aimed at his back.

Sam was already moving. She smashed her baton down onto the Bringer's wrist with a sickening crack, and the dagger dropped to the carpet. She followed at once with a hard headbutt, her forehead smashing into its hooded face, and the Bringer staggered backward into the wall, and Sam went in after it and brought the steel baton down twice across its hooded skull until it slid down the wallpaper and did not get up.

Lucian was standing off to the far left. He saw the Bringer on Sam's left side, trying to stab her while she was busy.

He stepped in with no wasted motion and thrust the longsword straight forward, simple, powerful and direct, and the blade punched clean through the Bringer's head. The body dropped to the carpet at once, and the sword stayed in it for a second before Lucian ripped it free.

It was raw and heavy, with no flourish in it at all, nothing but strength and a straight line.

Near the couch, Tara stood gripping a white oak staff in both hands, her eyes glowing a faint green. She spoke clearly and firmly.

Tara said, "Mother of earth, creature of life, lend to us your wood…"

The wooden stairs came alive in hard, stuttering jerks, the crystal's static fighting every inch of the spell, and Tara's knuckles went white on the staff as she dragged it through anyway.

The steps and railings twisted and wrapped around the last Bringer still standing on them, like living vines. The wood coiled tight around its legs, torso and arms and yanked it down hard onto the stairs, flowing over its whole body, lower and upper, and then it hardened instantly from liquid-smooth to solid oak. The Bringer was entombed from neck to boots, with only its hooded head still visible, its face twisted in shock, and it couldn't move.

The wood had moved like something alive and aggressive, fast, organic and unstoppable.

The last remaining Bringer saw the stairs turn against it, and it sprinted straight at Lucian from the right side, its dagger raised high for a downward stab.

Out in the street, half buried under the noise of the fight, tires shrieked against the curb and a car door banged open.

The Slayer Scythe came flying in through the broken window from the right, thrown hard from outside, spinning through the air with its red blade flashing. It sang as it came, a high, wavering, wobbling ring that cut through the room. It passed inches from Lucian's face, close enough that the wind of it moved his hair.

The Scythe slammed into the Bringer with full force and pinned it to the wall by the chest, the blade burying itself deep in the drywall. Its legs went limp at once, and its dagger clattered to the floor. It hung there, impaled, twitched once, and then went still.

The Scythe's magic hummed faintly. On the stairs, the entombed Bringer creaked as it struggled uselessly inside the wood.

The living room was a wreck. There were bodies on the floor, one Bringer sealed in living wood on the stairs, and another pinned to the wall by the Scythe.

Lucian stood there breathing hard with the sword still in his hand, looking at the Scythe that had just saved him.

Outside, a second engine growled up behind the first and more doors slammed, and the GTX's single headlight swept across the broken bay window.

Buffy called from outside, her voice coming closer, "We're here!"

CHAPTER 4
Marzipan Bingo

Back in the wrecked Summers living room, the sheet of plywood across the front entry scraped aside on the floorboards and banged against the wall.

Buffy came in first with her arms crossed over her chest, her eyes going straight to the Scythe she had thrown, still buried in the wall across the room. Willow was right behind her shoulder, her eyes scanning the wreckage. Faith came in on Buffy's left, already clocking the dead Bringers and the one sealed in wood on the stairs. Angel brought up the rear, quiet, taking everything in. Spike came in with them in his trench coat, cigarette out, his eyes hard on the bodies.

The living room was a mess, with bodies on the floor and blood on the carpet, one Bringer entombed in living wood on the stairs and another pinned to the wall by the Scythe, and Dawn, who had stayed crouched in the kitchen doorway with a carving knife in her fist the whole time the footsteps pounded overhead, had come out to stand in the wreckage at Tara's side with her arms wrapped tight around herself.

Buffy stopped in the middle of the room and looked up at the dead Bringer hanging from the Scythe in the wall. Her arms were still crossed, and her face was completely deadpan.

Buffy said, "That'll put marzipan in your pie plate, bingo."

Riley was standing near the bottom of the stairs with his baton still in his hand. He turned, confused as hell.

Riley said, "…What?"

Buffy finally uncrossed her arms, stepped forward, and yanked the Scythe out of the wall in one clean motion. The Bringer's body dropped onto the carpet with a wet thud.

Buffy said, without looking at him, "Inside joke."

Riley said flatly, "Uhuh… right."

She hefted the Scythe in both hands for a second, feeling its weight. Then she walked straight over to Faith and held it out to her.

Faith looked at the weapon, then at Buffy.

Faith said, "You sure, B?"

Buffy just nodded once, her eyes on the red blade in Faith's hands.

Faith took the Scythe and turned it slowly in her hands, feeling the power move through the metal and wood. Her expression shifted into respect and hunger, and something almost like awe.

Faith said quietly, almost to herself, "Wicked…"

The red blade caught the light and flashed across her eyes.

Buffy turned her head and looked at the Bringer still trapped in the living wood on the stairs. Then she looked at Angel. There were no words, just a look.

Angel nodded once and walked over to the stairs without hesitation.

Buffy turned her back to him and looked at the dark window instead.

Behind her came a single, sickening crack as Angel stepped down hard on the Bringer's head. Buffy winced at the sound, her eyes closing for half a second and then opening again. She didn't turn around.

Angel stepped back down off the bottom stair and wiped the sole of his boot slowly on the carpet.

Buffy looked across the room at Willow, Dawn and Tara.

Buffy said, clear and decisive, "Plan 4 in motion. Now."

Tara nodded at once and turned to Faith, already moving.

Tara said, quick and focused, "Faith — I need your strength in the garage. We have to move the scrolls and the ritual things into Angel's car. Fast."

Faith looked at her for half a second, then nodded. She slapped her hands together once, like she was shaking off the weight of the Scythe, and handed it back to Buffy.

Faith said, "Let's go."

The two of them headed toward the garage. With the Magic Box gone, the garage had become their working armory and the library for the dangerous scrolls.

Buffy turned to the rest of the group, Riley, Lucian and Sam. Her voice was steady but tired.

Buffy said, "Good work. All of you."

Riley lowered his baton, breathing hard and still wired from the fight.

Riley said, "What is Plan 4?"

Buffy let out a slow breath and raised her eyebrows, and a small smile pulled at the corner of her mouth, tired but determined.

Buffy said, "We're moving camp."

The words landed in the wrecked living room like a final verdict.

Outside, the last of the fire along the front of the house crackled down to a smoulder on the scorched siding and the blackened lawn, and the night went quiet again around the wreck of the house.

Slowly, Buffy's smile faded, and the weight of what moving camp really meant settled in her eyes.

CHAPTER 5
Moving Camp

Two cars moved through the quiet streets of Sunnydale just before dawn, as the sky shifted from black to a cold blue-grey. Angel's black 1967 Plymouth GTX led the way with its one headlight burning and the boxes of scrolls riding in its trunk, Faith beside Angel in front and Spike, Riley and Sam crammed together on the back bench out of the wind that poured in through the empty windshield frame, and behind it came Lucian's black 1981 Pontiac Firebird Trans Am Special Edition with its gold trim, its engine rumbling low.

Inside the Trans Am Lucian drove with his hands steady on the wheel, and Buffy sat in the front passenger seat staring out the window. She held Mr. Gordo in her lap and absently stroked the stuffed pig's head with her thumb, her expression distant and tired, but calm.

In the back seat Willow sat by the left window, Tara in the middle and Dawn on the right, squeezed in shoulder to shoulder with their knees jammed against the front seats.

They turned off onto a quiet residential street, swung out onto a wider main road, and finally pulled into the parking lot of a large, brutalist concrete building. It was the Sunnydale Public Pool, a three-story block of smooth poured concrete with rounded edges. The sign above the entrance read SUNNYDALE PUBLIC POOL in faded letters, and the whole building looked like a fortress made of cement.

Buffy sighed softly, turned her head and gave Lucian a side glance. Without saying anything, she leaned across the center console and gently kissed him on the cheek. It was quiet and tender, and right there in front of everyone.

Dawn said, complaining at once from the back, "Ugh, I'm getting mushed back here. Can you two not do the gross couple thing while I'm trapped?"

Tara said with a soft sigh, "Dawn…"

Willow said, also sighing, but smiling a little, "Yeah… we can hear the smooching from back here."

Buffy didn't look embarrassed. She just turned around in her seat to face the back, keeping one hand resting lightly on her stomach. It was still perfectly flat, not showing at all.

Willow said, gentle and careful, "So… the house. After everything. If we survive this… are we ever going back?"

Buffy was quiet for a second, and then she answered honestly.

Buffy said, "I was thinking about leaving for at least nine months, Will. With Lucian. Someplace quiet, so I can give birth in peace. I hope."

Willow's eyes widened slightly. She nodded, trying to process it fast, and her voice came out a little nervous and higher than usual.

Willow said, "Oh. Yeah. Sure, Buffy. That… that makes sense. Totally."

Buffy said, calm and steady, "You guys will still have Faith while I'm taking a motherly break."

Tara smiled softly at Buffy from the back seat but didn't say anything. She just reached over and gently squeezed Willow's hand.

For a while the five of them were quiet together. The pre-dawn light was starting to creep into the car, and there was only the hum of the engine and the noise of the road.

In the pool parking lot both cars pulled in and stopped, and the doors opened.

Dawn said, climbing out and stretching dramatically, "Finally. My legs were dying."

Faith and Buffy walked straight to the back of Angel's GTX and popped the trunk. They started lifting out awkward wooden boxes crammed with books, scrolls and ritual items.

Lucian came around from his Trans Am and stepped up behind Buffy without a word. After she grabbed the last box, he slammed the trunk shut for her. She gave him a small nod of thanks.

Meanwhile, Riley and Sam were already moving toward the main entrance of the pool building in tight formation, rifles up. They checked the corners and cleared the doorway, then disappeared inside to sweep the building.

Buffy stood in the parking lot holding one of the boxes, with Mr. Gordo tucked under her other arm, and looked up at the big concrete pool building as the sky kept getting lighter. The place felt cold and solid, and strangely peaceful in the quiet before dawn. Angel and Spike got everyone inside with minutes to spare before the sun came up.

CHAPTER 6
Now We Fight Back

Inside the Sunnydale Public Pool the space was huge, a vast brutalist indoor swimming complex with a solid cement roof and thick concrete walls. Long rows of fluorescent lights hung from heavy steel beams high above. The main pool ran the whole length of the building, deep blue water with the lane lines and starting blocks still in place. On the left side, empty bleachers rose toward the high ceiling. At the far end a large glass wall looked out onto the parking lot.

Riley stood near a control panel on the right wall, and he hit a button.

With a heavy mechanical clunk, thick steel shutters began rolling down from the ceiling and slowly covering the large windows. The room grew darker as the daylight was cut off.

Angel came in carrying a heavy wooden box filled with scrolls, keeping to the dark strip under the bleachers, well away from the grey dawn light still falling through the glass at the far end, and set it down on a folding table near the shallow end of the pool where the shadow lay deepest, brushed his hands together, and looked over at Buffy.

Angel said, "That's all of them."

Buffy nodded and turned to Willow.

Buffy said, "Test it."

Willow kicked off her shoes and socks, rolled her pants up to her knees. She stepped carefully into the shallow end, where the water only came up to her calves. She took a few scrolls from the box, crouched down, and slowly lowered them under the water.

She watched the ink under the rippling water for a long moment, and nothing happened; the writing stayed sharp, with no bleeding and no dissolving at all, and Willow looked up at Buffy and nodded.

Buffy said, quiet but firm, "Dump 'em in. All of them. Now."

Faith, Sam, Angel and Riley grabbed the remaining boxes without hesitation. One by one, they walked to the edge of the pool and dropped them in. The heavy boxes hit the water with deep splashes and sank slowly, bubbles rising as they disappeared into the deep end.

Riley said, watching them go down, "Nice location."

Buffy said, "Yeah. It's three blocks from the high school… and they can't break in here anymore."

She walked toward the large window on the far side of the pool while the steel shutters were still coming down. She stopped and looked up at the sky.

Above Sunnydale, a huge, angry demonic face had formed in the clouds, swirling grey and black, with a cold blue light kindling deep in the pits of its eyes the way it had over Revello Drive the morning before and a mouth hanging open in a silent snarl, and it stared down at the pool building, the First with no borrowed body at all, out here beyond any crystal's bubble and watching everything.

Buffy stood with her arms folded, staring up at it, and her voice was low, steady and cold.

Buffy said, "Yesterday I said come and get me… Now I'm coming for you."

Lightning cracked inside the clouds and lit up the demonic face for an instant.

Riley hit the final button. The last steel shutter slammed down with a heavy boom and cut off the view completely. The automatic lights all through the building flickered on with a low electrical hum and filled the pool area with cold white light.

Buffy turned away from the sealed window and looked at Tara and Faith.

Buffy said, "Food run."

From across the pool, Lucian tossed his car keys in a clean arc toward Willow, and she caught them easily without even looking. She tossed them straight on to Faith.

Tara nodded at Faith, and Faith nodded back. The three women headed toward the office area on their way out to Lucian's Trans Am.

Riley stepped up behind Buffy and kept his voice low.

Riley said, "What now?"

Buffy slowly turned around to face him, her expression utterly deadpan.

Buffy said, "Now we fight back."

Her face didn't change. The lights hummed low overhead and the water lapped gently in the pool, and nothing else made a sound.

CHAPTER 7
Supply Run

Later that morning, the parking lot of a small gas station on the edge of town was mostly empty, and a few abandoned cars sat with their doors open. The front windows of the small gas station store were intact, but the place felt deserted, like people had left in a hurry and never come back.

Lucian's black 1981 Pontiac Firebird Trans Am Special Edition with the gold trim pulled into the lot and stopped. Faith was driving, Willow rode shotgun, and Tara was in the back seat.

They got out, and Faith swung up the Trans Am's glass rear hatch. All three of them grabbed empty boxes from the cargo well and headed inside.

Inside the store it was quiet, and the shelves were still mostly stocked. The power was out, but the big front windows let in enough light to see by.

They moved fast and efficiently. Willow grabbed bread, sandwich meat, hot dog buns and packages of red meat, and Tara loaded marshmallows, chocolate bars and jars of peanut butter, and then laid her palm flat on the lid of the store's chest freezer, found it still holding its cold with the power only lately gone, and stacked eight frozen pizzas into the bottom of a second box. Faith grabbed a portable gas stove, three gas cans, a first aid kit and glow sticks, and then she started filling another box with Sobe drinks, sodas and bottled water.

They worked in silence for a minute, and the only sound was things dropping into the bottoms of the boxes.

Then a girl's voice came from outside, raw and terrified, screaming.

Kennedy screamed from outside, "HELP! SOMEBODY HELP ME!"

Kennedy came sprinting across the parking lot at full speed, a teenage girl with an athletic build and long dark hair pulled back, her eyes wide with panic. A single Bringer was chasing her with its brown robes flapping and a dagger in its hand, moving with that eerie, silent coordination.

Kennedy saw the Trans Am and the open store door, and she ran straight for it.

She burst through the door and nearly crashed into Willow.

Kennedy screamed, "HELP ME! PLEASE— THERE'S SOMETHING CHASING ME!"

Willow caught her by the shoulders to steady her. For a split second Willow's eyes went unfocused as she sensed something in the girl, a Slayer's potential, and a strong one, humming under her skin.

Before anyone could speak, the Bringer slammed through the door behind her.

Faith reacted instantly. She dropped the box she was holding and pulled her long knife from the sheath on her thigh in one smooth motion.

Faith said sharply, "Willow! Tara! Get her back!"

Tara pulled Kennedy behind the counter with her, and Willow stayed close, already pulling power into her hands.

The Bringer lunged at Faith with its dagger, and Faith sidestepped and slashed low with her long knife, forcing the Bringer back toward the drink coolers. The Bringer was fast; it feinted left and then slashed upward, and Faith barely twisted out of the way as the blade sliced through the air inches from her ribs.

They circled each other in the narrow aisle between the shelves. The Bringer was silent, and Faith was breathing hard, but she was focused.

The Bringer lunged again, and Faith caught its wrist with her free hand and slammed it into the shelf, knocking cans everywhere. She tried to drive her knife into its side, but the Bringer twisted violently and headbutted her, and Faith staggered back with her already-broken nose bleeding all over again.

The Bringer pressed the advantage, slashing wildly. Faith blocked one strike with her left forearm, the edge opening a shallow cut along it, and grunted in pain, then countered with a brutal elbow to its hooded face. It stumbled, and Faith followed with a hard kick to its knee that dropped it to one leg.

The Bringer still didn't make a sound.

Faith stepped in fast, grabbed the Bringer by the front of its robe, and drove her long knife up under its chin in one savage thrust, and the blade punched through the top of its head. The Bringer convulsed once, and then it went still.

Faith yanked the knife out and let the body drop. Her chest was heaving, and blood ran from her nose down over her mouth.

Tara said from behind the counter, urgent, "Faith!"

Faith said, wiping her nose with the back of her hand, "I'm good."

Willow slowly stepped out from behind the counter, staring at Kennedy. She reached out and gently touched the girl's arm, and her eyes widened slightly.

Willow said, soft and surprised, "You… you're like Buffy. I can feel it. You could be a Slayer."

Kennedy looked at her like she was crazy, still shaking from the adrenaline.

Kennedy said, "What the hell are you talking about?!"

Faith cleaned her knife on the Bringer's robe and stood up.

Faith said, "Doesn't matter right now. We take her with us. Let's go!"

She grabbed two of the boxes, and Tara and Willow grabbed the others. Kennedy, still confused and breathing hard, picked up a box without being asked.

They moved quickly out to the Trans Am, and Faith swung the rear hatch up again. They loaded everything in, the food, the gas stove, the drinks, the first aid kit and the glow sticks. It was a tight fit, but they made it work.

Once everything was loaded, they piled back into the car. Faith got behind the wheel again, and Kennedy ended up squeezed into the back between Willow and Tara.

Faith started the engine, and the Trans Am rumbled to life.

Faith said, looking in the rearview mirror at Kennedy, "Buckle up, kid. You just got drafted."

She threw the car in reverse, peeled out of the parking lot, and headed back toward the pool.

The Trans Am drove away, and the gas station was quiet again. The dead Bringer lay inside on the floor between the aisles, and a few cans were still rolling across the linoleum.

CHAPTER 8
White Table

Back at the pool, it was a small, sterile room off the main pool area, with white walls and a white tile floor. A plain white plastic table stood in the middle, with mismatched white plastic chairs arranged around it in a loose circle. It looked like the most depressing war room in history.

Buffy, Angel, Spike, Riley and Sam sat around the table, while Lucian leaned against the wall by the door and Dawn sat cross-legged on the white tile beside Buffy's chair with her back against the wall and her knees pulled up.

In the dead center of the table sat Mr. Gordo, Buffy's old stuffed pig, facing Spike like a tiny pink judge.

Spike stared at the pig with his head tilted slightly to the side and an unlit cigarette between his fingers. His eyes were soft in a way that almost hurt, like he was looking at something he could never have. He wanted it, and he had no claim to it.

Riley coughed awkwardly, trying to break the silence.

Buffy said, quiet, with steel underneath, "Now we need to attack it instead of sitting around waiting for the next punch."

She looked at each of them in turn, slow and deliberate.

Buffy said, "We have to wait until sundown, of course."

Her eyes landed on Angel and Spike. Angel gave a small, grim nod, and Spike just smirked, but it didn't reach his eyes.

Buffy said, "So we've got until then to think of something."

Riley said, half laughing and half incredulous, "Wait a minute… most of this has been seat-of-the-pants until now?"

Buffy let out a short, genuine laugh, the kind that hurt a little.

Buffy said, "It can spy on us. It can listen in. But it can't read thoughts."

She tapped two fingers against her temple.

Buffy said, "That's still ours."

Riley said, "It can't touch anything either. Can't use weapons."

Spike said, still staring at Mr. Gordo, his voice low and dry, "Yeah… but it can remember everything the dead person did. Or was. When it puts on their face… it wears them like a bloody suit."

For a moment nobody spoke, and Spike turned the unlit cigarette slowly between his fingers.

Buffy said, "From now on, no one goes to the bathroom or sleeps or showers alone."

She looked straight at Angel.

Buffy said, "That means you too, Angel."

Angel winced hard as the memory flashed across his face. Amends, the snow, the First driving him to the edge. He looked down at the table for a second.

Lucian said quietly, from where he stood against the wall, "I hope we all make it out of this one together at the end…"

No one said anything. The silence was brutal, and even Spike didn't crack a joke.

Buffy cleared her throat and stood up.

She put both hands to the small of her back and arched, stretching; her body already felt subtly different since the pregnancy, and she was still getting used to it. She exhaled through her nose.

Buffy said, "We have to get to the high school."

Everyone looked at her.

Buffy said, "That's where the final battle is going to be. I know it is."

Riley said, "How do you know that?"

Buffy said, "The Seal is under it. Willow can tell you more when she gets back. She translated the entire thing after she made a copy… when we went there together a week or so ago."

She let it sit for a second, and then she gave a small, tired smile.

Buffy said, "Guess we're all going back to high school."

CHAPTER 9
The Shower Chain

Late in the day, with sundown still an hour off, the women's locker room showers had three stalls side by side, each with a simple white fabric curtain that reached mid-thigh. Hot water and thick steam filled the room, and it felt soft and glowing and safe. The overhead lights came warm and soft through the heavy steam, casting a gentle amber glow on skin and curtains.

All three women stood behind their own white curtains, with only their outlines showing through, shoulders and backs, and now and then a shape from the side. Buffy was in the middle stall, with Tara on her right and Willow on her left.

The water ran steadily and the steam swirled. The three of them stood under their own showers and let the hot water hit their shoulders and backs.

Tara said, stuttering softly, her voice full of love and worry, "I know w we can't talk about the p plan because it can hear us… But are you sh sure, Buffy? About going forward this way?"

Tara's hands lathered a bar of soap. Through the curtain and the steam there was only the shape of her back and shoulders as she gently scrubbed under her arms.

Buffy said, her voice steady but tired, the water cascading over her, "It's all we've got, Tara… I just hope we don't lose too many people… because it's ready for us. We know that."

Buffy looked over the top of her stall toward Tara and Willow, and her face was raw, honest and scared.

Buffy said, "If one of you… any of you… die during the fight… I cannot stop. I have to keep going. I'm sorry…"

Tara looked down at the wet white tile wall and nodded quietly to herself, her eyes soft and sad but accepting. On the other side, Willow did the same, with a small, determined nod.

Without a word, Tara and Willow reached their hands over the tops of the stalls, and Buffy reached out too. Their three hands met and held in a quiet chain above the curtains, fingers gently intertwined. Water and steam swirled around their joined hands.

Buffy said softly, breaking a little, "I love you, Tara… I love you, Willow."

Tara said, her voice full of warmth and tears, "I love you too, sweetie… so much."

Willow said, her voice thick with emotion, "I love all of you…"

They held the chain for a long, quiet moment while the steam rose and the water ran over their joined fingers.

Tara and Willow slowly let go, and their showers turned off one after the other. Then came the soft sounds of getting dressed, towels and the rustle of fabric, while Buffy stayed inside her stall.

Buffy put both hands against the wet tile wall and closed her eyes. She breathed slowly and deeply, and her hands trembled just a little as the weight of everything hit her.

The other two showers were silent now, and the quiet sounds of dressing went on in the background, just around the tiled corner where Tara and Willow had stepped with their towels and their clothes, out of sight but close enough to hear her if she called.

Buffy reached up and turned off her own water, wiped both hands down her face once, wrapped a towel tightly around herself and stepped out into the main bathroom area.

The big mirror was completely fogged with steam, and it showed nothing of her at all. She walked to the sink.

Suddenly a voice came from her right.

Spike said, "Buffy…"

Buffy turned toward the voice. Steam curled off her shoulders and collarbone, and the heat of the shower still radiated from her skin.

Spike stepped into view. His face was raw and his eyes were wild with need. He breathed in deeply, and the scent of her hit him hard. The warmth coming off her body pulled at him like a physical thing, and whatever he had promised himself was breaking against it.

Spike said, desperate, low and rough, "I can smell you, Buffy. The heat still rising off your skin from that shower… it's all over you. I can feel it in my lungs. I want you more than anything in this world. More than blood. More than breath. This soul came back for you… and every day it's knives. I remember what I did. What I am. But it doesn't stop the wanting. It makes it scream. I need you, love. I can't stop needing you."

He moved in fast and grabbed her firmly by the upper arms, holding on like he was afraid she would vanish. His grip was desperate and starving, not cruel, but unable to let go.

His hands stayed locked on her upper arms, and behind them the mirror was still a solid wall of fog.

Buffy looked straight into his eyes, steady. Then she slowly lowered her gaze to his hands gripping her and studied them for a long moment, before she lifted her eyes back to his.

Buffy said, "That coldness you are putting into my body? That is the reason why I married Lucian."

She pulled herself away, carefully but with clear finality, and stepped back to put distance between them. Her eyes never left his.

Spike said, his voice cracking, broken, "Buffy… please…"

Buffy said, quiet and final, "Go."

Spike stared at her, completely shattered, his hands hanging empty at his sides. After a long moment he turned, walked to the door, and left without another word. The door swung shut behind him.

Just around the tiled corner, Tara and Willow had gone still the moment they heard his voice, Willow with her sweater caught halfway over her head and Tara with one hand flat against the cool tile, and they had looked at each other through the steam and stayed exactly where they were, close enough to be at Buffy's side in two steps if she called for them, and deliberately quiet, giving her the moment to handle on her own.

Buffy stayed at the sink and exhaled slowly while the steam swirled around her. She put her hands on the edge of the counter with her head slightly bowed, and the weight of it all settled into her shoulders.

The mirror stayed fogged over, a solid grey wall, and there was only the sound of dripping water and her own breathing, and her hands stayed braced on the cold edge of the counter until they stopped shaking.

CHAPTER 10
The Seal Awakens

That night the rebuilt Sunnydale High sat under a black sky with low clouds, its brick face and its rows of tall classroom windows dark, its bell tower a black shape against the clouds, and the floodlights along its walls cast harsh white pools across the front lawn and the cement walk before the doors, so that for all its fresh paint the whole place looked cold and institutional and wrong in the dark, like a building that had been put up to hold something in rather than to teach kids.

This was what Willow had seen in her vision at the vineyard, a Turok-Han army waiting under the cracked seal of the high school. Now the ground itself confirmed it.

Six Bringers stood in a perfect line on the front lawn, holding three terrified Sunnydale students. They were two boys and a girl, teenagers in normal clothes. The students struggled weakly, their mouths taped and their eyes wide with horror.

The Bringers moved in unison, and each one raised a long, curved dagger. In one silent, coordinated motion, they stabbed the students in the middle of the body, with clean, brutal, ritual thrusts.

The students jerked once and went limp, then dropped to their knees and fell forward onto the cement.

Blood began to flow from their wounds, but it did not pool randomly. It crawled slowly and deliberately across the ground in perfect straight lines, following invisible grooves carved into the cement. The red streams formed a large, ancient symbol on the pavement. It was the unmistakable mark of the First Evil, a jagged circle with sharp spikes and a void at its center.

One thick line of blood completed the symbol, and then it slid on across the sidewalk in front of the school.

The air suddenly shimmered and rippled like heat haze. A huge, translucent magical shield snapped into place over the entire high school, front to back and side to side. It glowed a faint, see-through dark purple and hummed with dark energy.

Now there were thirteen Bringers, the six who had performed the ritual and seven more who came out of the shadows. They fanned out in perfect silence from the admin building and the ritual space, all at the exact same moment, moving like one creature. They took their positions along the inside of the new shield and stood there motionless, hooded heads slightly tilted, eyeless faces turned outward.

And there they waited, silent and utterly still.

The rebuilt high school was sealed now, guarded by a silent army of Bringers. The blood symbol glowed faintly on the ground, and the magical shield hummed in the night. There was only the low wind and the faint electrical hum of the shield, and the dread settled over all of it.
