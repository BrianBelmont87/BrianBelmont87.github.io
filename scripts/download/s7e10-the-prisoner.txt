Buffy the Vampire Slayer. Season seven, episode 10. The Prisoner.

CHAPTER 1
Bridal Whispers

Early that morning, the first school morning since the preacher fell, warm sunrise light came through the stained-glass panes of the Magic Box's front windows and laid long golden shafts across the floorboards, catching the dust motes drifting slowly in the air while the shop lamps burned low against the honeyed amber shelves. The whole place smelled of fresh coffee and sage incense, the old leather couch creaked whenever anyone shifted on it, and birds were starting up out in the cool street. After last night's sundown goodbyes, with Angel and Faith already gone down the highway toward Fred's crisis in LA, the shop sat calm and quiet in a way it had not been for weeks, a calm with room in it for wedding thoughts to bloom.

They had all come in together before eight, piling out of the white Highlander so Tara could open the shop on time, and Lucian, who had called in to Daniels Technologies and taken the day for the first time in weeks, had kicked off his boots with Buffy the moment the two of them reached the couch.

Tara stood behind the counter wrapping a purple bath bomb in tissue paper, the bandage on her lower left side near her belly button healing quietly under a sage-green peasant blouse with white embroidery at the neckline, worn with light-wash ripped jeans and ankle boots, her long golden dirty-blonde hair in a loose side braid and her silver crescent necklace resting at her throat. On the other side of the glass a suburban mom in her mid-forties, in yoga pants and a UC Sunnydale sweatshirt, with her hair in a messy bun and a reusable tote on her shoulder, stood turning the bath bomb over and over in her hands.

The customer said, still turning the bath bomb in her hands, "Are you sure I can just drop this into my bathtub?"

Tara smiled softly, the genuine caretaker in her rising to the question.

Tara said, "Yes, that is what it's for. It will start fizzing the second it hits the water, just make sure not to use cold water, only warm or hot."

The customer brightened and tucked the bath bomb against her chest as she said, "Okay… that sounds good… I'll take one for now, and if it works good? I will come back and buy more."

Tara gave a soft laugh as she rang it up. Tara said, "That will be twelve thirty-two."

The customer handed over a crisp twenty, and Tara counted the change out into her palm with a quiet clink of coins.

Tara said, bright and sincere, "Thank you so much — enjoy your soak."

Across the sunlit shop, past the glowing jars of herbs, the crystals on their shelves and the tall wooden library ladder, stood the red leather couch.

Buffy was curled sideways in Lucian's lap with her bare legs across his thighs and her feet hooked behind his calf, in a soft white ribbed tank tucked into high-waisted light-denim shorts, her hair up in a messy top-knot with loose tendrils falling around her face, her engagement ring catching the light on her finger, and the faint, fading hickey from yesterday afternoon still showing on her left shoulder near her neck. Lucian wore a fitted black turtleneck and dark jeans, his bare feet propped on the coffee table and his long jet-black hair still mussed from sleep, and he had one arm around her waist with his hand resting on her hip. An open bridal magazine lay across both their laps, their heads bent close over it in a low, intimate heat, his right hand resting easy on the edge of the page, and the healing Bringer wound low on his left side did not stop him from holding her close.

Buffy said, soft and awed, tracing a lace gown in one of the photos with her fingertip, "They are all so beautiful, Lucian… it's hard to decide… but look at the price tags… Jesus…"

Lucian chuckled warmly and pressed a slow kiss to her temple. Lucian said, "Yeah, you are only supposed to buy one of them your entire life so… I guess it makes sense, right?"

For a long breathing moment the pages turned slowly between them while the golden morning light lay across Buffy's bare legs and the black of Lucian's turtleneck. Then Buffy closed the magazine with a soft snap, her eyes sparkling.

Buffy said, "I know the dress I want to wear for myself with you now."

Lucian raised one eyebrow, a slow cocky smirk spreading across his face. Lucian said, "Really?"

Buffy said, "Yes… It'll be a surprise…"

Buffy rolled over in his lap, her bare legs sliding across his, wound her arms around his neck and kissed him slowly and deeply with a raw morning passion, and Lucian leaned into it with one hand sliding up her back and the other resting on her bare thigh, and the two of them stayed locked together like that on the red couch.

CHAPTER 2
Arya

Over at the counter, in the same golden sunrise light slanting through the front windows, Tara watched the couch from behind the register with a gentle, fond expression and a tiny private smile, and let out a soft sigh.

Willow came out of the shop's little bathroom wiping her hands on her black leggings, in an oversized heather-grey sweater with the sleeves rolled, fuzzy purple socks with stars on them and her silver bracelet, her red hair loose and still tousled from sleep, and under the easy quiet of the morning her magic was still thin and damaged the way it had been since the construction site, and no amount of sunrise was going to put it back.

Willow said, "Did I miss anything?"

Tara said, smiling, soft and easy, "Nope… just a slow morning still. We are doing okay though."

Willow came around behind the counter, saw Buffy and Lucian still kissing on the couch like teenagers, and with a private, loving smile she leaned up to give Tara a gentle peck.

Tara caught her at the waist and pulled her in tight and turned the peck into a deep, passionate kiss, cradling Willow's face with her fingers threaded through the red hair, and Willow melted into it with her arms around Tara's back, and the two of them stayed locked together behind the counter while across the shop Buffy and Lucian kept up their slow kiss on the couch, a tangle of legs and bare feet.

A polite throat-clearing came from the other side of the counter.

Arya said, "Ahem… Excuse me?"

Tara broke the kiss, breathless, wiped her lip with the back of her hand and turned to the customer with her cheeks flushed.

Arya stood at the counter, a young woman of twenty-three, about five foot two, with pale freckled skin, a red-blonde pixie cut and three earrings in her left ear, wearing a turquoise UC Sunnydale tee under a brown leather jacket lined with white fur, white pants and white tennis shoes, with her college books tucked under one arm.

Tara said, flustered but warm, the stutter slipping in, "Yes, I am sorry. What did you ne ne need help with?"

Willow coughed gently and walked away from the counter with a private smile on her lips.

Arya smiled and held out her hand across the counter, and Arya said, "Hi, I'm Arya."

Tara took her hand gently by the fingers and shook it.

Tara said, still flustered, soft and genuine, "N n nice to meet you, Arya…"

Arya said, "I'm looking for a book that has to do with projecting magic energy and raising magic energy inside of a person, like a book that helps someone's connection to magic itself."

Tara looked at her for a long moment, really looking, seeing past the surface with a quiet, knowing gaze, and then she gave a slow nod, walked to the third book rack, slid a thick leather-bound tome out from the shelf and brought it back to hand it over.

Tara said, gentle but serious, "This isn't just for beginners… It also covers astral projection, focused projection and other energy works."

Arya's eyes lit up as she flipped a page. Arya said, "Oh, it's cool. My mother was a witch too… So I know some stuff, I just want to get back into it after moving away is all…"

Tara said, "It's not cheap… It's thirty-four fifty."

Arya handed over two twenties, and Tara rang the book up and counted out her change, and Arya gave her a grateful little wave and headed out, the bell above the door jingling behind her.

Tara watched the door swing shut with a thoughtful smile, Willow peeked back at her from across the shop with loving eyes, Buffy and Lucian stayed tangled together on the red couch, and the golden morning filled the Magic Box again.

CHAPTER 3
A+ and Josh

Later that morning across town, in a classroom at the new Sunnydale High School, the bright, harsh fluorescents mixed with the natural daylight pouring through the tall windows, and the whole room had that clean, hopeful feel a high school had on an ordinary day when nothing had tried to eat anybody yet.

Dawn sat at her desk in a charcoal-grey tee under an open light-pink hoodie with the sleeves pushed to her elbows, dark blue jeans and white low-top sneakers, her hair in a high ponytail and her black backpack at her feet, her pencil moving steadily and a soft smile on her face. On the corner of the desk lay her graded paper with a big red A-plus in the top right corner, and when the bell rang she slid it carefully into her bag and joined the flow of students out the door.

The hallway outside was lively with posters on the walls, lockers slamming, sneakers squeaking on the linoleum and chatter echoing everywhere, and after the week they'd had, all that ordinary noise felt to Dawn like a small victory.

Josh came up behind her, seventeen and a little shorter than Dawn, stocky and strong, with his short blonde buzz cut, bright blue eyes and the small silver stud in his left ear, in a black hoodie over a grey band tee, dark cargo pants and black sneakers, his backpack slung over one shoulder. He leaned in and kissed her softly on the cheek, and Dawn smiled, turned and kissed him back, then took his hand with their fingers interlacing, and the two of them walked into the next classroom still holding hands, their shoulders relaxed, disappearing through the doorway while the noise of the hallway swelled up behind them.

CHAPTER 4
The Prisoner

At that same hour, far beneath the school, the basement around the Seal sat in almost no light at all, only one weak overhead bulb flickering and a faint, dirty daylight leaking through a high grate, leaving deep pools of shadow across the cool blue-grey stone and a sickly yellow sheen on every wound and every inch of sweat-slick skin, as though the Seal itself were drinking whatever light came near it.

The only sounds were the occasional creak of a chain, a labored, wet breathing, a single drip of water echoing somewhere in the dark, and the faint, muffled noise of the school far overhead, almost too quiet to hear, while the Bringers made no sound at all.

In the center of the concrete floor lay the ancient circular stone of the Seal, its carved symbols dark and ominous under a faint, unnatural sheen, and around it stood a loose circle of five silent Bringers in identical hooded brown robes, eyeless and tongueless, their featureless faces turned inward toward the stone, not breathing and not moving at all.

Against the far wall hung Spike, shirtless, with brutal purple-and-yellow bruises across his ribs and abdomen, shallow cuts still weeping, dried blood on a split lip and one eye swollen shut, his pale skin clammy and streaked with dirt and sweat. Iron manacles held both wrists chained to the wall at shoulder height, stretching his body so that his bare feet dangled two or three inches above the cold concrete, his toes curled from the strain, his torn, blood-stained dark jeans riding low on his hips, his feet dirty, bruised and raw, his face gaunt and his hair matted flat.

Spike had not been seen in almost a week, not since the Bringers had come for him in his crypt and dragged him out of the dark there, and down here under Sunnydale High the chip in his head, which still fired at everyone in the world except Buffy, had done nothing at all to save him from them, while the watchers around the Seal stood in their terrible stillness and let every bruise and cut on him speak for itself.

CHAPTER 5
The Calling

Later that morning, back at the Magic Box, the warm light of the shop took on a cool, uneasy undertone, and the shadows seemed to deepen across Buffy's face as something began to reach for her.

Buffy broke the kiss with Lucian and pulled back just enough to look past him.

Buffy whispered, "The school…"

Lucian said, "The school?"

Buffy nodded and slid to her feet, one hand pressing flat against the center of her chest.

Buffy said, "It's calling me… inside of me… I can feel it…"

Concern crossed Willow's face immediately as she turned toward the couch.

Willow said, "Buffy, it's not the First trying to get to you, is it? Like lure you into a trap?"

Tara stepped forward from the counter, her eyes soft but focused.

Tara said, "Your energy is spiking inside of you… I can see it…"

Buffy folded her arms and looked from one of them to the other, her voice dropping to a whisper.

Buffy said, "Spike… Spike… we have not seen anything from him in a while now… almost a week…" Then she drew a slow breath, and her voice steadied, heavy. "I don't know why… but I have to go there… I can just feel it like a ringing sound inside my ears and this feeling in my chest… it's calling me there…"

She sat down on the edge of the couch and pulled on a pair of bright yellow socks, the left foot first and then the right, their cheerful color an odd, grounding contrast to the look on her face, and then she worked her boots on one at a time and laced them with quick, practiced pulls, double-knotting each one, and none of them rushed her while she did it.

Then she stood, went to the weapons in the back of the shop, unzipped the long shoulder bag, and packed it piece by piece with quiet purpose: stakes first, then the small crossbow and a handful of bolts, and last of all the short axe.

Willow, Lucian and Tara stood in a loose, supportive line behind her, Willow's hand resting on Tara's arm, Lucian's hands open and ready at his sides, Tara's face full of deep, caretaking concern, and none of them tried to stop her; they simply stood with her, three people who loved her keeping silent company at her back while she zipped the bag shut and the weight of the decision settled over all of them.

CHAPTER 6
Principal Wood

In the bright sun of the late morning, with the whole campus looking deceptively normal, Tara pulled the white Highlander into the front lot of Sunnydale High and cut the engine, and the doors opened and closed one after another while the distant sounds of the school drifted across the pavement.

Willow leaned in through the driver's window and kissed Tara's cheek.

Willow said, "Stay here, baby… you're in the sun out here so you know… protected?"

Tara said, "Be careful…"

Buffy walked in the center with the weapon bag over her shoulder, Lucian on her right in the long black woolen trench coat and dark boots he had pulled on back at the shop, the coat standing out darkly against the bright grounds, and Willow on her left, and the three of them crossed the campus with purpose while the school around them looked familiar and yet slightly off, the calling humming in the air, and Tara waited behind them in the Highlander, parked in the full sun of the front lot.

Inside, the main hallway was lit by fluorescents and the spill of daylight from the windows, long shadows stretching down the corridor and their footsteps echoing on the linoleum. The three of them walked straight past reception, where a student called out, "Hello? Can I help you?" and got no answer, and three doors down they reached the one with the nameplate that read ROBIN WOOD, and Buffy's hand hesitated a full beat against the glass before she knocked once, firmly.

Robin Wood said from inside, "Come in, please."

Buffy opened the door quickly and stepped in with Lucian at her right and Willow at her left, and the door shut behind them with a soft click. The principal's office was warmer and more orderly than the hallway, lit by a desk lamp and the window, and the weapon bag looked badly out of place in it. Robin Wood sat behind the desk in his professional principal's clothes, and his eyes flicked to the bag and stayed there a beat longer than was polite before he clasped his hands and leaned forward, a recent, healing cut visible across one palm.

Robin Wood said, "Can I help you? Let me guess… Buffy Anne Summers? Sister and caretaker of Dawn Summers?"

Buffy scanned the office and found nothing evil in it, then walked around behind the desk while Wood slid his chair back in confusion, and she opened the drawers one at a time, some of them ripping right off their runners and crashing to the floor, until she reached the locked one and tore it open.

Inside lay a single photograph from the seventies of Wood's mother in a long coat near a subway station, one foot propped up, looking stone-cold awesome straight into the camera, and beneath the photograph lay a long knife.

Buffy picked up the photo carefully, then rolled her eyes and picked up the knife too.

Buffy said, "Really?"

Wood stayed calm in his chair with both hands raised.

Robin Wood said, "That is my mother. And as for the knife? Every single principal this school has ever had… has met an unfortunate violent end. You cannot blame me for wanting protection close by…"

Willow stepped up behind Buffy, took the photo from her, stared into it for a long moment, and then looked up at Wood.

Willow said, "Buffy… this woman… she is a Slayer… I can see it…"

Wood rose to his feet with his fists bunched, and sweat started to bead on his smooth head.

Robin Wood said, "How do you know that?"

Wood lunged to grab the photo across the desk, and Willow raised one hand gently and an invisible force pushed him back into his chair and rolled it against the wall; her damaged magic still answered when she truly needed it, and she felt the small cost of it settle behind her eyes.

Willow sighed, lowering her hand slowly back to her side. "Don't do that again…"

Robin Wood said, "I see…"

Wood held his palms out in front of him, stressed but trying to keep his voice level.

Robin Wood said, "Mind telling me why you are destroying my office? I would like an intelligent explanation please, if possible?"

Buffy looked down at him for a long moment, then turned to Willow.

Buffy said, "It's not coming from him… I can tell…"

Willow said, "Good… I would hate to have the school needing another principal… already…"

Robin Wood said, "What did you just say to me? How did you know what my mother is? Who the hell are you?"

Willow started to answer, low and steady, taking one slow step toward the desk.

Willow said, "I am the thing monsters fear…"

Buffy held up a hand and stopped her, then pressed the photo gently against Wood's white dress shirt over his heart and laid her other hand firmly on his shoulder, meeting his eyes directly.

Buffy said, soft and honest, "I am sorry about your mother… I am the Slayer now… I need your help… I have to get under the school… I can feel something bad is happening there right now… I can't tell you what it is… But I know it's happening right now…"

For a long moment Wood stood there processing it, his eyes moving from her face to the photograph and back, and then he gave a slow nod, took his keys from his pocket, and stepped out from behind the desk.

Robin Wood said, "Follow me…"

They left the office and turned left down the hallway past the classrooms, kids staring out at them through the open doors, and in one of those rooms Dawn looked up and saw Buffy, Lucian and Willow go by, watched them for a second, and then went quietly back to her work.

At the end of the hall stood a tall white steel basement door, freshly painted, and Wood fumbled through his keys, unlocked it and pulled it open on a dark stairwell. Buffy handed his knife back to him, stepped in front, and drew the single-handed axe from the bag, Lucian loaded the crossbow and fell in close behind her, and the four of them went down into the darkness, leaving the white door standing open against the black below.

CHAPTER 7
Basement Approach

Down the basement stairwell and into the boiler room, the only light came from a few dim emergency fixtures and a faint leak from above, the shadows deep, cool and claustrophobic, and the only sounds were their quiet footsteps on the concrete, their own faint breathing, the distant hum of the boiler and the slow drip of water somewhere in the dark.

Buffy raised two fingers to her lips for silence and worked her way down to the bottom of the boiler room checking each corner one at a time, her head panning up and side to side and her eyes darting, sweat beading on her forehead and on the forearms wrapped around the axe, and at the bottom she turned left into a long hallway.

Buffy whispered, "Where does this go?"

Robin Wood whispered, "I don't know… I don't come down here… Just maintenance people do, for the swimming pool and water system…"

Buffy gave a slow nod without taking her eyes off the dark hallway ahead.

Buffy said, "Willow, get ready… they are close, whatever it is…"

Willow closed her eyes and powered up, faint heat waves rippling off her body and then settling, and she gave Buffy a silent nod that she was ready, pushing her damaged magic anyway.

Lucian took the left flank with the crossbow held low, Wood took the right, and Willow covered the rear with her hands open and fingers spread, ready to cast, and with Buffy at the point with the axe they held that formation at the mouth of the long, dark hallway while the tension thickened around them.

CHAPTER 8
Ambush

Down the long, dark hallway, under the low emergency lights and the faint, dirty daylight, the cool blue-grey concrete was all deep shadow and sickly yellow highlights on sweat, and their footsteps stayed quiet right up until the violence began. Ahead, a large jagged hole had been smashed through the smooth cement wall, its ragged edges still shedding dust, and Buffy threw up her left hand in a sharp signal to freeze and then led with her right foot over the broken edge.

A Bringer's knife slashed horizontally at her face out of the dark on her left, nothing but a blur, and Buffy snapped her head back forty-five degrees with her chin tucked and rotated her torso to the right, planting her right foot on the jagged lip, but her left ankle caught on a jut of broken concrete and twisted inside her boot in a painful wrench, her knee buckled, and she fell backward twisting, still gripping the axe, until her shoulder and hip thudded onto the floor.

Wood lunged forward, caught her under the left arm and hauled her up and back clear of the hole, and Buffy reset instantly with the axe raised, ignoring the throb in her left ankle. Behind them Willow closed her eyes for a split second and centered herself, then brought her hands up with her fingers rigid and straight and her palms facing forward.

Willow shouted, "SAXA, FITE DENTES!"

The words commanded the broken stone around the hole to become teeth, and as two Bringers rushed through the hole side by side, the cement edges around them warped and erupted into massive razor-sharp stone teeth that slammed shut with a grinding crack like a stone bear trap, impaling both robed bodies, and when the teeth crumbled back they collapsed at Willow's feet with their blood pooling on the concrete.

Willow swayed, her face draining pale, and her knees buckled under her so that she went down on one of them on the concrete while a drop of blood ran from her left nostril to her upper lip and the red of her hair darkened at the roots as the spell drained out of her. She shook her head hard, and when Lucian reached to steady her she shook him off too and pushed herself back up, her eyes locked on the threat, paying her damaged magic's price and refusing to stop.

Willow said, "Two down. Three to go."

Buffy gave one sharp nod and leapt through the hole with an explosive push-off, rotating her hips a full one-eighty in mid-air with the axe raised high, and she landed in a low stance on the sandy floor beyond and swung the axe in a flat horizontal arc at neck height that took the nearest Bringer's head clean off in a spray of arterial blood, and its body collapsed.

She ducked extremely low as the second Bringer's blade whooshed over her head, and came up with a rising diagonal cut that severed its left knee, and it went over and crashed onto its back in a soundless, open-mouthed spasm.

Lucian brought the crossbow up to eye level and put a bolt into the center of the last Bringer's chest, and it staggered and swayed with its knife still rising to within inches of Buffy's face, its blank, eyeless face tipping slowly back, and then it collapsed backward with the bolt buried in it.

On the floor the one-legged Bringer was trying to push itself up.

Buffy said, sharp and fed up, "Getting up, someone has to tag you first!"

Buffy exhaled hard through her nose, her eyebrows drawing down low over narrowed, fed-up eyes, and she slid her right hand up the haft until it rested just beneath the head and locked her left hand tight around the base of the grip as she lifted the axe high over her head. She sucked one sharp breath in through her teeth, shifted her weight onto her good right foot and brought the axe straight down in a hard, chopping arc with her whole body driving behind it, and the blade buried itself in the back of the Bringer's head with a wet, final crack that echoed off the stone, and its body went stiff and its knife clattered onto the concrete.

She pinned its back with her right boot and yanked the axe free with a wet sound, and Willow turned her head away for one pained second before she reset, and then the four of them pushed on through into the massive chamber beyond.

CHAPTER 9
I Choose Life

The chamber beyond was empty of guards now, the five Bringers who had circled the Seal lying dead and still across the sandy floor and the rubble of the broken wall behind them, and in the center of the floor the large, ominous Seal sat carved into the stone with its symbols giving off a faint, unnatural glow, while on the far wall Spike hung in his chains, shirtless and battered, his face swollen and cut, bruised and bloodied, his bare feet dangling as he sagged in the manacles.

Buffy pressed her left hand flat over her heart and winced as the calling hit her hard, a jolt of instinctive recognition.

Buffy said, "This is it right here, Will… this is the thing giving me the Slayer wiggins…"

Buffy said, "The Seal, Will… record everything on it. All the symbols, everything…"

Willow said, "I don't have any paper, Buffy…"

Buffy said, "I need to borrow this…"

She grabbed the front of Wood's clean white dress shirt in the middle and ripped it completely off him with a single yank, buttons flying, and laid it flat across the Seal, and Willow swept a hand over the fabric while black ink etched every one of the symbols onto it exactly, glowing faintly with her magic.

Buffy lifted the imprinted shirt off the Seal by its collar and handed it carefully to Willow.

Buffy said, "Don't lose this."

Willow wrapped the torn shirt around her waist and tied it in a secure knot at her hip, while Lucian stood at the hole they had come through with the crossbow scanning the hallway behind them, and on the far wall Spike lifted his head weakly, the cords of his neck straining with the effort.

Spike said, "Buffy… Buffy…"

Lucian, Buffy, Wood and Willow all looked up at him with shock and pain on their faces, and Willow touched her own lips as if she could feel his split one, while Buffy's eyes stayed on his swollen, beaten face with fury and pity and old love and iron resolve all moving in her at once. The secret from the Trio year stayed private behind her eyes, and she did not speak it; she spoke what the moment needed instead.

Buffy said, "Spike I can't let you out of here if you are going to keep coming after me, and obsessing over me…"

Spike whispered, broken, "I love you…"

Buffy's arms trembled, the axe in her right hand and her left hand clenched into a fist, and then she jabbed her index finger up at him.

Buffy shouted, "NO YOU CAN'T LOVE ANYTHING! YOU DON'T GET TO SAY THAT TO ME!"

She kept yelling, raw. "I could never let you inside of me… like cold worms inside the earth… the grave…"

She reached back with her left hand and took Lucian's hand tightly, their fingers interlacing like a lifeline, and looked up at Spike fierce and bright with tears.

Buffy said, "I choose life! I am marrying this man Spike! He gives me warmth you could never give me… He warmed my bones, my soul and brought me back to life!"

Spike's eyes filled with tears, and he sobbed quietly with his head bowed while the chains shook above him, the last of his hope breaking.

For ten long seconds nobody in the chamber said anything at all, and every face held still in the silence.

Buffy spoke again, lower and firm. "You have to give me your word Spike… no more games… you will respect my life and choices…"

Spike barely whispered, "I give you my word…"

Buffy said, "Get him down…"

Willow waved her left hand over the wall where Spike hung, and the chains unlocked with loud metallic clangs and the manacles sprang open, and Spike fell forward limp. Buffy dropped the axe, and Wood scooped it up off the floor while she caught Spike under the arms and hauled his battered body upright, his head lolling onto her shoulder and his blood and sweat smearing across her tank.

Buffy said, "Time to check you out of the pain motel…"

CHAPTER 10
Tunnel Extraction

Back through the basement, the faint emergency lights showed cool concrete gone mold-green with damp, the space claustrophobic around them, their footsteps echoing and dripping water ticking somewhere close, while Spike's wet, labored breathing rasped against Buffy's shoulder and the lunch bell waited somewhere far above them.

Wood brought his watch close to his face in the dim light and read eleven forty-two, and his eyes widened.

Robin Wood said, urgent and already moving, "Lunch bell rings in eighteen minutes. I have to be back in my office before then. I can't be missing when the hallways fill up."

He pointed past a run of old piping to a heavy, rusted metal door.

Robin Wood said, "There's an old maintenance tunnel access back here. It runs under the west side of the school. Comes out behind the maintenance shed near the edge of the parking lot. It's shaded by the building and trees. Did you come in a car? Can somebody bring it around?"

Buffy nodded, holding Spike up against her with one arm, and Willow dug her cell phone out of the pocket of her leggings and called Tara in the Highlander, telling her in a quick, low voice to bring it around to the west side maintenance exit, the shaded one, right now.

Wood unlocked the padlock with a rusty groan and yanked the door open on a dark, damp tunnel lit only by a few faint emergency lights.

Robin Wood said, "Straight as you can, then take the left fork after the big pipe. You'll see daylight at the end. Go. I have to get back upstairs."

He held an unreadable look on Spike for one long beat, then slid the axe back into Buffy's bag and jogged back toward the stairwell, his footsteps fading above them.

Buffy got under Spike's right arm with her left arm around his back gripping his side, favoring the left ankle she had wrenched at the broken wall, though it had already eased to a dull, hot throb the way a Slayer's hurts always did, and Lucian took his left arm after shrugging out of the long trench coat and draping it over Spike's head and torso like a hood and cloak, the healing Bringer wound low on his own left side pulling with every step of the carry. Willow brought up the rear with a soft magical light glowing in her palm, and when she had pulled her heather-grey sweater off over her head, leaving her in a fitted soft cotton t-shirt in a quiet moss green, she handed it to Buffy, who tucked it around Spike's bare torso under the coat.

Spike was barely conscious, his bare feet dragging along the tunnel floor and his knees buckling every few steps, and Buffy kept her focus tight on the way ahead while Lucian held up his side of him, steady and silent.

At the end of the tunnel a grate opened outward into a shaded alcove behind the maintenance shed, where Tara had already pulled the Highlander close with the engine running and the back door standing open. Lucian kept the coat over Spike through the last few feet, and together they lowered him, limp and heavy, into the back seat, propped half sitting against the far door with his legs stretched across the floor, and Willow tucked the sweater snug around his chest while the rest of the trench coat lay over him like a blanket with the collar pulled over his head and only part of his face showing. Buffy climbed into the back sideways beside him with one hand on his chest and the other holding his hand, Willow squeezed in at the near door with a hand on Spike's knee, and Lucian took the front passenger seat.

Tara drove smoothly, keeping to the shaded side streets for as long as she could before she turned onto the road toward the Magic Box, and as the Highlander pulled away, up on the main floor Wood came out of the stairwell, glanced once through a hallway window toward the departing car, straightened what was left of his clothes, and hurried toward his office as the lunch bell rang through the halls.

In his office the noon light came through the blinds in warm stripes across the mess Buffy had left, and the room felt quiet and heavy, the way a place did after a storm had gone through it. Wood stepped in among the pulled drawers, the scattered papers and the chair knocked askew, went to the wall closet and took out a fresh white dress shirt still in its dry-cleaner plastic, and buttoned it with numb precision, tucked it in, and knotted his tie in the closet mirror. Then he set his mother's photograph on the top shelf and let his thumb brush across her face for a long moment with an aching tenderness, before he closed the closet with a soft click and began, slowly and wearily, to right the chair, slide the drawers back in and gather up the papers.

CHAPTER 11
Blood Offering

Back at the Magic Box a little past noon, they came in through the heavy rear door off the alley, which opened inward so that no direct sunlight reached the entrance, into the training room, where soft, diffused daylight fell from the high windows in a shaded, safe light and the quiet sat heavy after the chaos at the school.

Buffy and Lucian carried Spike's weight between them with Willow close behind, his bare feet dragging across the mats, and they lowered him onto the old sofa against the training-room wall, where his body slumped under the trench coat like a shroud.

Buffy knelt beside him without hesitation, drew a small sharp knife from her boot, and made a shallow, clean cut across her left forearm, and as the blood welled up she slid her right hand under the back of Spike's matted head, lifted it slightly, and pressed the bleeding cut to his mouth, calm and intense about it, a matter of necessity and nothing like romance.

Lucian tensed and took a step forward with one hand half-raised, and Willow put a hand flat on his chest to stop him.

Willow said, "No… she's right. He's too hurt. She has to."

Lucian froze where he stood, protectiveness radiating off him, and watched with one hand curling into a fist and uncurling again at his side.

Spike's mouth latched onto the cut and he drew on it in slow, desperate pulls, his throat working through five deep, audible swallows, one after another, while a faint color crept back into his face and her blood smeared across his mouth and chin, and then Buffy pulled her arm away and his head fell back against the cushion, his eyes fluttering and closing as he dropped into a deep blackout, his body going slack and his breathing slowly evening out toward sleep.

Buffy stood, cradling her bleeding forearm, went into the little bathroom and wound a clean towel tight around the cut, tucking the end in with her teeth and her one free hand, and came back with the wrapped arm held against her body to stop at the foot of the sofa and look down at Spike's unconscious form, Lucian still tense nearby and Willow watching her with soft, understanding eyes, while the early-afternoon light slanted across the training-room floor.

CHAPTER 12
Open Sign

By late afternoon the light through the training room's high windows had softened into a warm slant, and the quiet in the back room was heavy with what had happened, while out front the ordinary business of the shop sat waiting to start again.

Spike lay unconscious on the old sofa under the trench coat, and Buffy sat on the floor beside him with her hand on his arm and her bandaged forearm in plain view, Lucian standing behind her with a hand on her shoulder and his thumb moving in slow, absent strokes, while Willow knelt on the other side wiping the dried blood from Spike's face with a damp cloth, soft and focused, her own nosebleed long since cleaned away but the cost of the stone teeth still sitting heavy in her.

Tara stood in the doorway between the training room and the front of the shop, exhausted but steady.

Tara said, "I have to go back out front… reopen the shop. Anya left me in charge. Customers are gonna start lining up soon. I can't leave it closed."

Buffy looked up and gave her a slow, understanding nod, Lucian squeezed Buffy's shoulder, and Willow offered a small, tired smile.

Tara looked to Willow, her voice soft. "I'll be right out there if you need me."

Willow nodded, and Tara lingered one moment longer in the doorway looking at the three of them, Buffy on the floor, Lucian behind her and Willow tending to Spike, before she turned and walked out to the front.

Out front the late-afternoon light came in through the windows, and Tara flipped the sign on the door to Open, switched on the small lamp by the register, straightened the crystals on their shelf and brushed the dust off the glass case, and then stood for a moment with both hands flat on the counter and her head bowed, just breathing, before she lifted her head and put on her soft, welcoming face, the exhaustion still there behind it. The bell over the front door chimed, and a woman in her mid-forties with a tote bag on her shoulder came in.

Tara said, "Hi… welcome in. Let me know if you need help finding anything."

Tara helped her with polite, warm attention, the exhaustion and the quiet weight of the day still visible behind her face, and from the training room came soft sounds, the rustle of cloth, someone shifting on the old sofa, low voices, life going on in the back while Tara held down the front. She stood at the counter in the late-afternoon light with the Open sign glowing in the window, and through the open doorway behind her she could see Buffy still on the floor beside Spike with Lucian's hand on her shoulder and Willow close by, the normal world carrying on out front while the aftermath sat quietly in the back.
