Buffy the Vampire Slayer. Season six, episode 08. Morning Light.

CHAPTER 1
The Weight of Dawn

At dawn in Buffy's bedroom, the only sounds that reached her were muffled ones bleeding up through the floorboards from the kitchen below: laughter, clinking bowls, the soft voices of a normal, happy morning that felt wrong against the quiet of her room. One beam of golden light cut across the unmade bed and crawled up the knitted grey blankets. Dust motes drifted in it like floating ash.

Buffy lay on top of the covers in her mom's old grey sleep shirt, the cotton stretched out at the collar from years of washing and still smelling faintly of detergent. Her bare legs lay still against the blanket, and her blonde hair was matted flat from a night of sleep that hadn't rested her at all. For five long seconds, her chest didn't rise even once.

Her skin was pale, with no rosy undertone at all, her lips dry and slightly parted, and dark bruised circles sat under her eyes. When her eyelids finally opened, they moved with a slow, mechanical heaviness. The golden light caught in her hazel irises for one fleeting second before the life drained out of them and left them flat and glassy. She stared up at the popcorn ceiling, her collarbones sharp against the shirt, until a single slow, ragged breath entered her body as if it hurt.

The household sounds kept bleeding up from downstairs: muffled laughter, the clink of cereal bowls, Tara's soft voice, and Willow humming a disjointed, upbeat tune. A tiny wince pulled at the corner of Buffy's mouth. She forced another breath into herself.

She sat up slowly and swung her legs over the edge of the bed, then rose like a sleepwalker, with none of her usual Slayer grace. She crossed the hallway with her bare feet dragging on the hardwood, never looking left or right, the oversized shirt hanging loose on her frame, and went into the bathroom.

CHAPTER 2
The Cracking Mirror

In the upstairs bathroom, in the flat light from the small frosted window, Buffy faced the mirror. She squeezed paste onto her toothbrush with trembling fingers and began brushing like a robot, staring dead into her own reflection. The faucet dripped into the sink, and the buzzing of the light fixture grew louder until it became a low, sickening hum that vibrated in her skull.

She was working the toothbrush into her back molars on the left side, staring absently into the mirror, when her eyes in the glass suddenly became hollow black voids and the flesh of her cheeks bulged unnaturally, mottled and necrotic grey. Thick, segmented worms burst through the skin of her reflection and writhed as they crawled out of her face, dropping wetly into the sink with visceral squelches. They had real weight, and they left wet trails across the white porcelain.

Buffy flinched backward in a hard, full-body spasm, eyes wide with terror. Her Slayer grip clamped down on the toothbrush, and the plastic snapped in half with a loud crack. In that same instant the hallucination vanished.

The wet sounds of the worms cut off, and only the dripping faucet remained. She stared down at the broken pieces in her hand for a long, exhausted moment while the panic bled away, and her face drooped back into the same crushing numbness.

She dropped the pieces into the trash and rinsed the mint taste from her mouth. When she looked up at her reflection one last time, it was a blank, perfect, empty mask. Then she turned off the harsh light and headed back across the hall to her bedroom, her bare feet making soft wet sounds on the tile.

CHAPTER 3
The Kitchen Tension

Tara slid the spatula under the eggs and turned them gently, one after the other, her shoulders held a little too high for someone who was only making breakfast. When the last egg settled back into the pan, she glanced over her shoulder at Willow, smoothed down the front of her cardigan, and took a slow breath.

Tara said, in a low but steady voice, "Willow… we need to talk. About the magic. Again."

Willow looked up quickly from her stool at the kitchen island, where she sat in a fitted maroon top with both hands wrapped around a steaming coffee mug. She set the mug down a little too hard, so that it clinked sharply against the countertop, and tried to keep her tone light, and failed.

Willow said, "Tara, not now. Everything’s already—"

Tara's voice rose slightly, and her face showed clear hurt as she cut in.

Tara said, "No. Now. I know what you did last week, Will. The Lethe’s bramble. You used it on me. Over a simple argument about you using magic too much. You wiped my memory like it was nothing."

Tara said, "I can feel how much magic you’re pulling lately. It’s changing you. Your eyes… even when you’re not casting, they look different. Hungrier."

Willow's face hardened, then softened into guilt. She stood and reached toward Tara with both hands, only to stop short with her fingers hovering in the air between them, her voice breaking.

Willow said, "Tara… I was just trying to keep the peace. You were so upset and I didn’t want us fighting in front of Buffy or Dawn. I fixed it. That’s all. I’m in control. I know what I’m doing."

Tara shook her head, the tears showing plainly now, and took one small step closer, her voice unwavering.

The two women locked eyes in silence, Tara's face holding raw love mixed with real fear while Willow's mouth worked once without a sound. Then, from upstairs, came the faint click of Buffy's bedroom door as she came back out, echoing down the stairwell.

Tara quickly wiped her eyes with the sleeve of her cardigan and turned back to the stove, forcing a softer expression onto her face. Willow sat back down and stared into her coffee while the sound of Buffy coming down the stairs carried through the floorboards.

CHAPTER 4
Buffy Descends

A few moments later, the smell of burning toast and strong coffee filled the kitchen, and steam rose from the stove into the light.

Buffy came down still in the faded grey sleep shirt, with an oversized dark hoodie pulled over it and the hood partly up. Her feet were bare, her hair a mess, her shoulders hunched and stiff.

Dawn was slumped over the kitchen island in an oversized knit sweater of clashing pinks, yellows and blues, devouring a bowl of heavily sugared cereal, her spoon scraping the bottom of the bowl with every pass. Each loud, wet crunch made Buffy wince, as if the noise were scraping raw nerves.

Tara glanced over her shoulder, the spatula pausing mid-turn, and her face softened with careful concern as she spoke, stuttering slightly.

Tara said, "You sure you’ll be okay here by yourself today, Buffy? W-w-we can stay if you need us. Or… you could come with us. I’ve got Intro to Scandinavian Folklore at ten with Professor Halloran. Buffy said, "I’m the Slayer. I can handle one day of mindless talk shows and mixing up everyone’s socks on purpose. Go. Live your lives."

Willow said, "I love you, Buffy."

Buffy looked down at her with no warmth and nothing in her gaze, just an empty, distant echo, and gave the smallest nod.

Buffy whispered, "Ditto."

Tara stood behind Willow, one hand resting protectively on her shoulder. After a moment she sighed and turned back to finish the eggs.

CHAPTER 5
The Silence and the Breakdown

After the others had left for the day, the house was suddenly empty, and the only sound was the methodical tick-tock of the antique clock in the living room. The morning light had moved on, leaving the rooms cooler and flatter, full of long shadows.

Buffy pushed the hood of her hoodie down and moved to the sink. She turned the water on and let it run over her hands and felt nothing, her eyes glazed. She finished the few dishes with robotic stiffness and dried her hands slowly, staring at the rough texture of the towel, before she turned and walked into the living room, and stopped dead.

She was standing directly in front of the floral couch, the exact couch where she had found Joyce's lifeless body. Her whole body went rigid, and every breath grew thinner, until the air itself seemed to squeeze her, gently, as if it were made of cotton. She could not breathe at all, and her hands and feet went numb and tingling.

Then the memory took her, and she was back in this room on that afternoon.

Her mother lay on the couch with her eyes open, staring up at nothing, and Buffy's own voice came out small and uncertain.

Buffy said, "Mom?… Mom?"

Joyce didn't answer, and when Buffy grabbed her by the shoulders and shook her, her mother's body moved limp and heavy under her hands, and the head lolled.

Buffy screamed, "MOM, MOM, MOM!"

Nothing came back. She dragged her mother off the couch and onto the floor, locked her hands together on Joyce's chest, and started CPR, pushing down hard again and again and counting under her breath, while Joyce's dead eyes kept staring upward into nothing.

The present slammed back into place.

Buffy's knees buckled, and she dropped hard onto the carpet in the exact same spot. Her palms pressed against the sides of her face and her fingers dug into her hair, as if she were trying to hold her skull together, and a raw, broken sound escaped her throat before the sobs fully hit.

Her whole body convulsed inside the oversized hoodie as ugly sobs tore out of her. Her mouth hung open at first in a silent scream before the sound broke loose, her chest heaving erratically and tears streaming down her pale face while her eyes stayed wide and panicked, and her voice came out shattered and rasping between the sobs.

Buffy said, "Why... Why am I alive...?"

She collapsed forward and curled into a tight, defensive ball on the carpet, and she stayed there until the sobs burned themselves out. Nothing was left but hollow, ragged breathing, her body trembling with aftershocks and her face hidden against her knees, while the tick-tock of the clock went on through all of it.

CHAPTER 6
The Commute

When the last of the shaking had passed, Buffy pushed herself up off the carpet with both palms and padded out to the front hall. Her old high-top sneakers sat slumped against the wall by the door, faded to a tired white, the toes scuffed from too many nights in cemeteries. She lowered herself onto the bottom step and hooked the nearest one by its heel with her right hand, dragging it across the floorboards toward her. Bent low over her knees, her hair falling forward around her face, she drew the frayed laces tight one eyelet at a time, her red-rimmed eyes fixed on the rubber toes as if they belonged to someone else. For a long moment after the last knot she just sat there, forearms resting on her knees. Then she pulled herself up by the banister with her left hand, tugged the hood back over her hair, and let herself out into the bright morning.

Later that morning, out on the Sunnydale streets, harsh sunlight sat over manicured lawns and white picket fences, and cheerful suburban life swung along all around her: a golden retriever barking, a postman whistling, sprinklers hissing and lawnmowers humming in the distance.

Buffy moved through all of it with her hood pulled up to shadow her face, her hands shoved deep into the front pocket, her sneakers scuffing softly over the warm sidewalk. Her hair was still a little disheveled from the breakdown, and her eyes were glassy and unfocused, fixed on nothing.

She walked like a ghost through the sunshine, every footstep measured and the opposite of Slayer grace. She didn't flinch at the barking dog or the bright light, and the sprinklers and the distant laughter faded behind her.

She had nowhere to be, so she just kept walking while the morning wore away into the afternoon around her, and by the time her feet brought her to the Magic Box, Xander was just reaching the door, straight from his shift.

CHAPTER 7
Arrival and Redirection

Inside the Magic Box that afternoon, warm sunlight slanted through the shop windows and lit the floating dust as Xander came in first. He carried a grease-stained white Krispy Kreme box of glazed donuts in one hand, the sleeves of his faded blue-and-grey plaid flannel rolled up over a plain white t-shirt, a faint five-o'clock shadow on his jaw and his scuffed brown work boots thudding on the floorboards.

Buffy followed behind him with the hood partly up and her hands deep in the pocket, her eyes still red-rimmed from the morning and her jaw set like stone.

Xander held the door open, and once Buffy had stepped in behind him he walked straight to the counter, dropped the box, and leaned over the register to catch the back of Anya's neck for a deep, familiar kiss, and Anya leaned into it happily in her form-fitting blouse and practical skirt, completely ignoring the customers.

Buffy didn't even blink, because the casual, easy affection didn't register at all. Without stopping to greet Anya or looking at the donuts, she bypassed the main counter and drifted to a spinning display rack of healing crystals, where she stood perfectly still and stared through the colorful stones while the ordinary life of the shop carried on behind her.

Xander rubbed his thumb with a small groan.

Xander said, "And I brought splinters. Framed out an entire load-bearing wall this morning and the pine practically attacked me."

Anya said, "A splinter is a small price to pay for capitalistic progress, Xander. At least your wood didn't try to curse you. I just had a woman try to return a fertility amulet without a receipt because it gave her a rash. Xander said, "Well, the BBB doesn't cover hexes. Want a glazed?"

Buffy stayed entirely detached. After five more seconds she turned and walked straight for the heavy oak door to the back training room, which shut behind her with a dull, final thud.

Xander and Anya shared a concerned look toward the back room, where Buffy had gone again instead of staying out here with everyone else.

CHAPTER 8
The Quiet Intervention

Out on the retail floor a few moments later, Willow sat on the worn velvet couch with her legs tucked under her and a weathered volume of Irish mythology on her lap. Her eyes were on the text, though the guilt from that morning's talk with Tara still sat behind them.

Her gaze drifted across the floor and landed on Lucian, who stood near the arcane ingredients section turning a jar slowly in both hands, his thumb tracing the embossed label. His long black hair was tied back at the nape of his neck, and the silver raven buckle on his dark trousers caught the light. He kept to himself, relaxed but grounded, reading the label without really taking it in.

Willow studied him for a long, calculating moment before her expression softened into something sad and protective. She closed the book, set it aside, and waved him over with a warm, inviting smile.

Lucian set the jar back on the shelf with care and walked over, his boots surprisingly quiet on the old floorboards, his face polite and open. Willow's tone came out as a perfect, bubbly imitation of casual warmth.

Willow said, "Hey, Lucian. I just wanted to say… I’ve really been noticing your focus in the meditation classes lately. You’re doing so good! It’s just really nice seeing you around the shop more often."

Lucian's shoulders eased a fraction at the praise, and he gave a genuine small nod as he turned to face her fully. Behind him, the jar's label still faced neatly forward on the shelf.

Lucian said, "I appreciate that, Willow. Honestly, that technique you recommended — the prana conserving before sleeping? It’s been incredibly effective. A very logical way to organize the mind at the end of the day."

Willow's eyes lit up with genuine nerdy enthusiasm, and her hands started shaping the idea in the air between them.

Willow said, "Right? Prana channeling is completely underrated! It’s basically like defragging your spiritual hard drive. It clears out all the corrupted background files so the whole system just runs smoother!"

Her smile lingered, but her eyes moved deliberately past him and locked onto the door to the back room. She took a slow, steadying breath, and her voice dropped into something carefully casual, but loaded with intent.

Willow said, "Speaking of bleeding off tension… Buffy just went into the back room. It’s actually a full training gym back there. Free weights, heavy leather bags, the works."

Lucian's eyes brightened, and he glanced toward the door and back down to Willow with quiet anticipation.

Lucian said, "A gym? Are you sure? I didn’t think students or customers were allowed back there."

Willow's bubbly front dropped away, and something sad, protective and directive replaced it as she looked up at him, her gaze thick with unspoken instruction.

Willow said softly, "It’s fine. Go on… please."

Lucian studied her face for a fraction of a second and felt the emotional weight behind the simple request, and he understood without being told twice. He gave a slow, respectful nod and glanced once toward the door and back to Willow, his right hand settling on his belt buckle out of habit. Then he straightened and walked quietly toward the training room.

He pushed the door open with his palm flat against the wood and stepped through, and it swung shut behind him with a solid thud that cut the retail floor off from the sounds within.

After Lucian went through, Tara came over and slid in beside Willow on the couch, leaning in to whisper into Willow's left ear.

Tara said, "Anya put me on break. I have to go to the bathroom… want to join me?"

Willow's eyes lit up with surprise and eager desire, and she nodded as a soft, breathy "yes" escaped her and the tension of the day broke into something playful and urgent. Giggling softly together, Tara and Willow made their way down the back hallway to the small bathroom, where a single low old bulb cast warm, soft shadows across the tiled walls.

They shut the small bathroom door, still giggling, and locked it with a click, and then Willow stared at Tara for a long few seconds before she slid the backs of her fingers down Tara's face in a slow, sensual motion, and Tara closed her eyes to the touch and bit her lower lip in answer.

After the morning's confrontation, and the weight of magic and fear, they chose each other with gentle hands. Willow kissed her and pressed Tara's body back against the bathroom door with care, and Tara's breasts rose against the laced edge of her corset as she put her arms loosely around Willow's neck, her fingers threading gently into red hair. They kissed long and slow, like two people who had known each other for many years, deep but tender, and Tara's breathing deepened as a soft shiver ran through her shoulders, her fingers curling into the fabric at Willow's neck, her eyes closed and the tension in her jaw easing into the moment.

Willow ended the kiss by gently sucking on Tara's bottom lip before pulling away, affectionate and familiar, and then her left shoulder dipped as she slowly traced Tara's bare leg with the backs of her fingers and slid her hand up under the hem of the long skirt, with reverence rather than urgency.

Tara's eyes squeezed shut as she tilted her head back against the door, her breath coming harder and her face flushed with warmth and trust, a single strand of hair sticking to the sweat at her temple, and a sharp, involuntary breath escaped her as her back arched against the wood and her thighs tensed, one hand gripping Willow's shoulder for an anchor while her chest rose and fell in quick, shallow breaths and the fabric of her skirt shifted and rustled softly, until her voice came out tight and breathless.

CHAPTER 9
Iron and Leather

At the same time, in the training room, overhead fluorescent bulbs cast a functional glow over the equipment, cooler and flatter than the light on the retail floor. The room smelled of old leather, cold iron and dust.

Lucian was already on the incline chest press in a fitted black tank top, his outer shirt folded on a nearby bench and the large black raven-wing tattoo across his chest and left shoulder on show. Sweat streaked his pale arms, and on every press he let out a low, rhythmic hiss of breath.

Buffy stood a few feet away, leaning against a wooden rack of medieval battle-axes and broadswords. She pulled the oversized hoodie off over her head and dropped it onto a nearby bench, the grey sleep shirt underneath sweat-damp at the collar.

She watched him for a moment in silence, her face an unreadable mask, her eyes flat. Lucian caught her stare reflected in the chrome edge of the machine, but he didn't hold her gaze and he didn't break his rhythm, giving her room to look without making it a confrontation. Something flickered at the corner of her mouth, the first tiny spark of interest she had shown all day, before the numbness buried it again. When she spoke, her voice was flat and empty of energy.

Buffy said, "Not bad."

Lucian exhaled sharply and racked the weight with a metal clang, then sat up and grabbed a towel to wipe his face. He offered her the faintest half-smile without pushing the conversation.

Lucian said, "I find it helps to move heavy things when the brain won’t shut up. You looked like you could use the quiet back here."

He stood and gestured to the padded leather seat of the machine with an overly formal, courtly bow, holding out his right hand in mock invitation.

Lucian said, "Your turn. Unless you're a cardio girl?"

The corner of Buffy's mouth twitched, not quite a smile, but the first flicker of life. Without stretching or warming up, she reached down to the steel weight stack, pulled the pin all the way out, and dropped it into the bottom slot for maximum weight.

She sat down, settled her back against the pad, grabbed the handles, and began to push. The plates rose and fell with an effortless, rhythmic clank, clank, clank, no grunting and no straining, hundreds of pounds of solid iron moving as if they were styrofoam. Only after the tenth quick rep did a faint flicker of real effort touch her face.

Lucian went perfectly still with the towel in his hand, staring at the entire stack of weights floating up and down and then at her slight, twenty-year-old frame.

Lucian said, "Wait a minute here…"

Buffy let the weights down slowly until the stack settled with an echoing crash, and she sat forward with her elbows on her knees and her hands clasped loosely, looking up at him, exhausted. A sigh escaped her. Her eyes closed briefly against the weight of the words, and when they opened again she made herself meet his, curling inward slightly.

Buffy said, "Oh. Right. We need to have the talk."

Giles's voice came from nearby.

Giles said, "Yes. Indeed we do."

Lucian turned sharply as Rupert Giles stepped out from the shadows near the archival stairs, deadly serious in a tweed jacket, and took off his wire-rimmed glasses and tucked them into his breast pocket. Lucian recovered quickly, stepping forward to offer his hand with firm, traditional etiquette.

Lucian said, "Mr. Giles."

Giles looked at the hand for a fraction of a second before he took it and tested the grip, which was firm and respectful and made no attempt to establish dominance. He smiled faintly, entirely without warmth.

Giles said, "Good form, Lucian. Please, sit."

He motioned toward a worn red leather sofa against the brick wall, then went to a small side table and made tea with a steaming electric pot. He brought back two cups and handed one to Buffy, who grabbed the sugar dispenser without looking at anything in particular and dumped three heaping spoonfuls into her cup, still winded from the weights. Giles winced visibly, cleared his throat, and locked his gaze onto Lucian.

Giles said, "I’ve noticed you around the shop recently, Lucian. Attending Willow’s meditation classes. Lingering near the arcane texts. I make it a point to notice everyone who steps into my store. Particularly those who take a sudden, deeply personal interest in my Slayer."

Lucian's posture stayed open, but his fingers tightened slightly on the arm of the sofa as he chose his words carefully.

Lucian said politely, "It’s a fascinating store, Mr. Giles. And Willow is an excellent teacher."

Giles's eyes narrowed, and he leaned forward, every syllable carrying the full weight of the Watcher.

Giles said, "Let us skip the pleasantries. I have lived a very long, very complicated life. I have seen the absolute darkest parts of humanity… and the other world, quite literally. I know how to spot a threat. And more importantly, I know how to spot a parasite."

Lucian's polite smile vanished, and he sat up a little straighter, but Giles went on without giving him room.

Giles said, "Buffy is not a normal woman. She is a weapon against evil. Lucian blinked, caught off guard, and for half a second his smooth confidence faltered. The polished calm cracked. His eyes darted away before he forced them back to Giles, and his voice lost its even rhythm.

Lucian said, "I… no. That’s not — I’m not trying to save anyone. I just… I saw her in the shop and she looked like she could use someone who isn’t… I mean, I’m just here."

Giles's eyes narrowed, utterly unimpressed. He took a slow sip of his tea and let the silence crush Lucian, while Buffy watched the exchange with an unreadable expression, her grip tightening slightly on her teacup. Then the door banged open and hit the wall with a loud crack as Anya burst into the training room with jittery, caffeinated energy. Giles said, "I think you understand why we are having this conversation?"

Lucian nodded, still recovering from the whiplash, and looked over at Buffy with something warm under the awe.

Lucian said, "Yes, sir. I understand completely."

Giles set his cup down, leaned forward, and tapped Lucian on the shoulder with a firm, approving pat, his voice warming.

Giles said, "Good lad."

Buffy's voice was still slightly raspy from choking.

Buffy said, "Well, at least he isn’t running out the door screaming in terror after finding out monsters are real."

Lucian reached out and gently, deliberately placed his right hand on Buffy's shoulder.

Lucian said, "You’re not a monster, Buffy."

She stood coldly, shrugging his hand off, put her teacup down on the bench with a harsh clatter, and marched straight over to the leather punching bag hanging from the ceiling.

CHAPTER 10
The Sugar Tax and Teenage Anxiety

Dawn dragged herself inside looking exhausted, her backpack hanging off one shoulder and her sweater even more rumpled than it had been that morning. Her face had the pinched, tired look of a fifteen-year-old carrying a bad day at school on top of a family that still hadn't recovered from losing her mother.

She spotted Xander behind the main counter, actively violating store policy as he sat on a stool next to the register chewing a glazed donut and trying to sort a box of incredibly dangerous-looking crystals, sawdust from his shift still clinging to his flannel.

Without a word of hello, Dawn marched straight to the counter and dropped her overstuffed backpack onto the floor with a thump that shook the display cases. She stared directly at the donut box by Xander's elbow until he paused mid-chew, a flake of glaze on his lower lip, and looked from Dawn to the box.

Xander said, "These are for the actual, contributing members of the Sunnydale workforce."

Dawn just raised a single eyebrow, perfectly mimicking Buffy's signature 'don't test me' look, then opened the lid, pulled out a glazed donut, and took a huge bite, talking with her mouth full.

Dawn said, "Consider it a tax. School was literal torture. Josh spent the whole period tapping his stupid boot, and I think I failed geometry."

Xander sighed, giving up the fight, and wiped the sticky glaze off his fingers onto a paper napkin.

Xander said, "Well, lucky for you, math is useless in the real world unless you are trying to calculate the exact trajectory of a fleeing demon."

Dawn leaned back against the glass counter and looked out across the quiet store, chewing slowly, her eyes drifting down the dim aisles toward the back room. When she swallowed, she tried to sound casual and failed completely. Her free hand found the edge of the counter and held on, and the question came out in a rush, with a quick, nervous swallow after it.

Dawn said, "So… is the tall, dark, and gloomy guy still here?"

Xander said, "Lucian? Yeah. He’s in the back gym. Doing… whatever it is guys in leather coats do to stay fit. Brooding lifts, probably."

Dawn took another small bite, picking nervously at a flake of sugar on the donut, and stared down at her fingernails.

Dawn said, "Is he, like… her boyfriend boyfriend?"

Xander stopped sorting the crystals and looked up, sensing the real teenage anxiety under the question, and he leaned on the counter and shifted into big-brother mode.

Xander said, "Dawnie, I have absolutely no idea what he is. Honestly, your sister is operating on a frequency right now that the rest of us aren’t picking up. But he hasn’t been chased out of the Magic Box with a battle-axe yet, so that’s a plus."

Dawn paced a few steps, getting worked up, her boots scuffing the floor.

Dawn said, "You don’t know that! First, they work out together. Then he’s hanging around the house. Then suddenly his weird Victorian boots are in the hallway. I am not sharing a bathroom with a stranger, Xander. I’m just not. He wears more silver than I do. The counter space will be a disaster."

Xander let out a sharp, genuine laugh that filled the quiet space.

Xander said, "Whoa, moving in? Dawn, the guy just started showing up to magic class. I don’t think he’s picking out curtains for the guest room just yet."

Dawn's shoulders slumped as the last of the bluster drained out of her, and she stared at the donut in her hand as if it held answers. Her voice came out small and cracked open.

Dawn said, "It’s just… everything already changed so much when she came back. And she’s so quiet all the time. If someone else comes in… what if it gets worse? What if he takes up whatever little space she has left?"

Xander's goofy manner vanished, and his face softened with protective empathy. He walked around the counter, stepped into the aisle with her, and leaned against the wood paneling with his arms crossed.

Xander said, "Look at me."

Dawn looked up with her eyes wide and vulnerable, her lower lip trembling briefly before it firmed, braced for the reassurance she both wanted and feared.

Xander said, "Buffy isn’t going anywhere. She is right here. And if this Lucian guy even thinks about making things harder for her—or for you—I will personally introduce him to the wrong end of a framing hammer."

Dawn managed a tiny, reluctant half-smile.

Dawn said, "You’d probably just hit your own thumb."

Xander's voice went deadpan.

Xander said, "I would absolutely hit my own thumb. But the sheer volume of my screaming would terrify him into submission. It’s a tactical maneuver."

Dawn rolled her eyes, but the tension finally went out of her, and she took the last bite of her donut and chewed it thoughtfully.

Dawn said, "I still call dibs on the shower schedule if he stays over. I’m not waking up at six a.m. just so Dracula can condition his hair."

Xander laughed and reached over to give her shoulder a light shove, and the touch grounded her.

Xander said, "Fair enough. Shower dibs are legally binding. Now grab a rag, you’re eating into store profits and Anya is going to make me pay for that donut if you don’t do some manual labor."

Dawn groaned loudly, but there was no real heat behind it, and she dragged her feet toward the back counter to find some glass cleaner.

CHAPTER 11
The Secret Invitation

A little later, Willow and Tara came back out front at last, laughing softly, tender and flushed and a little disheveled, because their break had ended a while ago and they had lingered in the quiet back hallway, and Tara fixed Willow's hair with careful fingers while Willow gave her a lingering kiss on the cheek. Xander was wiping down the glass counter, and Dawn sprayed a display case with blue glass cleaner and ignored the affection completely, as if it were entirely normal.

Beneath the quiet chatter of the store came the faint sounds of customers outside and Anya's voice in the background, and under all of it a steady thud, thud, thud still echoed through the walls: Buffy was still punishing the leather bag in the training room.

The training room door finally clicked open, and Lucian stepped out with his shirt pulled back on over the tank top. He paused in the doorframe with his palm flat on the jamb and his eyes closed for half a second, then rolled his neck once, pulled his shirt hem straight with both hands, and adjusted the cuffs.

He looked exhausted, the weight of his conversation with Giles still on his shoulders, and he kept his head down and made straight for the front exit.

Willow spotted him, and her soft, romantic manner dropped instantly as that sharp, calculating focus replaced it. She gently pulled away from Tara.

Willow whispered, "Be right back, sweetie."

Willow caught Lucian just before he reached the front door, stepping smoothly into his path with a bright, entirely fabricated smile.

Willow said, "Hey, Lucian! Heading out?"

Lucian's voice was low and rough with fatigue, the words coming out on a slow exhale. His eyes looked tired, and for a moment the weight of the day showed in the slump of his posture before he made himself straighten.

Lucian said, "Yes. It’s been a… long afternoon."

Willow bubbled and reached out her hand.

Willow said, "Well, I’m really looking forward to seeing you tomorrow again, Lucian. For the meditation class."

Lucian took her hand for a polite shake, and as their palms met, Willow's thumb pressed a small, tightly folded square of paper into his palm. His eyes flicked down in surprise before his stoic front covered it, and Willow pulled her hand back with a deliberate, exaggerated wink.

Lucian's fingers closed around the hidden paper, and the faintest flicker of confusion crossed his eyes before the polite mask locked back into place.

Lucian said steadily, "Right. Of course. See you then."

He didn't open the paper, only shoved his hand deep into his trouser pocket and pushed through the front door, and the brass bell jingled cheerfully behind him.

CHAPTER 12
The Note

Out on the street in front of the Magic Box, the late afternoon sun sat harsh against the pavement, and long shadows stretched across the sidewalk. Lucian stopped beside his car, a sleek black 1981 Pontiac Firebird Trans Am Special Edition with the gold firebird across the hood, the T-tops and the tan seats, and leaned against the fender to read, hunched slightly with the weight of the afternoon.

He looked deeply uneasy at the cryptic, highly specific instruction. He looked back at the Magic Box and down at the paper again, then sighed, folded the note into his pocket, climbed into the driver's seat, and drove off into the Sunnydale afternoon.

CHAPTER 13
The Breaking Point

That night at the Summers house, Willow glanced up at the clock on the kitchen wall and saw that it was almost half past ten.

A moment later, total domestic warfare filled the rooms, three voices screaming over each other and echoing off the walls. Willow's came from the kitchen, shrill and vibrating with angry authority.

Willow said, "It’s a highly volatile Class-3 binding component, Dawn! It doesn't just 'fall' into a canvas backpack! You took it from the shop!"

Dawn screamed back, deeply defensive and panicked.

Dawn said, "I didn't steal it! Someone else put it there! Why do you automatically assume I’m lying?!"

Tara tried to mediate, but her voice rose so she could be heard.

Tara said, "Will, please, let’s just take a breath. Yelling at her isn't—"

Willow cut in from the kitchen.

Willow said, "Don't take her side, Tara! She’s been acting out for months and we are supposed to be the responsible ones here!"

The living room was pitch black but for the harsh, erratic strobe of the television, which flashed through infomercials, static, news anchors and cartoons, painting Buffy's face in sickening, changing colors.

Buffy sat on the floral couch with her knees pulled tight to her chest and her bare feet on the edge of the cushions, staring blankly at the TV. Her thumb mashed the Channel Up button in a hard, steady rhythm, and her jaw locked tighter as the screaming grew louder.

Dawn's voice broke in the kitchen, a sudden hysterical sob cutting through her yell.

Dawn said, "You're not my mom, Willow! Stop trying to be!"

The screaming hit a fever pitch. Buffy dropped the remote and slammed both hands over her ears, eyes squeezed shut, chest heaving. The sensory overload was physically tearing at her, and she couldn't take it.

She leapt off the couch and power-walked straight for the front door in the hoodie she still had on, without stopping for shoes, yanked the door open, and escaped into the night, slamming it shut behind her to cut off the noise.

CHAPTER 14
The Bench

Out on Revello Drive that night, a single streetlamp cast a pale yellow glow, and the cold air bit at her bare feet and face, while across the street the Summers house sat mostly dark with one window still flickering from the TV.

Buffy sat on the green wooden bus bench, pulled her knees up, and wrapped her arms around her legs in a tight, defensive ball, and she let out a long, ragged sigh.

The yelling from inside the house was still audible, muffled now, bleeding out through the closed windows like a distant storm.

Footsteps approached calmly on the pavement, and Lucian walked into the glow of the streetlamp in a long dark leather coat of Victorian cut, his black hair loose now and his buckle catching the light. He looked at the Summers house and heard the muffled screaming before he looked down at Buffy, sitting barefoot on the bench.

Lucian said, "Mind if I sit?"

Buffy didn't look up at him. She rested her chin on her knees and stared dead-eyed across the street at her own front door, her fingers digging a little deeper into her arms. The mask was holding, but the tension showed in the cords of her neck, and her voice came out flat, dripping with dry defense.

Buffy said, "Is stalking a prerequisite for the brooding, dark aesthetic, or just a fun weekend hobby?"

Lucian ignored the barb and sat down on the green wooden slats beside her, leaving a respectful foot of space between them, then stretched his long legs out and crossed them at the ankles.

Lucian said, "It’s difficult to stalk a house that is projecting its domestic disputes three blocks away."

Buffy flinched slightly at the truth of it and closed her eyes. The exhaustion showed in every line of her, in the slump of her shoulders inside the hoodie and the way her chin sank back down onto her knees. A single tear welled at the corner of one eye without falling, and her voice cracked with pure weariness.

Buffy said, "It’s so loud down here."

Lucian said, "The fighting?"

Buffy said, "Everything."

She dropped her legs and leaned forward, resting her elbows on her knees and looking down at the concrete.

Buffy said, "Before… where I was… it was quiet. It was finished. There was no pain, no fear, no having to figure out how to pay the water bill or stop my sister from crying. It was just… warm. And now I’m back here, and everything is sharp. The colors are too bright, the air feels heavy, and the noise… God, the noise just scrapes against the inside of my skull. It's all just a chaotic, pointless, screaming mess. Except when I'm hitting things. That's the only time it makes any sense."

Through the entire confession Lucian didn't fidget, didn't offer a sympathetic murmur, and didn't reach for her, keeping his crossed ankles still and his hands resting loose on his knees.

When she finished, she looked over at him with her eyes wide and defensive, silently daring him to offer her a meaningless platitude or to tell her she should be grateful to be alive. Lucian met her gaze without flinching, gave a single small nod for the weight of what she had said, and did neither. He didn't look at her like a broken thing that needed fixing, but like an equal, and his voice was low, a deep, steady rumble.

Lucian said, "I can't turn the volume down for you, Buffy. And I won't insult you by telling you to look for the silver lining. But I will sit here in the noise with you. For as long as it takes."

He said it simply, with no expectation in his voice and no demand, and Buffy sat very still beside him while it sank in. Then the muffled screaming from the house stopped abruptly, and total silence fell over the neighborhood. Buffy slowly stood up from the bench, and Lucian stood with her, his tall frame towering slightly over her but his posture relaxed and unthreatening.

Buffy stepped into his personal space, and for the first time the defensive, stoic Slayer armor dropped completely as her small pale hands wrapped gently around his right hand and took it in both of hers as if it were something fragile and precious, and she slowly lifted it to her face and closed her eyes, surrendering completely to the feeling of it.

She pressed the back of his hand against her nose and breathed in deeply, a slow, shuddering, almost desperate breath, as if she were trying to pull the reality of another living person into her own lungs as proof that she was still here.

She smelled the leather of his coat and the cold night air on his skin, the faint clean trace of his soap, and something quietly stable that was simply Lucian, and it hit her like the first real thing she had felt since clawing her way out of the grave, a visible shudder running through her shoulders and down her spine as her lips parted, and she pressed a single soft, lingering kiss to the back of his hand and held it there for a long moment, breathing against his skin.

In the yellow glow of the streetlamp, a warm flush of color bloomed across Buffy's pale cheeks and spread down her neck and across the top of her chest, and when she opened her eyes they were wet but no longer glassy, and she searched his face for one long second to make sure he was real, and said nothing.

Then she lowered his hand, stepped back, and turned to walk barefoot across the street, her posture still exhausted but no longer completely locked, and she opened the front door, stepped inside, and pulled it shut with a soft, deliberate click.

Lucian stood alone by the green bench and watched the closed door for a long moment, then let out a deep sigh, shoved his hands into the pockets of his coat, and walked away down the quiet dark street toward his car.

CHAPTER 15
Two A.M. and the Crypt

At two o'clock in the morning the Summers house was dark, painted in soft silver moonlight that crept slowly and coldly across the floors, the only thing in the house that moved.

In her bedroom, Dawn lay on her side in bed, wide awake, staring at the nightstand in her rumpled sweater, pulled over a t-shirt for sleep, while her backpack slumped on the floor nearby.

A small framed photograph of Joyce stood on the nightstand. Dawn reached out from under the blankets, her fingers trembling slightly, and rested her fingertips against the cold glass over her mother's face. The fear of being abandoned again was etched deep in her tired young eyes, and a single tear pooled without falling.

In Willow and Tara's bedroom, Tara slept peacefully beside Willow, her chest rising and falling in a slow, steady rhythm under the duvet, in a simple nightgown with a soft earthy-toned cardigan draped over the chair nearby, while Willow lay next to her completely awake in her slept-in maroon top, staring up at her own hands held above her face in the moonlight.

In Buffy's bedroom, Buffy lay exactly as she had at the start of the day, flat on her back and staring up at the popcorn ceiling, still in the sleep shirt and the hoodie with the hood pushed back.

The numbness from the morning was gone. Instead, she slowly traced the knuckles of her right hand with her left thumb, over and over, the same place where, on his hand, she had pressed her lips under the streetlamp. Her eyes were wide as she took in the terrifying reality that a small spark of humanity was waking back up inside her. Her breathing was no longer a corpse's stillness but something subtle and rhythmic, as if she were afraid the feeling might disappear if she breathed too deeply.

Earlier that night, at half past ten out on Revello Drive, while Buffy sat on the bench with Lucian, Spike had been walking up the street toward the Summers house to see her. They had kissed at the end of that strange, singing night, and he hadn't seen her since, and though it had only been a week, it had felt long, and he had half a mind to ask her out.

Then he saw her in the distance and stopped, because she was not alone, and he stepped back into the pitch-black shadows of an old oak tree a long way down the street, and from there he watched the exact moment Buffy kissed the back of Lucian's hand.

For a moment he only stood there, and the hurt came quietly, a flicker across his face that looked almost human. Then it twisted. His fangs slid down with a slick sound, his brow ridged into his demon face, and his eyes flashed a predatory, luminous yellow in the dark while his leather duster stirred in the night breeze. He muttered bitterly into the dark, low and lethal.

Spike said, "He’ll never be able to tame that monster."

Then he turned sharply on his heel, the heavy leather duster snapping in the cold night wind, and stalked off into the darkness, back toward his crypt.

When he got back to his crypt, Spike burst through the iron doors with the rusted hinges screaming, into a tomb lit only by a few scattered candles that cast long, erratic shadows across the stone walls and burial markers.

Completely unhinged, he grabbed a wooden chair and hurled it across the room with supernatural force. It smashed into a stone pillar and splintered into hundreds of jagged pieces with a crack that echoed through the crypt. He didn't stop. He whirled around, his duster snapping, and drove his bare fist into the solid stone of a burial marker again and again until the rock fractured under the impact, and he never felt the pain in his bloody knuckles.

What he felt was the crushing reality of his own limits. He stared at his torn hands, fangs bared in a feral snarl. If Lucian were a demon, Spike would already have ripped his throat out right there on the street. But Lucian was human. The chip in his head meant he couldn't lay a finger on the mortal without it splitting his skull with pain. He was a caged predator, forced to watch a fragile ticking heartbeat touch the woman he was obsessed with.

He stopped pacing and walked over to the cold stone altar, planted his bloody hands flat on the rock, and stared dead-eyed into the flame of a single thick white candle. Slowly the manic rage drained out of his face. The feral snarl smoothed into something colder, a calculating and terrifying resolve, and the demon ridges receded from his brow as the yellow faded from his eyes to leave them an icy, predatory blue. His voice dropped to a lethal, gravelly whisper.

Spike said, "Flesh and bone. That’s all he is. Let’s see how long the mortal lasts when the real monsters come out to play."

Spike leaned forward and blew the candle out, and the crypt dropped into total darkness and a dead, heavy silence.
