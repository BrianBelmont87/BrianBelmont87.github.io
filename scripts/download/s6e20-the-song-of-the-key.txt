Buffy the Vampire Slayer. Season six, episode 20. The Song of the Key.

CHAPTER 1
The First Date

Warm low light from two floor lamps pooled across the lived-in room. Through the bay window the night was cool blue-black, and the television sat on mute, an old black-and-white sitcom flickering silently, its canned laughter replaced by the house settling and the refrigerator humming in the kitchen.

Dawn, sixteen, sat on the carpet in the center of the room, legs tucked to one side. She wore a faded gray t-shirt and blue jean shorts, still in her "at home" clothes, not yet changed for the date, and her shoulders hunched inward like she was trying to disappear into the floor. She kept her eyes down, nervously picking at a loose thread on her shorts.

Willow sat on the left side of the couch, leaning forward with her elbows on her knees, her hair loose and a little messy from a long day.

Tara sat beside her, upright but relaxed, hands folded neatly in her lap, her face open and completely present.

Buffy stood near the kitchen doorway like she might bolt, arms crossed tight, her worn brown leather jacket still on, her weight shifting from one foot to the other.

The room was quiet except for the soft rustle of fabric when Buffy moved and the faint click of the TV’s mute light. Dawn’s fingers worked the thread on her shorts in small, repetitive motions, Willow leaned in a fraction more, and Tara’s eyes stayed on Buffy, patient and ready.

When Buffy spoke, her voice came low, tight, and fast, the sarcasm not quite covering the raw, protective fear underneath, like she was trying to outrun her own worry.

Buffy said, "I’m not saying I’m going to follow them. I’m just saying I should probably… check in. Once. Maybe twice. Just to be sure."

She uncrossed her arms and re-crossed them the opposite way. Her eyes flicked to the front door, already calculating the distance to the drive-in, the running time of the movie, every possible thing that could go wrong in the dark.

Willow said, light and warm, deliberately cutting the tension, "Buffy, it’s a date. At a drive-in. Not a frat party in the Hellmouth. Nothing is going to happen. Josh seems sweet. He asked her in person and everything. That’s practically vintage."

Tara said in a soft, measured voice, "He does seem like a nice kid. Quiet. And it is her first real date. She’s been talking about it for days. Her whole face changes when she says his name."

Buffy exhaled hard through her nose, half laugh, half scoff, and started a short, restless pace in front of the archway, the leather jacket creaking softly with each step.

Her voice came sharper now, the sarcasm turning brittle and tired, real history and fear bleeding through underneath.

Buffy said, "Yeah, well, excited is how it starts. Then suddenly there’s a demon or a curse or the cute boy’s ex turns out to be made of bugs. Or he’s a robot. Or he’s dead. Dawn is sixteen, Willow. Sixteen. She should be worrying about homework and whether her hair looks stupid, not whether the guy taking her to the movies is going to sprout fangs the second the lights go down. This town doesn’t do normal. Not for us."

Dawn said quietly, almost mumbling into her lap, "You have Lucian."

The words hit the room like a dropped plate. Buffy stopped pacing mid-step, and her head turned slowly. Her eyes narrowed a fraction, the shift from worry to wounded visible in the sudden stillness of her body.

Buffy said, flat and dangerously quiet, "What?"

Dawn finally lifted her head, her cheeks flushed, eyes bright with a messy mix of embarrassment, anger, and the desperate need to be seen as more than "the kid." Her lower lip trembled once, then firmed.

Dawn said, her voice rising and cracking with emotion, the words tumbling out faster, "You have Lucian. You’re not… alone anymore. So maybe stop acting like the world is going to eat me the second I leave the house. I just want one normal night. One. Is that so much to ask?"

Buffy’s arms dropped to her sides. She took one step closer, nostrils flaring with the inhale, and the big-sister volume she rarely used anymore came out hard.

Buffy said, fierce and protective, pain and love tangled so tight it came out like armor, "That’s not what this is about."

Dawn stood now, tears threatening but her voice steady with defiance.

Dawn said at full volume, "Isn’t it? You got your person. So now I’m not allowed to have anything normal? I’m not you, Buffy. I don’t have to save the world. I just want to go to a stupid movie with a boy who likes me."

Willow sat up straighter, hands coming up in a peacemaking motion.

Willow said, still trying for light but strained, "Okay, hey, let’s not do this right now. Dawn, honey, maybe we can all take a breath—"

Tara said, calm but firmer now, "Josh is a teenager, Buffy. He isn’t a monster. And Buffy… we can be smart about this. We set a curfew. We know where they’re going. We can even ‘accidentally’ show up for milkshakes later if it feels off. But she deserves the chance to feel like a regular girl for a few hours. That light in her when she talks about him… it doesn’t come easy these days. We can protect her and still let her have the nice time. Both things can be true."

Buffy’s face hardened, and something raw and old flashed in her eyes.

Buffy said, her voice suddenly rising to a raw, cracking shout, "Same difference!"

The room froze. Willow’s mouth opened, then closed, and Tara’s brow creased with quiet concern.

Willow said softly, worried, almost in a whisper, "Buffy… Josh isn’t Angel..."

Buffy was breathing hard now, hands curling into loose fists.

Dawn was still standing in the center of the carpet. A dark wet stain had spread across the crotch of her blue jean shorts, heavy, fresh blood soaking through the denim. A small but unmistakable amount had dripped onto the beige carpet between her feet.

Dawn’s entire body went rigid, and color drained from her face in a visible wave, leaving her pale under the warm lamp light. Her eyes went wide with mortification and shock, and she stared down at herself, her lower lip quivering. She curled inward, as if trying to fold her body away from the evidence. A single tear welled at the corner of one eye but clung, refusing to fall.

She looked straight up, a silent horrified plea, and screamed, a raw piercing scream of pure mortification and terror that echoed sharply through the small room.

CHAPTER 2
First Blood — The Aftermath

The scream still echoed for half a second before it cut off.

Tara was already moving, two quick, graceful steps across the carpet, and she placed a steady gentle hand on Dawn’s shoulder, cream blouse catching a faint smear of blood on one sleeve as she shielded Dawn from view. Her other hand hovered near Dawn’s back. Tara’s lips pressed into a soft protective line; her eyes stayed warm and steady, no judgment.

Tara said, soft and kind, "Come on, sweetie. Let’s get you upstairs, honey."

Dawn was frozen, eyes glassy, but she gave the smallest nod. Tara kept her body between Dawn and the others as they moved toward the stairs, Dawn walking with an awkward, stiff-legged gait, and they disappeared up the steps. A moment later the bathroom door closed, and the lock clicked.

The living room fell into ringing quiet.

Buffy let out a long, controlled breath through her nose, disappeared into the kitchen, and returned with carpet cleaner and a ratty old rag. She dropped hard to her knees and sprayed the dark stain in short, aggressive bursts, then scrubbed with both hands, the muscles in her forearms working, small grunts of effort escaping. Blood smeared across the rag, her fingers, and the knee of her jeans. Her leather jacket stayed on, sleeves shoved up to her elbows, and a faint vein stood out at her temple. Each scrub was short and furious, as if she could erase the moment itself.

Willow hadn’t moved from the couch, watching with a small, lopsided half-smile, half amused and half deeply sympathetic.

Willow said softly, almost to herself, with a little wry warmth, "Well… that escalated."

Buffy didn’t look up from the carpet, and she kept scrubbing at the stain in short, hard strokes.

Buffy said, quippy on the surface but rough and tired underneath, "Dawn’s a woman now. Just like us. Welcome to the club, kid. Population: too many ruined pairs of shorts and zero warning labels."

There was a knock at the front door, three polite, even taps.

Buffy paused mid-scrub, rag still in her bloody hand. She looked at the door, eyes narrowing, pushed herself up, wiped her palms on the ruined rag, and crossed to the door to open it.

Lucian stood on the porch under the yellow porch light, jacket open, his silver cross earring catching the light, his jet-black hair loose, and a small paper bag in his hand. His bright aqua-blue eyes dropped to the bloody rag in Buffy’s hand, then to the smears on her fingers and the knee of her jeans. His eyebrows lifted slightly, concern mixed with dry, ready humor, and the corner of his mouth twitched once, then stilled.

Lucian said, his voice low and calm, "…Should I be worried, or is this one of those ‘you should see the other guy’ situations?"

Buffy stared at him for a second, then let out a heavy sigh and leaned against the doorframe.

Buffy said, sarcastic and bone-tired but fond now, "Yeah. The carpet really had it coming."

Before Lucian could answer, another figure stepped up behind him on the porch.

Josh stood there looking freshly showered and nervous. He was seventeen, his hair neatly combed, and he held a small bouquet of slightly wilted gas-station flowers. He had that awkward, sweet, trying-too-hard energy of a teenage boy on his first real date.

Josh said, polite and a little formal, trying to sound mature, "Hello, Mrs. Summers."

Buffy’s eyes flicked to him, and she rolled them hard at the "Mrs. Summers" old-lady energy. Without breaking eye contact she reached to her left, just inside the doorframe, and picked up a thick steel bar the door crew had left leaning there.

She looked straight at Josh, her voice calm. Too calm.

Buffy said, "Have a nice date with my little sister."

Then, holding the bar in both hands at chest height, she slowly began to bend it.

The sound was loud in the quiet night, a deep, grinding creak of metal twisting under impossible pressure. The bar slowly deformed into a rough pretzel shape with visible strain lines and sharp kinks. The muscles in her forearms and biceps corded beneath the pushed-up jacket sleeves, yet her face showed no effort at all. Her breathing stayed even, a tiny bead of sweat forming at her temple without falling, and her eyes never left Josh’s face. The final twist made a sharp metallic crack as it locked into its new useless shape.

She dropped the twisted bar on the porch right at Josh’s feet. It landed with a heavy clang.

Buffy turned without another word and headed upstairs, leaving the door open.

Josh stared down at the pretzel-shaped steel bar like it might bite him. His face had gone pale, his eyes wide enough that the whites showed all around the irises, and his Adam’s apple bobbed once as he swallowed. Pure fear crossed his face, chased immediately by something else: awe, adrenaline, a strange spark of attraction.

Once Buffy was out of earshot, Josh said quietly, stunned, "…That was hot."

Lucian slowly turned his head to look at Josh, one eyebrow raised, his expression somewhere between impressed and amused.

Lucian said, his voice dry and low, "You have no idea."

Willow called from the couch, warm and casual, trying to rescue the moment.

Willow said, "Josh, honey, come on in. We don’t bite. Usually."

Lucian stepped aside to let Josh enter and glanced once more at the twisted steel on the porch, then back at Buffy’s retreating form on the stairs. A small private smile touched the corner of his mouth, equal parts pride and that’s-my-girl.

CHAPTER 3
The Staircase Moment

Later, the warm lamp light was lower and softer, creating gentle pools of amber across the room and leaving the corners in deeper shadow. A single floor lamp behind the couch threw the slow, swinging shadow of the antique clock’s pendulum across the wall. The television was off, and the only movement in the light was the slow turning of a book page.

Lucian sat in the armchair facing the stairs, leaning forward with his elbows on his knees, hands loosely clasped, his jacket draped over the chair back. His left thumb rubbed the knuckle of his right in a tiny self-soothing motion.

Josh sat on the couch across from him, uncomfortable, fingers tapping his thighs, his right knee bouncing once before he forced it still.

Willow was curled in the far corner of the couch with a hardcover book open in her lap, one leg tucked under her. She turned pages slowly, her eyes moving without absorbing much.

The room was almost completely silent except for the steady tick and tock of the antique clock. Every so often came the crisp sound of Willow turning a page, the faint rustle of paper followed by the brush of her fingers smoothing it down.

Lucian and Josh sat facing each other in total silence, neither one sure what to say. It was awkward rather than hostile: just two guys who didn’t know each other yet, waiting for the same staircase. Josh shifted on the cushion and tugged at the cuff of his shirt, and the clock’s shadow went on swinging slowly across the wall.

After several long seconds, footsteps sounded on the stairs.

Dawn came down first in a princess-style dress of shot silk, iridescent pink shot with cyan, the colors shifting with every step. It had an open collar with delicate fringe and a layered organza skirt that moved like petals, and she wore white knee-high boots with twenty-eyelet laces. Her hair fell in permed waves across her shoulders, and she wore soft pink eyeshadow, soft pink lipstick and a touch of blush. She looked excited and self-conscious at once, her eyes flicking to Josh, then away; she caught her lower lip between her teeth for a second before releasing it.

Buffy followed a step behind in a short white skirt and a matching white top, her leather jacket on over it; Slayers ran hot, and the cold barely bothered her. A white purse with a golden heart clasp hung from a thin white leather strap at her shoulder. For once her makeup was done properly: smooth foundation, a light blush, ash-blue eyeshadow, striking eyeliner and dark red lipstick. Slim gold drop earrings, each with a small pearl, swung as she came down in her white pumps. Her gaze swept the room, and the tension in her shoulders eased the instant she saw Lucian.

As the sisters reached the bottom of the stairs, Lucian and Josh both stood at exactly the same time.

Lucian swallowed hard, his eyes widening as they settled on Buffy. His shoulders straightened, and he gave a small genuine smile and a soft exhale.

Josh looked dazed, mouth slightly open, staring at Dawn like he’d never seen her before, his eyes huge and a faint flush on his cheeks.

Both of them let out a low appreciative whistle at the same time, Josh at Dawn and Lucian at Buffy, completely in sync without planning it.

Buffy let out a small genuine laugh, light and warm, some of the earlier tension finally easing, and turned to Dawn with a softer expression.

Buffy said, lighter than before but still with that protective undercurrent, "Me and Dawn talked about it. I’m going to be all the way on the other side of the drive-in. Not near you. Not watching you. But I am going to be there… because my senses are giving me the wiggins right now."

Josh slicked his hair back, trying to play it cool.

Josh said, his voice cracking a little at the end, "Yeah… okay. Sure."

Buffy turned her head and looked directly at him, calm but with a clear warning, one eyebrow lifting a fraction.

Buffy said evenly, with a slight edge of dry amusement, "Careful. It’s not ‘Mrs. Summers.’ It’s just Buffy."

Josh’s hand froze mid-slick, and he nodded quickly, eyes wide, the flush deepening.

Lucian watched with a small, private smile, then stepped forward and offered his hand to Buffy, palm up, old-fashioned and steady. Buffy took it without hesitation, and they linked arms naturally.

Lucian said, his voice low and warm, with gentle teasing underneath, "You look beautiful. Both of you."

The group moved toward the front door, Buffy and Lucian leading arm in arm and Dawn following, still glowing from the whistles with a tiny, delighted smile, while Josh trailed slightly behind her, dazed but smiling, stealing glances at the dress as the fabric shifted color.

At the door Buffy glanced back once.

Willow stayed on the couch with her book, giving them a small wave and soft smile. Tara appeared at the top of the stairs, leaning on the railing with a gentle approving expression. Neither moved to join.

Buffy said, "We’ll be back later. Don’t wait up."

The front door stuck in its crooked frame. Buffy gave it a practiced shove, and it opened, letting the night air rush in. Buffy and Lucian went out arm in arm, Dawn and Josh following, and the door closed behind them.

Willow and Tara remained in the quiet house, and Willow turned another page.

CHAPTER 4
Drive-In — The Illusion Begins

At the drive-in, the projection booth was old, cramped, and dusty, with a single bare bulb hanging from the ceiling, and the air smelled like old celluloid, machine oil, and decades of cigarette smoke.

An older man stood at a cluttered workbench, in his late sixties, balding with a thin fringe of gray hair and hunched from years at projectors. He pulled an old, slightly scuffed film reel from a dusty cardboard box on a high shelf, squinted at the faded label through his glasses, then chuckled quietly to himself.

The Projectionist said, half to himself, with a little smile, "This is going to be one hell of a show…"

He slid the reel onto the projector spindle, threaded the film through the gate, and flipped the switch. The old machine whirred to life, and as soon as the projector lamp ignited and light hit the film, something shifted.

The Projectionist’s body stiffened, and his eyes flashed with a brief, faint golden glow. In a quick, almost gentle dissolve, his worn work clothes transformed into weathered but noble knight armor: chainmail under a surcoat, a white kite shield with a red dragon slung on his back, a sword at his hip. Soft golden light shifted fabric and metal without pain, as if the film itself were casting him in the role the movie needed.

His posture straightened and the hunch disappeared, and for a split second his face looked younger, more heroic, before settling into calm focus. He didn’t look confused; he looked like he’d always belonged in the armor. He turned and walked out of the booth toward the stairs that led down to the lot. The projector light flickered behind him.

Down in the lot, the drive-in was mostly full, with cars parked in rows facing the giant screen, some with speakers hooked on their windows and others tuned in on their radios. The night had been clear when they arrived, and the big screen glowed with crisp picture quality for an old-school drive-in.

Josh had pulled the Integra in on the middle left of the lot, about eight rows back from the screen, and Lucian had rolled the Firebird all the way across to the far right corner at the very back, so far off, past so many rows of cars and speaker posts and the murmur of a hundred car radios, that from the Integra the black Trans Am was nothing more than one more dark shape lost among the others.

Josh’s 2000 Acura Integra Type R sat in its row, its Championship White paint catching the screen light, a clean Autobot sticker centered on the hood. Inside, it was clean but lived-in.

Josh had his right arm draped comfortably around Dawn’s shoulders. She leaned into him, smiling softly as the movie started. The film opened on a distant stone castle at dusk. Inside the great hall, armored lords sat around a heavy wooden table with a weary king.

On screen, the King pounded his fist on the table.

The King said in a deep voice, "We need more men!"

One of the lords leaned forward.

The Lord said, "The princess will bring the sun when she sings. She will drive the undead back into the dark. We only have to hold until she arrives."

Dawn watched, quietly enchanted. Josh glanced at her, clearly more interested in her reaction than the movie, his thumb stroking small, slow circles on her shoulder.

They started kissing, slow and shy, just lips brushing, soft and careful. Josh’s hand slid from her shoulder to rest on her knee, then began to move up toward the hem of her skirt.

Dawn tensed and caught his wrist, gently but firmly, stopping him. She looked at him, a little embarrassed but direct, with a faint apologetic tilt of her head.

Dawn said, awkward but honest, with a small, self-deprecating smile, "No, sorry… Aunt Flo."

Josh stared at her for a second, processing, until it clicked; his face went red, and he pulled his hand back at once.

Josh said, flustered, "Oh. Sorry, Dawn. I didn’t know."

He didn’t push or get weird. He simply put his arm around her shoulders again and drew her close, his hand settling on her upper arm.

Josh said quietly, a little awkward but genuinely trying to be cool about it, "Hey… it’s all good. We can just watch the movie. I’m good right here."

Josh looked at Dawn’s face, and she returned his look, and he took her hand and kissed the top of it.

Josh said, "Dawn, despite all the peer pressure, I’m in no hurry, you know? I’m just glad to be here with you, just like this."

Dawn turned her head and kissed him slowly on the mouth, then pulled away and snuggled back into him.

She relaxed, visibly relieved, with a small, grateful smile, and they settled back into watching the screen.

Lucian’s car sat far from everyone else, almost at the very rear of the lot, its windows already starting to fog up.

Inside, things had escalated quickly. Buffy was on her back across the back seat, white pumps braced against the roof of the car for leverage, with Lucian on top of her, still mostly clothed, and the car rocked forward and back in a steady, rhythmic motion behind the heavily steamed windows.

Buffy’s hands gripped the back of Lucian’s shirt tightly, knuckles white as she anchored herself. Her head turned slightly to the side, eyes closed and mouth parted, her breathing heavy and getting faster, and a focused, intense look of pleasure crossed her face, brows drawn together in a small crease, eyelids fluttering, as a flush spread across her cheekbones.

Buffy said, breathy and low, the words coming out between heavier breaths, "Yes… right there… just like that…"

She let out a soft involuntary moan as her back arched slightly against the seat. Her fingers tightened on his shirt, nails digging small crescents into the fabric. Lucian’s eyes were half-lidded, locked on her face with reverent focus; a small muscle jumped in his jaw with each roll of her hips. His hands gripped her waist, fingers pressing with gentle possessive pressure.

Outside the steamed windows, the world was beginning to change.

A thick, unnatural fog started rolling across the lot as if a smoke machine had been turned on full blast, moving low and fast and curling between the cars. The clear night sky was gone, replaced by a heavy, oppressive darkness, and the fog had a subtle shimmer at its edges, almost oily in its movement, nothing like natural weather.

Twisted gnarled dead trees began appearing between the rows of cars, their branches looking like broken bones. The ground around the vehicles shifted, the pavement becoming dark, muddy earth, and the entire landscape turned barren, cold, and wrong.

In the far distance, on top of a jagged mountain of black rock, a dark castle appeared, lights flickering in its high windows.

The movie on the big screen continued playing… but now the fantasy world on screen and the world outside the cars were starting to bleed into each other. For a moment, the on-screen castle and the distant one aligned in perspective, as if the screen were a window rather than a projection.

Faint translucent overlays of armored figures briefly flickered between cars, gone when looked at directly. The illusion had begun.

CHAPTER 5
The Tingle Arrives

In Lucian’s car, the rocking had slowed to a gentle sway, the windows heavily fogged.

Buffy was now on top, straddling Lucian, her white skirt bunched up just enough to hide where they were joined. She moved over him slowly but with deliberate, insistent rolls of her hips, her body finding a steady rhythm, one hand planted on his chest for leverage and the other gripping the back of the seat. Her breathing was heavy, and Lucian’s hands were on her waist, guiding her through each roll.

She suddenly stopped moving.

Her head turned toward the driver’s side window. She stared at the fogged glass for half a second, then lifted her right hand and wiped the condensation away in one fast, hard motion. The glass cleared just enough for her to see outside. Her eyes narrowed sharply, the pleasure-flush draining from her face in an instant, replaced by focused threat assessment. Her brow furrowed, lips pressing into a thin line.

Lucian froze beneath her. His hands stayed on her waist but stopped guiding; he followed her gaze through the cleared glass, breath catching.

Lucian said, breathless and slightly concerned, "What’s wrong?"

Buffy didn’t answer right away. Her body stayed tense on top of him as she stared out the window, every muscle in her shoulders locked.

Buffy said, low and serious, the pleasure gone from her voice, "You know that tingle I said I had earlier…? It’s arrived."

Outside, the fog had thickened dramatically, and a cold rain had started to fall. Twisted dead trees now stood between many of the cars, and the ground had turned dark and muddy. In the distance, the black castle on jagged rocks loomed with lights flickering in its windows.

Then something moved in the fog.

A column of zombies in rusted metal armor began marching between the rows of cars, their heavy steps clanking, their swords and axes dragging. At first they seemed to ignore the vehicles entirely, like they were part of some larger formation. Their yellow eyes glowed faintly, heads turning in unison on some unseen command.

A woman in one of the cars suddenly screamed, high, sharp and terrified.

The marching column reacted to the sound at once.

A small group of skeleton knights broke off from the main column and charged the car the scream had come from and started hacking at the doors and windows with rusted blades. The people inside panicked, trying to crawl into the back seat as metal rang and glass cracked.

From the other side of the lot, knights on horseback came charging in, their armor dented but still shining in places. The lead knight carried a white kite shield with a red dragon painted on it, and he raised his sword.

The Lead Knight said, his voice a bellow over the din, "To arms! Save the peasants! Drive these abominations back!"

The knights charged into the skeleton soldiers. What followed was fast and brutal.

A knight on horseback swung low and took the head off one skeleton, the blade singing through the air and connecting with a wet crack of separating vertebrae. The skull tumbled end over end, jaw still working for a half-second before the glow in its eye sockets went out like a snuffed candle. But two more skeletons latched onto the knight’s stirrup and leg, and one yanked hard. The horse screamed and reared. The knight’s face twisted from triumph to shock as he toppled backward into the mud, and a rusted blade punched through the gap under his pauldron with a grinding scrape of metal on metal. His eyes bulged; a choked, gurgling gasp escaped before his body went limp.

Another knight was pulled down from his mount by three skeletons and landed on his back with a heavy splash, and they stabbed repeatedly through the gaps in his armor, blades rising and falling in a piston rhythm, each impact a dull, wet thud. His arms flailed once, then stilled, and muddy water bubbled from the wounds.

A heavy mace shattered a skeleton soldier’s spine with a sickening crunch of bone and rusted plate folding. It collapsed but kept crawling forward on its hands, yellow eyes still burning, fingers clawing at the mud, until a second knight finished it, driving his sword down through the eye socket with a final wet squelch, and the glow died.

Horses reared and screamed as undead hands grabbed at their legs. One horse went down in a tangle of limbs and panicked whinnying.

The fight was loud, chaotic, and very real-looking. Metal clashed against metal in a constant ringing, scraping din. People in nearby cars were screaming and trying to drive away, but the mud and the sudden trees made it difficult. Tires spun, flinging mud, engines revving uselessly.

In the Integra, Josh and Dawn were crammed down in the back seat, holding each other tightly, Dawn’s face pressed against Josh’s chest and his arms wrapped around her, one hand protectively on the back of her head. Both of them were dead silent, eyes wide with terror as they listened to the fighting and screaming outside.

The car rocked slightly as something heavy slammed into another vehicle nearby.

Dawn was shaking, small, uncontrollable tremors running through her body. Josh was trying to stay still, but his breathing was fast and shallow against her hair. His eyes darted, trying to see through the glass without lifting his head.

Neither of them made a sound. They just held each other in the cramped back seat and waited, listening to the steel and the screaming outside.

CHAPTER 6
Clocking In

Buffy climbed off Lucian quickly and opened the car door, and cold night air and rain hit her. She was still breathing hard, her hair messy and her lips still parted, the flush of pleasure fading fast under the sudden threat.

Buffy said firmly, already shifting into Slayer mode, "Stay in the car. It’s not fish men this time."

She took one deep breath, reached down, and pulled off her white pumps one at a time, tossing them into the car without looking, then stepped out barefoot into the mud. Her feet sank slightly as she adjusted her stance, toes spreading for purchase, weight settling low and balanced, and muscle memory took over.

Buffy said under her breath, "Time to clock in."

She spotted an undead soldier nearby carrying a large rusted sword, yellow eyes glowing faintly inside its skull. It hadn’t noticed her yet; its head was turned toward the main battle.

Buffy launched herself forward across the mud at a dead run.

Mud splattered up her legs and across the front of her white skirt as she moved, but she didn’t hesitate. The undead turned at the last second, eyes flaring brighter as it raised its sword for a heavy downward swing, its jaw opening in a silent hiss.

Buffy ducked under it cleanly, a sharp drop at the knees, torso folding forward, eyes never leaving the blade’s arc. She grabbed both of its wrists, using its own momentum to judo-throw it over her shoulder. As it hit the ground she ripped the sword from its grip and brought it down hard, driving the blade straight through the top of its skull. The body went limp instantly, the yellow light in its eyes snuffing out.

Buffy pulled the sword free, breathing steadily now. She looked at the blade for a second, one eyebrow lifting slightly, and the corner of her mouth curled.

Buffy said dryly, almost conversationally, "It’ll do."

In the car, Lucian was quickly zipping his pants back up and scrambling forward into the driver’s seat, staying low. He watched Buffy through the windshield as she fought, his eyes wide, tracking every move.

A massive troll-like creature suddenly lumbered past from the left, moving across the front of the car, huge and gray-skinned, with thick arms and a heavy club. It stopped, its nose twitching hard as it sniffed the air over and over in long wet inhales.

The Troll said in a low, almost curious rumble, "…Wo-man…"

The troll placed one huge hand on the hood of Lucian’s car. The metal groaned and dented slightly under the weight, and the whole car shifted on its suspension.

Before the troll could do anything else, Buffy appeared behind it.

Buffy said, sharp and mocking, "Hey, big guy. My boyfriend’s car. Get your hand off it."

The troll roared and swung its club in a wide heavy arc. Buffy ducked under it easily, a fluid side-step and crouch, the club whooshing overhead, and slashed the stolen sword across the back of its left knee. The blade bit deep, dark blood welling, and the troll roared in pain and dropped to one knee with a ground-shaking thud.

It tried to grab her with its massive hand, but Buffy stepped back fast, and the troll missed, lost its balance, and face-planted into the mud with a heavy splat that sent mud exploding outward in a wave.

Buffy didn’t waste the opening. She jumped onto its back, raised the sword with both hands, and drove it down hard into the base of the troll’s neck. The creature let out one last wet groan and went still. Buffy gave the hilt a quick professional twist before pulling free.

Lucian watched through the fogged, rain-streaked windshield.

Buffy stood up slowly on top of the dead troll, caked in mud, her white skirt soaked and streaked with dirt, her bare feet and legs covered, her hair a mess. She was breathing hard, the sword still in her hand. A flash of lightning cracked across the sky behind her, briefly illuminating her silhouette, her mud-streaked face like war paint, eyes reflecting the flash for a split second, chest rising and falling. She looked like something ancient and terrifying and beautiful.

Lucian stared, completely still for a moment, his breath fogging a small patch on the windshield.

Lucian said quietly, almost reverently, with real wonder underneath, "…Goddamn."

He watched her like he was seeing something sacred and terrifying at the same time. His hand gripped the steering wheel, white-knuckled. The raven ink at his open collar sat dark against the faint glow from the distant castle lights.

CHAPTER 7
Through the Mud

Across the lot, the battlefield was pure chaos, knights and undead soldiers clashing between the cars. The fog was thick, the ground dark mud, and lightning flashed overhead now and then.

Buffy moved low and fast between the vehicles, barefoot, gripping the stolen sword. She wasn’t running wildly; she was moving with purpose, using the cars and the ongoing fights as cover, breathing shallow through her mouth to stay quiet. Her eyes kept moving, head on a swivel, and mud splashed her calves with each careful step.

She spotted two skeleton soldiers standing near the back of a station wagon, backs turned as they watched the main battle. Buffy moved up behind them silently, weight on the balls of her feet, sword held low and ready. She drove her sword up through the spine of the first one, the blade sliding between vertebrae with a soft wet crunch. The skeleton stiffened, and she yanked the blade free and swung it in a tight arc, taking the head off the second before it could turn around. Both bodies collapsed quietly into the mud.

She didn’t wait to watch them fall; she kept moving, low and quick, toward the next row of cars.

She reached a car with a surfboard strapped to the roof. Just as she was about to move past it, she heard the heavy sound of hooves and quickly dropped and slid underneath the car, flat in the mud, her cheek against the cold, wet earth, one eye closed against a speck of mud and the other open and tracking.

A massive black horse walked slowly past the vehicle. On its back sat the Enemy Commander — black armor with sharp aggressive angles and blades jutting from the pauldrons and gauntlets. Between the plates of his armor, a dim orange glow pulsed like living energy. His faceplate was fierce and angular. He carried a long black lance and surveyed the battlefield like a general. The horse’s hooves were caked in mud, each step a heavy sucking sound.

Buffy stayed perfectly still under the car, face inches from the mud, watching his horse’s hooves pass by. The commander’s head turned slightly in her direction for a moment, the orange eyes behind the slit flaring brighter, scanning, then turned forward again. He didn’t see her.

Once he was past, Buffy slowly slid out from under the car and kept moving, staying low. A single drop of sweat rolled from her temple into the mud. She took out one more skeleton soldier quietly, this time by yanking it down behind a car and driving her sword through its chest before it could make noise. The blade punched through rusted plate and bone with a muffled crunch; she lowered the body silently.

Finally she reached Josh’s white Integra and, staying low, moved to the passenger-side window, peering inside.

Inside, Josh and Dawn were still huddled in the back seat, holding each other. Josh’s head turned toward the window.

When he saw Buffy’s face — completely caked in mud, hair messy, eyes intense — he squealed in pure terror, thinking she was one of the undead, and yanked Dawn closer protectively, his face a mask of panic, eyes huge.

Dawn’s eyes went wide, and she slapped her hand over Josh’s mouth to shut him up, her own eyes flicking to the window. When she saw it was Buffy, her body sagged with relief, but she kept her hand over Josh’s mouth for another second to make sure he stayed quiet. Her eyes filled with a quick, desperate hope.

Buffy gave them a small nod through the glass, then signaled for them to stay down, a flat palm pressing downward twice. Her expression was calm but urgent.

CHAPTER 8
The Car

The battle raged around the cars as fog drifted between the vehicles and the sound of clashing metal and screams filled the air.

Buffy stayed low beside Josh’s car, glanced around quickly, then tapped on the passenger window, two quick, soft taps.

Inside, Dawn carefully lifted her head and looked out the window, saw Buffy, and started to roll the window down slightly so they could talk, a small, hopeful smile beginning to form on her lips.

Dawn said, scared but relieved, "Buffy—"

Before she could finish, movement caught Buffy’s eye.

A small group of six skeleton knights in rusted armor suddenly stopped fighting. One of them pointed directly at Dawn’s face in the car window, a bony finger extended, head tilted as if cross-referencing a locket portrait. They turned as one and started moving toward the Acura with purpose, in sync, with no wasted motion, like a single organism.

Buffy said urgently to Dawn, "Roll the window up! Now!"

Dawn quickly rolled it back up, eyes wide with fear, the smile dying instantly.

The six skeleton knights broke into a run, two of them carrying a heavy wooden battering ram between them, and charged straight at the side of the car.

The battering ram slammed into the driver’s side door with a heavy, splintering crunch of wood on metal, rocking the car violently as the metal buckled inward. Josh threw himself over Dawn, shielding her with his body as another impact hit the car, his face buried against her shoulder, teeth gritted, eyes squeezed shut.

Outside, Buffy moved fast, running around the back of the car toward the skeletons as they kept hammering the driver’s side. One of them broke off and charged her with its sword raised, yellow eyes flaring.

She ducked under the swing with a clean, low pivot, grabbed the skeleton’s arm, and used its momentum to slam it into the side of another car. The impact rang, and she drove her sword up through its ribcage and yanked it free as it collapsed, the body twitching once.

Another skeleton tried to grab her from behind. She spun and slashed low, taking out its legs before finishing it with a downward strike to the skull. The second body dropped.

The battering ram hit the Integra again, harder this time. The door caved in with a shriek of tortured metal. Josh was still shielding Dawn when one of the skeleton knights reached through the broken window and drove a sword straight into his back.

The blade entered with a sickening, soft sound under the fabric, and Josh’s back arched involuntarily, his eyes flying wide and his mouth opening in a sharp gasp of pain. Blood started soaking through his shirt in a fast, dark bloom, and his hands spasmed against Dawn’s back.

He looked down at Dawn, who was staring up at him in horror. Even as he was dying, he managed a small, oddly romantic smile, lips barely curving, eyes soft and sad and full of love. A single tear tracked through the dirt on his cheek.

Josh said, weak and strangely gentle, "…Worth it… if it’s you."

His body went limp, and as the ruined door sagged open on its hinges, he slumped sideways out of the car and face-down into the mud, the sword hilt jutting from his back.

Dawn said, her voice raw and breaking, "Josh…? Josh—!"

She scrambled out after him, grabbing his shirt, trying to pull him back, and her face crumpled as the reality hit her. Tears started falling as she let out a broken, anguished cry, her shoulders shaking violently, her hands clutching at him with desperate, useless strength.

Dawn said between sobs, "No! No no no— Josh!"

A knight on horseback suddenly rode up beside the car and saw Dawn crying over Josh’s body, his eyes widening in recognition behind the slits of his helm. He quickly dismounted and approached her, his boots splashing through the mud.

The Knight said, reverent but urgent, "My lady… Princess of the Sun. We have been searching for you. Only you can bring back the light and drive the darkness from this land."

He pulled a small silver locket from around his neck and opened it. Inside was a painted portrait of Dawn, the resemblance unmistakable even in the chaos.

Dawn looked up at him through her tears, completely overwhelmed, and glanced between the knight and Buffy, the fight gone out of her for a moment.

Dawn said quietly, almost numb, "…I am the Key again?"

For a moment nobody answered her, and the knight only bowed his helmeted head.

Lucian arrived at a run, breathing hard, carrying a sword he’d picked up from the ground and a dented, beaten-up shield, and moved straight to Buffy’s side without hesitation, his eyes flicking over her quickly, checking for injury, then settling on her face.

Lucian said, steady and firm, leaving no room for argument, "I’m not staying in the car. Not while you’re out here."

Buffy gave him a quick look, part annoyed and part relieved, but didn’t argue; she nodded once, her eyes softening a little.

The remaining four skeleton knights turned toward them, weapons raised.

Buffy and Lucian moved together.

Lucian stepped forward first, raising his shield and bashing one skeleton hard in the chest with a short, explosive shove from the shoulder, the shield rim catching the creature’s sternum and staggering it. Buffy followed up at once, slashing upward and taking its head off in one clean motion, and the head spun away as the body dropped.

Another skeleton attacked from the side. Lucian blocked with his shield while Buffy ducked under his arm, fluid, hair whipping, and drove her sword up through its ribs. They moved in rhythm, Lucian creating openings with his shield and Buffy finishing the kills, with no words, just a quick shared glance of understanding.

A third skeleton tried to flank them. Lucian turned and slammed his shield into its face, knocking it off balance. The impact jarred his arm, a small wince crossing his face, and Buffy spun and severed its spine with a powerful horizontal cut. The body folded.

The last skeleton charged. Lucian blocked its overhead swing with his shield, the impact ringing loud, his knees bending with the force, then shoved it back hard, using his legs. Buffy stepped in and drove her sword straight through its skull. The blade punched out the back with a wet crunch.

One by one, all six of the skeleton knights lay broken and still in the mud around Josh’s ruined car.

Buffy and Lucian stood back-to-back for a second, breathing hard, covered in mud and dirt. They exchanged a quick look, no words needed, and Buffy’s free hand briefly touched his arm in a silent check; he gave a tight, reassuring smile.

Dawn was still on her knees beside Josh’s body, crying quietly, her hands stroking his face, tears mixing with the mud on his skin.

CHAPTER 9
The Commander

Across the lot, the remaining knights and undead were still clashing in the background, but the air felt heavier now. The fog seemed to part slightly around the approaching figure.

The Enemy Commander rode slowly out of the fog on his massive black horse, his long black lance in hand, the orange glow pulsing between his plates like living fire. The horse stopped in front of Buffy, and the Commander slowly tilted his helmet down to look at her, his eyes glowing bright orange behind the black steel, the glow pulsing once, brighter, as if recognizing her.

The Commander said in a low, deep, otherworldly voice, like many voices layered together, "You…"

Buffy stared up at him, breathing hard, covered in mud, and tilted her head slightly, sizing him up. One corner of her mouth curled in classic sarcasm, but her eyes stayed deadly serious, cataloging every weak point in the armor.

Buffy said dryly, "Wow. You know, most villains go for the whole ‘dark and mysterious’ thing, but you really leaned into the ‘I fell into a blacksmith’s forge and got stuck’ aesthetic. Bold choice."

She didn’t wait for a response; she turned her head toward Lucian without taking her eyes off the Commander.

Buffy said, serious now, "Lucian. Stand back. Stay with Dawn. No matter what happens."

Lucian hesitated for half a second, then nodded firmly.

Lucian said steadily, "I’ve got her."

He moved back toward Dawn, who was still kneeling beside Josh’s body, and placed a gentle hand on her shoulder, his shield raised protectively.

The Commander dropped his lance, and it clattered into the mud. He reached behind him and pulled a long wooden handle from his saddle, a heavy chain flail with a spiked metal head attached to it, and let the head drop; the chain uncoiled and went taut with a metallic rattle.

Then he spurred the black horse forward and began to bear down on Buffy, the flail swinging low at his side.

The Commander was fast for his size, swinging the flail in wide, brutal arcs, the spiked head whistling through the air. Buffy tried to dodge and counter, but he was stronger, and the reach of the weapon put her at a disadvantage.

She got clipped on the shoulder and sent stumbling, the impact bone-jarring, and her face flashed a quick grimace of pain, teeth bared, eyes squeezing shut, then snapping open. She recovered and slashed at his leg, but his armor took the hit with a shower of sparks, and he backhanded her with his gauntlet, sending her crashing into the side of a car. Her head snapped sideways, hair flying across her face, and she hit the door with a metallic boom, denting it. A thin line of blood appeared at her hairline. She shook her head once, hard, her eyes refocusing with pure Slayer will.

Buffy was clearly starting to lose, breathing harder and moving slower. The Commander swung down from his horse, grabbed her by the throat with one massive hand and slammed her against the door of a tall lifted pickup truck. Both her hands flew up, fingers digging into the unyielding metal of his grip, trying to pry it loose. Her face flushed dark red, veins standing out in her neck and forehead, and a strangled choking sound escaped her. She kicked at his knee; he absorbed it without shifting.

The Commander lifted her higher, and her bare feet left the mud completely, dangling and kicking weakly in the air, toes flexing uselessly as she lost all purchase. Her face darkened from red to purple, and her eyes bulged wider, then started to lose focus, pupils dilating as black spots crept into her vision. A small involuntary twitch ran through her legs. Her struggling weakened, and her fingers slipped from his gauntlet. She tried once more to drive a knee into his armor and found nothing, no leverage, no air, no opening, and a thin, reedy choking sound escaped her throat as she began to black out. For one terrifying second her body went limp, head lolling forward, and only the iron grip on her neck kept her upright. She was the Slayer, and she could not break free.

Beside Dawn, Lucian’s face changed. He looked at Dawn, then Buffy, then Dawn again. Every muscle in his body strained toward Buffy, but he had promised to protect Dawn, so he planted his feet in the mud beside her, his arm thrown out in front of her and his shield dropping an inch. The sound tore out of him before he could stop it, raw and breaking.

Lucian said, "NOOOOO!"

His voice cracked, but he didn’t move from Dawn’s side.

Lucian said, still screaming, "Buffy, fight back! Don’t give up!"

Dawn was still on her knees in the mud beside Josh, shaking, tears running down her cheeks, her thumb moving in a slow, rhythmic stroke across his mud-streaked cheek even as her own face crumpled.

Dawn’s eyes were glassy, but something shifted behind the grief, a faint, instinctive recognition. The story on the screen had always had one rule: the princess sings, the light returns, the curse breaks. She felt that rule pulling at her like a memory that wasn’t hers. Her power as the Key was resonating with the film’s own logic. If she completed the song, the film’s ending would apply to the bleed. If she stopped, the deaths would stay permanent because the movie’s mercy would never activate.

She began to sing to Josh, slow and soft at first, her voice shaky and emotional. It was haunting, ethereal and powerful, and it grew stronger as she went. The Key in her answered the film the way a tuning fork answers a note it was made for.

Dawn sang, "Where there is doubt… let me bring faith
Where there is silence… let me speak your name
Where there is darkness… let me bring your light
Even if I have to break to make it right
Where there is fear… let me stand anyway
Where there is cold… let me burn it away
Where there is loss… let me carry the weight
Even if it leaves me hollow in the end"

Her voice gained strength as she went on gently touching Josh’s face, her spine beginning to straighten, her shoulders lifting, the words gaining resonance, almost vibrating in the air.

Dawn sang, "I don’t know if I’m enough…
But I’ll give whatever’s left of me
If it means the sun will rise again
If it means the dark won’t win
Where there is night… let me bring the dawn
Where there is pain… let me sing until it’s gone
Where there is shadow… I’ll stand in the flame
Even if it burns me, I’ll call your name"

She hit the high emotional peak right as the clouds began to glow and part. Her eyes lifted, staring upward, her face a mask of desperate hope and grief-fueled power. Tears streaked her cheeks, but her voice did not waver. Her chest heaved with the effort, her free hand clenching into a fist in the mud.

Dawn sang, "Let there be light…
Let there be light…
Let there be light again…"

Dawn’s voice rose from fragile and cracking to a powerful, resonant, emotionally raw belt that cut through the battle noise like a blade of pure sound. The high notes had an almost harmonic otherworldly quality. Around her the mud seemed to lighten by degrees; the air itself leaned toward her voice as if the movie had been waiting for this exact girl to finish the song.

The Commander had Buffy pinned against the pickup truck by her throat, her feet off the ground, and he raised his other hand, ready to deliver a final blow with his spiked gauntlet. Buffy was barely struggling now, her kicks weakening. She was not winning. She was not even holding.

Just as he was about to strike—

Dawn hit a high powerful note.

The clouds fully parted.

A burst of sunlight broke through and shone down over the lot, warm, golden, and impossibly bright after the oppressive dark. It hit the Commander like a physical force.

The Enemy Commander froze. His body began to dissolve into black mist, starting from the glowing orange energy in his armor and spreading outward. The orange in his eyes flickered wildly like a dying bulb, a brief moment of what might have been ancient confusion or fear crossing his posture before the helm began to flake, and he let out a distorted, echoing roar as he disintegrated into smoke that the wind carried away.

And then the entire illusion shattered around them like a pane of black glass.

The movie’s own story had completed. Because Dawn had sung the princess’s song while the bleed was active, everyone who had died inside it was restored, exactly as the film would have restored them at the end of its story.

The fog vanished as if sucked away by a giant vacuum, the twisted dead trees disappeared, and the muddy ground became normal pavement again with a strange, instantaneous ripple, while the distant black castle faded away like a bad dream.

All around the drive-in, people who had been "killed" suddenly sat up or stood, completely unharmed and confused. The undead and knights were gone as if they had never existed. A collective gasp and confused murmurs rose from the cars.

A man in a nearby convertible sat up, looked at his hands, then at the now-clear sky and the movie still playing on the screen as if nothing had happened.

The man said, confused and shaken, "What the hell…?"

A couple in another car clutched each other, the woman crying in relief but staring at the intact windshield in disbelief.

Over by the Firebird, the hood the troll’s hand had buckled was smooth and whole again, the black and gold paint without a scratch. Up in the booth, the projectionist stood blinking in his worn work clothes, the armor gone as if it had never been there.

Buffy dropped hard, face-first onto the now-normal ground, then pushed herself up slowly, spitting out a bit of grit, and looked around at the completely normal drive-in. A thin trickle of blood ran from her hairline down her temple; she ignored it.

Buffy said, exhausted and dry, with a small huff of a laugh, "…Well. At least it wasn’t at the house again."

She looked over at Dawn, who was still kneeling beside Josh — except Josh was now alive again, sitting up and looking extremely confused and shaken, the sword wound gone. He blinked rapidly, touching his own chest in disbelief.

Dawn stared at him, tears still streaking her face, but her breathing changed, a sharp, stunned inhale through parted lips. In that instant she understood: it wasn’t random power, it was the movie’s own ending. She had sung the sun back, and the story had kept its promise.

Dawn whispered, almost to herself, raw and cracked with exhaustion, "...It worked because of the movie..."

CHAPTER 10
Early Morning — Summers House

Back at the Summers house, the sun was just barely starting to rise, and soft pale light filtered in through the dining-room windows from the yard. The house was quiet, and a few birds chirped faintly outside.

Willow, Tara, Dawn, Buffy, Lucian and Josh sat around the dining-room table with a scattering of mugs between them. The mood was tired but calm, the kind of quiet that came after surviving something intense, and the early light painted soft gold stripes across the table and faces.

Josh was in the middle of talking, his hands moving as he recounted what had happened.

Lucian listened without interrupting. Josh still looked shaken, his eyes darting around the room as if he expected skeletons to climb out of the cupboards, his hands gesturing wildly, then dropping.

Josh said nervously, still processing everything, "—And then this giant skeleton guy with a battering ram just started smashing into the side of my car. I swear I thought we were dead. I mean, the whole thing was just… insane. There were knights and zombies and this huge guy on a black horse with glowing eyes and—"

Willow listened quietly, nodding every so often with a small, sympathetic expression, her hands wrapped around her mug, thumbs stroking the ceramic in a soothing rhythm.

Tara leaned slightly toward Dawn and placed a light hand on her forearm.

Tara whispered, barely loud enough for Dawn to hear, "Remember to change the thing soon, like how I showed you, okay?"

Dawn, in the hoodie she’d pulled on when they got home, nodded without saying anything, her face still a little pale from everything that had happened. She quietly pushed her chair back and stood up, her movements slow and tired.

Dawn said softly, "Yeah… I will."

She headed upstairs without another word, shoulders slumped, the weight of the night still on her.

Buffy stayed seated with her arms crossed over her chest. She hadn’t said much since they got home; she had just watched and listened, her expression part protective, part exhausted. A faint bruise was forming at her temple where the gauntlet had caught her, the thin cut at her hairline had crusted over, and the red marks of the Commander’s fingers were still fading from either side of her throat. She hadn’t bothered to clean the dried mud from under her nails.

Lucian sat beside her, quietly sipping tea from a large bright yellow mug. He didn’t say anything either, just occasionally glanced at Buffy like he was making sure she was okay. His free hand rested on the table near hers, not quite touching.

The table fell into a short silence after Dawn left. Josh looked around at everyone, clearly still rattled but trying to play it cool.

Buffy finally uncrossed her arms and leaned back in her chair, her eyes crinkling a little at the edges.

Buffy said, tired but with a dry, warm edge of humor, "Welcome to the Summers house, Josh. Where first dates end with undead armies, your girlfriend turns out to be some kind of sun princess, and you get stabbed in the back by a skeleton."

She paused for half a second and shrugged.

Buffy said, "We’ve got great snacks though. And the cable’s decent. So… you know. Could be worse."

Josh just stared at her for a moment, not sure if he should laugh or be terrified, and eventually let out a weak, nervous chuckle, a flicker of wonder underneath the fear now.

Lucian hid a small smile behind his yellow mug as he took another sip. His eyes stayed on Buffy over the rim, softened with quiet pride.

The early morning light continued to slowly fill the room. Soft gold crept across the table, catching the rim of Lucian’s yellow mug and the dried mud still flecked along Buffy’s knuckles. Outside, a car passed on Revello Drive, ordinary and distant. Everyone sat in the quiet, the weight of the night still in the air. Dawn was upstairs changing, Josh was still shaking off a death that had been real for a few minutes, and Buffy could still taste the absence of air. But the worst of it was finally over.
