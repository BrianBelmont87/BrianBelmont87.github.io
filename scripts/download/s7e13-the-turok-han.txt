Buffy the Vampire Slayer. Season seven, episode 13. The Turok-Han.

CHAPTER 1
Bathroom

Night had settled hard over Revello Drive, and in the front entry of the Summers house Buffy's palm was still flat on the rough plywood she had shoved tight into the frame behind Angel and Faith, her forehead only just lifted from the wood. Outside, the roar came again, wet and hungry and closer than before, and it rattled the glass in the front windows, while behind her in the living room low voices overlapped, the words buried under the dread. The crystal's jagged static still lay over the house and the three houses on every side of it, so the First could not see or listen in on anything said inside them, but it did not need to listen to send what was coming down the street. Angel and Faith had made it inside before the dark finished falling, and everyone in the house had a weapon within reach.

Buffy didn't speak to the room. She turned away from the plywood, crossed the entry in three quick steps to where Faith stood just inside the living room still shaking the road out of her shoulders, and took hold of her left forearm, firm.

Buffy said, low and urgent, only for Faith, "Bathroom. Now."

Faith read the look on her face and nodded once, and Buffy let go of her arm and headed for the stairs without waiting, taking them two at a time with her right hand trailing the banister, while Faith came up one step behind her, still in her road-worn jacket, the fresh cut above her eyebrow shiny at the edges and a streak of dried blood down her collar.

Below them the house never stopped moving. Riley racked his rifle at the front window while Sam thumbed a fresh magazine home beside him, Tara lifted her white-oak staff from where it leaned against the arm of the couch, and Lucian watched the empty stairs with his jaw tight and his double-bladed axe resting against his leg. Spike lit a cigarette by the wall and let the smoke out slowly through his nose, Dawn sat hugging her knees on the bottom step, and across the room Angel and Lucian traded one tense nod over all of it, while the fridge hummed in the kitchen and under everything the roar came again.

On the landing Faith slipped past Buffy with a Slayer's quickness and reached the bathroom door first, pushed it open with the flat of her hand and slapped the light switch, and the overhead fluorescent snapped on with a cool buzz, and she flicked on the fan beside it so that it started to whir and fill the little room with its noise.

Buffy came in behind her and pulled the door shut with a soft click. She was still in the war clothes she had pulled on that morning after the face in the sky, the black cargo pants, the long-sleeved black-and-white v-neck and the combat boots, and she snatched a washcloth off the rail with her right hand, cranked the hot tap on full and held the cloth under it until it steamed. Then she stood at the sink and wiped her face slowly in the mirror, her eyes tired, bracing herself, while the steam from the running tap began to feather the edges of the glass.

Behind her, Faith put the toilet seat down, unbuckled her belt with a metallic clink, and worked her jeans down to her ankles, and she sat without ceremony, her eyes on Buffy's reflection the whole time with no shame and no performance in it, and peed quietly while she listened.

Buffy watched her own reflection and not Faith's, her jaw set and the warm washcloth still pressed against her cheek, and she knew exactly what she was about to say, and her breath stayed steady while she found the words.

Buffy said quietly, her eyes on the mirror, "I'm pregnant. Two weeks. I'm keeping her, Faith."

In the reflection over Buffy's shoulder, surprise cracked straight through Faith's tough edge, and her eyebrows shot up toward the cut above them.

Faith said, "You serious?"

Buffy nodded once in the mirror, dead serious, and met Faith's reflected eyes.

Buffy said, "Yeah."

Faith went still on the seat, her mouth opening a fraction and her breath catching, and then she let it out slowly through her nose while it landed, and her eyes narrowed with something deeper than shock, respect and fear and a fierce protectiveness all arriving at once.

The mirror was steaming over now. Faith stood, pulled her jeans back up, zipped them and buckled her belt with a clink, and flushed, and then she came around to Buffy's left side at the sink, tugging her jacket straight, and looked at her with raised, worried brows.

Faith hooked a thumb in her waistband and pulled it down just far enough to show the old scar low across her stomach. She did it on purpose, and she wasn't shy about it.

Faith said, "Where you stabbed me. Graduation roof. They had to take it all out."

Buffy's face crumpled. She covered her mouth and stumbled half a step back until the bathroom door stopped her. Faith grabbed her shoulder to steady her and looked straight into her eyes, unflinching.

Faith breathed in and let it out, almost a sigh.

Faith said softly, with no pity, just the truth, "B… don't cry. Don't you ever cry for me, I was never gonna be a mother anyway. We know that."

Buffy swallowed hard, thinking of what Faith had finally told her on one of the long late-night phone calls from L.A. that week, about the warm glow that had started in her chest in Tara's arms in the backyard and never quite gone out, and she took Faith's right hand and pressed it firmly against her own lower stomach.

Buffy said, "This is the glow Tara showed you. This is what we're fighting for. Right here. Right now."

Faith closed her eyes for a moment with her palm still flat under Buffy's hand, and when she opened them again she yanked Buffy into a tight hug, their elbows knocking the towel rail in the cramped little bathroom, and held on hard anyway.

Then the lights cut out, the fan whirred down into silence, and the whole house went black around them, and there was nothing left in the dark but the hot tap still running into the sink and the sound of the two of them breathing.

CHAPTER 2
The Turok-Han

Downstairs the Summers living room had gone pitch black, and for half a second nothing in it moved.

Then Riley flicked open a Zippo, and a small orange flame bloomed low at his hip, and his eyes searched the darkness toward the bay window, where the faint glow of the street had gone out with everything else.

Riley said, "Agent 3 and 4, report."

No answer came back. Then a soldier's body came crashing through the center of the bay window, dead weight in black tactical gear, and glass exploded across the room in a glittering spray that rained over Riley and Sam and skittered across the carpet, and the body tumbled over the sill and landed between the window and the coffee table, while out on the dark lawn, where his partner had been standing watch, nothing answered at all.

Dawn screamed and bolted through the kitchen doorway, Angel drew his sword with a rasp of steel, and Lucian gripped his double-bladed axe and swung toward the foot of the stairs just as Buffy came down out of the dark above in three long bounds and landed in the middle of the room with her weight forward and her eyes on the window, with Faith's boots pounding down the steps somewhere behind her. Riley and Sam snapped their rifles to their shoulders, Willow and Tara raised the wand and the white-oak staff, and Spike dropped low by the wall, flicked his cigarette away and started to snarl.

The lighter flame trembled in Riley's fist, dust hung in the thin grey moonlight spilling through the broken glass, and out on the lawn something else was coming.

The Turok-Han filled the shattered window frame, a tattered brown leather trench coat hanging off a body with skin as grey as a corpse's, long fingers curled at its sides and a bald, heavily ridged skull, and it never once looked back the way it had come; it looked only at them.

It came through the window low and landed in a crouch, both palms slapping the carpet, head up, teeth bared, eyes sweeping the room.

Without even turning, it kicked out backward, straight and flat, and drove its heel into Riley's stomach just below the sternum, and the air burst out of him as he folded forward and slid back. The rifle jerked off his shoulder and cracked him across the cheekbone, and he stumbled back into Sam, and the two of them staggered into the room together, rifles tangled, neither one able to fire.

The creature rose to its full height in front of the shattered window, all seven feet of it, its ridged head nearly brushing the ceiling and its shoulders blotting out what was left of the moonlight behind it.

Angel closed in from the fireplace and swung a fist at its temple, but the Turok-Han snapped its head aside, and the punch hit nothing but air. Then it backhanded him across the chest so hard it lifted him off his feet, and Angel flew six feet backward, crashed into the fireplace mantel and slumped to the hearth as his sword clattered away.

Faith came sprinting from the stairs and hit the creature from behind, locking both arms around its chest in a bear hug and pinning its long fingers against its own ribs, and she dug her chin into its shoulder blade, braced her feet wide and held on with every bit of Slayer strength she had.

Faith shouted into the creature's back, "BUFFY NOW!"

The Turok-Han strained forward, and Faith's boots slid two inches across the carpet, but she held.

Buffy dropped into a crouch beside it, grabbed a leg of the coffee table, and wrenched it free with a splintering crack. She came up in one motion with the table leg cocked like a stake and drove it straight at the creature's heart with her full body weight behind it.

And nothing happened. The tip stopped dead at its skin and the wood splintered against its hide like a broom handle driven into an oak, and the Turok-Han didn't even flinch; its mouth only curled, almost amused, while Buffy staggered back a step with the shattered table leg in her fist and her eyes flashing with disbelief for one long beat.

The creature snapped its head backward and smashed the back of its skull into Faith's face, and her nose broke with an audible crack, blood spraying in a fine mist. Her grip went slack for half a second and her knees buckled, but she didn't let go.

It didn't need her to. The Turok-Han spun around, grabbed Buffy by both shoulders with its long fingers and drove her backward into the couch, and her back slammed into the cushions in a puff of dust before she slid down the front of the couch onto the floor with her legs splayed out in front of her, the wind knocked out of her and her hair in her face, while the stake fell from her hand and clattered on the carpet.

The creature didn't pounce. It stood over her with its boots on either side of her knees and bent forward at the waist, slowly, deliberately, until its face hovered in front of hers, and it held her eyes first. Then its nostrils flared, and it sniffed once, its head tilting to the side like a predator reading a scent.

Its right hand reached toward her lower stomach, moving slowly and trembling at the wrist, the fingers curled slightly, not clawing but curious, and over three long seconds it crept down until it hovered two inches above her belly. It never touched her; only its shadow fell across her clothes. The creature's mouth pulled into a slow, wet, satisfied smile as it studied her, testing her.

Disgust hit Buffy first: her nose wrinkled hard and her lip curled, and she tried to turn her face away, pressing her cheek into the couch cushion, her whole body revolted. Then came anger. Both her hands shot up, her left hand grabbing its wrist and digging into the tendons to wrench the hand away while her right palm shoved flat against its chest, and her back arched off the floor as her body rejected it. Then came rage. A scream built in her chest, her shoulders shook and her face flushed, her eyes went wet and white-hot, and every muscle she had locked, the Slayer's fury overriding her fear.

Disgust and terror tore through the rage in Buffy's voice, and it came out raw, cracking and then hardening. "No. NO—"

Angel was back on his feet. He sprinted three steps, grabbed the collar of the creature's trench coat in both fists and ripped it off her in one motion, and Buffy collapsed against the couch, gasping. Angel twisted his hips and hurled the Turok-Han up and over his head, and it slammed into the ceiling, punching a crater in the drywall, while plaster rained down and it crashed flat onto the carpet four feet away.

Buffy rolled onto her knees. Lucian was already stepping forward with his axe held out, and she took it from his hand while the creature pushed itself up on its palms, snarling, trying to rise.

Buffy stood with the axe raised high over her right shoulder and brought it down on a diagonal with her whole body behind it, and the blade went clean through its neck. The head came away, and black dust exploded upward.

Buffy stood over the dust with her boots planted wide, chest heaving, the rage still in her jaw. Behind the couch Faith was still on her feet with blood streaming from her broken nose over her mouth and chin, Angel stood braced by the fireplace with plaster dust in his hair, and Lucian had come up at Buffy's side with his empty hands open, his axe still in hers.

More snarls came from outside, and through the broken window shadows jerked across the lawn: two more Turok-Han were coming. Willow and Tara stood waiting with the wand and the staff raised.

Buffy didn't turn around. She just snapped her head over her left shoulder and found them through the hanging dust.

Buffy shouted, a command, not a request, "Now!"

Willow and Tara stepped together until the insides of their wrists touched. They lined up the tips of the wand and the staff and began to chant in unison, their voices steady even though their hands were shaking.

Willow and Tara said together, "Magna Mater, audi preces nostras; sit haec terra sancta in nomine tuo."

They were asking the Great Mother to hear their prayers and make this ground holy in her name, and the ground listened, but not the way it would have a month ago. Bright blue light burst from their joined wrists in jagged, stuttering spurts instead of one clean flow, the crystal's static chewing at every inch of it, and Willow's teeth set hard as she forced it through anyway, with Tara's free hand clamped around her forearm to steady the stream. The translucent shield swelled outward in lurching pulses, racing along the floorboards, up the walls and across the ceiling, flickering out and catching again, until it covered the house and the three houses on every side of it and settled into a low hum that wavered every few seconds like a radio losing its station. The moment it held, Willow's knees buckled and she went down onto one of them on the carpet, a thin line of blood running from her nose to her lip and the red of her hair darkening again at the roots, and Tara went down with her and kept an arm around her while the blue light steadied on the walls.

On the front lawn, the two Turok-Han were lifted off their feet and thrown ten feet back. They hit the grass hard and clawed at the shimmering barrier, but they couldn't get through, so they turned and sprinted across the street toward the neighbor's porch, and screams broke out over there at once.

Buffy stood with her boots planted and Lucian's axe in both hands and her eyes on the window, the screams from across the street hardening her face, while around her the house came alive again, everyone moving and breathing, plaster dust drifting down out of the crater in the ceiling and the jittery blue glow of the shield wavering on the walls.

CHAPTER 3
Street Kill

Back in the Summers living room, Willow got up off her knee with Tara's hand under her elbow and lowered the wand a little, breathing hard, with sweat on her upper lip, and wiped the blood from under her nose with the back of her free hand.

Willow said, "The shield will hold until morning. But not past that."

Riley pushed himself up, his vest creaking, and helped Sam to her feet. She checked her magazine with quick, practiced fingers.

Faith stepped forward, wiping her bleeding nose on her sleeve, and looked at Angel and Spike.

Faith said, "I'll go with Angel and Spike."

Angel gave one tight nod, and Spike cracked his neck and grinned, and the three of them grabbed swords off the coffee table where it sagged on its three remaining legs, Faith spinning a short sword once around her wrist, Angel testing the edge of a long blade with his thumb, and Spike twirling a longsword through one lazy figure eight. Then they went out through the shattered bay window, glass crunching under their boots and their coats flapping, and the blue shield gave around Angel and Spike like warm water instead of throwing them back, because it had been raised from inside a home that had asked both of them in, and the three of them ran on into the dark street.

Across the street, the neighbor's porch light cast a warm glow over the clapboard while the two Turok-Han rammed the front door, the wood splintering and the hinges screaming. From inside, the homeowner fired a shotgun point-blank into the lead creature's face, and the muzzle flash lit up the doorway, but the buckshot only dimpled and chipped its skin, and it didn't even flinch. It tore the door off its frame and tossed it aside, and the second creature pushed past into the porch light. Then both of them turned toward the street, because they could sense who was coming.

Spike was twenty feet out and closing fast, longsword in his right hand, coat tails flying, with Angel running close behind him on the left and Faith just behind Angel on the right, her nose still bleeding.

Spike planted his left foot at the edge of the porch and drove his right knee into the back of the lead creature's knee, and the joint buckled, dropping the Turok-Han onto the porch boards. Spike didn't pause; he gripped the longsword in both hands and drove the point down through its back and into its heart, and the body stiffened upright for one beat before it burst into black dust.

The second creature was already spinning toward him. It punched Spike square in the chest, and he flew ten feet backward, landed hard on the grass and skidded, his sword tumbling away.

Angel planted his foot on the lawn and drove a side kick into its stomach, and Faith slid in behind it on the wet grass and slashed her short sword down across its spine, between the shoulder blades. The creature arched and dug its claws into the doorframe, splintering the wood, then whipped a claw back at Faith's face, and she snapped her blade up and blocked it with a loud ring of metal.

Angel swung his longsword overhead and took the creature's right arm off at the shoulder in one clean arc. Black blood sprayed across the porch light. Faith slashed at once and took the left arm too, while it was still rising from the block. The creature screamed, its eyes locked on Faith, its fangs bared. Faith swung her blade level across its throat, and its head came off and tumbled from the porch into the grass. The body turned to dust and blew away on the night wind.

All three of them stood on the neighbor's lawn breathing hard with their weapons lowered and black dust on their clothes and faces, and Spike pushed himself up off the grass, picked up his sword and cracked his neck once. Behind them the neighbor's porch light still burned over the torn-off door, and somewhere far off the screams were fading. On the way back across the street Angel stopped at the edge of the Summers side yard, where the second of Riley's perimeter soldiers lay face down in the dark beside the hedge, and he crouched there for a moment with one hand flat on the man's back before he straightened and came on.

CHAPTER 4
Night Aftermath

Faith, Angel and Spike climbed back in through the broken window, glass crunching under their boots.

Angel walked straight to Buffy and gave her a small, tired nod. Buffy nodded back, her expression heavy.

Buffy said, "It tested us. Like we knew it would."

Angel stepped behind her, and out of habit his hand started toward her shoulder. Then he caught Lucian watching from the other side of the room and pulled it back fast.

Angel said, "Sorry. Force of habit."

Lucian gave one calm nod with no anger in it, and when Buffy sank down onto the couch he sat down right beside her and took her hand openly in front of all of them.

Around them, nobody in the room had settled yet. Dawn stood in the kitchen doorway with her arms wrapped around herself, watching with wide eyes. Tara kept a hand on Willow's lower back and glanced at the broken window. Willow checked the blue glow of the shield on the walls, pale and quick-eyed. Spike leaned against the wall by the window with his arms crossed and one eyebrow raised, watching Angel and Lucian. Riley and Sam stood watch at the window, and Sam gave Riley's arm a quick squeeze.

Then the lights flickered twice and came back, the shield pulsed blue across the walls and ceiling, and the bulbs steadied and stayed on, while the fridge shuddered back to life in the kitchen with a loud hum, a lamp clicked on by the stairs, and the TV snapped on by itself at full volume with a loud, bright cola commercial full of cheering crowds, until Riley reached over and thumbed the volume down.

Buffy wiped her face hard with both hands and pushed her hair back. She looked at Willow, tired but focused.

Buffy said, "Willow… anything on that ancient Slayers weapon yet?"

Willow shook her head, frustrated. She lowered a scroll and rubbed the back of her neck with her free hand.

Willow said, "No. I'm sorry. We've gone over everything we have here backwards and forwards. Nothing. No leads."

Buffy nodded once and let out a long, slow breath through her nose, and her fingers laced tighter through Lucian's, and he squeezed back with his thumb brushing over her knuckles, while Angel watched the two of them quietly with his face giving nothing away.

Angel said, "I could smell the morning sickness. I wasn't going to bring it up."

Buffy looked straight at him, firm and calm.

Buffy said, "I'm keeping it, Angel."

Angel stepped forward and glanced once at Lucian. Then he leaned down and hugged Buffy carefully, his arms around her shoulders and one hand gently at the back of her head. His voice was low and sincere.

Angel said, "Everything always happens all at once for you doesn't it?"

He sighed while he held her, a breath he didn't need, let out more for Buffy than for anyone else, and Buffy's hands slowly came up and squeezed his back while her cheek rested near his jaw.

Angel said, "This is what I wanted for you. You know that, Buffy."

Faith had slipped off to the kitchen, and now she walked back in with two foil-wrapped burritos. She had just taken a huge bite of one, sauce was dripping down her chin, and she was chewing loudly with her mouth full.

Faith said, "What? Someone die already while I was kitchen bound?"

A short, stiff, tired laugh went around the room, shoulders shaking, heads nodding and mouths curving in spite of everything.

Tara slipped out of the kitchen with a ceramic plate in both hands, walked straight to Faith and held it out without a word. Faith looked at the plate, then at Tara, and gave a sheepish shrug. She took the plate in her free hand and kept eating the burrito over it.

A heavy, uneasy quiet settled over the room, but nobody stood still. Dawn shifted from foot to foot in the kitchen doorway, her eyes moving from the broken window to Buffy. Tara rubbed small circles on Willow's lower back and tucked a stray strand of hair behind her ear. Willow kept glancing at the faint blue on the ceiling, tapping the wand against her side. Spike's cigarette smoke drifted by the wall. Riley rolled his shoulders, and Sam squeezed his arm again. Lucian kept his thumb moving over Buffy's hand, scanning the room, protective. Angel stood half a step back with his arms folded, tired.

The house breathed around them, gear shifting, the shield humming low in the background.

CHAPTER 5
The Eleven O'Clock News

The night pressed dark against the half-drawn heavy curtains, and the lamps were on. The blue shield still glowed faintly on the walls and ceiling, flickering every few seconds with a soft hum. The barrier Willow and Tara had raised was still holding. It was supposed to hold until morning.

It was just after eleven, and everyone had found something to keep their hands busy. Tara folded a blanket over the arm of the couch while Willow stood in the kitchen doorway with a scroll in one hand, rubbing her temple with the other, and Lucian sat on the end of the couch beside Buffy tightening a knife sheath on his belt. Riley and Sam adjusted their vests by the broken window, Spike lit a cigarette against the wall, Angel stood by the fireplace with his arms crossed, and Dawn sat on the arm of the couch with the remote in her lap.

Suddenly Dawn stiffened. Her arm shot out, pointing at the TV, and her hand was visibly shaking.

Dawn said, her voice small and shaky, "Guys… look."

Every head in the room snapped toward the television.

The late news filled the screen. A Sunnydale reporter stood in front of a black iron gate strung with yellow police tape. He was a balding, middle-aged white man in a grey suit and glasses, with a yellow and white striped tie, and he held his microphone the way people in Sunnydale did when they had stopped being surprised by anything.

The reporter said, "Good evening. I'm standing outside the Sunnydale Vineyard on Old Vine Road, where tonight police discovered the bodies of six people. All six had been slashed to death with what appeared to be knives. Witnesses tell us the last thing they saw at the scene were several hooded figures in brown robes. One witness insists the figures had no eyes and no mouths, before they vanished into the night. Sunnydale police are calling it gang-related, and a department spokesman says the suspects were most likely gang members on PCP. The vineyard has been closed until further notice, and residents are asked to stay indoors after dark. Which, in Sunnydale, is probably good advice any night of the week. Back to you, Barberia."

Dawn slowly lowered her arm. Her face was pale, and she swallowed hard, her hand gripping the couch cushion until her knuckles went white.

Tara's hand flew to her mouth, her eyes wide with quiet horror. The blanket she'd been folding dropped to the floor, forgotten.

Willow went pale beside her and glanced sideways at Tara with worry, her fingers tightening on the scroll until it creased.

Lucian leaned forward in his seat, his jaw flexing, his eyes locked on the screen, his hand drifting closer to Buffy's knee without his noticing.

By the fireplace, Angel's face gave nothing away, but his arms came uncrossed as he took one step forward toward the screen.

Spike flicked his lighter and lit another cigarette. His eyes narrowed, and he let out a small, sharp breath of smoke.

Riley and Sam looked grim. Riley's fist clenched at his side. Sam's hand found her rifle strap, and she straightened, fully alert.

Buffy was still on the couch. She stared at the screen for a long beat. Her eyes narrowed, and she sat up straighter. She was quiet, and she was certain.

Buffy said, "They wanted something there. Or they still want something there. That's where we need to go. I can feel it."

Near the wall, Faith uncrossed her arms and stepped closer to Riley and Spike. She spoke low and serious, gesturing with one hand at Riley, and Spike and Angel leaned in to listen.

Faith said, "The First likes to show up looking like dead people you used to know. Gets in your head. It can't actually touch anything though. So if you ever think someone isn't who they say they are… touch them. You'll know right away."

She glanced at the faint blue glow at the edges of the room.

Faith said, "This shield around the house? It can't get through it. We're safe in here. But the second we step outside…"

Riley cut in, his brow furrowed, thinking out loud.

Riley said, "It can't touch anything? Then it can't use weapons either. That makes it pretty useless, doesn't it?"

Faith shook her head, steady, with a small chopping motion of her hand.

Faith said, "No. You're the weapon it tries to use."

Riley stared at the floor for a moment, working it through. Slowly he nodded as he understood.

Around them the others listened, each in their own way. Tara picked up the dropped blanket and folded it with careful hands. Willow's eyes moved between the TV and Tara. Dawn rocked slightly on the arm of the couch. Lucian's hand rested protectively on Buffy's knee. Angel watched with his arms crossed. Spike breathed out smoke and eyed everyone. The lamplight flickered across their faces and their weapons, and the shield hummed softly.

CHAPTER 6
Vineyard Strategy

Buffy sat back on the couch with her eyes still on the TV, where the news had moved on to the weather, and Angel lowered himself into the armchair across from her. Behind them the others kept to their tasks: Tara was folding blankets, Willow had her wand in her hand, Lucian was checking the edges of his weapons one by one, Riley and Sam kept watch at the broken window, Dawn sat perched on the arm of the couch, and Spike leaned against the wall with his arms folded.

Buffy leaned forward, thinking, her elbow on her knee and her chin in her palm, and her eyes went from the TV down to the carpet and back again. Then she stood and looked at everyone, deliberately, one face at a time.

Buffy said, "Whatever they want from that place, we have to find out what it is… I can feel it's important…"

She paced with her hands clasped tightly behind her back, three steps toward the fireplace and three steps back, and took a deep breath and let it out in a sigh.

Buffy said, "Tara, Riley, Sam, Dawn, you stay here at the house. Angel and Faith will take West Brook and Oak View. Me and Spike and Willow will take Raven Crest and Maple Court on the way to the vineyard. We will use cars…"

She turned to Faith, her face taut with tension.

Buffy said, "If you see any humans being attacked… you have to keep driving until the vineyard… You can't stop and help them…"

Riley flinched a little at the order, but he didn't say anything.

Faith said, "But B…"

Buffy cut her off with one raised hand.

Buffy said, "We have to get there now… It's going to try and stop us, test us again… It's going to be listening once we are outside of the house area again… So you cannot tell it anything it does not know already…"

Faith nodded, sad and angry at once, her arms folding tight across her chest.

Buffy looked at all of them again.

Buffy said, "If we make one single mistake, we are finished… It will never stop using any weakness we show it once it has one…"

She let that sit for a moment in the quiet room, with nothing under it but the waver of the shield on the walls.

Buffy said, "That thing out there has been waiting thousands of years to pull this off…"

Angel said, "If we are going to do this, we need to go now…"

Lucian dug his car keys out of his pocket and quietly tossed them across the room to Willow, who caught them against her chest with both hands.

Lucian said, "Keep her safe for me, Red…"

Willow looked at him, the keys still cupped against her chest.

Willow said, "This is always going to be the hardest part… the waiting…"

Lucian nodded slowly.

Lucian said, "I will never get used to it… never…"

Buffy looked at Angel and Faith.

Buffy said, "Let's go…"

They all headed out past the plywood and down the porch steps into the cold night, under the blue shimmer of the shield arching over the lawn, and Willow slid behind the wheel of Lucian's car where it sat in the driveway. It was a sleek black 1981 Pontiac Firebird Trans Am Special Edition, with the gold firebird across the hood, the T-tops and the tan seats, and she flexed her fingers on the steering wheel a few times while Buffy dropped into the passenger seat and Spike folded himself into the back.

Willow said, "I have always wanted to drive his car…"

Her voice bubbled with barely contained excitement, and she flexed her fingers over the steering wheel again and shot Buffy a wide sideways smile, because this was a real muscle car and she was finally the one behind the wheel.

Buffy sighed.

Buffy said, "The little things, right, Wills?"

Willow turned the key, and the engine rumbled to life under them, and she backed out of the driveway and swung left down Revello Drive, while at the curb Angel's black 1967 Plymouth GTX coughed awake behind its one good headlight and pulled away to the right.

CHAPTER 7
Under This Sky

Inside Angel's black 1967 Plymouth GTX, the streetlights flashed past the empty windshield frame, and the night wind poured in over the dashboard and tugged at their hair and collars, carrying faint screams in from different directions across the dark town.

Angel winced at the screams. He flexed his hands on the wheel once and glanced at Faith in the passenger seat.

Angel said, "How are you holding up inside?"

Faith had her hands laced behind her head, leaning back in the seat with the wind whipping her hair across her swollen nose, completely casual, and she said it coldly, without looking at him.

Faith said, "Five by five, boss…"

Angel winced a little at that. He kept driving and took the overpass onto the main highway, his hands steady and his eyes on the road as the streetlights flashed by.

Out on the road in the Trans Am, orange streetlamps passed overhead, throwing moving shadows across their faces every second. Somewhere in the distance, people were screaming. Buffy stared silently through the windshield, focused, ready, thinking. Her body was still, her hands resting on her knees.

Willow drove, and she glanced over at Buffy.

Willow said, "You think they will get there first?"

A cold blue light flickered across the back seat beside Spike, and a low hum rose under the engine noise and settled there, cold and steady, and a voice answered from where the light had been. It was Drusilla's voice.

The First Evil was suddenly sitting next to Spike, wearing Drusilla's face, her pale hands folded in her lap and her dark hair falling loose around her shoulders. It had no body anyone could touch, but they were out past the crystal's zone now, where it could see and hear everything, and it was inside the car with them, and there was something wrong about the whole shape of it, like a nursery rhyme gone sour.

The First said, as Drusilla, "We are already there… aren't we, Spikey?"

Spike flinched hard against his door, because the First was sitting right beside him wearing the face he had loved for more than a hundred years.

Buffy said, "Keep driving, Wills… Do not look at it…"

Willow's eyes flicked to the rearview mirror anyway, out of pure driver's instinct. In the glass, Drusilla's face sat where the empty back seat should have been. It grinned, pale, and its head tilted slowly like a broken doll's. Willow's knuckles went white on the wheel and her breath caught, and for one sick second something cold turned over behind her eyes, a doubt with no memory underneath it for her to hold it up against. Buffy had kept the truth about the Trio from her, and the First was planting doubt in the empty place where that memory should have been.

The First said, as Drusilla, in a singsong nursery-rhyme voice, its eyes locked on Willow in the mirror and not on Buffy, "Little witch, little witch, down in the hole you played, Basement below, basement below — splish-splash, splash, splash. Three little boys in a row, three little boys all still, Who broke them dead in the dark? Naughty puppy. Naughty will."

Willow's mouth opened, but no sound came out, and the reflection trembled with the car's vibration, and for a moment she looked like she might twist around in her seat, but she didn't, she couldn't, and she kept her eyes on the road while the mirror held her anyway.

The First said, as Drusilla, with a soft giggle, leaning closer to the glass, its voice rising and falling like a lullaby gone wrong, "Innocent, innocent, Buffy sang sweet and low, Such a good lie, such a kind lie — hush now, good girl, don't know. Naughty girls, naughty girls, believe what they're told, While the pretty red puddle runs chilly and cold… don't they, Red?"

Willow swallowed hard.

Willow whispered, shaking, "You're lying…"

The First said, as Drusilla, slower now, almost tender, "Ring-a-ring o' roses, a pocket full of lies, She couldn't tell little Red till she blinked — till she blinked — and forgot with her eyes. Am I lying, am I lying? Ask the boys in the floor. Little witch, little witch… you know what you did in the dark, pet. You know."

Buffy said firmly, without turning around, her eyes on the road as if she could steer through Willow, "Wills. Eyes forward. It's not her. It's not Dru. It's the thing that hates us. Do not let it in."

Willow blinked fast and nodded once. It was a small nod, not convinced, but she kept going. Her hands flexed on the wheel.

Buffy said, "Spike, take my hand, focus on my heartbeat, listen to it, only it."

Buffy reached her left hand back between the seats. Spike took it in both of his, closed his eyes and leaned forward, muttering quietly, holding on to the sound of her heartbeat and nothing else.

The First said, as Drusilla, "You have nowhere to go, under this sky… It all belongs to me now… Every crack, in the dark, every space in the light. It's all mine now…"

Buffy turned her head and looked it in the face.

Buffy said, "I killed a god once. I will kill you too. I am going to dig you up myself, personally and put my hands around your neck and strangle you until you die…"

The First, still wearing Drusilla, smiled coldly from the back seat. It leaned forward, its eyes on Buffy's face.

The First said, as Drusilla, "You will try..."

Then the cold blue light folded in on itself, the hum that had been sitting under the engine noise dropped away into silence, and the back seat beside Spike was empty again.

CHAPTER 8
Magna Mater

On a night flight high over the dark middle of the country, the cabin lights were dimmed low, the little reading lamps were off in row after row, and the aisle strip lights were barely on, and outside the windows there was nothing but black night until the lightning came.

Far from Sunnydale, Giles sat on the first leg out of Houston with Xander and Anya beside him, a long layover still ahead of them that would eat most of the next day before the last short flight into Sunnydale. Xander looked stronger than he used to, with more muscle under his shirt, sharper and more confident eyes, and a new steadiness in the way he sat, while Anya was green around the gills and miserable.

Anya sat beside Giles, complaining in short, clipped bursts about how airsick she felt. Xander kept a hand on her arm and reassured her, low and steady and patient.

Giles murmured a polite excuse and got up to go to the restroom.

He stepped into the aisle and paused, looking out through the black window over the wing.

Lightning flashed, a stark white strobe.

There was a demon on the wing. Its face was pressed right up against the window glass, snarling at him. Its claws were tearing at the wing where the number two engine joined it, and the metal screamed.

Giles took off his glasses quickly. There was no panic in him, only a Watcher's steel.

Giles said, "That's quite enough out of you…"

He closed his eyes and pressed both hands flat against the inside of the plane, against the frame of the fuselage, the metal body of the aircraft itself.

Nose to tail, wingtip to wingtip, the plane was a cross, the biggest one Giles had ever stood inside. And the rain was falling on all of it.

He began to chant in Latin, low and absolute, with no theatrics at all.

Giles chanted, his eyes closed and his hands pressed to the metal, "Magna Mater, benedic creaturam aquae in servitium tuum."

He was asking the Great Mother to bless the water and take it into her service, and nothing glowed when he did. There was no light and no sign of magic at all, none of the passengers noticed a thing, the hum of the cabin never changed, and the rain kept falling on the wings as if nothing were happening.

The rain on the wing was holy water now.

Then the demon outside shrieked. Its hands jerked on and off the plane's skin as if the metal had suddenly turned burning hot, and it couldn't hold on. Its claws raked empty air, and its face twisted away from the glass.

It flew one full circle past the window, wings beating against the storm, rain sheeting off the wing. Then it was gone into the night. The lightning flashed again on an empty wing. The number two engine was still attached, and for one flash of light the scrape marks showed on its casing.

Giles opened his eyes. He put his glasses back on with slightly unsteady fingers, straightened his jacket and walked on down the aisle toward the restroom as if nothing had happened.

CHAPTER 9
Air Like Nectar

Out at the Sunnydale Vineyard on Old Vine Road the moon was a thin smear behind the clouds, and the Trans Am's headlights swept across row after row of dead vines as Willow pulled in, catching the police tape in a dull yellow while the black iron gate stood in silhouette against the dark. Taking Raven Crest and Maple Court, they had beaten Angel and Faith's GTX there.

Willow, Buffy and Spike climbed out into the cold, and Willow drew her dark-orange wooden wand with its copper inlays, and as they walked toward the gate her green eyes flashed dark orange for a moment while her magic powered up, smooth and strong out here past the crystal's static. It was not the black-eyed rush of dark Willow but something held and controlled, though every spell she cast under this much stress still took its toll on a witch who was still healing.

Spike walked with his classic strut, leather coat on and longsword ready, loose and lethal and confident.

Buffy was focused, with the double-bladed axe in both hands, ready to fight. The doubt the First had planted in Willow was still there, but no one said a word about it.

The vineyard was a huge tangle of dead vines choking the rows. The police were gone. Yellow tape was stretched across the black iron gate, and DO NOT ENTER signs were posted. The crime scene had been abandoned to the night.

They went over and under the tape. Buffy ducked under it, Willow lifted a strand, and Spike stepped over the gate's lower rail.

Buffy turned to Willow and gave the order.

Buffy said, "You're the rear. Spike, flank me…"

They walked deeper inside together, dead vines brushing their shoulders, gravel and mulch crunching under their boots.

Six Bringers rose out of the shadows between the rows all at once in their hooded brown robes, with smooth, empty skin where their eyes should have been and thin, silent mouths that had never held a tongue, and their knives came out at the same moment and they ran at the three of them in perfect rhythm, like soldiers out of a nightmare.

Willow cocked her head to the side. Without looking, Buffy stepped out of the way to give Willow a clear line, still ready to fight.

Willow flicked the wand once and raised it.

Willow said, "Air like nectar, thick as onyx... hold mine victim as in tar."

The air in front of the Bringers shimmered like heat off asphalt, thick as nectar and dark as onyx. The Bringers slowed to a stop in mid-charge. One froze in mid-step with its front foot hanging and its knife still raised, the hem of its robe not even settling.

Willow said, panting, "You only got three seconds…"

Spike moved first and ran one through the chest with his longsword, and it dropped into the row with a puff of dry dirt kicking up around it.

Buffy swung the double-bladed axe in a flat horizontal sweep that took the first Bringer across the chest with the leading blade, then spun on her heel and brought the back blade around into the second in the same motion, and both of them fell before the shimmer faded.

Then the spell cracked, and the last three twitched and could move again.

One of them ran straight for Willow, sensing she was tiring, its knife held low. Buffy threw the axe, and it buried itself in the middle of the Bringer's back, and it fell forward, dead.

Another one thrust its dagger at Buffy's throat, and she ducked. It swept a kick at her and turned the whole motion into a slash at her stomach, but Buffy caught its wrist and drove her elbow into the inside of its arm, and the arm snapped. She took the dagger from its hand and cut its throat with it, and the body dropped.

At the same moment, behind Buffy, Spike was fighting the last one. It blocked his longsword as he swung at its shoulders, left, right, left, the dagger meeting his blade every time with a ringing of metal. Then it kicked dirt straight into his face, and Spike's eyes shut. He listened, and when he heard its foot scrape on the gravel, he spun left and slashed hard across, and the longsword took the Bringer's head clean off. It rolled away down the row and stopped against the root of a vine.

Buffy set her boot on the last Bringer's back and pulled the axe free, and the three of them stood breathing hard among six fallen robes while the dead vines swayed in a faint wind around them and, back behind them, the yellow tape still hung across the gate.

CHAPTER 10
The Slayer Scythe

Thin moonlight filtered through the dead vines and overgrown trees. The Trans Am's headlights had long since been switched off, and deep shadows lay between the rows. A single shaft of pale light cut down through the branches onto the center of the vineyard like a spotlight. Ahead, something stood in that light, with a faint warm glow coming from inside it.

Buffy led the way with the double-bladed axe still in her hand. Spike walked a little to her right with his sword resting on his shoulder. Willow brought up the rear, her wand lowered but ready. They moved carefully, but with purpose, through the dead rows.

The vineyard seemed endless, row after row of twisted, leafless vines choking the land. In the center, rising out of the earth as if it had always been there, stood a small, ancient round stone building, like a miniature Greek temple. Six weathered stone columns held up a domed roof topped with a simple finial. Stone steps circled the outside and then led down into darkness. Even the ground sloped gently toward it, as if the land had been pulling everything toward this one spot for centuries.

Buffy slowed at the end of the last row, staring at it.

Buffy said, "That's it."

They walked the rest of the way in silence, gravel crunching under their boots. When they reached the outer ring of columns, Buffy nodded toward the steps, and the three of them carefully began to climb down into the earth.

Down in the underground temple hallway it was almost completely dark, except for a single shaft of light far away at the end of the corridor. It was soft, white and steady, like moonlight but stronger. Their footsteps echoed on the stone. Roots creaked. A low hum grew slowly louder, and so did their breathing.

The hallway was carved out of dark stone. Thick tree roots and vines had burst through cracks in the walls and ceiling, and some of them hung down like dead snakes. The air was cool and still. Buffy led with the axe ready. Spike followed, his eyes checking every shadow. Willow stayed close behind with one hand lightly touching the wall for balance.

At the end of the hallway, the corridor opened into a small round chamber. In its center, bathed in that single shaft of pure white light from above, stood a low altar of glossy black obsidian. The stone was so smooth it looked almost liquid, and its natural facets caught the light like dark glass. Standing upright in the center of the altar was the Slayer Scythe.

It was an ancient weapon. One end was a broad, wickedly curved axe head, and the other was a shorter, brutal spike of a blade. The handle was dark wood, wrapped in places with old leather. Red metal inlays and fittings caught the light and made it look almost alive. The whole Scythe sat in a natural cradle in the obsidian, as if the stone had grown around it.

The three of them stopped together in the doorway of the chamber, the white light falling across the toes of their boots.

Willow said, quiet and awed, "That's it… what we were looking for, Buffy. I can feel it."

Buffy said, "Yeah. Me too."

They stepped into the chamber and slowly circled the altar, keeping a respectful distance at first. Buffy's eyes never left the weapon. Without looking, she handed her axe to Spike, and he took it without a word.

Buffy slowly reached out. Willow held her breath, her shoulders tense.

Buffy's fingers closed around the worn wooden handle. She paused for half a second. Then she pulled.

The Scythe came free with a soft, ringing sound, like a blade drawn from oiled leather. She hefted it in both hands and turned it slowly, and both blades caught the shaft of light and shone with a cold, sharp gleam.

Spike said, "Bloody hell. Looks like something a fireman brings when the building's already gone."

Buffy didn't laugh; she was still staring at the weapon in her hands, feeling the weight of it settle into her arms.

Buffy said quietly, "I can feel something holding it. It's a power. It's subtle… but it's filling me. Like… I don't know. It feels powerful."

Willow watched her carefully.

Willow said, "Can I see it?"

Buffy nodded and handed the Scythe over without hesitating.

Willow took it in both hands and closed her eyes, centering herself the way she did before big magic. For a moment the only sound in the stone chamber was their breathing.

Then there was a sudden, violent flash.

In the vision, hundreds of Turok-Han, the uber vampires, were screaming and clawing over each other in a writhing mass. They were pouring up through the cracked seal in the floor of the basement of Sunnydale High School. There were claws, fangs, dead eyes and raw hunger, and the roar of hundreds of voices was deafening.

Willow gasped and flinched hard, and her eyes snapped open. She shoved the Scythe back at Buffy as if it had burned her.

Willow said, shaken, "Whoa…"

She looked at Buffy, her face pale.

Willow said, "We got a problem."

Buffy took the Scythe back. The three of them stood in the shaft of light. Buffy's and Willow's faces reflected in the black obsidian of the altar. Where Spike stood, the stone showed nothing. The low hum in the chamber had grown a little louder.

CHAPTER 11
The Thing

The great stone dome still pulsed with faint leftover magic. Dust and ancient debris floated in the air like glitter in hell.

Buffy, Spike and Willow came out of the mouth of the dome into the night. Buffy walked in the center, holding the Slayer Scythe by the rear of its handle with the blade angled down like a battle standard. Its red metal edge caught the moonlight and threw off a blood-red glint. Spike walked on her left, his black leather duster flaring and a fresh cigarette glowing between his lips. He swaggered, smoke curling lazily around his smirk. Willow trailed half a step behind, her eyes still glowing a faint dark orange with leftover magic, breathing hard but steady.

A low, throaty engine growl cut through the quiet, and Angel's black 1967 Plymouth GTX came rolling up the dirt path and stopped hard, its matte black paint scarred from years of demon fights, its hood still buckled up in a jagged V, its windshield frame empty, and its chrome grille gleaming around the one headlight that still worked. The single beam swept across the three of them, crawled up Buffy's boots, over the Scythe and across Spike's leather, and then snapped off with a heavy clunk, and Spike eyed the battered car the way he might have sized up a threat… or an old drinking buddy who had finally lost a fight. Then the GTX's engine cut out, and the vineyard went silent.

Faith kicked the passenger door open with a combat boot and hopped out in one fluid motion. She wore dark jeans and a tight black tank under a half-zipped leather jacket, her hair was wild from the wind that had poured in through the empty windshield frame the whole way there, and her broken nose was swollen and purpling across the bridge under the dark crust of the cut above her eyebrow. She spotted Buffy right away, and a grin split her face. She pointed straight at the Scythe with two fingers, like she was calling a play.

Faith said, "Is that the thing?"

Buffy lifted the Scythe smoothly and twisted it once in her grip. The red blade caught the moonlight and flared like fresh blood on steel. She smiled, small and dangerous and cocky as hell, the same smile she used to give the Master right before she ended him.

Buffy said, "Yup. This is the thing we're gonna shove right up the First Evil's ass."

Spike let out a low, appreciative chuckle and flicked the ash off his cigarette. Willow smirked beside Buffy, exhausted but proud.

The five of them moved fast, Buffy, Willow and Spike folding back into the Trans Am and Faith vaulting into the GTX beside Angel, and both engines roared up into the night, because the shield on Revello Drive would only hold until morning, and every minute they spent out here was another minute for it to wear thin.
