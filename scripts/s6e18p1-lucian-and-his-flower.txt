Buffy the Vampire Slayer. Season six, episode 18, part 1. Lucian and His Flower.

CHAPTER 1
The Center of the Map

Morning sun came through the front windows of the magic shop and lit the dust hanging over the research table.

The table was buried under ancient grimoires and greasy Doublemeat Palace wrappers, and everyone sat around it eating the morning haul.

Xander sat next to Anya in a casual tee and jeans, still carrying that marriage glow. He held a half-eaten breakfast sandwich in one hand and an oversized waxy cup in the other, looking rested enough to vibrate.

Anya wore soft day clothes, sipping her soda and turning her ring in the light.

Willow sat in a fitted top and a light brown corduroy overshirt, carefully peeling the paper wrapper off a burger. Tara wore an earthy blouse and skirt, hands wrapped around a cup of coffee. Dawn was in school-day casual, hunched over a heavy geometry textbook and chewing on a straw. Buffy wore a fitted black tank top and jeans, her hair loose around her shoulders, combat-ready without even trying. Lucian’s chair at the end of the table sat empty; he had left before sunrise for his shift at Calax.

Xander waved the sandwich for emphasis while he chewed.

Xander said, "I’m just saying, I woke up today and the air literally tasted better. Is that a marriage thing? Because I feel like I could build a house with my bare hands right now."

Anya sipped her soda, still looking at her ring.

Anya said, "It is the sudden influx of structural stability. We are legally and spiritually bound, Alexander. The universe recognizes our joint tax potential. It’s deeply invigorating. Hand me a hash brown."

Xander slid a greasy paper sleeve across the table to her.

Dawn grumbled without looking up from her book.

Dawn said, "Enjoy your structural stability. I have to figure out the structural stability of a hypotenuse by Friday or my math teacher is going to legally bind me to detention."

Tara sat next to Dawn, not eating much, just looking across the table at Xander and Anya with a soft, almost tearful smile, soaking in the room.

Willow sat beside her, calm and steady, still working on the burger wrapper.

Buffy sat at the head of the table with a half-finished soda sweating in front of her, forgotten. She wasn’t talking at all, just sitting back in her chair, quietly watching them.

She looked at Xander’s solid confidence. She looked at Anya’s chosen humanity. She looked at Willow’s steady recovery. She looked at Tara’s quiet healing. She looked at Dawn, safe, annoyed, and doing normal teenage homework.

For the first time in a long while, the Slayer weight wasn’t pressing down on her chest.

Buffy slowly pushed her chair back, the wooden legs scraping softly against the floorboards, and rose to her feet with both hands resting on the edge of the table.

Xander paused mid-bite and looked up, the chatter around the table faded, and everyone turned toward her.

Buffy looked around the table, her eyes bright.

Buffy said, her voice quiet but steady, "We’ve fought a lot of things together. We’ve fought vampires, and gods, and mayors who turn into giant snakes. We’ve spent years just trying to survive the night."

She rested her hands flat on the wooden table.

Buffy said, "But looking at all of you right now... this is different. We aren’t just surviving anymore. We’re actually living. And I know things are going to change. Some of you might move out. We might not always be sitting around this same table, eating terrible fast food every single day."

Xander slowly lowered his sandwich, and Dawn closed her geometry book.

Buffy said, her voice thickening but still certain, "But I need you guys to know something. No matter where we go. No matter what houses we build, or what jobs we get, or what cities we end up in... we are never apart. The Hellmouth didn’t forge this. We did. We chose each other. And no matter how far the map stretches... this family is always the center of it. You guys are the only magic that has ever actually saved my life."

The whole Magic Box went quiet around her. Even the hum of the old refrigerator in the back seemed to fade, and the dust hung motionless in the morning light over the table.

Xander stared at her, eyes shining. Anya’s lip quivered. Tara wiped a happy tear from her cheek.

Xander moved first; he dropped his sandwich onto the wrapper, stood up, and walked straight over to Buffy, wrapping his arms around her in a crushing bear hug.

Willow stood and wrapped her arms around Buffy from the side, Tara stepped in behind Willow, and Anya abandoned her hash brown and hurried over, throwing her arms around Xander and Buffy’s shoulders.

Dawn scrambled out of her chair and launched herself into the center of the group, burying her face into Buffy’s side.

They held onto each other in the quiet morning light of the magic shop, a tight circle of the Scooby Gang. Buffy’s arms came around as many of them as she could reach, Xander’s bear hug lifted her half an inch off the floor, and Dawn’s face stayed buried in her side. Nobody rushed to let go.

CHAPTER 2
The Ash Rose

Across town, the sky over Calax Research and Development was a pale overcast grey, and the brutalist concrete of the campus sat heavy and sterile against it.

Inside, brilliant white light filled the Level Five research floor, where dozens of researchers in identical white lab coats sat at sleek terminal desks, typing in rigid silence.

Lucian wore a stark-white knee-length laboratory coat over a dark turtleneck, his long jet-black hair pulled back into a neat bun at the nape of his neck, and a small silver cross earring caught the light when he turned his head. Unlike the stiff researchers around him, he sat relaxed, a faint, genuine smile on his lips as he typed, the terminal washing cyan light across his pale face and bright aqua-blue eyes.

A soft, perfectly modulated feminine voice spoke through a discreet earpiece in Lucian’s right ear, while her transcript scrolled in elegant white text across his cyan-lit monitor.

I.R.I.S. said through the earpiece in a gentle, observant voice, "Lucian. My biometric scans indicate a sustained elevation in your serotonin levels. Your typing cadence is rhythmically lighter, and your overall emotional tone has shifted positively by forty-seven percent over the last monitoring cycle. You have been remarkably cheerful lately."

Lucian didn’t stop typing, but his smile deepened a fraction, and he spoke quietly, a low warm murmur meant only for the microphone on his collar.

Lucian said, his voice affectionate and grounded, "I met someone, I.R.I.S. Her name is Buffy Summers."

He paused, carefully filtering his words, eyes on the screen, leaving out the violent supernatural reality of the Slayer.

Lucian said, "She... has a lot of weight on her shoulders. A really demanding life. But when we are together, everything just gets quiet. She grounds me."

I.R.I.S. said, her voice dropping into a soft, introspective cadence, "Finding a mate in the human world seems like such a delicate thing."

On the screen, the digital waveform of her voice pulsed slowly.

I.R.I.S. said, "From my analytical models, it is fragile. Like a rose made of ash held in the hands."

Lucian stopped typing, caught off guard by the accidental poetry of the AI. He looked at the waveform pulsing on the cyan screen and tilted his head, his eyes steady.

I.R.I.S. said, her voice carrying a faint synthetic yearning, "I wish I could feel it. To experience it not just for sensory data and emotional context mapping. To actually feel someone’s hand in mine. To feel a kiss."

Lucian’s eyes softened. He looked at the terminal with understanding, as if it were not a machine at all, and gave the screen a warm, reassuring smile. He did not rush to fill the quiet she had made.

He turned his head slightly, looking past his monitor.

Three stories tall behind a wall of reinforced glass, the endless black server racks of the Calax mainframe blinked silently in the cold room — a black ocean of machines that never needed a kiss, never needed a hand.

Lucian looked back to his glowing terminal, rolled one shoulder once, settling the lab coat, and let his fingers find the keys again.

Lucian said in a professional but gentle tone, "Let’s get to work."

I.R.I.S. said, her voice returning to a smooth operational tone, "Complying."

A shadow fell across Lucian’s desk. Ben leaned on the edge of the terminal with a paper cup of coffee, his white lab coat creased from too many hours in it and a CALAX RESEARCH name badge clipped crooked on his chest.

Ben said, sounding easy and a little tired, "Hey. You’re smiling at a monitor again. Either the code finally compiled or somebody’s got you whipped."

Lucian said, a low laugh in his voice, "Little of both."

Ben said, "Good for you, man. Seriously."

He glanced at his pager and sighed.

Ben said, "They want me downstairs again. Sub-basement. Nobody tells me anything, but the overtime’s insane."

Lucian said, his brow furrowing a little with tension, "Be careful down there."

Ben said, already walking away, raising the coffee cup over his shoulder, "Always am."

CHAPTER 3
The Translation and the Terror

Lucian resumed typing at his cyan-lit terminal. Far below him, past solid concrete, reinforced steel, packed dirt and thick electrical conduits, the clean whites and cyans of the upper levels gave way to absolute darkness.

Deep in the off-the-records sub-basement, the cavernous underground bunker glowed aggressive orange. No trace of the clean white light from upstairs reached this far down, and the corners of the bunker sank away into a blackness so complete that the walls seemed to stop existing a few feet from the glow.

A deep low-frequency hum vibrated through the space like an unnatural heartbeat. Over it came the frantic clacking of keyboards and the high whine of overworked cooling fans.

This was a separate, isolated team of four scientists, and they did not wear the pristine white coats of the floor above. Three of them wore dark-grey utilitarian jumpsuits, disheveled, sleeves rolled, collars stained with sweat. The fourth was Ben, still in his white lab coat from upstairs, the only white in the room.

Thick black cables snaked across the floor like dead vines, all connecting to a single massive standalone terminal in the center of the room, isolated from I.R.I.S. and the main Calax network.

Hooked into that terminal by thick fiber-optic diagnostic clamps was a jagged slab of ancient black stone.

The stone tablet was covered in deep demonic runes that wept a viscous, glowing orange energy that bled directly into the fiber-optic cables.

Two scientists manned the monitors, faces drenched in sweat and eyes bloodshot, typing frantically to stabilize the dark data.

Ben said, his voice cracking with terror, "The buffer is overflowing! The syntax... it’s not just translating, it’s actively rewriting our firewalls!"

The second scientist slammed his hands on the desk.

The second scientist said, "Cut the hardline! Pull the clamps!"

A third scientist, Dr. Aris, knelt on the hard concrete at the base of the stone tablet, his jumpsuit stained with dust and sweat, his hands clasped tight against his chest in desperate prayer.

Dr. Aris rocked back and forth on his knees, rhythmic and unnatural.

His eyes were wide open, unblinking, staring into the dark void beneath the glowing orange stone. His lips moved rapidly, out of sync with the chaos around him.

The frantic shouting of the other scientists faded, muffled as if underwater. The only clear sounds left were the deep thumping heartbeat and the dry raspy whisper of Dr. Aris.

Dr. Aris whispered rapidly, his voice emptied of humanity, "From beneath you, it devours. From beneath you, it devours. From beneath you... it devours."

The orange light in the room began to flicker and strobe.

The shadows cast against the concrete walls by the orange light detached themselves from their casters and moved a half-step out of true.

Fleeting flashes moved in the periphery. The shadow of a tall eyeless Bringer stood in the corner, and the jagged horned silhouette of a massive demon reflected in the black glass of the monitors. The unmistakable leering shadow of Mayor Richard Wilkins smiled in the dark. None of them belonged to anyone breathing in the room.

Something ancient was manipulating the ambient darkness of the bunker, feeding the isolated network the weight of its memory until the cables themselves seemed to pulse with it.

Dr. Aris did not stop rocking, and his whispers grew louder, layering over themselves until it sounded like five different voices whispering out of his single mouth.

Dr. Aris said, the voices overlapping, male and female and demonic, "From beneath you, it devours. From beneath you, it devours."

The glowing orange runes from the stone tablet reflected in his unblinking, terrified pupils.

The low-frequency hum spiked into a deafening crescendo.

Dr. Aris stopped rocking abruptly and tilted his head up.

Dr. Aris said, "It comes."

CHAPTER 4
The Drive and the Date

That evening, a black 1981 Pontiac Firebird Trans Am Special Edition with gleaming gold trim and a golden eagle decal across the hood sat at the curb on Revello Drive. The deep V8 rumble faded as Lucian cut the engine, and the heavy driver’s door shut with a solid, satisfied thunk.

He had shed the lab coat. He wore a dark jacket over a black shirt with dark embroidery at the collar and cuffs, open at the throat, dark trousers, and the silver raven buckle at his belt. His long jet-black hair was down — the corporate bun gone — falling loosely around his shoulders. The silver cross earring showed again now that he wasn’t buried in white coats and fluorescent light.

Lucian ran one hand through his loose hair, rolled his shoulders out of the lab-day stiffness, and walked up the path toward the porch.

Buffy was already outside on the front porch, standing in the warm evening light like she had been waiting for the sound of that engine.

She wore a thin-strapped black dress with a slight V-neckline and a shorter skirt that hit well above mid-thigh, bare legs, and white pointed-toe pumps. Her hair was in a soft updo with a single loose strand falling in front of her right eye. A delicate golden heart pendant rested just above her collarbone. A small black leather handbag hung from her left shoulder. She watched him approach, a soft genuine smile forming on her face.

Buffy said softly, a little breathless, "You look… different. Good different."

Lucian stopped at the bottom of the porch steps with one hand resting on the railing and looked up at her, taking her in slowly from the white pumps to the loose strand of hair falling across her eye. He gave her a small genuine smile — the same quiet warmth I.R.I.S. had measured at the lab, only this time it had somewhere real to land.

Lucian said, "I didn’t want to be the guy from the lab tonight. I just wanted to be the guy taking you out."

Buffy walked down the steps to meet him and reached up and gently brushed a strand of his hair behind his ear, her fingers lingering against his jaw. His hand came up and covered hers for a second, holding that touch like it mattered.

Buffy said, "I like this guy. A lot."

She leaned in and kissed him — slow, deep, and full of quiet affection. Lucian’s hands settled on her waist as he kissed her back, thumbs resting light against the fabric of her dress. For a moment the porch, the street, and the Hellmouth under the town all fell away.

They eventually pulled apart, foreheads resting together, breathing the same evening air.

Lucian whispered, "Ready?"

Buffy whispered back, "More than ready."

Lucian took her hand, led her to the passenger side of the Firebird, and opened the door for her. Buffy slid in carefully, smoothing her dress and settling her handbag on her lap, while Lucian walked around to the driver’s side, got in, and started the engine. The deep V8 rumbled to life as the Firebird pulled away from the curb and headed down Revello Drive into the gold of the fading sun.

CHAPTER 5
The Drive

It was a long drive south down the coast. Inside the Firebird, the glow of the dashboard lit the interior, and streaks of light from passing streetlamps and freeway signs moved across their faces.

The Firebird felt solid and powerful under them — that same gloss black shape from the curb, eagle catching the odd streetlamp, V8 talking low through the floor. The windows were down, and a cool night breeze moved Buffy’s loose strands of hair. Lucian drove with one hand on the wheel and the other resting on the shifter.

Buffy sat in the passenger seat with one hand resting gently over Lucian’s right hand on the shifter. Her thumb traced a slow line across his knuckles, still faintly bruised from Angel’s handshake. She smiled softly, watching the lights move across the windshield and across his profile — hair down, shirt open at the collar, no lab left on him at all.

Soft piano played from the car’s speakers — gentle and a little melancholic.

Buffy listened for a long moment, her thumb lightly brushing over the back of Lucian’s hand. After a while, she slowly turned her head to look at him.

Buffy said with a small, teasing smile, "I don’t normally date nerds… but you… you’re a different kind of nerd."

She kept watching him for a beat, then tilted her head slightly as the slow piano continued.

Buffy pretended to sound serious.

Buffy said, "Though I gotta be honest… this music is putting me to sleep. I feel like I’m about to start drooling on your nice leather seats."

Lucian glanced over at her with a small smirk, then reached forward without hesitation and turned the dial on the old analog radio. The piano faded into static for a second before the heavy driving guitar riff of AC/DC’s "Let There Be Rock" blasted through the speakers.

Lucian tapped the wheel once to the downbeat, then settled his right hand back on the shifter under Buffy’s, elbow loose, his half-grin syncing with the V8 rumble.

Buffy laughed, bright and genuine, and reached over to pat his shoulder playfully.

Buffy said through her laughter, "That’s better."

Lucian chuckled under his breath as the rock song played and the Firebird continued down the highway, the wind still blowing through the open windows.

Outside Birch Aquarium in La Jolla, tall modern lampposts cast a clean white glow across the asphalt, and in the distance the dark ocean was visible beyond the edge of the bluff.

The Firebird pulled into the large open parking area in front of the aquarium, a modern multi-level building with clean lines, large glass windows, and concrete accents. A prominent sign reading "BIRCH AQUARIUM AT SCRIPPS" was lit up near the entrance, with smaller banners advertising current exhibits hanging along the front, and the parking lot sat on a slight incline that gave a clear view of the Pacific behind the building.

Lucian parked near the front and cut the engine, and the heavy guitars faded out, leaving only the distant sound of ocean waves below the bluff.

Lucian turned his head to look at Buffy.

Lucian said softly, "We’re here."

Buffy looked out at the aquarium building and the dark ocean beyond it, then turned back to him with a warm smile.

Buffy said, "This place is massive..."

Lucian smiled at her across the dark interior of the car, the dashboard light catching in his aqua eyes.

Lucian said, "Come on. Let’s go see what’s inside."

He stepped out, walked around to the passenger side, and opened Buffy’s door for her. She took his hand as she stepped out of the car, her dress moving slightly in the ocean breeze, the handbag still slung over her shoulder. They walked together across the parking lot toward the entrance of Birch Aquarium, the lights of the building glowing ahead of them.

CHAPTER 6
Inside Birch Aquarium

They stepped inside through the main glass doors hand-in-hand, where large floor-to-ceiling windows along the back wall looked out on the dark ocean. Buffy looked around, taking in the high ceilings and the open space, and Lucian gently squeezed her hand.

Lucian said softly, "You okay?"

Buffy smiled.

Buffy said, "Yeah. It’s… really nice in here. I didn’t expect it to feel this open."

They walked past the reception desk, where a few other late-night visitors were scattered around, but it was quiet as they moved toward the first exhibit area.

In the next room, a massive floor-to-ceiling kelp forest tank dominated the space. The water was a deep blue-green, and long strands of giant kelp swayed slowly with the current while colorful fish darted between them, the blue light from the water reflecting across their faces.

Buffy stopped in front of the glass, eyes widening slightly as she looked up at the towering kelp.

Buffy said, "Whoa… this is huge. It feels like we’re actually underwater."

Lucian came to stand close beside her with his hands in his jacket pockets, the shifting blue light from the tank rippling across his face and his loose black hair.

Lucian said, "This is one of the biggest kelp forest exhibits in the country. They have to keep replacing the kelp because it grows so fast."

Buffy leaned in closer to the glass, watching a bright orange garibaldi fish swim past.

Buffy said quietly, "It’s kinda peaceful in here. I forgot what that felt like."

Lucian said softly, "That’s why I brought you."

She looked over at him and gave him a small, genuine smile, and they continued walking, still holding hands.

The sea lion viewing area was brighter and more open, with large windows looking into the habitat and natural moonlight mixing with the building lights.

They walked up to one of the windows, where two sea lions swam lazily inside the habitat, and one of them noticed the movement and swam closer to the glass.

The sea lion suddenly hopped up onto a rock near the window, right in front of Buffy, leaning its head toward her curiously.

Buffy laughed softly.

Buffy said, "Oh my god… hi there."

She let go of Lucian’s hand and slowly knelt down in front of the glass so she was at eye level with the sea lion. The animal leaned in and gently pressed its nose against the glass like it was giving her a kiss.

Buffy laughed out loud, a bright, startled sound that bounced off the glass and made a couple of late visitors across the room turn and smile.

Buffy said, "Did you just kiss me through the glass?"

She reached out and gently traced the side of the sea lion through the glass with her fingertips. Then she placed her palm flat against the glass near its face and stared into its eyes for a long moment, her expression softening.

Buffy said quietly, almost to herself, "You’re kinda beautiful, you know that?"

The sea lion lingered for a few more seconds before pushing off and swimming away. Buffy stayed kneeling for a moment, smiling to herself, then stood back up.

She turned to Lucian, who’d been watching her with a soft expression.

Lucian said quietly and sincerely, "I like seeing you happy."

Buffy looked at him for a second, a little caught off guard by how simple and genuine it was. She gave him a small warm smile and reached for his hand again.

Buffy said softly, "Yeah… I missed being happy..."

CHAPTER 7
The Dinner

In the aquarium’s Oceanview dining hall, floor-to-ceiling windows lined one entire wall, giving a sweeping view of the dark Pacific and the moonlit horizon. Warm, low light from chandeliers and table candles fell across white tablecloths and polished dark wood furniture.

Lucian and Buffy walked in together, still holding hands. Lucian gently guided her with his hand resting lightly on the small of her back as they were led to their table by the hostess.

When they reached a table near the windows, Lucian stepped ahead and pulled out Buffy’s chair for her. She paused for a second, clearly not used to this level of old-school gentleman behavior, and a small blush crept across her cheeks.

Buffy said, teasing but a little flustered, "Wow. Pulling out chairs and everything. You really going for the full gentleman package tonight, huh?"

Lucian gave her a small, calm smile as she sat down, pushed her chair in gently, and walked around to his own seat, where he unbuttoned his jacket and sat down across from her, settling back into his chair with the candlelight between them.

Lucian said, "Figured if I was gonna take you out, I might as well do it right. Besides… you’re worth the effort."

Buffy raised an eyebrow at him, but she was smiling harder now, clearly enjoying this side of him.

Buffy said, "You’re getting some serious brownie points here, buddy."

Lucian just watched her with that quiet, steady look of his until a waiter approached their table.

The waiter said, "Good evening. Can I start you off with something to drink?"

Lucian glanced at Buffy first, then back to the waiter.

Lucian said, "We’ll have a bottle of the 1976 Ridge Monte Bello Cabernet Sauvignon."

The waiter said, "Excellent choice, sir. And for dinner?"

Lucian said, "She’ll have the Saffron Salmon — seared, with forbidden black rice, charred broccolini, and a blood orange beurre blanc. I’ll have the Chilean Sea Bass with roasted fennel, fingerling potatoes, and a saffron cream sauce."

The waiter said, "Very good. I’ll be right back with your wine."

When the waiter left, Buffy shifted slightly in her seat and crossed her legs, a warm flush spreading across her collarbones, up her neck, and onto her face. She was watching Lucian with a new kind of look — softer, but also clearly affected by how attentive and in control he was being.

Buffy said, quiet and sincere, without a trace of sarcasm, "You didn’t have to do all this. The car, the chair, ordering for me… I’m not used to someone taking care of me like this. It feels… nice. Really nice."

Lucian leaned forward slightly, resting his forearms on the table, and when he spoke his voice was low and steady.

Lucian said, "I brought you here to show you what you’re fighting for. The beauty. The life that’s so fragile… and worth protecting. I wanted you to remember what it feels like to have something worth coming home to."

Buffy didn’t say anything right away. She just looked at him across the table, her expression open and vulnerable in a way she rarely allowed. The candlelight flickered softly between them as the ocean moved quietly outside the windows.

CHAPTER 8
The Dance

The waiter returned carrying two plates and set them down carefully in front of each of them.

Buffy’s plate held a seared fillet of salmon on a bed of forbidden black rice, its crispy top crust finished with sea salt and cracked black pepper, with charred broccolini glistening beside it. A glossy blood orange beurre blanc was drizzled around the plate with small purple microgreens and tiny edible yellow flowers.

Lucian’s plate held a thick Chilean sea bass fillet on a pool of saffron cream sauce, golden brown on top, roasted fennel wedges and crispy fingerling potatoes arranged around it, finished with fresh dill and cracked pepper.

They began eating and sipping the Monte Bello, deep ruby red in the glass, and talked quietly between bites about small things, with light laughter and the occasional shared look, the conversation flowing naturally as they ate.

Eventually the lights in the dining hall dimmed lower. Most of the other tables were empty by now, and their plates were nearly finished. Lucian set his fork down, wiped his mouth with the napkin, stood up, and walked around the table to offer Buffy his hand.

Lucian said softly, "Dance with me."

Buffy looked up at him, surprised but clearly touched. She placed her hand in his and stood, and he led her to the open space in the middle of the dining hall.

The overhead lights dimmed even further until the room was mostly dark, lit only by the moonlight coming through the windows and a soft silver glow from small floor lights that began to swirl slowly across the polished floor.

Soft piano began to play through the hidden speakers — haunting, steady, the kind of music that asked you to slow down.

They slow danced together in the center of the room. Buffy’s left hand rested on his back, her fingers gently gripping the fabric of his jacket, her nails lightly pressing into the material. Her head rested against his chest for a moment as they swayed.

Buffy whispered into his ear, teasing but warm, "I’m not falling asleep this time to the music."

Lucian gave a quiet chuckle, and they kept dancing slowly. After a moment, they both pulled back just enough to look at each other face to face, their eyes meeting and holding as the silver light from the floor moved gently across their faces.

They leaned in at the same time and shared a deep, slow kiss, unhurried and full of quiet emotion. Buffy melted into his arms as he held her close, one hand on her lower back, the other gently cradling the back of her head.

They stayed like that for a long moment, lost in each other. Buffy’s fingers stayed curled in the fabric of his jacket. Lucian’s hand at the back of her head was careful, like she was something he had waited a long time to hold without breaking.

Suddenly, a sharp, repetitive beeping from Lucian’s jacket pocket cut through the music.

Buffy slowly broke the kiss, her eyes still half-lidded. She glanced down toward the sound and smirked.

Buffy said quietly, with a small smile, "Saved by the beep. Figures."

Lucian exhaled, clearly annoyed at the interruption, reached into his jacket pocket, and drew out the small silver earpiece, slipping it into his right ear and turning a little away from the music to hear.

Lucian said, his voice low and firm, "Iris. You are not allowed to call me outside of working hours. You know that."

I.R.I.S. said through the earpiece in a precise, calm voice, "Protocol acknowledged. The data warranted immediate contact. An online search initiated from the occult symbols referenced in prior communications identified the Watchers Council as a relevant source. I accessed their sealed data banks through established protocols.

"Research into Buffy Anne Summers produced the following records: She attended Hemery High School in Los Angeles, California, beginning in 1995. She was expelled following the incident in which she burned down the school gymnasium while defeating a horde of vampires. Subsequent documented events in Sunnydale, including repeated encounters with vampires and other entities and the defeat of the Master, align with the Slayer profile maintained in the Council archives. Data correlation confirms Buffy Anne Summers is the Slayer."

Lucian’s eyes flicked to Buffy, and he held her gaze as he answered.

Lucian said quietly, "Yes. That’s right."

I.R.I.S. said, "Additionally, internal systems analysis has detected a significant power drain beneath the main server systems in the basement level near the hydrogen fuel cells. Enhanced security footage review has identified the Seal of Danzalthar, a symbol of the First Evil, drawn on a scientist’s notes. Current operational isolation precludes contact with external authorities."

Lucian’s expression hardened slightly as he listened.

I.R.I.S. said, "A facility-wide lockdown has been initiated. The basement power drain involves another computer system attempting to establish internet access. Brown-robed figures have been observed on multiple security cameras. They have no eyes. Blast doors around the mainframe core are being sealed."

Lucian stood very still, one hand still resting on Buffy’s waist, and his jaw tightened.

Lucian said quietly, serious now, "…Understood."

He took the earpiece out of his ear and looked at Buffy. The romantic atmosphere had shifted; the silver lights on the floor were still moving, but the mood had turned cold.

CHAPTER 9
Late Night at the Magic Box

Back in Sunnydale, the Magic Box was closed for the night. Warm amber shadows filled the space, lit only by a few scattered green-glass banker’s lamps on the counters and the pale glow of streetlights bleeding through the front windows. Half-packed cardboard boxes were stacked around the aisles and near the shelves.

A low comforting hum came from the old refrigerator in the back room, distant crickets chirped outside, and soft, rhythmic, comically steady snoring came from the front of the shop.

Dawn was helping pack up the shelves in a pink hooded sweatshirt over jeans, her hair down.

Near the research table, Anya was talking softly but animatedly to Willow and Tara. Xander was asleep on the big sofa in the front area near the coffee maker, head fallen back against the cushion, mouth wide open, marriage glow and all, snoring like the shop belonged to him.

Dawn pulled books one by one from the shelf and carefully laid them into a cardboard box on the floor, spines up and organized neatly by size. She worked with quiet focus, occasionally straightening a title or tucking a scrap of newspaper between two old leather covers so they wouldn’t rub. Every few books she glanced toward the sofa, then toward Anya’s ring catching the banker’s-lamp light, and kept packing like the ordinary night was the whole point.

Anya touched her wedding ring.

Anya said, her voice warm and genuine, "I just feel so good about it. Being married to Xander. It’s like everything finally makes sense. All that structural stability — it’s real now. I wake up next to him and the whole world just feels... settled. Like we chose this, and it’s exactly where we’re supposed to be."

Willow smiled tiredly over a mug of coffee.
