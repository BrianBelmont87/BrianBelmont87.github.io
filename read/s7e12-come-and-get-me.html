Come and Get Me

Buffy the Vampire Slayer. Season seven, episode 12. Come and Get Me.

CHAPTER 1 Rock in the River

A week after the wedding, in the middle of the morning, the Summers living room still smelled faintly of wedding cake and of fresh paint from the walls Xander had patched the morning after the wedding, where the First's last earthquake had cracked them, before he and Anya had to hurry back to the airport for his job in Texas, while the sheet of plywood Caleb had left them in place of a front door still stood across the entry, waiting its turn. Outside the birds were busy in the trees, and the wind chime on the porch rang softly whenever the air moved, and on the coffee table sat half-finished mugs of tea with rings dried into their bottoms beside Dawn's homework notebooks, lying open where she had left them.

Buffy sat on the couch in grey sweatpants and a black cut-off gym tank, barefoot, with her left leg tucked up under her and the cordless phone held to her ear against her shoulder, while her free hand traced the throw pillow beside her without her thinking about it, her thumb finding the same worn seam again and again. There was color in her cheeks, her hair was messy, and one strand had stuck at the corner of her mouth that she didn't bother to fix, and for the first morning in a long time her body felt like it belonged to her instead of to the next emergency.

Willow sat curled in the armchair with both hands wrapped around a mug of tea she wasn't drinking, a tightness sitting in her shoulders that she hadn't been able to shake all week, and Tara sat perched on the arm of the chair beside her, watching Willow the way she would watch a spell that might go wrong, not scared of her but scared for her. Dawn, home on a teacher work day with no classes, sat cross-legged on the floor at the coffee table with her loose ponytail sliding forward over one shoulder, her pencil moving through homework she kept almost finishing.

Buffy said softly, surprised, as though she were hearing herself for the first time that morning, "I feel different inside… not just two-weeks-pregnant different. Like… stable. Like a rock in the river where everything just goes around it instead of dragging me downstream."

Xander's voice came back through the phone warm and sideways, all the way from Abilene. "Well yeah, Buff. You finally picked the guy who doesn't burst into flames or try to end the world. Progress."

She laughed, a real, light, happy laugh, and the sound surprised her a little, the way a full breath surprised lungs that had been holding one in too long. Willow glanced up from her mug with a quick, soft smile, the corner of Tara's mouth tipped up, and Dawn pretended not to be eavesdropping and failed completely, her eyes fixed on her page and her ears wide open.

Buffy shifted the phone against her shoulder with the smile still in her voice and said, "Thank you for fixing the house again, by the way. After everything…"

Xander said through the phone, "No charge, Buffster. Consider it my very expensive wedding gift."

She laughed again, more softly, and the smile stayed on her face long after the sound had gone, and when they had said their goodbyes she pressed the button with her thumb to end the call and set the cordless phone down on the pillow beside her. For a moment she just sat there with both hands free in her lap, feeling for the new steadiness inside her to see whether it would leave along with Xander's voice, and it didn't. For years this house had been a place where things happened to her, graves and screaming and mornings when she couldn't feel her own hands, and now the noise in her head had somewhere to go around. She drew one slow breath through her nose and let her shoulders drop, and she didn't trust the feeling completely, but she trusted it enough to sit still.

Willow and Tara leaned down together over Dawn's homework, pages turning and their voices low and murmuring, Willow resting the end of a pencil against her bottom lip while she read, and Buffy listened to them and let the ordinary sound of it fill the room. From where she sat, Willow's shoulders still looked wrong to her, but that was Willow's fight to open when she was ready and not hers to force, and Dawn's pencil scratched once across the page and then paused.

Then, without any warning at all, the whole house lurched hard. It was nothing like the usual California shrug. It was a violent sideways yank, as though something enormous had reached up from below, grabbed the foundation in its fist and shaken it once to get their attention, and every picture on the walls jumped and rattled on its hook, the windows buzzed in their frames, and one of the mugs walked off the edge of the coffee table, sailed past the rug and shattered on the hardwood, sending ceramic skittering across the floor in every direction while the cold tea splashed out dark and spreading. Willow grabbed the arms of her chair so hard her knuckles went white, Tara reached for Dawn without even thinking and put her own body between the girl and the falling world, and Dawn ducked down under the edge of the coffee table with her homework forgotten. Buffy was on her feet before the shaking had even stopped, barefoot, the phone forgotten on the pillow behind her, already moving for the plywood across the front entry, because whatever that had been, she wanted eyes on it.

Outside, every bird in the street had gone silent at once, and the wind chime on the porch swung and settled with a few soft, uncertain notes, and then there was nothing at all.

CHAPTER 2 Come and Get Me

Buffy dragged the plywood aside and came out past it and down the steps onto the lawn just as the morning sun was swallowed whole, and it was swallowed wrong. The sky over Sunnydale darkened in one long inhale, the clouds boiling up and stacking on top of one another far too fast, rolling in from every horizon at once and turning from ash-blue to deep purple to black like a bruise spreading under skin, until the bright mid-morning was gone and the whole street lay under a sky that felt like the inside of a fist. A wind came up out of nowhere, lifting Buffy's hair off her shoulders, fluttering the thin tank against her ribs and bending the grass of every lawn on the block flat toward the street, and underneath it all came a deep, slow rumble, like a breath that didn't belong to any weather, the kind of sound that lived in the chest before it ever reached the ears.

She stopped in the middle of the lawn with her back to the house and her face to the street, her head tipped back and her eyes on the churning sky, her bare toes flexing down through the grass into the dry dirt beneath it to find their purchase. Her hands hung loose at her sides, then crossed over her chest, then fell loose again, and she wasn't fidgeting; she was deciding.

Willow called from the open front doorway behind her, her voice urgent, "Buffy… the sun…"

Buffy nodded once, slowly, her eyes still on the sky, and the wind pushed her hair across her mouth and she didn't bother to swipe it away, and a certainty settled into her like something she had never needed to put on.

Buffy said, quiet and certain, "It's starting."

Then the clouds over Revello Drive began to sculpt themselves with an unnatural precision into a face, and it filled the sky from one end of the street to the other, massive and grotesque, its hollow eyes kindling with a cold blue light that did not belong to any sun. A wide, snarling mouth carved itself out of the churning black, jagged tooth-shapes grinding and shifting along its edges as if the sky itself were chewing, and the whole enormous thing tipped its gaze down and stared straight at Buffy, standing small and barefoot on her lawn. Wherever it leaned down over the house and the three houses on every side of it, the face broke apart in the crystal's static, its jaw tearing into jittering bands of cloud and cold light that stuttered and smeared and snapped back into place, a vast picture that could no longer hold itself together over her home or see inside it. It showed its true face anyway, and the message arrived without a single word: *I see you. I'm coming.*

Buffy stood perfectly still with her arms crossed while the wind whipped her hair across her face and throat, and then, slowly, she uncrossed them and let them hang at her sides with her palms turned open toward the thing in the sky that wanted her afraid, and she didn't flinch and she didn't look away, her breathing slow and even, holding her ground.

Buffy called back over her shoulder, steady and loud enough to carry over the wind, "Everyone get inside. Get weapons."

Willow, Tara and Dawn scrambled back past the sheet of plywood propped open across the doorway, knocking it banging against the frame behind them, their footsteps scattering through the house and someone's sharp breath cutting off as they disappeared inside.

Buffy tipped her face back up to the sky, and her bare right foot found a patch of dry dirt between the grass, and she pressed her big toe down into it and drew a clear line across the lawn in front of her, slow and deliberate, a challenge and a boundary at once, and she finished it without ever looking down, the way she finished most of the things that mattered.

Buffy lifted her chin, defiant, and when she spoke her voice came out low and clear, almost conversational. "Come and get me."

The wind rose to a howl that bent the trees along the whole street as the face churned and stared down at her and then began to come apart, the jaw first and then the brow and the cheeks, until only the cold blue eyes were left, holding her one second longer than everything else, and then those went too and there was only dark, ordinary cloud where a mouth had been. The threat was delivered, and Buffy stood barefoot in the grass staring up at the place where it had been, her hair wild and her shoulders square, the toe-line dark in the dirt at her feet like a signature. Her stomach flipped once, and it was the baby and not fear, and she waited it out without putting a hand there, breathing slowly through it until the wave passed. Behind her stood the house with everybody she loved inside it, and ahead of her was whatever came next, and now the First and the Slayer both knew exactly where they stood.

Then the last of the daylight drained out of the clouds, and the sky over Sunnydale settled into a heavy storm-dark so deep it felt like dusk in the middle of the morning, the streetlights along Revello Drive flickering on one by one as though the whole town had lost track of the hour.

CHAPTER 3 Gear Up

Buffy stepped back inside, glanced once through the gap at the dark sky and dragged the sheet of plywood back into place across the doorway, then left her palm flat on the rough wood for a second, feeling the house hold around her and the storm press back against it from outside. She was barefoot and windblown, with a calm urgency in her shoulders, and a leaf had stuck to one heel, which she scraped off against the edge of the rug without looking.

Dawn came down the stairs with the gear without being asked, black cargo pants, a long-sleeved black-and-white v-neck, white socks and combat boots, and set the bundle on the couch as though she had done this before, then stepped back out of the way. Buffy laid both hands flat on the pile and took one breath, then pulled the tank off over her head and turned her bare back to the room, and the room gave her that much privacy without anyone needing to ask. She hooked her thumbs into the waistband of her sweatpants, pushed them down and stepped out of them, then tugged the cargo pants up over her hips and did up the button and the zipper, the war clothes settling into place against skin that still remembered the grass and the wind.

As she pulled the pants up, she looked across the room at Willow, who stood holding her dark-orange wooden wand with its copper inlays pointed at the floor, her knuckles pale around the wood, while Tara stood beside her with her white-oak staff resting on her shoulder, as steady as a promise. All three of them knew without saying it that Willow's magic was still damaged, its pathways under repair and not yet restored, and that here inside the crystal's zone it would come to her jagged and uneven, spiking and stalling instead of running like a river, enough for small things but a struggle for anything that needed a steady flow.

Buffy said, "Will… the plan. Remember?"

Willow nodded with her chin up, even though her throat worked once as she swallowed. "Yeah. I remember."

Tara's voice came soft and clear, and a quiet pride warmed her face as she glanced at Willow. "Willow's been repairing her magic pathways. She's way stronger now than she was when we killed the preacher."

Buffy pulled the v-neck on over her head, tucked it into the cargo pants and tugged the hem straight, and the fabric settled over her stomach without any ceremony, no pause and no flinch, and she met Tara's eyes across the room with a small nod of acknowledgment that needed no speech.

Buffy said, "Good."

She knelt on the rug and pulled on the white socks one at a time, then worked her feet into the combat boots and pulled the laces tight, yanking each double knot twice until the pressure felt right against her ankles, and then she stood up and rolled her shoulders until something clicked into place and looked over at Willow, while the room still smelled of spilled tea and the broken mug lay in pieces on the hardwood waiting to be swept up.

Buffy said, "Call Giles and let him know. Then I have someone I need to call."

Willow headed for the kitchen with the wand in her left hand, already reaching ahead of herself for the phone on the wall, while Tara stayed where she was with her eyes on Buffy, and Dawn hovered near the bottom of the stairs, caught between wanting to help and knowing the next part wasn't hers.

Buffy tugged the hem of her v-neck straight at her hip, looked out at the dark windows and then back at Tara, and for a moment the only sounds were the wind outside and Willow's low voice starting up in the kitchen, the sound of a plan beginning to move.

CHAPTER 4 Giles

In the kitchen Willow sat sideways on a stool at the island with one elbow on the counter and the receiver of the old yellow wall phone at her ear, its long coiled cord stretched across the room and looped once around her fingers the way she used to loop friendship bracelets back when worrying was simpler. Her wand lay on the counter beside her, its copper inlays catching what little light the storm hadn't eaten, and she was already on the call, her free hand tracing the edge of the Formica as though the edge might keep her grounded when her magic couldn't.

Out in the living room Buffy talked low with Tara, her hands drawing small plan-shapes in the air between them, while Dawn swung her legs once off the arm of the couch, stood up and drifted through to the dining-room table for a handful of chips. Tara folded a throw blanket with careful corners as she listened, glancing toward the kitchen once and then again, reading Willow from a distance the way she always did.

Willow kept her voice quiet and direct, her fingers tightening around the yellow cord. "It started."

Giles's voice came back across the ocean calm and unsurprised, in the Watcher's tone that had long ago learned not to waste surprise on the inevitable. "Yes. It was only a matter of time. I suspect you have prepared yourself for this?"

Willow lifted her chin a little, her voice soft but certain. "Yes… I am ready now. I wasn't before. But I am now."

For a moment there was only the faint static of the long-distance line, and then she heard Giles draw in a breath, a man choosing his next words the way he chose his weapons, carefully, knowing the wrong one could cut the wrong way.

Giles said, "You have to be careful, Willow. Your magic must not come from a place of hate, or anger… but of love for your family when you use it. That is the difference between you and what you are fighting against."

Willow's eyes shone once before she blinked it back, before it could become anything she would have to name, and her fingers traced slow loops around the yellow cord, around and around, the same slow circle her own thumb made on Tara's forearm whenever words weren't enough.

Willow said softly, almost to herself, "I know."

Giles said, "I just purchased my ticket. I'm on my way now. It'll be two days because of the layover. I'll pick up Xander and Anya in Houston before the final flight into Sunnydale."

Willow nodded at nobody in the empty kitchen. Behind her in the living room Buffy had started pacing as she talked with Tara, three steps, a turn and three steps back, her boots soft on the hardwood, Dawn stood at the dining-room table with a chip halfway to her mouth, listening without even pretending not to, and this time Tara's glance toward the kitchen held a little longer.

Willow let out a slow breath, and her voice came out quiet and tired, with hope still holding on underneath it. "Giles… I hope this is the last time."

There was a pause on the line, and then Giles sighed, warm and honest with no Watcher polish left on it at all, a man who had loved these people far too long to lie to them.

Giles said, "It's never going to be the last time for us."

Behind him she could hear the soft noise of an airport terminal, the rattle of a luggage cart and a gate call echoing somewhere in the distance, before it faded under his voice again.

Giles said, "Goodbye, Willow. I'll see you soon. Send my love to everyone."

The line clicked, and the dial tone hummed for half a second before Willow stood, reached across and hung the receiver back on its hook on the wall beside the fridge, then sat down again on the stool and stared at the counter with her fingers still resting on the yellow coils of the cord and the wand lying still beside her. The weight of what she had just promised sat in her chest like a second heartbeat, love and not hate, family and not rage, and she held on to it the way she had once been taught to hold a candle in a dark room.

In the living room Buffy stopped pacing with her arms crossed, and she, Tara and Dawn all glanced toward the kitchen at the same moment, drawn by the silence after the hang-up. Dawn said something too quiet to catch, and Buffy nodded once with her eyes still on the kitchen doorway, her right hand coming to rest briefly at her left elbow as though she were steadying something only she could feel.

Willow sat at the island with her mouth pressed into a thin line and her red-auburn hair falling forward around her face, while behind her the living room went on moving without needing her to watch it, a house full of people that kept breathing even while one of them was learning how to breathe again.

CHAPTER 5 Grounded

Buffy went through the kitchen, opened the garage door at the top of the steps and went down into the garage that served as the house's home gym, where the overhead light burned against the dim grey bars of storm light falling through the small high windows. The punching bag hung on its chain, the weight bench and a folding chair stood against the wall, and the whole room smelled of rubber mats, old metal and a little motor oil.

Buffy stood in the middle of the mats in her gear with her hair still wild from the lawn, one hand on her hip and the cordless phone at her ear, while the storm pressed against the high windows without getting in.

Buffy said, "How soon can you get here?"

Faith's voice came through raw and energized, the grin audible across all the miles between Sunnydale and Los Angeles. "Angel's gotta wait a few hours — no clouds here in L.A. But as soon as the sun goes down? We're on the way. Four hours max, he says."

Buffy said, "Good."

It came out simple and flat, with all her relief packed under that one syllable where it settled into her ribs, and after a pause Faith's voice came back softer, its edge filed down just enough to show the care underneath.

Faith said, "I'm sorry I missed the wedding… we had our own apocalypse happening here in L.A."

Buffy softened and nodded at the empty garage as though Faith could see her, then leaned her shoulder against the wall and knocked the heel of her boot once against the concrete, a small sound in the big, quiet room.

Buffy said, "It's okay. We saved a video for everyone. You know… on tape. For fun."

She was thinking of the tape Clem had shot from his chair at the end of the aisle, the old boxy camcorder propped on his loose-skinned shoulder and its little red light glowing through the vows, the whole wedding sitting safe now in its plastic case on the shelf under the living-room TV.

After a moment her voice dropped into honesty, and she glanced down at her boots and felt the word she was about to say gather in her chest before it found her mouth.

Buffy said, "I feel so… different, Faith. I feel… I don't know. Grounded. Not scared at all. I wonder if that's a good thing?"

The word sat differently than it had on the couch with Xander, less a speech than a discovery she was still turning over in her hands, and she didn't know yet whether it made her safer or only harder to move when she needed to run. It was the same body that had died and come back, with a different weight inside it now, and she glanced at the hanging bag, at the bench, at the grey light through the high windows, and waited for Faith to tell her what that weight meant.

After a pause Faith said, direct but not unkind, "Grounded ain't the same as soft, B. Means you finally stopped waiting for the next hit to knock you down and decided to plant your feet instead. Scared was never really your color anyway."

Buffy let out a small breath that was almost a laugh, the kind that hurt a little because it was true and because someone who knew her blood had said it out loud, and she pushed off the wall and paced a slow circle around the mats with the cordless phone at her ear and her left hand finding her hip again, and the punching bag swayed the smallest bit in the air she moved through.

Buffy said, "Tell Angel thanks for everything."

Faith said, "I will. I'll see you soon, B."

Buffy ended the call with her thumb and stood looking down at the phone for a second, the white plastic warm from her palm and Faith's voice still echoing in the quiet like heat leaving metal, then tossed it onto the folding chair beside the weight bench with a soft clatter and stood with both hands on her hips, looking at nothing, feeling the grounded thing hold. Then she turned, climbed the steps and walked back into the kitchen, calm and purposeful, leaving the garage door ajar behind her, and in the empty garage the punching bag swayed in the draft she left and its chain ticked once against itself.

CHAPTER 6 Black Hawk

Outside, the storm clouds hung low and heavy over the roof, and the cool, dim light pressed down on the whole street when the rotor wash of a Black Hawk helicopter hit the front yard before anyone could even see it, an aggressive pounding gale of air that shook every window in its frame and rattled the porch chimes into a panicked chatter. The grass of the lawn flattened in rolling waves, and somewhere down the block a plastic trash can lid went skittering across the asphalt toward the street until it fetched up against the curb.

Then the helicopter itself came down out of the cloud, a dark shape against the darker sky, its side door already sliding open while it was still in the air, and inside crouched four soldiers in black tactical gear with helmets, full-face masks and sleek black prototype rifles. It dropped low over the front lawn until its wheels nearly kissed the grass, and the lead soldier hand-signaled once, and all four of them jumped down and ran for the front entry in practiced, low postures, their boots hitting the lawn, then the walkway, then the porch steps. Behind them the Black Hawk lifted away, banked hard toward the far side of the storm and disappeared into the cloud, and the rotor wash died back down into ordinary wind, and the silence it left behind felt heavier than all the noise.

Inside, Buffy had just come back through the kitchen from the garage and was crossing toward the stairs when the sound hit, and she stopped in mid-step, looked up at the ceiling as though it had personally offended her, and rolled her eyes hard enough that Dawn would have been proud. Tara froze in mid-reach with a plate over the dining-room table, Willow stood in the kitchen doorway with her wand still in her left hand, and Dawn rose from where she sat on the stairs with one socked foot hovering over the next step.

Buffy said, flat and annoyed, "What now?"

Three sharp, professional knocks sounded on the sheet of plywood that stood in for the front door.

Buffy dragged it aside with one eyebrow raised, then folded her arms and stared at the four masked soldiers standing on her porch, while behind her Tara, Willow and Dawn watched from different corners of the living room, bracing for the next wrong thing the way everyone in this house had learned to brace.

The lead soldier pulled off his helmet and mask in one smooth motion, and it was Riley Finn, older and harder than she remembered him, his hair cut shorter, new lines at the corners of his serious, direct eyes, in full black tactical gear with a vest and his black gloves still on, sweat beading at his hairline where the helmet had been.

Riley said, serious and direct, "We need to talk."

Buffy kept her arms crossed and Riley stayed tense on the doormat with the other three masked soldiers behind him and the storm clouds looming low over the roofline, while inside the family watched, having seen too many visitors like this to like how it usually ended.

One of the soldiers behind him spoke in a muffled, electronic female voice through the filter of her helmet. "Site secure, sir?"

As she spoke she turned her masked head slightly toward Buffy, curiosity leaking through even the mask and the filter in the tilt of someone already taking measurements.

Riley turned to his team with a calm authority, the old field voice still intact under the years, and said, "Site secure."

The soldier pulled off her helmet and mask, clipped them to her tactical belt and ran a hand back through her hair. She stood a good eleven inches taller than Buffy, with a strong jaw and an open curiosity on her face as she looked down, her eyes taking inventory of everything in front of her: the Slayer, the doorway, the house and the storm.

Sam said, casual and blunt, "I thought she would be bigger. Stronger looking."

Riley cut her off with a sharp stop gesture of one gloved hand without even looking at her. "Not now, soldier."

Sam straightened into a professional stance, her spine finding the line. "Yes, sir."

Buffy took all of it in with disbelief and annoyance and old history flickering behind her eyes like film catching the light, her arms still crossed and one boot shifting its weight on the threshold.

Buffy said, "What the hell are you doing here? And why now?"

Riley met her eyes, the tired, straightforward Riley rather than the polished officer, with no smile and no apology for dropping a Black Hawk onto her lawn as though the world owed him a runway.

Riley said, "The sensors at command started going off like crazy. We knew something big was happening here. We've been waiting for this."

Buffy let out a short, humorless breath and stepped back with a sarcastic flourish, sweeping one arm across the doorway like a maître d' with a death wish and far too much practice.

Buffy said, "Well. Come on in then. Tea? Crumpets? I'll even dig out the tea cozy if it makes this feel more official."

She turned and walked back into the house without waiting for them, leaving the plywood leaned open against the wall, her boots loud on the hardwood as she passed the broken mug still lying where it had shattered that morning, its tea stain darkening into the grain.

Riley watched her go for a second, the old ache of knowing her and not knowing her anymore sitting quietly in his chest, then turned to his team with a short nod.

Riley said, "Stay out here. Perimeter watch. Eyes on the sky."

The other two masked soldiers fanned out without a word, one toward the side yard and the other toward the edge of the street, and Riley stepped inside with Sam and set the plywood back across the doorway behind them, leaving the two outside on the perimeter with their eyes on the sky and the storm watching back.

Riley stopped a few steps inside the entryway with his helmet tucked under one arm, Buffy waited ahead of him in the living room with her arms crossed, and the only sound was the faint rumble of the storm outside, while Sam's height made the ceiling feel lower, the way tall people rearranged a room just by standing in it.

CHAPTER 7 Worth Fighting For

In the living room Buffy stood facing Riley and Sam, with Tara and Willow close by, while Dawn sat on the stairs gripping the banister so hard her fingers went white, as though the wood might keep the next thing from happening.

Buffy said, flat and direct, "Who's that?"

Riley gave a small backward nod toward Sam. "That's my wife, Buffy. Samantha. Or Sam. Rank Specialist under my command."

Buffy gave Sam a cool, polite nod, a tip of the chin with her eyes measuring height and tone and marriage all at once and filing it all away where Slayers filed the things that might matter later.

Buffy said, "Nice to meet you."

Sam gave no reaction at all, her professional face set like stone as she looked down at Buffy with that same curiosity, as though Buffy were a briefing she hadn't finished reading and wasn't sure she liked the summary of.

Buffy dropped onto the couch, Slayer-ready, with her boots planted and her elbows on her knees for half a second before she sat back straight, her body remembering how to wait without looking like it was waiting. Tara came in from the kitchen carrying a tray with a pitcher of orange juice, six glasses and a plate of cookies, in a cream apron smudged with flour and taco sauce, calm and deliberate and warm and not at all fazed by the armed soldiers in her living room, and she smiled at her new guests the way she smiled at anyone who walked into this house needing something.

Tara said with a soft, genuine hostess's smile, "Hello guests. Welcome to our home. Please enjoy."

She set the tray down on the coffee table with a soft clink, the pitcher already sweating cold against the wood, and stepped back without hovering, her hostess grace intact under the apocalypse weather.

Riley said, relieved, "At ease."

He said it to Sam, but it sounded as though he were saying it to himself as well.

Sam sat down in the chair across from Buffy, filled a glass to the brim with orange juice and drank the whole thing in one long, thirsty go, her throat working without a pause, the drink of someone who had been running on worse for far too long, then wiped her mouth with the back of her hand and stared at the empty glass, impressed, as though the orange juice had just made a better argument than any briefing.

Sam said, deadpan and sincere, "Now that is worth fighting for…"

Riley sighed and sank into the armchair with his hands on his knees and one leg bouncing, an old tell he had never managed to kill no matter how much command had settled on him, while Tara went back through to the dining-room table, Willow edged a little closer, and Dawn's eyes went wide on the stairs as she soaked up every detail for later the way she always had.

Buffy settled back, sensing something moving under the storm, and glanced at the windows, where the dark mass of cloud showed no face this time, only pressure, and her hand flexed once on her knee.

Buffy kept her eyes on the glass, her voice quiet and certain. "It's going to send something soon… Probably after the sun goes down… I can feel it."

Riley met her eyes with an old understanding that needed no Initiative to translate it, the kind that lived in shared history and bad nights and surviving them.

Riley said, "Yeah… probably. That is one reason why we are here now."

He smiled at her then, a little warmth coming into his eyes, the years between them not quite erasing what they had been through together.

Riley said, "We— I wouldn't miss the end game. You know that."

Buffy pushed herself up off the couch, and her hand went to her stomach on instinct for a split second before she caught herself and curled her fingers away before anyone could name it. She had gone a little pale, and she swallowed hard with her mouth pressed tight as the wave came up through her like heat through a grate.

Buffy said, quiet and strained, trying to sound normal, "Excuse me…"

She walked quickly and steadily down the hall to the downstairs bathroom and shut the door with a soft click of the latch, and the rest of them stayed exactly where they were while the sounds came through the wall: the muffled sounds of her being sick, a cough, then the flush and the tap running. It was the ordinary, ugly sound of a human body in a house that had heard far worse and still flinched, a Slayer on her knees with her throat burning while soldiers drank orange juice in the next room. Sam's eyebrow twitched once, and Dawn looked down at her feet.

Riley watched Buffy go, then turned his head and looked directly at Willow, and Willow tried to look away and failed. He stood, stepped around the coffee table and knelt down in front of her so that his eyes were level with hers, his black tactical gloves still on, and Willow kept her head down for a long moment before she slowly lifted it and found she couldn't look away, her green eyes huge and the silence between them full of everything Buffy hadn't said.

Riley said, quiet and steady, with no judgment in it, "I know all about her special body, Willow… she doesn't ever get sick."

His right gloved hand settled on Willow's left forearm, grounding rather than threatening, the pressure careful and the leather warm from his palm, and Willow tried to look away again and forced herself to keep looking at him anyway, the way a person forced herself to stand in a doorway she had been avoiding. Her old high-school self came out all at once, unable to look away and unable to hold it in, and his direct, point-blank stare shattered the last of her cover, and she bit her bottom lip with her eyes searching his face and her brows furrowed hard enough to hurt.

Willow said, squeaky, almost in a whisper, "She's pregnant, Riley…"

Riley drew in one sharp breath and his whole body locked, and then he fell backward off his knees and landed hard on his ass on the carpet with his legs splayed out in front of him, staring dazed and wide-eyed up at Willow, his vest creaking and one gloved hand still half raised as though he had forgotten what hands were for.

Riley said, soft and stunned, "Who…?"

Willow said, small but clearer now, and protective, "It's Lucian's. And Buffy's. She got married last week."

Riley took a long, deep breath in through his nose and let it out slowly, and a tiny, disbelieving laugh escaped him, shock rather than mockery, while one of his boots scuffed the carpet. Sam watched without comment with her empty glass in her hand, filing away every syllable the way specialists filed the things that changed the mission.

Riley said, quiet, almost to himself, "Wow…"

He didn't get up. He shifted back until he was sitting on the carpet with his shoulders against the front of the couch and his forearms on his knees, and the facts stacked up in his head without finding a shelf that fit them, married last week and pregnant and Lucian's, and when the old history with Buffy tried to stand up inside him, he pushed it back down, because it wasn't his place anymore. Willow watched him as though she might have to catch him again, Tara had gone still at the dining-room table with a plate forgotten in her hands, and on the stairs Dawn's mouth had fallen slightly open until she noticed and closed it, while outside one of the perimeter soldiers shifted his weight and a porch board creaked under him, and the house held all of them in that strange quiet that came after a truth had landed.

CHAPTER 8 Fight From Love

In the kitchen the sink water ran in a steady ribbon and steam rose off everything Tara had cooked, and the room was a controlled disaster, dishes piled in the sink, pans crowding the stove, cutting boards sticky on the island and spice jars standing open, while through the doorway half the dining-room table now held a massive spread of tacos and burritos with steam curling up off them, as though the house itself were trying to feed everyone before the dark arrived.

Tara sank onto one of the stools at the island in her apron, wiped the sweat from her brow with the back of her wrist and let out a long, deep sigh that emptied her for a second, the kind of empty that came after giving a room everything it asked for.

Tara pushed a damp strand of hair off her forehead, tired and satisfied, her voice soft. "Well… when everyone gets here… at least they won't be hungry now."

She turned on the stool toward the sink, where Willow stood quietly washing the dishes without a word, soap bubbles clinging to her knuckles and popping one by one in tiny, soft sounds under the rush of the faucet.

Tara said quietly, "I didn't expect him here either… Riley."

From the living room came muffled voices and a small burst of laughter from Riley and Sam, the kind that tried a little too hard and still counted as human, with Buffy's quieter reply underneath it.

Willow reached forward with her left hand and turned off the faucet, pressing the thin metal handle down until the water stopped, and stayed facing the sink a moment longer, her green eyes following the last drip as it gathered on the spout and fell, as though it might tell her something useful.

She turned slowly to face Tara with the stress and worry lying open on her face, no spell to hide behind and no clever line, just Willow afraid of herself, the water still dripping from her fingers onto the linoleum in small dark spots and her eyes wet but not crying yet, and she bit gently at her right thumb near the nail before dropping the hand back to her side, a small nervous habit that had survived every version of her.

Willow said, quiet and scared, "I don't know if I can do it, Tara… not go to the dark place when it starts. I mean… when it really starts. You know what I mean?"

Tara's face changed as she listened, worry and love opening in it at the same time, and her lips parted as her eyes searched Willow's face and read the fear there without looking away, the way she had always read Willow, carefully and completely and without flinching from anything she found, and then she drew a slow breath through her nose, her shoulders dropping as though she were taking some of the weight onto herself, and stood up from the stool without any hesitation at all.

She crossed the kitchen and pulled Willow into a full, tight hug that answered her before any words could, her right hand cradling the back of Willow's head with her fingers sliding into the red-auburn hair and her left arm wrapping around Willow's waist to draw her in, and she pressed Willow's face softly against her chest, rested her cheek on top of Willow's head and left a slow, lingering kiss there, the kind of kiss that told her she didn't have to hold any of this alone. Willow's eyes squeezed shut against the front of Tara's apron, and a small shudder moved through her shoulders and slowly settled.

Tara's voice was soft and warm against Willow's hair, steady, with a gentle conviction in every word. "Baby… if you can hold onto this feeling right here — how safe you are, how loved you are — when it all starts… you won't go to that dark place. You'll fight from love. And that's how we win. You hear me, my girl?"

She rocked Willow gently without letting go, her right hand still stroking slowly through her hair, while the faucet dripped once into the piled dishes and laughter drifted in again from the living room, Riley's low voice and Sam's blunt reply, ordinary sounds carrying through the doorway while the two of them stood holding each other in the kitchen and breathing together.

Willow's fingers curled into the front of Tara's apron and held on, and she breathed against Tara's chest and let the dark place wait outside the hug for one more minute, letting love be louder than the thing she was afraid of becoming, while Tara kept her cheek on Willow's head with her eyes closed and her mouth soft against the red-auburn hair, as though the answer could be pressed into Willow's skin and stay there when the fighting started.

CHAPTER 9 William

A sharp knock came on the plywood across the front entry, harder than Riley's had been, less military and more urgent, and everyone in the house looked toward it at once while Dawn's feet stopped dead on the stairs, the whole house holding its breath the way it always did when a knock might mean help or something worse.

Buffy hauled the plywood aside, and Lucian stepped forward still in his work clothes, cupped her face in both hands and kissed her hard and deep right there in the doorway, passionate and relieved and desperate, as though he had been holding his breath the whole way to her, the healing wound on his left side quiet under his shirt. Buffy melted into it with her hands on his chest and her fingers fisting in the fabric, and then pulled back just far enough to breathe, her forehead nearly touching his and a smirk already forming through the nausea she refused to let the First have the satisfaction of owning.

Buffy was still a little breathless, a tired smirk tugging at the corner of her mouth. "Sorry… morning sickness there."

Lucian gave a short, warm laugh with his forehead still close to hers, his thumb brushing gently along her jaw, a husband's warmth and quiet attention in the same small gesture.

Lucian said, "It's been one of those days… hasn't it?"

Behind Lucian, on the porch under the storm-dark sky that had swallowed the sun, Spike stood just outside the doorframe in his long black coat, having ridden over hunched low across the back seat of the Trans Am with the storm-dark keeping the sun off him, his right hand tracing the invisible barrier he could not cross, one eyebrow raised and a tiny smirk on his face as he watched the kiss like a man who had learned to watch without claiming. Buffy looked past Lucian and held her eyes on Spike for a long, quiet moment, and something complicated and clean moved behind them, gratitude and grief and need, with no claim attached to any of it.

Buffy said, quiet and clear, putting the weight on his human name, "William… you may come inside."

Spike's hand dropped, and he stepped over the threshold with a small nod of thanks, and then he caught sight of Riley still sitting on the carpet with his back against the couch, and a slow, crooked grin bloomed across his face as though he had been waiting years to use it on exactly this picture.

Spike said, "Well, well. Look what the wind blew in. Initiative's finest, sitting on his arse in the middle of the living room. Didn't expect to see you again, Captain Cardboard."

Riley got up off the floor and brushed himself off, carpet lint and dignity both, tired rather than hostile, his vest still sitting crooked from the fall.

Riley said, "Hello again, Hostile 17… I mean — Spike."

Spike smirked, gave a lazy nod and dropped onto the couch across from Sam's chair as though he had done it a hundred times before in a hundred different rooms, glanced at her, then at Riley, then back at Sam with one eyebrow raised, and propped his boots up just far enough to be rude without committing to it.

Spike said, "Don't worry, love. I don't bite… much. Unless you ask nicely."

Sam didn't even blink, evaluating the vampire with a blunt curiosity, like a piece of kit she had been briefed on and found shorter than the file suggested.

Sam said, "You're shorter than I expected."

Spike gave a short laugh and leaned back with one arm along the top of the couch, the grief and hunger and self-control tucked under the joke where they always lived, because watching Buffy lean into her husband was its own kind of fight, the kind you only won by never starting it.

Spike said, "Yeah, well. Most people are disappointed the first time they meet me. Gets better after the third or fourth apocalypse."

Lucian's arm settled loosely around Buffy's waist with his hand resting at her hip and a quiet amusement in the set of his mouth, and he glanced at Buffy and then at Spike, having already done the math on needing Spike long before Buffy said it out loud.

Lucian said, "I had to pick him up from downtown on my way over here. He was waving… and everything."

Buffy leaned into Lucian's side, and her hand found his at her waist and stayed there, warm over his.

Buffy said, "It's okay. We need him."

Lucian gave her a small, knowing smile, the kind a husband gave when he had already finished the equation.

Lucian said, "Yeah. I figured you might think that."

The living room filled up around them, Spike lounging on the couch, Riley still brushing the carpet off his vest, Buffy and Lucian standing in the light from the doorway, Tara at the dining-room table with a plate she still hadn't set down, Willow watching from the kitchen doorway with her hands still wet from the dishes, and Dawn on the stairs with both hands on the banister, while outside the two masked soldiers kept their watch on the sky and the storm went on pressing at the windows of a house gathering its people before the dark finished arriving.

CHAPTER 10 Magic Bomb

On the couch, Spike turned his face toward Lucian, not angry and not jealous but quieter than that, older and tired, with a gentle melancholy that had nowhere left to go, and then his gaze moved past Lucian and came to rest on Buffy, and something old and unfinished settled into the set of his mouth and stayed there without asking anyone to notice.

At the dining-room table Tara straightened the plates around the spread, Willow stood in the kitchen doorway with her wand hand restless at her side, and Dawn sat half hidden on the stairs with her eyes on everyone, learning the shape of a war by watching the people who would fight it.

For several seconds Spike watched Buffy and Lucian standing close together, her body angled into his and his hand resting on the small of her back, their voices low enough that they didn't need the room, and a small, almost inaudible sigh left him. He closed his eyes, tipped his head back against the top of the couch and folded his arms across his chest, shutting the world out for a moment and choosing not to reach for what wasn't his anymore, and from her chair Sam glanced at him once, filed the melancholy away and said nothing.

Riley reached over for the remote and switched on the TV, and with a soft electronic pop the picture came up and filled the room with somewhere else: a sharply dressed African American reporter in a crisp red-and-white dress, her hair in a neat updo, with dark lipstick and eyeshadow, standing in front of a burning building under the black storm sky, its ugly orange light flaring behind her while arcs of water from the fire hoses fought a fire that didn't want to die, and across the bottom of the screen ran the words DOWNTOWN SUNNYDALE — MAGIC BOX BOOKSTORE ENGULFED IN FLAMES.

The reporter said, urgent and professional, "…firefighters are still battling the blaze. Witnesses report that five hooded figures were seen running from the building moments before the fire started. Police have not yet located the suspects. Again, the Magic Box bookstore on Main Street is currently burning out of control…"

Spike kept his eyes closed on the couch, choosing not to watch a place that had been so many things to so many of them burn, while Riley and Sam leaned forward, already counting hoods, and Willow stared at the screen with the color draining from her face. Tara froze in mid-motion with a plate still in her hands, watching the shop she had kept for Anya burn, Dawn stood all the way up on the stairs, and Lucian's hand found Buffy's waist again without his even looking, an anchor offered without any ceremony.

Buffy slowly crossed her arms over her chest and stepped away from Lucian's side, with a quiet command in the way she planted her boots on her own floor.

Buffy's voice came out quiet and certain, without a tremor in it. "We knew they were going to do that."

She looked around at all of them, Riley, Sam, Spike, Willow, Tara, Dawn and Lucian, counting them without counting, feeling the weight of each one of them in the room like stones in a riverbed.

Buffy said, "So all the books, items we need for this? They're in the garage. We moved them last week."

Willow nodded from near the kitchen doorway, small and fierce and scared, the nod of someone who had already done the hard thing and was still standing.

Willow said, "Yeah. We also got all the dangerous stuff too… ahem… in the garage now."

Riley's brow furrowed, and his vest shifted as he leaned in.

Riley said, "Dangerous stuff?"

Willow stepped further into the living room and met his eyes, her voice low and clear and without the squeak this time, grown into the truth she was about to name.

Willow said, "Some of the older scrolls. If they were burned like that… they'd release magic energy with no specific intent. No control."

Riley worked out the worst case aloud, finding the shape of the blast before anyone else could pretty it up.

Riley said, "That would create a magic bomb?"

Willow nodded once, still meeting his eyes, and Tara set her plate down very carefully on the table, as though any noise might tip over something that was already leaning.

Willow said, "Yes. It would."

Nobody moved for a long moment after that: Spike sat with his eyes closed and his arms folded, Buffy stood in the center of the room with her arms crossed and Lucian behind her, Riley and Sam stayed locked on Willow, Tara watched Buffy with a quiet intensity, and Willow stood between the kitchen and the living room with the weight of what she had said settling into the floorboards, while behind them all the TV flickered with the burning Magic Box and washed the walls in an orange light this house had never asked for.

CHAPTER 11 The Roar

Well after nightfall the lamps were on all through the Summers house and the windows had gone from storm-dark to truly black. Riley and Sam stood near the front windows checking their gear, a magazine check and a tug on each strap, with quiet professional mutters that filled the silence without pretending to comfort anyone, while Spike slouched on the couch with his coat open and his eyes half-lidded but awake. Lucian stood at the kitchen island with Tara and Willow with one hand resting on the counter's edge and the food still warm beside them, Dawn sat on the bottom step of the stairs in her socks and her nerves, and outside in the dark the two masked soldiers kept their watch on the perimeter, their eyes on a sky that had already shown them a face once today.

Buffy stood near the front entry with her arms crossed, waiting, and when the knock came she had the plywood dragged aside before the sound had even finished, her body moving ahead of her thoughts the way it always did when the next thing arrived.

Under the porch light stood Angel and Faith, road-worn and back from L.A., and behind them at the curb sat Angel's black 1967 Plymouth GTX with its hood still buckled up in a V, its windshield gone and one headlight dead, which was why Faith's hair was wind-messed from the road and a film of road dust lay across Angel's shoulders. Faith had a fresh cut above her eyebrow that was still shiny at the edges, a dark streak of dried blood down her jacket and the faint smell of burgers on her, with a hungry edge still live in the set of her mouth, while Angel stood quiet and steady in his long coat with that familiar broody stillness that somehow counted as a greeting, and whatever they had left unfinished in L.A. traveled with them in the quiet between them.

Buffy said, dry and unimpressed, "Took your time."

Faith gave a short, tired laugh and shrugged one shoulder, as though the miles were a joke she had already told herself.

Faith said, "We stopped for burgers. You know me… I'm always hungry, horny, and ready to get down."

She punched her left fist into the open palm of her right hand with a sharp, meaty smack, her grin flashing and the cut above her eyebrow pulling when she smiled.

Buffy rolled her eyes hard, a long, exhausted sigh leaving her like steam, and shifted her gaze to Angel, calm and silent and slightly broody, the man who could make a single word sound like a weather report and a confession at once.

Angel said, "Traffic."

Buffy stared at him for a second while the smallest hint of a smile found the corner of her mouth before she could stop it, and then she stepped back and pulled the plywood wider.

Buffy said, "Come on. Get inside before something else shows up."

Angel and Faith stepped inside, and Buffy set the plywood back across the doorway behind them and shoved it tight into the frame with a solid, final thud, her hand lingering flat on the rough wood a breath longer than it needed to. Behind her the room came alive, Riley turning from the window in the middle of his gear check, Spike sitting up with a rustle of his coat, Tara coming forward from the kitchen with a quiet welcome in her face and Willow's hand finding hers for a second on the way, and Dawn rising half a step on the stairs, while Lucian's eyes found Angel for one long, measuring moment and then softened.

For a moment there was a brief, strange relief in the room, low voices and the echo of the plywood settling into its frame, and then a deep, guttural roar came rolling down the street, not one voice but several layered together, low and wet and hungry, and it rattled the windows in their frames, distant but not nearly far enough, the kind of sound that found the marrow before it ever found the ear, and everyone in the house froze exactly where they stood.

Buffy stayed at the entry with her palm still flat on the plywood and didn't turn around. She closed her eyes for a second, let out a breath through her nose and rested her forehead against the cool, rough wood with her boots planted on her own floor, married barely a week, pregnant, grounded and waiting, with every person she had left standing in the house behind her. She had drawn her line on the lawn that morning and the First had shown her its face, and now the street was answering back.

The roar echoed again, closer.
