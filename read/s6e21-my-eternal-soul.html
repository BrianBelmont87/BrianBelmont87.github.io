My Eternal Soul

Download this script (.txt)

Buffy the Vampire Slayer. Season six, episode 21. My Eternal Soul.

CHAPTER 1 Arrival

Outside an African coastal harbor, the last sliver of sun had just slipped below the horizon. The sky ran deep indigo into purple at the edges, and the final gold skimmed across the dark harbor water in long shimmering lines toward the docks. Sodium lights began to flicker on along the waterfront. The air sat heavy and humid on the skin.

A large weathered cargo ship glided into the harbor, hull dark and salt-stained. The only sounds were the low rhythmic chug of the engines and the gentle slap of water against the pilings.

Inside a large reinforced wooden shipping crate in its hold, it was pitch black except for two faint pinpricks of yellow light: Spike’s eyes. He sat with his back against one wall, knees drawn up, long black coat pulled tight around him, perfectly still, while the crate swayed gently with the ship and a single bead of condensation rolled down the inside wall.

Spike said, very low, almost to himself, "Almost there, love…"

His eyes closed, and the yellow glow faded.

The ship docked with a heavy metallic groan and the sound of thick ropes being thrown and caught. Dockworkers moved with practiced efficiency under the growing harbor lights.

A large yellow crane arm swung slowly over the deck. Heavy chains and a hook descended toward a single large wooden shipping crate marked with faded stencils and international shipping labels. The crate was bigger than a standard coffin but not by much — clearly built to hold something, or someone, that needed to travel in complete darkness.

The crane hook locked onto the crate and lifted it off the deck, swaying slightly on the chains, then the arm swung out over the dock and lowered it with deliberate care until it touched down on the concrete with a solid, heavy thud of wood and metal that echoed across the water.

Three dockworkers in tank tops and shorts approached the crate, one older, with graying hair, another younger, maybe in his early twenties. They spoke in a mix of English and a local language, voices carrying in the humid evening air.

The older dockworker pointed at the crate, wiping sweat from his brow with the back of his arm, and said, "This one came on the afternoon manifest. Sealed tight. No air holes. Who the hell ships something like this during the day?"

The second dockworker circled it, suspicious, and said, "No name on the manifest. Just a number. And it’s heavy. Too heavy for normal cargo."

The third knocked on the side with his knuckles and said, "Maybe it’s a body. Or something that bites."

They laughed, but uneasily, and the older worker crouched and ran his hand along one of the reinforced metal bands.

The older dockworker said, "We should open it. See what’s inside before we sign for it."

The younger one reached for a crowbar leaning against a nearby pallet.

Inside the crate, Spike’s eyes snapped open, the yellow glow returning brighter now, and he shifted his weight, bracing his shoulder against the inside wall.

Outside, the younger dockworker stepped forward with the crowbar, wedging the tip under one of the lid slats.

Then, suddenly, something moved inside the crate. The whole crate jolted once, hard enough to scrape an inch across the concrete, and all three men froze where they stood.

Before anyone could react, a fist exploded outward through the top of the crate in a violent burst of splintered wood and bent metal, wood shards and metal fragments flying, the harbor light catching the debris.

The fist was pale, scarred, and powerful. It opened, fingers spreading, then gripped the edge of the hole it had just made.

Spike rose from the crate like something being born in reverse. He pushed the broken lid aside with both hands, the long black leather duster rumpled and salt-stained from the voyage, leather creaking as he climbed out in one fluid predatory motion. Under the coat he wore a black button-down open at the throat, dark trousers and scuffed boots, the familiar silver ring on one hand. His bleached hair was messy and damp at the roots, his skin pale with a faint sheen of sea damp on his neck and collarbones, and his face sat in that familiar mix of exhaustion, determination, and barely contained rage. He stood on the dock and rolled his neck once, a man shaking off a long confinement, and the coat flared when he moved.

The three dockworkers stared, frozen. One of them took an instinctive step back.

Spike looked at them, his eyes still faintly yellow for half a second before fading to blue. He didn’t speak; he simply tilted his head slightly, a silent warning.

The younger one slowly lowered the crowbar, then dropped it entirely. All three backed away without a word.

Spike turned his head toward the dark horizon beyond the harbor lights, inland, toward the cave. He took one slow breath through his nose, then started walking, coat flaring behind him.

He moved away from the crate and the stunned dockworkers, becoming a lone figure walking into the deepening night until he disappeared into the dark.

CHAPTER 2 The Butcher

Spike walked along the dock with purpose, his coat flaring slightly with each stride.

The harbor lights thinned out. He turned off the main dock road and headed toward a narrow side street that opened into a small night market and shopping row tucked at the end of the docks. One minute he was in the industrial harbor, the next he was in a more lived-in humid pocket of life.

Warm low light came from hanging lanterns and bare bulbs strung between wooden poles. The market was still active even at this hour, a narrow row of small shops and open stalls, with white cloths and faded cotton awnings hung from rough wooden sticks and poles to make soft, billowing dividers between vendors. Some of the cloths were stained from years of use, and others fluttered gently in the night breeze. The air smelled of raw meat, spices, charcoal, and the sea.

Near the end of the row sat a small open-front butcher shop. A metal counter ran across the front, and behind it large cuts of meat hung from hooks under a single bare bulb while flies buzzed lazily around the display. A handwritten sign in English and another language listed prices. The owner, a man in his forties in a stained apron over a simple shirt, was wiping down the counter with a rag.

Spike stopped in front of the counter, his face completely human, no ridges, no yellow eyes. He looked tired but calm as he rested one hand on the metal counter.

Spike said, low and even, with his dry British edge, trying to sound reasonable, "I need cow’s blood. Fresh as you’ve got. A liter if you can manage it."

The butcher looked him over. He kept both hands busy with the rag for a second, then reached up and touched a small dark necklace hanging around his neck, his fingers worrying the beads in a small, unconscious motion, and shook his head.

The butcher said, "No blood tonight. Comes in the morning. You come back then."

Spike said, still calm, but with a thread of impatience underneath, "I can’t wait till morning, mate. I need it now."

The butcher’s eyes flicked to Spike’s face, then down to his coat, and he kept rubbing the necklace between his fingers, his posture stiffening slightly.

The butcher said, "I told you. Morning. You want meat, I got meat. No blood."

Spike’s head tilted a fraction, and at first his expression didn’t change. Then, very suddenly, his face shifted.

The familiar ridges rose across his brow and cheeks, his eyes flashed yellow, and his fangs dropped, all in one sharp, practiced movement, no slow build, just the switch flipping.

The butcher’s eyes went wide, and he took a quick step back from the counter, the necklace now gripped tightly in his fist, his mouth opening without a sound at first.

Spike said, low and dangerous, almost conversational, as if he were discussing the weather, "Listen carefully. If you don’t feed me right now… I’m going to have to go find your family instead. And I’m not nearly as polite when I’m this hungry."

His eyes were unblinking, his head tilted a fraction like a bird deciding whether to strike, and the yellow in them held steady. A single muscle jumped once in his cheek when the butcher hesitated, but he didn’t raise his voice. The stillness was the threat.

The butcher stared at him for two full seconds, breathing fast. Then he turned quickly, disappeared into the back for a moment, and returned with a large metal cup. He poured dark thick cow’s blood from a covered container into the cup with shaking hands and set it on the counter without looking directly at Spike again.

The butcher said in a quiet, tight voice, "Take it. Go."

Spike’s face shifted back to human in an instant, and he picked up the cup, gave the butcher a small, almost polite nod, and turned away without another word.

He walked away from the stall, cup in hand, back through the narrow market row, the hanging white cloths fluttering softly as he passed, and lifted the cup to drink while he walked, not gulping desperately but at a steady, almost casual pace. A thin line of blood ran from the corner of his mouth down his chin, and he didn’t wipe it away.

His posture changed as he drank, the tired, tightly wound energy from the crate and the dock softening into something closer to his usual arrogant strut. By the time he reached the end of the shopping row, he was moving with that signature Spike swagger again — coat flaring, chin up, cup still in hand like it was a pint at the Bronze.

He kept walking toward the edge of the market, where the darker inland road began that would eventually lead to the cave.

CHAPTER 3 The Trolley

Spike walked out of the narrow market row, still holding the metal cup. He drained the last of the cow’s blood, wiped his mouth with the back of his hand, and tossed the empty cup into a barrel as he passed. The swagger was back in full now, chin up, that familiar predatory grace in every step.

Just beyond the last hanging white cloths and wooden poles of the market, a small cluster of people stood near a sturdy wooden trolley. It was a hand-pulled cart with a long bench seat on top for passengers and a hidden lower compartment underneath, used for cargo or discreet transport. The trolley owner, a tall, broad-shouldered Black man in his late thirties wearing a simple shirt and trousers, was checking the wheels and straps. A couple of locals lingered nearby, waiting for a ride.

Spike approached without hesitation, stopped in front of the trolley owner, and pulled a thick rolled wad of cash from inside his coat, holding it out.

Spike said, low and direct, without small talk, "I need to go inland. Far. You know the place I’m talking about."

He stood square, coat hanging salt-stained from his frame, one hand loose at his side, the other offering the cash without flourish. His weight was on the balls of his feet, ready to move but not posturing. The swagger was banked down to pure purpose. There was no smile and no threat in his body yet, just the eyes, flat and certain.

The trolley owner looked at the money, then slowly lifted his eyes to Spike’s face. He stared at him for a long, heavy moment, unblinking, assessing. The night market sounds continued around them, distant voices, cloth flapping, a radio playing somewhere, but this moment felt still.

The trolley owner said, deep and calm but with a clear warning underneath, "No one goes to that place and comes back. I can take you. But I will not wait. I will leave you there. You will have to get back to town on your own. Do you understand this?"

Spike didn’t blink. He simply raised one hand, palm out in a calm steady gesture, and nodded once.

Spike said quietly, almost casually, "It’s fine."

The trolley owner kept staring for another second, then looked right, then left, then right again — quick practiced checks of the area. Satisfied that no one important was watching, he gave a short nod.

The trolley owner said quietly, "Get in."

He moved to the side of the trolley and opened a concealed lower panel, a false-bottom compartment built into the undercarriage, just large enough for a person to lie flat. It was dark inside, lined with old blankets.

Spike didn’t hesitate. He handed over the thick roll of cash, climbed in smoothly, and lay down on his back. Once settled, he crossed his arms over his chest in a deliberate almost theatrical pose — exactly like a body in a coffin.

The trolley owner stared down at him for half a second, then quickly closed and latched the panel just as two local customers approached and climbed onto the bench seat above. They settled in, chatting quietly in their own language, completely unaware of what was hidden underneath them.

From inside the compartment, Spike’s voice was muffled but clear enough.

Spike said, dry and darkly amused, "Lovely. Nothing says luxury travel like riding under the paying customers. If I start to smell smoke, just keep walking, yeah?"

The two customers above him kept talking, casual everyday conversation about work, family, the price of something at another stall. Their voices were relaxed and normal. One of them laughed softly at something the other said.

The trolley owner checked the straps one last time, gripped the long wooden handle at the front, and leaned into it. The trolley lurched forward with a wooden creak and the soft crunch of wheels on the dirt road.

It began moving away from the harbor lights and the night market, heading inland into the darker countryside, the hanging white cloths of the market falling away behind them as the trolley rolled steadily into the night, until the sound of the wheels and the low murmur of the customers’ conversation faded into the dark.

CHAPTER 4 The Checkpoint

Later, the compartment was still dark and cramped. Spike lay on his back, arms loosely crossed over his chest. The constant rumble and creak of the wooden wheels on the dirt road vibrated through the floor beneath him.

The trolley suddenly slowed, then stopped completely.

For a long moment there was nothing but silence above him and the faint creak of the settling wood.

The hidden panel beside him slid open, and the trolley owner’s face appeared in the narrow gap, lit from the side and tense. Without speaking, he reached down and handed Spike a large metal cup with a long flexible straw already in it. Before they left the market he had quietly bought a second cup of the butcher’s blood and tucked it under the bench for the road, because a man who carried what he carried learned to plan ahead.

The trolley owner said in an urgent whisper, barely audible, "Stay down. Border patrol. Three of them. Machine guns. They see you, they kill us both. No sound. Understand?"

Spike’s eyes flicked up to the man’s face.

First came a quick flash of annoyance, the corner of his mouth tightening, one eyebrow twitching upward. Then, as he registered the blood, his brow relaxed and his eyes softened with something close to thanks. Then the annoyance came back, his eyes narrowing as the reality of staying hidden sank in.

He gave the man a single tight nod.

Spike took the cup, brought the straw to his mouth, and drank deeply. His throat worked as he pulled the blood through the straw in long steady pulls. His eyes stayed half-lidded. A small drop of blood escaped the corner of his mouth and ran down his cheek toward his ear, but he didn’t wipe it.

The trolley owner watched him for one more second, then quietly closed and latched the panel again.

Outside on the dirt road, three armed border patrol soldiers in dark uniforms stood blocking the way with assault rifles, one of them holding a flashlight. The trolley owner stood in front of them alone, holding out his papers with both hands, his posture respectful but tight.

The soldiers spoke to each other in low voices. One took the papers and shone the flashlight on them, then looked back at the trolley. Another soldier pointed down the road ahead. Their body language was casual but alert.

The trolley owner nodded quickly, took his papers back, and gave a short response. As soon as the soldiers stepped aside and waved him through, he gripped the long handle of the trolley with both hands, leaned forward, and started running.

The wheels bounced and rattled over the uneven dirt road as he pulled hard, putting distance between them and the checkpoint as fast as he could.

Inside the hidden space, Spike was jostled by the sudden speed, and he braced one hand against the side wall to keep from sliding. His expression was unreadable in the dark, but his eyes stayed open, staring upward at the wooden boards above him as the trolley bounced and shook.

The sound of the wheels and the man’s labored breathing filled the small space.

CHAPTER 5 Summers House — Early Afternoon

Back in Sunnydale, in the early afternoon, bright warm sunlight came through the bay window, painting soft gold rectangles across the carpet and furniture. Dust motes drifted lazily in the light. The room felt peaceful and lived-in, safe and quiet against whatever was happening on the other side of the world.

Willow sat cross-legged on the carpet in the middle of the room, back straight, both hands resting lightly on her knees, palms up, inside a perfect square of four clear quartz crystals. A brass incense burner sat on the coffee table nearby, a thin ribbon of fragrant smoke curling upward in a slow, steady spiral.

She wore soft pink yoga pants and a light purple strappy yoga top. Her eyes were closed, her breathing slow and even, in through the nose and out through the mouth, her face calm, almost serene.

The yellow wall phone in the kitchen began to ring.

It rang once. Twice. Three times.

Willow’s eyes slowly opened, and she let out a quiet sigh of mild frustration.

Willow said softly, to herself, "…Interrupted. Of course."

She unfolded her legs, stood up smoothly, walked into the kitchen, and picked up the yellow receiver from the wall.

Willow said, warm but slightly distracted, "Hello?"

Anya’s voice came through the phone, immediately launching in.

Anya said, "Willow? It’s me. This place is ridiculous. The people here are so… normal. They smile at everything and ask how you’re doing like they actually care and it’s exhausting. Also the coffee is terrible. Why does everyone here think weak coffee is acceptable?"

Willow leaned against the counter, a small smile already forming.

Willow said, gentle and amused, "Hi, Anya. It’s good to hear your voice too."

Anya went on without missing a beat.

Anya said, "But… it’s actually kind of great here. I have my own apartment. It has a window that looks at trees instead of a Hellmouth. And no one is trying to kill me on a weekly basis. It’s… peaceful. I think I like it. Mostly."

Willow’s smile softened.

Willow said, "That’s really good to hear, Anya."

Anya said, "Tell everyone I miss them. Especially Dawn. And tell Tara she better be taking good care of my store. It’s still my store! I built that place from nothing and I will come back and audit the inventory if I have to."

Willow laughed softly and said, "I’ll tell her. She’s doing a really good job, actually."

There was a brief pause on the other end, then a click as Xander picked up the other extension.

Xander said, bright and warm, "Hey, Will."

Anya said quickly, "Okay I’m hanging up now so you two can talk. Don’t do anything stupid without me."

The line clicked as Anya hung up on her end.

Willow’s expression shifted, softer and a little more vulnerable, and she adjusted her grip on the phone.

Willow said, warm and fond, "Hey, Xander."

Xander said, "How are you? Like… actually?"

Willow said, "I’m okay. Meditating a lot. Trying to keep the magic quiet. It’s… helping. How about you? New job treating you good?"

Xander said, "It’s actually really good. I’m supervising now. Can you believe that? Me. Supervising people. They even gave me my own hard hat with my name on it. I feel very official."

Willow laughed quietly.

Willow said, "That’s amazing. I’m really proud of you."

There was a comfortable pause. When Xander spoke again, his voice was quieter, more reflective.

Xander said, "I was thinking about that time we got trapped in that frat house during that whole… Halloween fear-demon thing. Remember how you kept making terrible jokes to keep us from freaking out?"

Willow said softly, "I remember. You were the one who found the way out. You always did."

Xander said, "Yeah, well… you were the one who made sure we didn’t lose our minds while I was figuring it out."

After another moment of silence, Xander’s voice shifted again, lighter but careful.

Xander said, "So… how’s Buffy doing? With Lucian, I mean. Are they… still good?"

Willow glanced toward the living room, her expression turning thoughtful.

Willow said, her voice honest and a little emotional, "She’s… the most alive I’ve seen her in a long time. Happy, even. It’s been a while since I could say that."

Xander said quietly, "Good. That’s really good. He seems like a solid guy. Odd as hell, but… solid. I’m glad she has that."

Willow’s eyes shone a little, and she swallowed once before answering.

Willow said, "Yeah. Me too."

There was another pause, heavier this time.

Xander said softly, "I miss you, Will."

Willow said, her voice cracking just slightly, "Same. I miss you too."

Xander said, "Take care of yourself, okay?"

Willow said, "You too."

They both lingered for half a second, neither quite ready to hang up. Then the line clicked softly as Xander ended the call.

Willow slowly lowered the receiver back onto the cradle and stood there for a moment, hand still resting on the phone, eyes distant, while the afternoon light continued to pour into the quiet house.

CHAPTER 6 The Cave

On a dirt road near a sandy hill, the trolley finally stopped. The owner opened the hidden panel and stepped back.

Spike climbed out stiffly into the warm night air, stood, and rolled his shoulders to work the cramp out of them, then turned to look left, up the sandy slope, where the dark mouth of the cave waited against the stars.

He gave the man a short nod, then started walking up the hill with his usual confident strut. Behind him, the trolley owner immediately grabbed the handle of his cart and ran at full speed back down the road, the wheels rattling loudly as he fled.

Spike reached the cave mouth. On either side, along the stone walls, were small offerings, bowls of food, dried fruit, and bread left by previous visitors, a few of them fresh but most old and hardened.

He wiped his right thumb once under his nose, then stepped into the darkness without hesitation.

Inside, the cave was dark, ancient, and exactly as the stories said: rough stone walls, uneven floor, deep shadows, and a heavy still atmosphere. Faded paintings and ritual markings covered sections of the rock, and the air was cool and dry.

Spike walked slowly deeper inside, boots scuffing on stone, glancing at the wall paintings as he passed.

In the deepest part of the chamber, a large figure waited in the thick shadows.

Lloyd, the cave demon, stood perfectly still. He was massive, nearly seven feet tall, broad and powerful, with thick, cracked, stone-like gray-brown skin and short curved horns that swept back from his temples. His eyes glowed with a steady amber-orange light from deep sockets, his hands ended in long dark claws, and he radiated age and quiet, dangerous patience.

Spike stopped a short distance away, and the two regarded each other in silence.

The demon finally spoke, his voice deep, layered, and resonant.

Lloyd said, his voice calm, with a hint of disdain, "You come here seeking strength. You wish to become what you once were. A warrior without weakness. Without chains."

He stepped forward slightly, the amber glow in his eyes brightening as he studied Spike.

Lloyd said, "Yet you reek of a Slayer. You have fallen in love with one. A legendary dark warrior… brought low by a girl. Tell me, vampire — why should I grant you anything when you have already allowed yourself to be broken by her?"

Spike didn’t flinch, and his voice stayed steady and defiant.

Spike said, rough and direct, "I’m not here for a lecture. I’m here because I want what I want. Make me what I was. So I can give her what she deserves."

The demon let out a low rumbling sound, almost a chuckle, but colder.

Lloyd said, "You still believe you are a demon. You are not. Not anymore. Not truly. And yet you ask to be restored to something you no longer fully are."

He tilted his head slightly, eyes narrowing.

Lloyd said, "Very well. If you truly desire this… you will face the trials. All of them. And if you survive, I will give you what you seek."

Spike stared at him for a long second, then gave a short sharp nod.

Spike said, "Bring it on, then."

The demon stepped back into the deeper shadows.

Lloyd said, his voice final, echoing slightly off the stone, "Begin."

CHAPTER 7 First Trial — The Flame-Fisted Lizard

The demon Lloyd raised one massive clawed hand and gestured toward a cleared area deeper in the cave. The uneven stone floor gave way to a rough circle of packed sand, about twenty feet across. Faint torchlight from deeper in the cave cast long dancing shadows across the walls.

Lloyd said again, deep and final, "Begin."

Spike walked toward the circle without hesitation. As he reached the edge of the sand, movement caught his eye.

From the darkness at the far side of the cave, another figure emerged, and the lizard demon stepped into the torchlight.

He was reptilian, with thick scaled skin in mottled green-brown tones, an elongated, iguana-like face with a heavy brow ridge, and yellow slit-pupil eyes. A long, powerful tail swayed behind him, and his hands were clawed and muscular. He moved with a low, predatory gait, tail whipping slowly side to side as he let out a long, low, threatening hiss.

He entered the sand circle first, never taking his eyes off Spike. He stopped in the center, brought both clawed fists together in front of his chest, and ignited them. Flames burst to life around his hands and forearms, bright, flickering orange-yellow fire that cast harsh light across the sand and the cave walls.

The lizard demon hissed again, louder this time, and dropped into a fighting stance, tail lashing behind him.

Spike rolled his shoulders once, then stepped fully into the circle.

For one breath neither of them moved, and then the fight began.

The lizard demon moved first, and fast, lunging with a flaming right hook. Spike tried to slip it, but the demon was quicker than he looked, and the burning fist clipped Spike across the left side of the face. Skin sizzled. Spike grunted in pain, head snapping to the side, a red burn mark already forming on his cheekbone.

Before he could recover, the demon spun and kicked him hard in the ribs, and as Spike staggered back, the lizard demon followed with a whipping tail strike. The scaled tail tore through his shirt and opened a nasty, bleeding gash across his chest, and blood immediately started running down his torso.

Spike’s face twisted in pain and anger as he touched the wound with one hand, then looked up at the demon with cold eyes.

Spike said, low and dangerous, breathing hard, "That all you got, lizard?"

The demon hissed and charged again.

Slowly, through the pain, Spike began to find his rhythm, and the wild brawling gave way to technique: every form he had stolen from a century of fighting men who knew what they were doing.

The demon threw another flaming punch. Spike met it with a sharp karate block, his left forearm rising at an angle to redirect the arm, then immediately countered with a front kick to the demon’s midsection, driving the ball of his foot into the scaled stomach. The lizard demon grunted and stumbled back.

Spike pressed forward, closed the distance fast, grabbed the demon’s right arm with both hands, and executed a clean judo throw, pivoting his hips and using the demon’s own momentum to flip him over his shoulder. The lizard demon slammed into the sand hard.

For a moment, Spike had the advantage, and he moved in to follow up.

But the demon’s tail lashed out from the ground and cracked Spike across the back of the legs, dropping him to one knee. The lizard demon rolled up and punched Spike in the shoulder with a flaming fist, the fire searing into muscle, and Spike roared in pain.

Spike was hurt now, his face and shoulder burned, his chest bleeding, but his eyes were sharp.

The demon came in with a combination of flaming punches. Spike answered with aikido redirection, circular movements that guided the demon’s arms past him instead of blocking directly, then turned that momentum to throw the creature off-balance again. He followed with a sharp karate elbow strike to the side of the demon’s head.

The lizard demon staggered. Spike went for a judo hip throw, but the demon twisted mid-air and landed on his feet, tail whipping around to lash Spike across the ribs, opening another cut.

Spike was starting to slow, and the demon was relentless.

The lizard demon grabbed Spike by the throat with one burning hand and slammed him down into the sand on his back. The impact knocked the wind out of him, and the demon raised one clawed foot, trying to stomp on Spike’s head.

At the last second, Spike kicked upward with both legs, catching the demon in the chest and flipping him backward. In one fluid motion, Spike snap-rolled over his shoulder, came up to his feet, and stood tall again, blood running down his chest and face.

He wiped blood from his mouth with the back of his hand and stared at the demon with pure murder in his eyes.

Spike said, low and cold, breathing ragged but steady, "You’re starting to piss me off."

The lizard demon charged one last time, both fists blazing.

Spike sidestepped at the last moment, using an aikido-style entry to catch the demon’s arm. He twisted violently, using the creature’s momentum against it, and threw him hard to the sand. Before the demon could roll away, Spike was on him, mounting from the side and trapping one arm.

The lizard demon thrashed, tail whipping wildly, hissing and spitting.

Spike grabbed the demon’s head with both hands, planted one knee on its chest for leverage, and with one sharp, brutal twist, snapped its neck.

There was a loud wet crack.

The lizard demon’s body jerked once, and its burning fists went out. It let out a final gurgling breath, its reptilian tongue flickered and then stilled, and the yellow eyes went dark.

Spike stayed on top of the corpse for a second, breathing hard, blood dripping from his face and chest onto the dead demon, his hands still gripping its head.

From the shadows, Lloyd’s voice rumbled with something close to approval.

Lloyd said, deep and satisfied, "You begin to understand the price."

Spike slowly pushed himself up off the dead demon. He stood in the sand circle, burned, bleeding, and breathing heavily, but still on his feet.

CHAPTER 8 Second Trial — The Visions

Lloyd gestured toward a narrow opening in the rock.

Lloyd said, deep and final, "Enter. Do not leave until the visions stop. If you do… you fail."

Spike stared at him, confused for a moment, then scoffed and ducked into the smaller chamber. The space was tight and dark, its floor soft with sand. He stood in the center, waiting.

Fifteen long seconds crawled past with nothing in the dark but the rasp of his own unnecessary breathing.

Then cold, delicate fingers traced slowly across the back of his neck from behind.

Drusilla said in a soft, sing-song, intimate voice, "All the little games we used to play…"

She leaned in close, lips nearly brushing the side of his face.

Drusilla said, "My sweet, wicked boy. You’ve been so very naughty. Running off without your mummy. Chasing after the nasty Slayer like a bad little puppy who doesn’t know any better. Bad puppies get taken to the vet, don’t they? They get their teeth pulled and their eyes put out so they stop seeing things they shouldn’t. Do you want me to take you to the vet, my Spike? Do you want me to make the bad thoughts go away?"

Spike’s breathing grew heavier as he slowly raised his left hand across his chest, reaching for her fingers…

Before his fingers could touch hers she was simply gone, and the chamber was empty again except for him and the faint cold where her hand had been.

Adam had formed out of the dark and stood perfectly still.

Adam said, his voice calm, cold, and precise, "You were never designed for this."

Adam said, "You were Hostile Seventeen. A weapon. Efficient. Focused. You killed without hesitation, without guilt, without the noise that now pollutes your mind. That was your function. That was your perfection."

Adam said, "And now look at you. Begging a demon for a soul like a child asking for a toy. You were built to be a predator. Instead you chose to become… this. Something that feels. Something that doubts. Something that weeps in the dirt over a name in the sand."

Adam tilted his head slightly, almost curious, and said, "Do you truly believe this soul will make you worthy of her? It won’t. It will only make you slower. Weaker. More human. And humans break so easily."

CHAPTER 9 The Gift — My Eternal Soul

Spike was still on both knees in the sand, head bowed low. Blood from his earlier wounds mixed with fresh tears. For a long moment there was only his ragged breathing and the weight of a dead heart that now had something to answer to.

Then came horror, the slow dawning comprehension of every single thing he had ever done, all at once, with no buffer of demon indifference. His face crumpled like a child’s, and the tears spilled again, not blood, not performance.

Spike said, his voice quiet and ruined, almost wondering, "...It hurts."

Then he said, "It’s supposed to."

He straightened as much as his body would let him. The swagger did not return. What walked out of the chamber was not the vampire who had walked in. It was a man who knew exactly what he was, and chose to keep walking anyway.

CHAPTER 10 The Road

On a dirt road in the African dark, Spike walked alone.

The cave was a dark mouth behind him on the hill. The road stretched long and empty ahead. His coat was torn in three places, his shirt was stiff with dried blood, and his face was a map of cuts and burns and the new, invisible damage. He did not swagger. He did not strut. He simply put one foot in front of the other, head slightly down, eyes on the eastern sky, counting how long he had before it paled and he would need to be under cover.

In the distance, the faint sound of a hand-pulled cart might have been coming back for him. Or it might not. He didn’t wait to find out.

So he walked, alone, his boots scuffing softly in the dust of the road.

He became a small solitary figure on the long road, a man carrying his soul like a wound, walking toward a future he had earned the right to suffer for.

Long before the sky could pale, he found a culvert under the road, crawled into its dark, and lay down to wait out the day.
