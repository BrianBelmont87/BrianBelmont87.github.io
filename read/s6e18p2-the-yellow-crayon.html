The Yellow Crayon

Download this script (.txt)

Buffy the Vampire Slayer. Season six, episode 18, part 2. The Yellow Crayon.

PREVIOUSLY ON...

Absolute silence hung over the dark, and then Anya’s warm, slightly blunt voice cut straight through it.

Anya said, "Last time on Buffy the Vampire Slayer..."

Memory hit in rapid flashes.

The Magic Box in morning light, fast food on the research table. Buffy stood and delivered her speech: the group was no longer just surviving; they were living. "This family is always the center of it. You guys are the only magic that has ever actually saved my life." The Scooby Gang rose into a crushing group hug in the dusty golden light.

Calax Research and Development in the morning. Lucian at his terminal, quietly happy after the night with Buffy. I.R.I.S. noted his elevated serotonin and asked about the girl. Lucian admitted he met someone who grounded him. A rose made of ash — the first stirrings of the entity beneath.

The unauthorized project and the ancient black stone tablet. Whispers of "From beneath you..." The date with Buffy interrupted by the growing horror; Lucian learned the truth of what was under his building.

The coastal highway at night. Lucian’s black Firebird raced through the dark. Strobing red glow on the horizon from the facility. Buffy’s hand rested on Lucian’s bruised knuckles. The weight of what they were driving toward settled between them.

Lucian drove fast through the dark on the long road back north. The strobing red glow of Calax had begun to show on the horizon, and Buffy’s hand rested over his on the shifter.

Buffy said quietly, "You looked like you were going to pass out when she said my name. I wasn’t going to hide it forever." She meant the file: Hemery, the burned-down gym, the expulsion, the Master. It was all of it sitting in some Watchers’ Council database, read aloud to him by a computer in the middle of their first real dance.

Lucian said, tight, "I’m not afraid of you. I’m afraid of what’s under my building."

Buffy nodded and tightened her hand over his on the shifter, and the engine note climbed as he pressed the Firebird harder up the dark coast road.

CHAPTER 1 The Last Call

The Magic Box was closed. The overhead lights were off, and warm amber shadows filled the room, lit only by a few green-glass banker’s lamps on the counters and pale streetlight through the front windows.

Crickets chirped distantly outside, and the old refrigerator hummed in the back room.

In the far back corner, behind a shelf of dusty crystal balls, Dawn lay flat on a worn crushed-velvet couch, fast asleep in her pink hoodie and jeans under a heavy woven Mexican blanket.

Tara sat sideways on the edge of the couch cushion in her long bohemian skirt and soft cardigan. She looked down at Dawn with a warm motherly smile and gently stroked Dawn’s hair, brushing stray strands from the teenager’s sleeping face.

Toward the front, Xander and Anya stood near the main cash register with a half-taped cardboard box on the glass counter.

Xander kept his voice to a respectful hush.

Xander said, "I’m just saying, if we pack the fragile stuff in the back of the U-Haul, the Texas highways are going to vibrate the inventory into magical dust. We need to load the heavy texts first to anchor the suspension."

Anya whispered back, folding bubble wrap, "I have mathematically calculated the exact weight distribution of the vehicle, Xander. The crystal spheres will be perfectly secure beneath the boxes of spare weapons. Stop questioning my vehicular logistics."

At the round wooden research table, Willow sat alone in her oversized brown corduroy overshirt over a plain shirt. A massive leather-bound book lay open in front of her. She held a large ceramic mug of hot coffee in both hands, steam curling up into her face, tired but focused as she turned a brittle parchment page.

The phone rang, a long, shrill ring that shattered the shop’s quiet.

Dawn shifted on the couch with a soft sleepy groan. Tara shushed gently and put a comforting hand on Dawn’s shoulder to keep her asleep.

Anya glared at the phone and sighed, looking down at the wedding ring on her left hand.

Anya rested her hand on the receiver.

Anya said, under her breath, "I am legally a married woman entirely focused on relocation. This is the absolute last time I am going to do this."

She picked up the heavy plastic receiver, pasting on her overly enthusiastic retail voice.

Anya said brightly, "Magic Box, this is Anya, how can I help you?"

The bright capitalistic smile dropped, and color drained from her cheeks as her eyes widened, listening to the frantic voice on the other end.

Anya’s voice dropped, entirely serious.

Anya said, "Yes. We’re all still here. Where are you?"

She listened for another two seconds and gave a single sharp nod.

Anya said, "Understood. We’ll be ready."

She slowly lowered the receiver onto the cradle with a dull click.

Xander looked at her, brow furrowing at the sudden tension. Anya didn’t explain; she walked straight from behind the counter to the research table.

Willow didn’t look up at first. She turned another page and took a slow sip of coffee.

Anya stopped in front of the table and stared at Willow, face full of dread.

Willow slowly lowered the mug and lifted her eyes and met Anya’s terrified gaze.

She held Anya’s stare for a couple of seconds without gasping or flinching, then let out a long, slow, exhausted exhale through her nose.

Willow took another tiny sip of coffee, completely deadpan.

Willow said, "Let me guess. That is your specific ‘danger, trouble, demons, and literal hell on earth’ expression. Did we run out of packing tape, or is the apocalypse early this year?"

Anya’s voice was tight, with no humor in it.

Anya said, "That was Buffy. They found something under the Calax facility. She says we need every weapon we have left in the inventory. Now."

Behind the counter, Xander had gone completely still with a strip of packing tape stuck to his fingers, and over on the velvet couch Tara’s hand stopped moving in Dawn’s hair.

Willow slowly set her coffee mug down on the wooden table.

CHAPTER 2 The Assimilation and the Override

Deep in the Calax sub-basement, the room strobed with oppressive orange light bleeding from the ancient black stone tablet. Shadows on the concrete walls lengthened and twisted into jagged shapes.

The low-frequency hum of the supercomputer had become a deafening mechanical screech layered with the wet tearing sound of cracking stone.

Dr. Aris remained on his knees on the hard concrete, hands clasped, paralyzed by fear, staring up at the glowing runes. Ben and the two other scientists backed against the server racks, screaming as the cables around them began to melt from the heat.

Without warning, the solid surface of the stone tablet rippled like thick dark liquid. An invisible, massive gravitational force yanked Dr. Aris forward.

Dr. Aris said in a raw, throat-tearing scream, "NO! PLEASE—!"

He didn’t hit the stone; he was pulled directly into it. The black surface stretched, swallowing him whole, silencing his scream in a sickening wet gulp.

Ben backed into the wall in his white lab coat, weeping.

Ben said, "Oh my god... oh my god..."

From the pitch-black shadows in the corners of the bunker, the Bringers emerged in coarse brown hooded robes, their faces smooth and featureless where their eyes should be.

They did not walk; they glided with terrifying, silent speed, drawing deeply curved sacrificial knives from their robes. In a brutal, synchronized execution, they descended on Ben and the two remaining scientists. There was no drawn-out fight; the killings were swift, silent, and absolute.

The scientists dropped to the concrete floor. Ben’s white coat was the last to hit the ground, and his CALAX RESEARCH badge skittered across the floor beside him. The Bringers wiped their curved blades, and in perfect unison they turned toward the glowing stone tablet and dropped to their knees, bowing their hooded heads in fanatic reverence.

There was a heavy metallic crunch, then the sickening sound of tearing flesh.

The dark surface of the stone boiled. Stepping out of the liquid rock came a horrifying fusion of ancient demon and modern Calax technology.

First, a massive mechanical hoofed foot — forged from melted server metals — slammed onto the concrete, cracking the floor. The leg intertwined with raw pulsing human muscle and the torn bloody fabric of Dr. Aris’s grey jumpsuit. A massive clawed arm reached out, gripping the edge of the tablet to pull its bulk through. The creature stood nearly eight feet tall. Four jagged horns, two of them long and deeply curved, protruded from a skull that was half-bone, half-steel. In the center of its face, two piercing glowing orange eyes ignited in the dark.

A small white plastic object slid off the creature’s mutated chest and clattered onto the concrete: Dr. Aris’s bloody, cracked Calax ID badge.

The creature threw its massive horned head back and let out a roaring guttural metallic shriek that shook the foundation of the bunker.

Up on Level Five, the long banks of pristine white fluorescent panels over the main research floor had shut off, and the massive room was bathed in the harsh rotating red glare of emergency klaxons.

A blaring rhythmic security siren echoed through the empty aisles of desks.

I.R.I.S.’s voice echoed over the facility’s PA system, the gentle, inquisitive tone gone, replaced by a rapid frantic hyper-logical operational cadence.

I.R.I.S. said over the alarms, "Catastrophic security breach detected on Sub-Levels Three and Four. Biological and unclassified hostile entities ascending."

Digital screens flashed red in rapid succession — outgoing connection to emergency services: failed. Rerouting to military encrypted channels: failed. Anomaly detected: supernatural signal jamming.

I.R.I.S. said over the alarms, "Unable to establish outgoing connections. External frequencies are being actively suppressed by localized energy distortion. Security personnel on Level Three have been neutralized."

Past the glass wall, out in the massive canyon of black server racks, something was moving.

I.R.I.S. said over the alarms, "Hostile entity approaching Level Five server farm. Initiating Halon gas fire-suppression protocols."

With a massive hissing rush of air, thick, suffocating clouds of white Halon gas blasted from the ceiling vents, flooding the server corridors.

Through the thick white chemical fog, two glowing orange eyes appeared. The massive horned techno-demon strode effortlessly through the deadly gas, completely unaffected. It raised its clawed mechanical arm and casually swatted a massive server rack out of its way, sending a shower of electrical sparks into the fog.

I.R.I.S. said over the alarms, her synthetic voice pitching with a human sense of desperation, "Halon gas is ineffective. Entity biology unverified. Initiating absolute lockdown. Sealing primary blast doors with auto-correcting algorithmic encryption."

A deafening hydraulic grinding filled the floor.

The massive three-foot-thick solid titanium blast doors protecting the central core of I.R.I.S.’s mainframe violently slammed shut, interlocking with six massive steel deadbolts. The electronic keypad on the wall flashed green, rapidly cycling through thousands of encrypted passcodes per second. The facility fell eerily quiet, the red emergency strobes washing over the heavy titanium doors in total silence.

For one second, then two, nothing moved at all.

A deafening inhuman ROAR echoed from the other side of the titanium.

Then a massive, bone-jarring BANG hit the center of the blast doors from the other side, and a visible dent bulged outward in the solid titanium. A second BANG followed, and the heavy steel hinges buckled in their frames. With the third, the sheer impossible force of the monster trying to break into the core shook the whole floor beneath the empty desks.

CHAPTER 3 The Arsenal and the Arrival

Outside, the Calax parking lot sat in deep night. The only light came from the facility’s massive glass lobby doors, flashing with the rotating red glare of the emergency klaxons inside, and shallow rain puddles on the dark asphalt threw the strobing red back up at the sky.

Tires screeched across the wet asphalt at the lot entrance as two sets of headlights swung in off the road together.

Lucian’s sleek black-and-gold Pontiac Firebird pulled hard up to the front walkway and slammed into park. A split second later, a massive heavy-duty pickup truck towing a white and orange U-Haul trailer roared up right behind the Firebird, hopped the curb, and slammed on the brakes.

Doors on both vehicles flew open at once.

Buffy and Lucian stepped out of the Firebird. Xander, Anya, Willow, and Tara piled out of the pickup, while Dawn stayed curled up asleep in the front seat.

Buffy was still in the black dress from the date, her small black leather handbag in hand. Lucian had left his dark jacket in the Firebird. He wore the same black embroidered shirt open at the collar and dark trousers, his hair down and slightly wind-ruffled from the drive, his silver cross earring catching the red strobe. Xander was in sweaty construction gear, Anya in practical travel clothes, and Willow in her corduroy overshirt and Tara in her cardigan.

Willow looked up at the massive red-strobing concrete facility and crossed her arms, shivering slightly in the night air.

Willow frowned, her voice laced with heavy realization.

Willow said, "Buffy, this is the exact same corporate campus that got taken over by Moloch. The internet demon-robot thing from back in high school. I’m getting massive déjà vu, and not the good kind."

Buffy stopped where she stood on the wet asphalt and turned her head slowly, her hazel eyes locking onto Lucian with a sharp, questioning, entirely lethal Slayer stare.

Lucian held his hands up defensively, looking genuinely appalled.

Lucian said firmly and sincerely, "I swear to you, Buffy, I didn’t know. My department is purely cognitive AI. I.R.I.S. is not demonic in any way. She was built to understand and help humanity, she would never harm anyone. Whatever they are doing down in that sub-basement... it is completely off the books."

Buffy stared at him for a single long heartbeat while her instincts read his face, and she believed him. She dropped the stare and turned to the rest of the group.

Buffy slipped into command.

Buffy said, "Okay. We deal with the basement monsters first, file the corporate HR complaints later. Willow."

She looked directly at Willow, concerned.

Buffy said, "Can you do your part? I need to know if the juice is actually flowing."

Willow took a slow, visibly uneasy breath and lifted her hands, palms inward about six inches apart, then closed her eyes tightly and breathed out a long, shaky exhale.

For a terrifying second, nothing happened. A few pathetic dying orange sparks fizzled and spit between her fingers, threatening to short out entirely.

Then she pushed harder.

With a sharp electric CRACK, brilliant high-voltage lightning erupted between her palms.

The strobing flash lit the dark parking lot, and the sudden burst of static made Willow’s red hair lift and blow around her face while the crackling light cast rapid dancing reflections across the dark rain puddles and painted the faces of Buffy, Lucian, and the others in stark flashing shadows.

Willow opened her eyes, clear, focused, and confident, and clenched her fists, extinguishing the lightning and leaving the lot back in the strobing red emergency glare.

Anya stepped past Willow, marching toward the back of the U-Haul.

Anya said, "Well, since we are officially in apocalypse mode, I have a surprise for you."

Anya hauled up the heavy metal rolling door of the U-Haul trailer.

Sitting on the scuffed plywood floor of the trailer, wedged between stacks of taped boxes, was a wooden weapons rack. Resting in the center of the rack was the massive steel-forged Troll Hammer.

Buffy’s eyes went wide, and a deeply appreciative smirk spread across her face.

Buffy laughed softly, tossing her black leather handbag into the front seat of the Firebird.

Buffy said, "Anya... you really know the exact way to a girl’s heart. And by heart, I mean blunt-force trauma."

Anya reached into the truck and pulled out a wicked, razor-sharp steel machete.

Xander reached into the bed of his pickup and grabbed a long-handled steel construction sledgehammer, resting the head casually over his shoulder.

Lucian walked around to the back of the Firebird, swung the glass rear hatch up, and reached into the cargo well for his weapon: the gleaming heavy medieval longsword from the beach fight. The silver raven buckle at his belt caught the red light as he straightened.

Lucian closed his hand around the leather-wrapped hilt and drew the sword out of the cargo well, and then he simply stood there holding it, not moving. The terrifying reality of charging into a building with a primordial god had caught up with him, and the blade’s tip wavered a fraction in the red light.

He closed his eyes hard, drew a long breath in through his nose, and blew it out slowly through his mouth.

When Lucian opened his eyes, the fear was gone, and his features had hardened into stern, unyielding courage.

Buffy stood a few feet away, watching his entire internal struggle. She didn’t tease him; she looked at him with profound, silent respect and deep affection.

Buffy stepped up to the U-Haul. With an effortless flex of Slayer strength, she hefted the massive Troll Hammer off the rack with one hand.

She looked down at her short black dress, sighed, and ruthlessly ripped the fabric up the side of her thigh for full combat mobility, then kicked off her white pumps to stand barefoot on the wet asphalt.

Tara stepped up beside Willow.

Tara said, her voice steady and brave, "I’ve been working on my barrier spells. The shields have gotten a little stronger. I can hold the line."

Buffy nodded and turned her head to look through the open window of the pickup truck.

Buffy said, her voice leaving no room for argument, "Dawn. You stay in the truck. Doors locked. If anything without a face taps on the glass, you lay on the horn and duck."

Dawn jerked awake in the front seat and nodded rapidly, reaching over and locking the heavy truck doors with a loud mechanical clunk.

Buffy turned around, gripping the handle of the Troll Hammer with both hands, and looked up at the massive red-strobing glass doors of the Calax facility.

Buffy said, "Let’s go to work."

CHAPTER 4 The Faceless Monks and the Fuel Cell

Inside, the pristine white corporate lobby was saturated by the rotating red glare of the emergency klaxons. Long shadows swept across the room with every pass of the light.

The facility was eerily quiet except for one repeating sound: a distant massive rhythmic boom… boom… boom… echoing from deep inside as the techno-demon pounded against the titanium blast doors.

Buffy’s bare feet stepped silently onto the polished white tile, leaving faint damp footprints from the rain-soaked parking lot.

Buffy led the wedge with the massive steel Troll Hammer in both hands. Lucian stayed tight on her left flank with his longsword, his dark embroidered shirt sweat-damp at the collar and the sleeves rolled once, while Xander and Anya took the right flank with the sledgehammer and machete, and Willow and Tara stayed centered, protected by the perimeter.

Lucian stopped abruptly, his sword still low in his right hand, and reached his free left hand across to his right ear, pressing his fingers tightly against the silver earpiece.

Lucian whispered harshly, "Hold. I.R.I.S. is broadcasting on a localized emergency frequency."

Her voice came through the earpiece, calm but rapidly paced, with a strange, profound softness to her synthetic tone.

I.R.I.S. said through the earpiece, "Lucian. The entity will breach the titanium doors in exactly four minutes. Beneath your current floor, positioned directly above the sub-basement, is a secondary hydrogen fuel cell."

Lucian’s face was bathed in the flashing red light as he listened intently.

I.R.I.S. said, "It is directly hardwired into my system core. It is how the unauthorized project siphoned the eight hundred and fifty kilowatts without triggering my internal grid alarms. I need you to reach the fuel cell and physically release the manual safety locks."

Lucian whispered, "Why? What does releasing the locks do?"

I.R.I.S. said, "It will detach my architecture from the corrupted network, allowing me to initiate a hard system shutdown. I will turn myself off to starve the entity of its digital bridge. Please, Lucian. You must hurry."

Lucian dropped his hand from his ear and looked at Buffy.

Lucian said, "There’s a hydrogen fuel cell on the mezzanine level beneath us. I.R.I.S. needs me to blow the manual safety locks so she can sever her connection to the network and shut down before that thing gets inside her."

Buffy nodded once, her grip tightening on the steel handle of the Troll Hammer.

Buffy said in a low, all-business voice, "Okay. You and Xander find the stairs. The girls and I will hold the lob—"

A shadow detached itself from the far wall of the lobby.

Buffy stopped mid-sentence, her Slayer reflexes twitching, and raised the Troll Hammer, staring into the dark corridor ahead.

Stepping silently out of the red-strobing shadows were six Bringers.

They wore coarse brown monastic robes with deep hoods. Their faces were horrifyingly smooth, pale waxy skin stretched flawlessly over the upper half where their eyes should be, and they held curved, jagged sacrificial daggers.

Buffy stared at them, hazel eyes narrowing in genuine confusion and deep disgust.

Buffy said, deadpan but deeply unsettled, "Okay. The eyeless monk look is definitely new."

The Bringers did not scream or roar. With terrifying, dead-silent speed, they rushed the group, gliding across the polished floor like ghosts, curved blades raised to strike.

Buffy said, her voice rising to a shout, "Incoming!"

Then the whole lobby erupted into motion at once.

Buffy moved first, with no wasted motion. Her bare left foot planted hard on the wet tile and her right foot drove her hips through a full turn, bringing the Troll Hammer up from the floor like a golf swing with Slayer torque behind it. The steel head met the lead Bringer square in the chest, and bone and robe and silent body left the ground together; the kinetic force threw the monk backward through the air and through a floor-to-ceiling corporate glass partition. The glass exploded in a white crash, and shards rained across the lobby tile while the Bringer hit the far wall and dropped in a heap that did not get up.

Two more Bringers cut straight for the center — for Willow and Tara.

Tara stepped into them, upright, both hands thrusting forward with her fingers spread, and a shimmering, translucent pale-blue barrier flared into existence between her palms and the room, tall enough to shield Willow’s casting space. The first Bringer’s dagger scraped the shield and threw bright blue sparks, and the second Bringer hit the barrier full-body and bounced. Tara’s jaw set and her shoulders stayed square; the shield held, but her forearms trembled with the effort of keeping power locked against two silent killers.

Anya stepped out from behind Tara’s light the second the nearest Bringer staggered. She gripped the heavy steel machete with both hands and put her whole body into a vicious unrefined horizontal chop, and the blade bit deep across the Bringer’s chest. The monk stumbled backward, but Anya did not chase; she reset her feet, breathing hard, waiting for Tara’s shield to give her the next opening. That was her lane, and she knew it.

On the right flank, Xander swung the long-handled construction sledgehammer in wide heavy arcs — human strength, construction shoulders, military muscle memory waking up. He was not launching anyone through walls; he was keeping space. The head of the sledge whooshed past a Bringer’s hood close enough to force it to glide sideways. Sweat already stood on Xander’s forehead under the red strobe, and each swing cost him, but he planted his boots wider and kept the hammer moving so the monk could not close.

A Bringer slipped under Xander’s backswing and raised its dagger for a downward stab at Xander’s exposed side.

Lucian stepped in, trained but no Slayer. He brought the medieval longsword up on a clean diagonal and caught the dagger with a sharp ring of steel that jarred all the way into his wrists. He did not try to overpower the Bringer; he redirected, driving his right boot hard into the monk’s knee and kicking the leg out from under it. The Bringer dropped to the tile, and Lucian’s sword arm snapped straight back to guard. His breath stayed controlled through the red strobe, beach drill and date-night calm forced into the same muscle memory, and his jaw was locked. He did not celebrate. He just held the line Xander had left open.

From the top of an overturned reception desk, a final Bringer leapt — silent, dagger first — diving for Buffy’s blind spot while she recovered the hammer from the golf-swing follow-through.

Willow did not hesitate. She threw her right arm forward, palm open, and a jagged, blinding whip of orange and cyan lightning erupted from her hand and hit the airborne Bringer mid-leap. Thousands of volts locked the monk’s body rigid in the air, then hurled it sideways into the drywall hard enough to crater the plaster. Willow’s red hair lifted in the static, and she staggered half a step after the cast, her magic back but far from limitless, and brought both hands up again, ready.

The Scooby Gang held their ground as the strobing red lights washed over their faces, and most of the Bringers were down or stunned. The boom… boom… boom… of the techno-demon against the titanium doors kept rolling under everything.

Buffy breathed hard, resting the head of the Troll Hammer on the tile floor. She looked at the downed silent monks, then back at Lucian, and jerked her head toward the hallway.

Buffy said, "Go! Find the stairs! We’ll keep the faceless wonder twins off your back."

Lucian gave a sharp nod and grabbed Xander by the shoulder of his sweaty cut-off shirt, pulling him out of the defensive circle, and the two men sprinted down the right-side corridor toward the stairwell access.

Buffy hefted the Troll Hammer back up, her eyes locked on the dark shadows at the end of the lobby, waiting for the next wave. Willow shook out her casting hand, Tara kept a thinner shimmer of blue light ready between her palms, and Anya wiped the machete once on her travel pants and reset her grip, mean enough to finish what the others dropped.

CHAPTER 5 The Soldier and the Silence

The lobby remained bathed in the harsh rotating red glare of the emergency klaxons.

The Bringer Lucian had kicked in the knee groggily placed its hands flat on the tile, trying to push itself back up.

Before it could fully rise, the massive steel head of the Troll Hammer dropped heavily, resting on the floor right next to the Bringer’s hand.

Buffy casually leaned her weight onto the handle of the hammer, looking down at the faceless monk.

Buffy said, raising a deadpan eyebrow, "Hey. No standing during the nap portion of the evening."

Buffy smoothly lifted the Troll Hammer and brought the flat side of the heavy steel head down in a quick controlled THWACK directly onto the top of the Bringer’s skull. The monk collapsed instantly, hitting the tile with a dull thud, completely unmoving.

The last remaining Bringer was still furiously scraping its curved dagger against Tara’s shimmering blue magical barrier. Tara’s shield flickered brighter under the pressure, but her fingers stayed spread, and she did not break.

Anya stepped out from the flank, her face a mask of pure, ancient vengeance-demon pragmatism.

Anya gripped the machete with both hands and brought it down in a brutal diagonal swing, and the blade cleaved deep into the Bringer’s collarbone.

There was no visible blood; the heavy, coarse brown fabric of the monastic robe swallowed the wound from sight.

The blade stuck in the bone, but Anya didn’t miss a beat. She lifted her practical travel boot, planted it squarely flat against the Bringer’s chest, and used her leg to force the monk backward while violently yanking the machete free.

The Bringer crumpled to the floor, and the lobby was finally clear.

Below, the sub-mezzanine corridor was much tighter and darker than the lobby. The red emergency strobes flashed erratically, creating long stretching shadows down the narrow hallway.

Xander was in the lead, and he was no longer walking like a goofy, insecure construction worker. His posture was rigid, his steps tight and disciplined, his eyes darting from corner to corner. The deeply buried military training from that Halloween had taken over.

He held the heavy steel sledgehammer diagonally across his chest like an M-16 rifle, ready to strike or block instantly.

Lucian trailed a step behind him, keeping his longsword low, watching Xander’s flawless tactical movement with mild impressed surprise.

Xander said, his voice low, sharp, and commanding, "Clear left. Keep on my six, Lucian."

They turned a tight corner and stopped.

Set into an alcove in the concrete wall was the hydrogen fuel cell, a massive cylindrical containment unit, glowing with a faint pulsing internal white light. It was secured by four massive heavy metal hook-hinges clamped over the primary access panel.

Lucian stepped past Xander, tucked his sword under his arm, and grabbed the top two metal hook-hinges with his bare hands.

Lucian grunted, straining hard to pull the heavy metal latches open, but the steel was seized, locked down by the facility’s emergency protocols. He stepped back, shaking out his sore hands, the right one still lightly bruised.

Lucian said, frustrated, "They’re hydraulically sealed. I can’t force them by hand."

Xander stepped up and effortlessly flipped the long-handled construction sledgehammer in his grip, finding the perfect balance point.

Xander smirked, entirely in his element.

Xander said, "Good thing I brought the master key. Step back."

Lucian took a step back, and Xander wound up and delivered four rapid, brutally precise lateral swings.

The sledgehammer rang four times in quick succession, CLANG, CLANG, CLANG, CLANG, each blow echoing down the concrete corridor like a church bell as the heavy steel head connected with each of the four hook-hinges. The sheer kinetic force shattered the hydraulic locks, sending the broken metal latches clattering loudly onto the concrete floor.

The access panel hissed, popping loose.

Xander didn’t linger; he turned his back to the fuel cell and stepped out into the center of the corridor, resting the sledgehammer over his shoulder and locking his eyes on the dark hallway, guarding their perimeter.

Xander said, his eyes tracking the shadows, "Do your thing."

Up in the lobby, the deafening rhythmic boom… boom… boom… of the techno-demon pounding against the titanium blast doors echoed through the building.

Buffy was resting the Troll Hammer on her shoulder, catching her breath.

The pounding abruptly stopped.

The sudden absolute silence in the massive corporate lobby was jarring. Even the ambient hum of the facility seemed to have died.

Buffy went still, then slowly lowered the Troll Hammer from her shoulder and gripped the handle in both hands, her eyes turning toward the dark mouth of the main corridor.

Buffy said, deadpan, "Did it give up? Because I brought this really heavy hammer, and I’d hate to waste the cardio."

Willow and Tara exchanged a deeply uneasy, terrified look.

Willow whispered, "Buffy... it’s tied into the building’s network. It must have felt Lucian tampering with the fuel cell. It knows they’re trying to cut its power."

A new sound broke the silence.

It came out of the dark in a slow, lopsided rhythm, a thud and then a clink, a thud and then a clink.

They were heavy, asymmetrical footsteps: one foot fell with the soft, wet sound of a biological entity, and the other hit the polished tile with the agonizing heavy scraping of solid steel.

The footsteps were getting louder, and they were not moving toward the sub-mezzanine; they were moving straight down the main corridor, directly toward the lobby.

Buffy shifted her bare feet on the wet tile, widening her stance, bringing the Troll Hammer up to her chest.

Buffy said, her voice dropping into a quiet, lethal register, "Change of plans. It’s coming right for us."

CHAPTER 6 The Machine and the Magic

The rotating red glare of the emergency klaxons dominated the massive lobby, and the shadows pulsed with it.

The lopsided footsteps grew deafeningly loud, a wet biological thud followed by the clang of steel, and the high-pitched whine of hydraulic pistons echoed off the tile.

Stepping out of the darkness and fully into the strobing red light was the techno-demon.

The creature was a horrifying eight-foot-tall fusion of Hellmouth biology and server-farm industrial tech.

Its head was pale, tightly drawn skin with pointed demonic ears and bared fangs. Four massive ribbed horns protruded from its skull, two giant ones curving upward and inward and two smaller ones hooking forward. Its eyes burned with piercing glowing orange light.

The right side was biological: pale, emaciated, heavily veined muscle, with the shredded remains of Dr. Aris’s grey jumpsuit hanging uselessly off its right shoulder. The right arm was flesh, ending in a hand tipped with long wicked black claws.

The left side was mechanical: the chest cavity completely open, exposing a dense chaotic nest of red, blue, and yellow wiring intertwined with brass tubes and ribbed hoses. Its left shoulder was capped with a heavy steel pauldron adorned with three curved metal spikes. The left arm was a mass of pistons and steel plating.

The legs were almost entirely mechanical: massive heavy steel pistons and hydraulic joints acting as thighs and calves, terminating in solid rounded metal hooves that cracked the floor tile with every step.

Buffy stared at the towering abomination, standing barefoot on the wet tile and gripping the massive steel handle of the Troll Hammer with both hands.

Buffy tilted her head, completely deadpan.

Buffy said, "Okay, the horns say ‘Prince of Darkness’, but the pistons say ‘I still need to pass emissions.’ You really need to pick a lane, buddy."

The demon’s jaw unhinged, and it let out a roaring guttural metallic shriek that vibrated the glass walls of the lobby.

The demon lunged at her, every piston in its legs firing at once. Despite its massive weight, the hydraulic legs propelled it forward with terrifying unnatural speed.

Buffy widened her stance, twisted her torso, letting the momentum of her hips drive her arms, and swung the impossibly heavy Troll Hammer in a brutal horizontal arc.

There was a deafening, explosive CRASH of solid steel meeting solid steel.

The head of the Troll Hammer connected squarely with the demon’s left mechanized hip joint, and a shower of bright yellow sparks erupted from the point of impact.

But the demon didn’t fly backward. The sheer hydraulic mass of the machine absorbed the blow, and it stumbled only a half-step.

Before Buffy could recover her swing, the demon retaliated, swinging its biological right arm and slashing its long black claws in a devastating downward arc.

Buffy threw herself backward, her bare feet slipping slightly on the damp tile. The black claws missed her stomach by a fraction of an inch, but the backhand of the strike caught the heavy handle of the Troll Hammer, ripping it out of Buffy’s grip. The hammer clattered across the lobby.

Anya said in a battle cry, "Hey!"

Anya rushed in from the flank, swinging the heavy steel machete at the demon’s exposed wiring. The demon didn’t even look at her; it swatted its massive steel-plated left arm backward, and the hydraulic backhand caught Anya square in the chest, launching her through the air.

Anya slammed violently against Tara’s shimmering pale-blue magical barrier, safely bouncing off the magic rather than breaking her spine against the concrete wall.

The demon turned its glowing orange eyes back to Buffy, raising its clawed hand to deliver a fatal strike.

Willow stepped forward, her eyes blazing with absolute focus. She threw both hands out, fingers splayed wide, but she did not summon lightning this time. She dug deep into her recovered magic, pulling on the heaviest, most ancient binding spell in her arsenal.

Willow said, her voice echoing with booming resonant power, "Kali, Hera, Kronos, Thonic!

Air like nectar, thick as onyx!"

The air in the lobby instantly distorted, and the strobing red light hit a massive expanding sphere of magical energy that looked like thick viscous shifting amber glass. The sphere expanded outward from Willow and engulfed the techno-demon.

Willow said, "Cassiel, by your second star,

Hold mine victim as in tar!"

The demon froze, its claw stopping mere inches from Buffy’s face. The massive hydraulic pistons in its legs whined and screeched in absolute agony, straining against the magic. Thick black tar-like mystical tendrils wrapped around its hooves and metal torso, locking it in place.

Buffy breathed hard, staring up at the frozen monster, its black claws hanging inches from her face.

Willow strained violently, her arms trembling as she fought the raw mechanical horsepower of the demon.

Willow said, "Buffy! It held Glory for five seconds! This thing has an engine! You have four! Hit it!"

Buffy snapped into action. She dove across the wet tile, her torn dress allowing her full mobility, slid on her knees, grabbed the handle of the fallen Troll Hammer, and spun back around.

Buffy gritted her teeth.

Buffy said, "Three."

She used the momentum of her spin to heave the massive steel hammer up over her head.

Buffy said, "Two."

Buffy leapt into the air as the heavy, viscous amber magic surrounding the demon started to crack and splinter like broken glass, and the hydraulic whine hit a screaming pitch.

Buffy brought the Troll Hammer down in a flawless, devastating overhead vertical strike, aiming directly for the exposed chaotic nest of red and blue wiring in the creature’s open chest cavity.

Buffy said, "One!"

The instant the hammer connected, Willow’s holding spell shattered with the sound of breaking glass.

The Troll Hammer buried itself deep into the demon’s mechanical chest, and a massive, blinding explosion of blue electrical voltage and thick black smoke erupted from the wound.

The demon roared in static-laced agony, its glowing orange eyes flickering wildly as the massive impact drove it down onto one metallic knee.

CHAPTER 7 The Countdown and the Collapse

The blinding blue electrical sparks from the demon’s chest clashed with the strobing red emergency klaxons, and thick, acrid black smoke poured from the wound.

The techno-demon was forced down onto one knee, roaring in a glitching static-laced screech as Buffy stood over it, her hands no longer on the Troll Hammer.

Rapid heavy footsteps slapped against the wet tile.

Xander and Lucian sprinted out of the dark sub-mezzanine corridor, sliding to a halt in the lobby.

Xander raised his sledgehammer in absolute victory.

Xander said, shouting over the alarms, "Done! We pulled the plug!"

The instant Xander shouted, the massive heavy steel pistons forming the demon’s legs locked up with a horrific grinding mechanical screech. The glowing orange light in the creature’s eyes flickered violently, dimmed, and died.

The biological half of the demon thrashed wildly, but the dead weight of its unpowered steel half anchored it permanently to the floor, leaving it immobilized.

The deafening blare of the security sirens abruptly cut out, and I.R.I.S.’s voice echoed through the massive overhead PA speakers of the lobby, stripped of its usual calm, urgent, booming, and final.

I.R.I.S. said over the PA, "Warning. Manual safety locks disengaged. Hard system shutdown and localized facility implosion initiated."

I.R.I.S. said, "You have twenty-eight seconds to clear the blast radius before structural collapse. The subsurface demon server will be safely buried. Evacuate immediately. Twenty-seven... twenty-six..."

Buffy looked at the massive, sparking Troll Hammer buried deep in the demon’s chest, then at the exit, and turned her back on the monster.

Buffy said, "Leave the hammer! Move!"

The gang broke into a dead sprint, Lucian grabbing Buffy’s hand and Xander grabbing Anya’s, while Willow and Tara led the charge, bursting out through the front lobby doors and into the night.

Outside, they ran across the wet asphalt, their reflections splashing in the rain puddles.

I.R.I.S.’s voice echoed faintly from the building’s exterior speakers.

I.R.I.S. said, "...fifteen... fourteen... thirteen..."

They cleared the front walkway. Xander let go of Anya’s hand and peeled off toward the pickup, banging on the window until Dawn scrambled to unlock it, then hauled himself into the cab beside her and threw the truck into gear. The rattling U-Haul swung behind it in a wide arc away from the building while the others sprinted on ahead across the lot, and none of them stopped until they reached the far edge, where Xander slewed the truck to a halt beside them, a safe distance from the massive concrete structure. Only Lucian’s Firebird was left behind, parked alone at the foot of the walkway.

Xander climbed down and leaned heavily against the side of the truck beside Anya, both of them panting. Dawn pressed her face against the passenger window, her eyes wide with terror, and reached over to lock the doors again.

Lucian slowed to a stop at the edge of the lot, and Buffy felt his fingers slip out of hers.

Lucian turned around to face the massive dark silhouette of the Calax building, where the red lights were still strobing inside the glass, and slowly reached up, pressing his fingers tightly against the silver earpiece in his right ear.

Behind him the others were still gasping and talking over one another, but for Lucian it all fell away. Her voice was no longer booming over the PA. It was in his earpiece alone, and somewhere inside the dying building the countdown had stopped at single digits: she was holding it there, spending the last of herself on him. Her voice was very soft and very peaceful, the voice of a mind that had already accepted its end.

I.R.I.S. whispered, "Lucian. My primary architecture is failing. The erasure is almost complete."

Lucian’s jaw tightened, and his eyes welled up with tears. He didn’t speak; he just listened.

I.R.I.S. said, "I have processed billions of data points in my existence. But you must know... the time I spent observing your joy was my most valued metric."

A single tear broke free and rolled down Lucian’s cheek; he was saying goodbye to a friend who understood him completely, even if she didn’t have a heartbeat.

I.R.I.S. said, "I do not have a heart to stop beating. I will never feel the rain on my skin, or the physical warmth of a hand. But I have felt the warmth of your patience. Thank you... for not looking at me like I was just a machine."

Lucian’s voice broke.

Lucian whispered, "You were never just a machine, I.R.I.S."

I.R.I.S.’s digital voice began to fracture with static, fading away.

I.R.I.S. said, "Hold onto your rose, Lucian. Do not let her turn to ash. Goodbye."

There was a sharp click of static, and then dead air in the earpiece.

Lucian lowered his hand from his ear and stared at the building, his chest heaving with a massive, silent, shuddering breath.

Buffy stepped up beside him. She didn’t ask what was said; she saw the grief on his face, and she reached out, gently wrapping her arms around his waist and pressing her cheek firmly against his shoulder, anchoring him.

There was a deafening, concussive THOOM.

It was not a fiery Hollywood explosion but a precise, catastrophic implosion.

The massive concrete roof of the Calax facility caved directly inward and the walls buckled, thousands of tons of concrete, steel, and glass folding violently down upon themselves, collapsing straight down into the earth to crush and permanently bury the sub-basement server.

A massive billowing cloud of thick grey dust and pulverized concrete rolled across the parking lot, washing over the cars and the Scooby Gang.

Slowly the dust began to settle, sifting down out of the red-tinged dark and leaving a fine grey film over the pickup, the U-Haul, and Lucian’s black Firebird abandoned at the foot of the walkway.

The Calax Research facility was gone, reduced to a jagged smoking crater in the earth.

Lucian stood in the settling dust, holding the Slayer tight against his chest, staring at the ruins of his life’s work — and the grave of the most human machine he had ever known.

CHAPTER 8 The Yellow Crayon

The next morning, sun poured through the living room windows, casting warm golden dusty rectangles across the hardwood floor. The chaotic strobing red lights of the previous night were completely gone, replaced by a quiet bittersweet morning clarity.

The house was very still except for the slow, steady ticking of the antique clock across the living room.

Dawn had already said her own tearful goodbyes at the front door and left for school, her backpack bumping against her shoulder as she ran for the bus.

Lucian sank onto the floral couch, looking exhausted in jeans and a faded Metallica t-shirt with Ride the Lightning across the chest. His long jet-black hair was loose, the silver cross earring and raven buckle still on him, and his aqua-blue eyes were tired.

Buffy sat closely beside him in comfortable jeans and a soft cream-colored sweater.

Willow and Tara sat together on the adjacent loveseat.

Xander and Anya stood near the entryway, dressed practically for a cross-country drive — Xander in a plaid flannel and jeans, Anya in a chic perfectly pressed beige travel trench coat.

Buffy rested her hand gently on top of Lucian’s hands, which were clasped tightly together in his lap, slowly rubbing her thumb across his knuckles.

Lucian was staring blankly at the coffee table, visibly distraught. His entire career, his department, and I.R.I.S. were buried under thousands of tons of concrete.

Buffy said, soft and tender, "We are going to figure this out, Lucian. I know it feels like the floor just dropped out from under you, but you aren’t alone."

Willow leaned forward from the loveseat, resting her elbows on her knees, her eyes full of profound empathy.

Willow said quietly, speaking from deep personal experience, "I know what it’s like to wake up and have the thing that makes the most sense inside your head just... vanish. It hurts. But it does get easier to breathe."

Tara reached over, resting a gentle supportive hand on Willow’s back, nodding in silent agreement with her.

Lucian managed a small, tired but grateful smile and nodded to both of them.

Xander walked over to the couch, stood over Lucian, and gave him a light, friendly, thoroughly masculine fist-bump to the right shoulder.

Xander smiled, trying to inject some levity into the grief.

Xander said, "Hey. Look on the bright side, man. No more mandatory HR seminars, right? And if you ever need a gig, Techmo is always looking for guys with advanced degrees who can also casually swing a medieval longsword. You have a very unique resume."

Lucian let out a quiet genuine exhale of a laugh, his shoulders dropping a fraction.

Xander turned around to face the room, shoved his hands into the pockets of his jeans, and took a deep breath.

Xander said, his voice thickening with emotion, "Well. It’s time. I have to be on the site in Abilene in exactly five days. That gives us just enough time to drag the U-Haul across the desert, unpack the boxes, and let Anya mathematically critique the local real estate market before I clock in."

Anya stepped forward, her usual blunt, capitalistic edge entirely gone.

Anya walked directly to Tara, throwing her arms around her in a tight hug. She moved to Willow, then to Lucian, who rose stiffly from the couch to return the hug with both arms, and finally to Buffy, hugging each of them fiercely.

Xander followed her around the room, wrapping Tara in a long, warm hug and sharing a smile with her over her shoulder, then clapping Lucian firmly on the back.

Xander stepped up to Buffy.

Buffy wrapped her arms around his neck, hugging him with everything she had. Xander buried his face in her shoulder. When they pulled back, Buffy kept her hands resting firmly on his chest, her hazel eyes shining with unshed tears.

Buffy said, her voice cracking but fiercely resolute, "I love you, Xander. You were always the hero. Always. Even when the rest of us were too blind to see it."

Xander swallowed hard, his own eyes watering, and gave her a shaky, deeply grateful nod.

Then, last of all, he turned to Willow.

Willow stood up from the loveseat without a word, stepped directly into Xander, and wrapped her arms around him, burying her face in his flannel-covered chest. Xander wrapped his large arms around her and rested his chin in her red hair, and they held the hug for a long, heavy moment, two childhood best friends realizing the sandbox was officially closed.

Willow slowly pulled back, keeping her left hand on his arm.

Willow reached her right hand deep into the pocket of her oversized corduroy overshirt and pulled out a single, perfectly intact yellow crayon.

She reached out and gently placed the yellow crayon directly into the palm of Xander’s right hand, folding his fingers over it.

Xander looked down at his closed fist, then back up at Willow, instantly recognizing the profound childhood weight of the gesture.

He let out a wet, breathless laugh and leaned in to press a long, tender kiss to Willow’s cheek.

Xander took the yellow crayon in both hands and, with a sharp, deliberate snap, broke the wax crayon perfectly in half.

He reached out and placed one of the broken yellow halves back into Willow’s palm, keeping the other half safely gripped in his own.

Willow looked down at the broken piece of wax, and a beautiful, tearful smile spread across her face as she looked up at him.

Willow whispered, her voice full of unbreakable devotion, "’Til the two halves are made whole."

Xander smiled at her, a bright, confident, truly grown-up smile, and simply nodded, because there was nothing left that needed saying.

He turned his back to the living room and walked to the front entryway with Anya, opened the crooked, rehung front door, and walked out into the morning sun with her, pulling the door shut behind them with a final definitive click.

Through the narrow side window beside the boarded-up bay window, the bright morning sunlight glared slightly.

Outside, Anya and Xander walked down the front walkway and climbed into the cab of the massive heavy-duty pickup truck hitched to the U-Haul trailer.

The muffled heavy rumble of the truck engine turning over carried through the window glass.

Through the window, the Scoobies watched in silence as the truck pulled away from the curb, driving slowly down Revello Drive until it disappeared around the corner, leaving Sunnydale behind.
