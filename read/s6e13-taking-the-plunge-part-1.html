Taking the Plunge Part 1

Download this script (.txt)

Buffy the Vampire Slayer. Season six, episode 13. Taking the Plunge Part 1.

CHAPTER 1 The Deep End

Out on the Sunnydale coastline that night it was so dark that the sea and the sky ran together into one black nothing. The only sound out there was a heavy, rhythmic splashing that didn’t sound like normal surf at all. Every crash of the water came in perfect time with something wet and guttural and echoing, like a huge set of lungs somewhere out in the black taking a deep, rattling breath. Under the moon the water looked like shifting oil, and a long way further down the beach a warm, glowing light waited in the distance.

That light was a big driftwood bonfire with half a dozen bamboo tiki torches stuck straight down into the sand around it, and the orange of it spilled out across the beach in a warm, flickering ring. Anywhere near that ring the breathing out on the water was drowned under the thumping bass of an upbeat pop-punk song blasting out of a silver boombox. Drunken college laughter rolled over the top of it, and glass bottles clinked together all around the fire.

Thirteen college kids were partying around that fire, loud and careless and alive the way only California kids on a beach at night could be. They were in bikini tops and board shorts and open flannels, dancing close to the flames, double-fisting red plastic Solo cups, feeding driftwood into the fire, and strumming a guitar nobody was listening to. A tall, lanky guy with heavily frosted tips was showing off in an unbuttoned Hawaiian shirt and a puka shell necklace.

Chloe was twenty, with a soft, curvy build and beautiful, deep brown skin, her thick shoulder-length braids done up with tiny gold cuffs that glinted every time she moved her head. She had a bright yellow bikini top on under an oversized, heavily faded denim shirt that hung open over frayed cut-off jean shorts. She was laughing with her head thrown back and a half-empty red Solo cup in one hand, the firelight catching the gold in her hair. She reached out with the other and gave the guy with the frosted tips a playful shove in the chest.

Chloe said, "Shut up, Brad! You did not jump off the pier! You’re terrified of seaweed!"

Brad stumbled back a step, grinning and throwing both hands up in defense of his honor.

Brad said, "I did! It was high tide! Ask Jason, he saw the whole thing!"

Chloe rolled her eyes, still smiling, and took a sip from her cup. The heat of the bonfire had her sweating now, her skin glistening a little in the firelight, and she pinched the front of her open shirt with two fingers and flapped it against her chest to get some air.

Chloe said, "Whatever. You guys are sweating me out. I’m gonna go cool my feet off."

She turned her back on the bonfire and the pounding pop-punk and walked away from the safety of the warm light, heading down the sloping sand toward the wet, dark shoreline. The further she went the more the orange glow on her face faded, until only the cold, pale blue of the moon was left on her skin. The song and the laughter behind her sank down into a muffled hum.

At the edge of the water she stopped, the wet sand squishing pleasantly up between her bare toes. She took a deep, easy breath of the salty ocean air and looked out over the vast black stretch of the Pacific. Then, for a split second, the song behind her dropped out. In the quiet the heavy, wet, rattling breath came back from the dark water, much closer now, as if it were coming from just underneath the crashing waves.

Chloe frowned and lowered her red Solo cup, squinting out into the darkness.

Chloe said, "Hello? Is someone out there?"

The ocean answered by pulling away from her. The water receded fast from the shoreline with a loud, sucking hiss and left a wide stretch of wet, glistening sand bare in front of her feet. Just beyond the break line, out where the water would have been chest-deep on a man, a huge, unnatural shadow breached the surface. It didn’t splash. It rose smoothly, thick and muscular and unmistakably shaped like a person, but with every proportion wrong. For a fraction of a second thick, translucent webbing caught the moonlight while a row of sharp, jagged spines broke the black water along its back.

Chloe froze. The cup slipped out of her fingers and hit the wet sand, splashing cheap beer over her toes. Out in the water the dark shape slowly began to stand up, rising taller than any normal man, water pouring off its scaled shoulders, and the wet, rattling breath hissed through the dark toward her.

CHAPTER 2 The Co-op Couch

In the Summers living room that Saturday morning, sunlight was streaming in through the side windows and around the edges of the plywood still nailed over the bay window, filling the room with a lazy, homey glow that had nothing of the beach in it. The whole downstairs rang with the rapid electronic punching and cartoon explosions of a video game, along with the frantic plastic clack-clack-clack of two people mashing buttons as hard as their thumbs would let them.

Dawn and Xander were lying flat on their stomachs on the living room rug in front of the glowing old television, two classic gray PlayStation controllers tethered to the console by tangled black cords that stretched across the carpet. On the screen was a bright, slightly pixelated side-scrolling beat-’em-up called Urban Enforcers: Mutant Mayhem, and two muscular cartoon brawlers were punching a glowing neon slime monster up and down an alleyway. Dawn was deep in the zone, her knees bent and her sock feet kicking together in the air behind her in a steady rhythm while her thumbs flew over the D-pad and the buttons with fierce, competitive focus. Beside her Xander was leaning hard to the left, his whole upper body tilting over without him even knowing it, as if moving his real shoulders would somehow help his little guy dodge.

Xander said, eyes locked on the screen and practically shouting at it, "Dawnie, cover the left flank! The radioactive trash cans are respawning! Hit them with the spinning piledriver!"

Dawn’s tongue poked out of the corner of her mouth in concentration as she jammed her thumb down on the triangle button over and over.

Dawn said, "I can’t! My special meter is completely empty because someone hogged the giant floating pizza slice!"

Xander didn’t let up on the buttons for a second, his elbows digging into the rug.

Xander said, "I needed the health! My guy was flashing red and making the annoying beeping sound! It’s tactical survival, Dawn, I’m the tank!"

Buffy was sitting dead center on the couch in her sweatpants with her legs tucked up underneath her, holding an oversized yellow ceramic mug in both hands and letting the heat of it soak into her palms. She wasn’t upset, and she wasn’t brooding; she was just profoundly bored. She took a slow sip of her coffee and stared over their heads at the blinking chaos on the screen. When you fought real monsters with your own two hands every night, digital violence just looked like a bunch of noisy, flashing pixels. Her eyes were half-lidded, and her mind was clearly somewhere else entirely.

She lowered the yellow mug a few inches.

Buffy said in a flat deadpan, "You guys have been punching the same glowing trash can for twenty minutes. How is this saving the digital world?"

Xander didn’t even blink. He refused to take his eyes off the screen.

Xander said, "It’s a boss battle, B! It requires endurance! And flawless pattern recognition!"

Dawn leaned forward on her elbows and mashed the buttons even harder.

Dawn said, "Yeah, Buffy! It’s about teamwork. Watch the sludge attack, Xander, move right!"

Buffy sighed softly, and in spite of not caring one bit about the game, a small, fond smile touched the corner of her mouth. She rested her chin near the rim of the mug and watched the two of them squirm and yell at the television. It was a wonderfully ordinary Saturday morning in the Summers house, and after the weeks they had all just come through, the plain, boring normalcy of it felt like a luxury.

CHAPTER 3 The Texas Anxiety

Up in the little laundry closet off the upstairs hall that morning, the washing machine was filling with a loud, sloshing rush of water, its heavy mechanical hum thrumming up through the floorboards like a steady pulse. A big, overflowing wicker laundry basket sat on the floor between Willow and Tara.

Willow was barefoot in a soft, slouchy cotton shirt, her red hair pulled back in a messy low ponytail. She was sorting clothes with jerky, nervous energy, snatching t-shirts and jeans out of the pile and practically throwing them into the open mouth of the washer. Tara stood calmly beside her in a cream cardigan and a long brown skirt, a bottle of liquid detergent held loosely in both hands, watching Willow’s frantic sorting with a soft, patient, understanding look on her face.

Willow’s hands were a blur, and so was her mouth.

Willow said, "It’s just... Texas, Tara. Texas! It’s geographically massive. And dusty. And what if he completely assimilates? What if he starts wearing oversized belt buckles and saying ‘howdy’ unironically? I don’t think my nervous system can handle Xander in a ten-gallon hat."

Tara unscrewed the cap, carefully measured out a capful of the blue detergent, and poured it smoothly into the running water before she answered, her voice gentle and endlessly patient.

Tara said, "Will... he’s just going to build things. He’s going to be an Assistant Supervisor. I don’t think Techmo requires cowboy boots for structural engineering."

Willow stopped dead and clutched a grey t-shirt to her chest with both hands.

Willow said, "I know. I’m not against it! I’m genuinely, really happy for him. And Anya. The wedding is going to be beautiful, assuming a hell-beast doesn’t eat the caterer. But... it means he’s actually leaving."

All of that frantic energy went out of her at once. She dropped the t-shirt into the machine, and when she spoke again her voice cracked a little around the edges.

Willow said, "He’s the glue, Tara. Giles is in England. Buffy is... Buffy. And Xander is moving a thousand miles away. What if the group just... breaks? What if we all just drift into our own separate lives and stop being us?"

Tara set the detergent bottle down on top of the dryer with a soft click and turned to face her, stepping in close. She reached out and rested both hands on Willow’s upper arms, holding her there and grounding her, her fingers relaxed and warm through the shirt. She looked straight into Willow’s eyes.

Tara said, "Families don’t break just because the address changes, sweetie. You and Xander have been best friends since kindergarten. A state line isn’t going to sever that. He’s growing up. We all are. And it’s scary... but it’s a good scary."

Willow looked back at her and took a deep, shuddering breath, letting Tara’s quiet certainty settle all the way down over her.

Willow whispered, "A good scary. Okay."

Tara smiled softly and gave her arms one small squeeze before she let go.

Tara said, "Okay. Now, let’s finish the darks before Buffy realizes we shrank her favorite sweater."

She turned back to the wicker basket, bent down, and pulled out a tangled handful of dark fabric. As she lifted it the knot fell loose, and she found herself holding up the bright red, heart-covered boxer shorts by the waistband, the loaners she had pulled out of Xander’s emergency stash for Lucian, which he had clearly never given back. They looked ridiculous dangling from her fingers in the middle of that plain little laundry closet. Tara paused, looked down at the boxers, and then looked over at Willow, and not one muscle in her face moved.

Tara said, "Well. At least we know the new guy is fully committed to the romantic aesthetic. Do you think he wears these while brooding, or is it strictly a weekend look?"

Willow stared at the bright red hearts, and a sharp snort of laughter burst out of her before she could stop it. She clapped a hand over her mouth, her eyes crinkling up as real, bubbling laughter finally broke through all the worry, and she wiped at the corners of her eyes with her knuckles.

Willow said through her giggles, "Oh my god. Please just put them in the wash. If Buffy catches us analyzing his underwear, she’ll stake us both on principle."

Tara smiled, bright and quietly triumphant at having pulled Willow out of her spiral, dropped the heart-covered boxers into the washer, and pushed the heavy lid shut with a solid, final clang.

CHAPTER 4 The Monthly Curse and the Morning News

In the downstairs bathroom under the stairs, just off the living room on the way to the kitchen, Buffy was sitting on the toilet with her grey sweatpants pushed down around her knees and a blank expression on her face, staring at the bathroom tiles. The distinct, sharp crinkle of a plastic tampon wrapper being torn open filled the little room. She dipped one shoulder and breathed out slowly, and her arm moved down between her legs. A minute later she stood up with a lightly annoyed look on her face, shifted her hips, and pulled her sweatpants back up.

She stepped over to the porcelain sink and, without even looking down, dropped the crumpled wrapper into the small wicker trash can tucked away under the counter. She turned on the faucet, and as she held her hands under the warm water, a tiny, stark streak of dark red blood showed on her fingertip. She watched it wash away and swirl down the silver drain. She let out a long, ordinary sigh, the sigh of a woman dealing with her cycle on top of saving the world, turned the water off, grabbed the hand towel, and pulled the bathroom door open.

Xander was standing right on the other side of it in the hallway. He looked pale. The goofy, relaxed guy who had been sprawled on the living room rug was gone, and in his place was somebody standing rigid and wide-eyed, with worry all over his face.

Buffy didn’t even flinch. She just quirked one blonde eyebrow at him.

Buffy said, "Not again... what now?"

Xander rubbed the back of his neck and tried to deliver the bad news like it was no big deal, his voice pitching up higher as he went.

Xander said, "Well, you know how we were having a perfectly nice, completely un-apocalyptic Saturday? Yeah, the universe called. It wants its peace and quiet back. Channel Six just interrupted the morning cartoons. There’s a breaking news bulletin about the beach. It has all the classic hallmarks of a major Sunnydale situation."

Buffy dropped the hand towel on the edge of the sink, wiped her still-damp hands on the back of her sweatpants, and pushed past him, heading straight into the living room.

She and Xander came quickly into the living room. Dawn was sitting cross-legged on the floor with the video game paused on the screen, clutching a throw pillow to her chest with both arms and staring at the television. The grim, overly serious voice of a local news anchor had taken over the room. Channel Six was showing live helicopter footage of the same beach from the night before, the sand cordoned off with bright yellow police tape and the black remains of the big driftwood bonfire still smoldering.

The anchor said over the pictures, "...authorities are closing off a two-mile stretch of the Sunnydale coastline this morning after a horrific scene was discovered at a college beach bonfire. Police have confirmed three fatalities, with at least ten other students currently missing. While official details are scarce, forensic teams on the scene have reportedly recovered large quantities of what appears to be shed, mutated human skin..."

Xander froze at the foot of the staircase, and the blood drained out of his face.

Xander whispered, "Shed... human skin. At the beach. Please tell me we aren’t doing a sequel to the Sunnydale Swim Team."

Buffy’s jaw set, and her hazel eyes darkened as the memory of those grotesque, mutated high school swimmers from years ago clicked into place behind them. Her voice came out flat and absolutely certain.

Buffy said, "Fish men."

The safe little Saturday morning shattered. Buffy stepped forward, planted a hand on the wooden banister, tipped her head back toward the second floor, and let her commanding Slayer voice ring through the whole house.

Buffy yelled, "Willow! Get down here!"

CHAPTER 5 The Headbutt and the Heist

Up in the laundry closet the wash had finished, and Willow and Tara were bent over the machines side by side. They were hauling damp laundry out with wet thwacks and tossing it into the dryer while they worked together at a stubborn knot of wet jeans and towels. Buffy’s voice came up the stairs again, louder this time.

Buffy yelled, "Willow! Get down here, we have trouble again!"

The sudden, commanding yell made Willow startle hard, and she jerked straight upright in the cramped space. There was a sharp, painful crack as the top of her head smashed right into the underside of Tara’s jaw. Tara’s head snapped back, the wet towel dropped out of her hands, and she brought a hand straight up to cup her chin with a sharp wince.

Willow’s eyes went wide, and the panic hit her all at once, both hands flying up in the air without knowing where to go.

Willow said, "Oh my god... Tara... I am sorry, baby..."

Tara kept her hand pressed firmly against her jaw and squeezed her eyes shut for a second, riding out the sting. Then she opened them and gave Willow a reassuring, if slightly strained, smile. She waved her free hand toward the hallway in a little shooing motion.

Tara said through her fingers, rubbing at her chin, "I’m okay. M-my tongue is intact. Just go."

Willow leaned in fast and pressed a gentle, apologetic kiss onto Tara’s sore chin, then turned and practically sprinted out of the laundry closet, her bare feet thudding quickly down the hallway.

She came jogging down the wooden stairs gripping the banister, already breathless before she reached the last few steps.

Willow said, "I’m here! Demon? Vampire? Did Dawn try and cook something again?"

Dawn glared at her from the floor for half a second before turning back to the TV. Buffy was standing by the television with her arms crossed, already in full Slayer mode, and Xander was pacing back and forth behind the couch, looking distinctly nauseous.

Buffy didn’t bother with hello.

Buffy said, "Worse. Beach party massacre last night. Three college kids dead, ten missing. The cops have the whole stretch of sand cordoned off."

Xander jabbed a finger at the screen.

Xander said, "And they found skin, Will. Shed, mutated human skin. The Sunnydale Swim Team is apparently hosting a reunion tour… and they’re hungry."

Willow stopped at the bottom of the stairs. Her brow furrowed as she forced herself into analytical mode, her hands already starting to move in the air the way they always did when she was thinking out loud. She was trying, maybe a little too hard, to sound like the old, confident Willow.

Willow said, "Fish men? But the original mutations were localized entirely to the high school swim team’s steroid cocktail. If they’re back and shedding, that means the mutation isn’t just holding — it’s sustaining itself. Or evolving."

She paced a tight little circle near the bottom step, biting at her thumbnail, and then pulled the thumb away from her teeth to point with it.

Willow said, "Okay, wait — we can’t just go in swinging. If they’re shedding skin, we absolutely need a sample. If I can get my hands on the epidermal casing, I can run a full cellular degradation analysis in the chem lab. I can cross-reference the mutagenic DNA sequencing with the original high school bio-markers. That’ll tell us if it’s the same brood… or if they’re breeding."

Xander stared at her, blinking slowly.

Xander said, "I understood about four words of that, but ‘breeding’ was one of them and now I want to throw up my cereal."

Buffy still had her arms crossed, and her face was grim.

Buffy said, "I remember Coach Marin trapping me down there with the whole monster swim team. He said they’d already eaten… and that his boys had ‘other needs.’"

Dawn made a disgusted gulping sound from the floor.

Willow stopped pacing. She suddenly looked nervous, and her fingers twisted into the hem of her shirt, but she pushed through it, trying to keep her voice steady and useful.

Willow said, "But… the cops will have already bagged the scene. The skin’s going to be locked up as bio-evidence. Which means…"

She looked over at Buffy with wide, worried eyes.

Willow said, "Which means one of us is going to have to sneak into the police station." She paused, and her voice came out smaller.

Willow said, "Again. And do an evidence locker heist."

Buffy nodded slowly, a determined, tactical glint coming into her eyes.

Buffy said, "Everyone pile into the car..."

Then she looked up at the ceiling for a moment and rolled her eyes just once.

Buffy said, "Here we go again."

CHAPTER 6 The Zombie Distraction and the Basement Drop

Inside the Sunnydale police station lobby that afternoon, the precinct hummed along the way it always did, telephones ringing somewhere in the back offices, but the area around the main booking desk had gone strangely quiet. Dawn was standing front and center, leaning with both forearms on the counter, and Xander stood right behind her with his arms crossed, nodding with deep, exaggerated solemnity like a very concerned guardian. On the other side of the counter the desk sergeant and three other uniformed officers were all leaning in, hanging on every word, one of them with his pen frozen in the air over a little notepad.

Dawn was gesturing wildly with both hands, totally invested in her story.

Dawn said, "...so then, the starting quarterback just completely loses his mind. He practically dives over the mashed potato station, grabs the middle linebacker by the letterman jacket, and screams that I am the sun. Like, the actual celestial body."

The desk sergeant blinked at her, entirely lost.

The desk sergeant said, "The sun?"

Dawn threw both hands up in the air.

Dawn said, "Yes! And then it just turned into a massive, groaning zombie pile-up. Like forty teenage boys just crawling over each other to get to the fire exit. It was a completely unprovoked, hormonally-driven riot. I think my shoe is still in there."

Xander shook his head gravely.

Xander said, "It’s a crisis of modern education, Sergeant. The cafeteria infrastructure simply cannot handle that level of unchecked pubescent aggression. We need to file a full incident report. In triplicate."

The cops traded baffled looks, sucked into the bizarre logistics of the story, and not one of them was looking at the glass double doors that led to the internal stairwell.

Just beyond that glass, two figures slipped silently down the concrete stairs toward the basement. Willow and Buffy were walking side by side, but they didn’t look like themselves. A faint, see-through blue static wrapped around both of their bodies. Underneath it they were still just Buffy in her sweatpants and Willow in her shirt, but their reflections in the stairwell’s safety glass showed two forensic technicians in crisp white lab coats, carrying clipboards.

Willow’s hands were glowing faintly blue down at her sides.

Willow whispered, "Keep your head down. The perception filter holds up to casual glances, but it won’t hold up to a direct conversation."

Buffy moved down the stairs with silent, predatory grace.

Buffy whispered back, "Just get us to the evidence locker. I want to be in and out before Xander starts monologuing about the decline of school lunches."

They reached the bottom of the stairs and stepped out into a dim cinderblock hallway, and thirty feet ahead of them was a heavy steel door marked "EVIDENCE".

Then Willow stopped dead in her tracks. Her eyes went wide, and she pressed the heel of her hand hard against her temple as a sharp phantom pain, the backlash from erasing her own memory five nights ago, spiked straight through her head. She swayed on her feet, her face going pale.

Willow said, "Oh... wow. Vertigo."

The magic came apart. The blue static around them flickered like a dying fluorescent bulb, and then with a sharp electric snap the whole illusion shattered into fading blue sparks. It left the two of them standing in their own clothes in a restricted hallway where anybody could see them.

Buffy turned to her.

Buffy said, "Will? What happened?"

Before Willow could answer, a heavy set of footsteps echoed around the corner, and a precinct guard in his thirties, solidly built, turned into the hallway with a ring of keys in his hand. He stopped short, looked at Buffy’s sweatpants, and then looked at Willow.

The guard said, "Hey! You can’t be down here!"

He dropped his hand toward the heavy black radio on his duty belt to call for backup. He never even touched it.

Buffy exploded forward. She used the narrow hallway against him, taking two quick steps, planting her right sneaker flat on the cinderblock wall, and launching herself sideways through the air. The guard barely had time to flinch before she landed behind him. Her left hand clamped over his mouth to smother his yell while her right arm snaked under his chin and locked into a textbook sleeper hold. She didn’t crush his windpipe. She put precise, careful pressure on the arteries in his neck and held him steady on his feet, her face calm, while his eyes rolled back. She leaned in close to his ear, sounding genuinely sorry about it.

Buffy whispered, "Sorry about this. Nothing personal. You’re just doing your job, but I’m heavily caffeinated and on a very tight schedule. Have a good nap."

The guard went completely limp. Buffy caught his dead weight without any effort at all and dragged him three feet down the hall. She opened the door of a janitor’s closet and carefully propped him up against a rolling mop bucket so he wouldn’t hit his head, and then she gently shut the door and clicked it locked.

She turned back to Willow, not even breathing hard, and dusted her hands off against each other.

Buffy said, "Okay. Filter is down, but the coast is clear. Let’s get our gross fish skin and get out of here."

Willow stared at the closet door, blinking, in pure awe at how neatly and gently the Slayer had just put a grown man to sleep without hurting him.

CHAPTER 7 Ghosts in the Locker

Buffy turned toward the heavy steel door of the evidence locker, and behind her back Willow hesitated. She reached deep into the pocket of her shirt and pulled out a small, corked glass vial. A faint, unnatural orange glow came off the thick liquid inside it, throwing a warm but sickly light across her palm. Willow stared down at it for a second with a look on her face that was half temptation and half raw anxiety. When she heard Buffy’s footsteps stop, she shoved the glowing vial back deep into her pocket before Buffy could turn around.

Buffy picked the simple tumbler lock on the steel door with a pair of bobby pins in a matter of seconds, and the door creaked open onto pitch black. She reached in and flicked the wall switch, and rows of harsh, buzzing fluorescent tubes flickered on one after another overhead, showing long aisles of chain-link cages and metal shelving stacked high with bagged evidence. The air in there was dead still and smelled of old cardboard and sterile plastic.

Buffy whispered, "Split up. Look for bio-hazard bags tagged from the beach last night."

Willow nodded and darted off down the left aisle, her eyes moving quickly over the neat police tags. Buffy took the right. She walked slowly, her eyes tracking over stolen car stereos, bagged handguns, and bloody clothing, and then she passed one particular shelf, stopped, and took a slow step backward.

Resting inside a clear evidence bag on that shelf was a beautiful and deadly tribal dagger with an intricate gold and silver hilt covered in metal scales and sharp, hooked hand-guards. Its wide, double-pronged blade was cut through with jagged openings running down the center, and the silver steel was stained with old, crusted blood.

Buffy froze, and the breath went right out of her lungs. It was the knife she had used to stab Faith.

Her hand trembled a little as she slowly reached out. She didn’t pick it up, but her fingertips gently grazed the thick plastic right over the crusted blood on the jagged blade, and the memory hit her body like a blow. The buzz of the fluorescent lights dropped out of her hearing, and the howling wind of a storm took its place.

She was back on the rooftop of Faith’s apartment building at night, in cold rain and shadow, and she felt every second of it in her bones. She and Faith were locked in a frantic struggle near the edge of the roof, Buffy’s face a mask of desperate rage. With a sickening, wet thunk Buffy drove the golden-hilted dagger deep into Faith’s stomach and yanked the jagged blade back out.

Faith stumbled backward. The fierce, cocky rogue Slayer didn’t scream. Instead a calm, almost peaceful smile spread across her face as she looked at Buffy, her blood soaking dark into her shirt, and her voice seemed to echo strangely out over the wind.

Faith said, "You should’a been there, B..."

Then Faith leaned backward, giving herself over to gravity, and fell off the edge of the roof into the bed of the moving truck below.

Buffy jerked back to the present. She was still standing in the quiet, dusty evidence room with her fingers resting against the plastic bag, her chest heaving and her eyes wide with the shock the memory had left behind. The guilt and the horror of what she had been willing to do that night sat heavy in her throat.

Willow’s voice came from somewhere down the far end of the aisles.

Willow called, "Buffy! Buffy!"

Buffy flinched and snatched her hand back from the dagger. Willow came jogging around the end of the aisle, triumphantly holding up a large, clear bio-hazard bag with a thick, horribly large sheet of grayish-green, scaly shed skin folded up inside it.

Willow said, "Snap out of it... I got what we needed! Let’s go!"

Buffy stared at the shed skin, and then she stole one last, haunted look at the bloody dagger on the shelf. She made herself stand up straight and swallowed the old trauma back down where it belonged, and she cleared her throat, her voice coming out a little rough.

Buffy said, "Right. Yeah. Let’s get out of here."

She turned her back on the shelf and followed Willow out through the steel door, leaving the ghosts of the past locked up in the dark.

CHAPTER 8 The Pick-me-up

Out in the basement corridor, Buffy pulled the heavy steel door shut behind them until the latch clicked and turned to Willow, ready to move. But Willow wasn’t moving. She was standing perfectly still in the dim cinderblock hallway, and the air around her felt wrong somehow, tight and humming, as she reached into her pocket and took out the small, corked vial of thick, glowing orange liquid. Without a word of explanation she popped the tiny cork out with her thumb and knocked the vial back like a shot of whiskey, swallowing the whole thing in one gulp.

She threw her head back with her eyes squeezed shut, and what happened next was terrifying and beautiful all at once. Faint, fiery orange veins lit up beneath the pale skin of her neck, and the glowing web of them spread fast up over her jawline, branching across her cheeks and filling in behind her closed eyelids. She swayed on her feet, and the empty vial slipped out of her fingers and shattered quietly on the concrete floor.

Buffy dropped her Slayer stance instantly, her eyes widening in alarm, and stepped forward with both hands reaching out to catch her friend.

Buffy said, "Will—"

Willow snapped her eyes open, her face flushed and strained and her whole body gone rigid as the potion hit, and she threw a palm out sharply between them, her breathing ragged and her eyes wild.

Willow said, "No! Don’t touch me..."

Buffy froze with her hands hovering in the air.

Willow took a short, ragged breath. The fiery glow under her skin faded fast, sinking back down deep inside her. She straightened as the vertigo and the exhaustion disappeared, and a hyper-focused, artificial calm took their place. She smoothed down the front of her shirt with both hands.

Willow said, "I am fine. Just... needed a second to stabilize."

Buffy stared at her, her hazel eyes searching Willow’s face for any last trace of those glowing veins, and her voice was thick with suspicion.

Buffy said, "What exactly was that? What did you just do?"

Willow was already turning toward the stairs.

Willow said, "I needed a pick-me-up. Let’s get back to the others."

Up in the lobby, Buffy and Willow pushed back through the glass double doors from the stairwell, and a very subtle, almost invisible wave of purple energy washed out from Willow and swept across the room. The desk sergeant and the other officers were just finishing up with Dawn and Xander, and not a single cop looked toward the stairwell doors. They acted as if it were perfectly normal for two women in street clothes to come wandering up out of the restricted basement.

The desk sergeant gave Dawn a firm, fatherly nod.

The desk sergeant said, "We will keep a squad car patrolling the cafeteria perimeter, Miss Summers. You kids stay safe out there."

Dawn smiled brightly up at him.

Dawn said, "Thanks, Officer!"

Willow walked right past the desk and led the group toward the main exit, her fake cheerfulness back in full force.

Willow said, "All right, gang! Statement filed? Excellent. To the car!"

Out in the parking lot the bright California sun beat down on the asphalt, and they all piled into Joyce’s little compact sedan, with Willow sliding in behind the wheel. She shut her door, but she didn’t put the key in the ignition. She looked straight at her own reflection in the rearview mirror, both hands gripping the top of the steering wheel so hard her knuckles went white, the artificial high of the potion fighting a brutal war with her damaged nervous system. A single drop of dark red blood welled up and ran slowly out of her right nostril. She wiped it away quickly with the back of her hand and sniffled, getting her breathing back under control.

Without looking over her shoulder, she grabbed the heavy bio-hazard bag with the scaly fish-man skin inside and blindly tossed it into the backseat, where it flew over the console and landed squarely in Dawn’s lap.

Dawn flinched so hard she smacked her elbow on the door, batting at the bag with both hands.

Dawn said, "Oh, YUCK! Oh my god, it’s slimy through the plastic!"

Xander reached over quickly from the other side of the backseat and snatched the bag off her lap, holding it up by the very corner with two fingers and a look of complete disgust on his face.

Xander said, "I’ve got it, Dawnie. Though I think I preferred it when we were just talking about apocalyptic cafeteria brawls."

In the passenger seat, Buffy wasn’t laughing. She didn’t look back at the banter at all. She crossed her arms tightly over her chest and angled her body just enough that she could watch Willow out of the corner of her eye. The worry was written plainly across the Slayer’s face as Willow finally turned the key and pulled the car out of the parking lot.

CHAPTER 9 UC Sunnydale and the Feline Distraction

Out on the UC Sunnydale campus, students were lounging on the grass, throwing frisbees, and reading on benches in the sun. It was a perfect, easy college afternoon that had no idea there was a bag of fish-monster skin riding along in Joyce’s car. The little sedan pulled up to the curb near the brutal grey concrete of the main Science Building, and the four of them piled out. Willow carried the clear evidence bag by its top corners and held it as far away from her legs as her arms would let her.

They started up the wide concrete steps toward the main glass doors, and Dawn was dragging her feet the whole way, clutching her stomach with both hands. She let her head fall back, her face twisted up in exaggerated teenage suffering and her eyes pleading.

Dawn groaned up at the sky, "Guys, I am literally wasting away. My stomach is eating itself. Can we please feed me before we start doing gross skin-autopsies?"

Xander patted his own stomach sympathetically and nodded along with her, his eyes going wide and earnest like he was already picturing the food.

Xander said, "The teenager makes a valid tactical point, Buffy. A Slayer army marches on its stomach. And honestly, the smell of formaldehyde always makes me crave curly fries."

Buffy stopped at the top of the stairs and looked up at the big science building, then across the quad at a busy, modern building on the other side, and she sighed.

Buffy said, "Fine. I don’t want either of you passing out in a petri dish. The student union cafeteria is open over there."

She turned to Willow, looking her friend over and checking that strangely calm face.

Buffy said, "Will, you good to fly solo for twenty minutes while I feed the bottomless pits?"

Willow smiled, completely unbothered.

Willow said, "Yes, Dawn, you can eat. I’ll be fine, Buffy. I just need to get this under a spectrometer and run a quick DNA sequencing string. Bring me back a diet soda?"

Buffy pointed a finger at her, only half joking.

Buffy said, "You got it. Don’t mutate anything while we’re gone."

Buffy took Dawn by the shoulder and herded her and Xander back down the steps toward the cafeteria, and Willow turned and went into the Science Building alone.

She walked briskly down the sterile, white-tiled hallway and stopped in front of a heavy wooden door with a frosted glass window that read Advanced Cellular Biology - Authorized Personnel Only. A magnetic card reader was mounted on the wall beside it. Willow pulled her flimsy plastic undergraduate student ID out of her pocket, and as she held it between her fingers, a faint, almost invisible pulse of purple energy traveled from her fingertips into the plastic. She swiped it. The card reader let out a loud, cheerful beep, and the magnetic lock clicked open with a thud, the system registering her as an authorized member of the faculty.

She pushed the door open with her hip and walked into a huge, high-tech laboratory full of stainless steel counters, centrifuges, and expensive microscopes. It was mostly empty for the weekend, except for one grad student in his mid-twenties, wearing a white lab coat and safety goggles, who was carefully transferring blue liquid from one test tube to another. He looked up as the door shut behind her, and his eyes went straight to the grayish-green, scaly skin inside the bio-hazard bag Willow had just dropped onto the nearest steel table.

He frowned and stepped out from behind his counter.

The grad student said, "Whoa, hey. The undergrad labs are on the second floor. And you absolutely cannot bring unsanctioned, unsterilized biological matter into a Class-3 clean room. I need to call Campus Security."

He reached for the phone mounted on the wall.

Willow didn’t panic. She didn’t even move toward him. She just stopped unpacking her equipment and turned her head slowly to look at him, and her voice dropped low, smooth and hypnotic.

Willow said, "You don’t want to call security. You’re exhausted."

For a fraction of a second, pitch black swallowed the whites of Willow’s eyes, and then they were back to their normal deep green. The grad student froze with his hand an inch from the receiver. His eyes glazed over, and he stared blankly at the white wall behind her, his voice gone flat and monotone.

The grad student said, "I don’t want to call security. I am exhausted."

Willow turned back to the bag and unzipped the seal, pulling on a pair of latex gloves.

Willow said, as casually as if she were talking about the weather, "In fact, you just remembered that you left the stove on. And your cat is waiting for you. You should probably just clock out and go home."

The grad student blinked slowly, completely under.

The grad student said, "The stove is on. And Mr. Mittens... he hates waiting for his wet food. I should just go home."

Without so much as a glance at the mutated fish skin on the table, he turned around, pulled off his safety goggles, and tossed them carelessly onto the counter. He grabbed his backpack and walked out the laboratory door, shutting it quietly behind him.

Willow was left completely alone in the multi-million-dollar lab. She reached into the bag, pulled out a scalpel, and sliced off a perfect two-inch square of the thick, scaly skin. She slid it expertly onto a glass microscope slide and carried it over to the most expensive, high-powered digital microscope in the room.

Willow murmured to herself as she leaned into the eyepiece, "Alright, Sunnydale Swim Team. Let’s see what you’ve been up to for the last four years."

CHAPTER 10 The Caloric Intake

Inside the UC Sunnydale student union cafeteria, the noise was an echoing roar, plastic trays clattering against metal rails and hundreds of college students talking over each other. The second they walked in, Dawn stopped complaining about her stomach. Her back straightened and her eyes landed on a table of upperclassmen from the university lacrosse team, big guys in grey sports t-shirts laughing and throwing fries at each other, and she stared at them, starstruck.

Xander noticed. He stepped smoothly into her line of sight, physically blocking her view of the athletes, and crossed his arms in full protective big-brother mode.

Xander said, "Put the eyeballs back in their sockets, Dawnie. Those are college males. They survive exclusively on cheap domestic beer, unwashed laundry, and stolen dining hall cereal. It’s a biological hazard zone."

Dawn leaned around him to keep looking.

Dawn said, "I’m just observing the local wildlife, Xander. It’s educational."

Xander shifted right along with her and blocked the view again.

Xander said, "Yeah, well, observe the buffet line."

They stepped up to the metal rails of the food stations, and Buffy grabbed three brown plastic trays off the stack and handed them out, looking up at the menu board overhead.

Buffy said, "Load up. It’s on me. I’ve got Slayer hazard pay burning a hole in my pocket."

She went to the register first and handed the bored student cashier a crumpled twenty-dollar bill. Before she took her change she grabbed a cold can of Diet Coke from the little impulse cooler by the register and a paper-wrapped plastic straw. She unzipped her small, dark leather shoulder bag and carefully tucked the can and the straw inside, keeping them safe for Willow.

They slid their trays down the metal railing, picking out their food. Dawn took two huge, perfectly rectangular cafeteria pizza squares with the grease already pooling on the pepperoni and added a little plastic bowl of sliced cantaloupe and grapes. Xander skipped the pizza entirely and zeroed in on the hot food station, coming away with an overflowing sloppy joe on a cheap hamburger bun, the dark red meat sauce already spilling over the sides onto the white paper plate. Buffy went for pure, dense calories, three foil-wrapped taco snack burritos and a shiny red apple tossed onto her tray.

They found an empty booth near the back windows and slid in, Dawn and Xander on one side and Buffy on the other, and for once they didn’t start talking about the apocalypse. They actually ate. Xander picked up the sloppy joe with both hands and took a huge, unashamed bite, and the thick, messy sauce smeared right across the corner of his mouth. He chewed happily and grabbed a paper napkin to wipe his chin without missing a beat. Dawn picked up one of her pizza squares, folded it straight down the middle the classic cafeteria way, and bit into it, pulling a long, satisfying string of melted mozzarella away from her mouth. Buffy unwrapped the foil from her first burrito and didn’t bother with delicate, polite little bites. She tore into it with the fierce hunger of a Slayer whose metabolism never stopped burning, chewing fast while her eyes swept the edges of the cafeteria out of pure habit.

Xander pointed at her tray, his mouth full of sloppy joe.

Xander said, "You know, for someone who regularly complains about the culinary standards of the underworld, you really put away those questionable meat cylinders."

Buffy swallowed a huge bite of burrito, not bothered in the least.

Buffy said, "It’s protein and mystery spices, Xander. When you punch things for a living, you stop asking what part of the cow it came from."

Dawn popped a green grape into her mouth and chewed, looking out the window toward the Science Building.

Dawn said, "So... how long does it actually take to do a DNA science thingy on a giant piece of fish skin? Because I really don’t want to carry it in my lap on the way home."

Buffy took a bite of her apple with a loud crunch.

Buffy said, "Not long. Willow’s fast. As soon as we’re done inhaling the food pyramid, we’ll go scrape her out of the lab."

CHAPTER 11 The Telepathic Flex

The cafeteria stayed loud and chaotic around them. Xander had demolished his sloppy joe, Dawn was picking the last few pepperoni slices off her pizza square, and Buffy was just finishing her first taco burrito and wiping her mouth with a paper napkin. Then a voice cut straight through all that noise without the noise fading one bit. It was perfectly clear, and it echoed right in the center of Buffy’s skull with a slight, unnatural metallic ring to it. It was Willow’s voice, speaking inside her head.

Willow said, "I’m done here. Meet me back at the car."

Buffy flinched hard. She dropped her napkin and snapped her head left, then right, scanning the crowded room with every Slayer reflex she had standing on edge, searching for whoever had just whispered right into her ear.

Buffy said, "Willow?"

Willow’s voice came again, still inside her skull.

Willow said, "No. I’m inside your head. Car. Two minutes."

Buffy rubbed her temple with two fingers, a deep scowl crossing her face, because the sheer, invasive power of it unsettled her down to the bone. She looked back at Xander and Dawn, who hadn’t noticed a thing, and stood up abruptly.

Buffy said, "Alright, wrap it up. Time to go. The science oven dinged."

Dawn groaned and wiped her hands, and Xander stood and stretched. Buffy looked down at her tray, and since she never wasted calories, she quickly grabbed the two remaining foil-wrapped burritos. She shoved them unceremoniously into her dark leather shoulder bag right next to Willow’s Diet Coke, and then she led them out of the cafeteria.

Out in the campus parking lot, Willow was already sitting in the driver’s seat of Joyce’s sedan with the windows rolled down, looking relaxed and drumming her fingers lightly on the steering wheel. Buffy got into the passenger seat, dropped her bag into the footwell, and turned to give Willow a long, hard, worried look, her voice low and serious.

Buffy said, "Keep your voice out of my brain, Will. It’s creepy, and it makes my teeth itch."

Willow smiled innocently and turned the key in the ignition.

Willow said, "Just saving minutes on the cell plan, Buffy. Buckle up."

The car engine roared to life.

CHAPTER 12 The Hive

Inside a sea cave in the jagged rock island off the north end of the shore, the ocean slammed against hollow stone with an echoing boom. The dark was total, until slowly a sickly, bioluminescent green glow began to creep across the cavern walls. The cave was huge and damp and reeked of salt and rot, and somewhere in it a young woman was crying, her voice hoarse from screaming, echoing off the wet rock. Her face was streaked with tears, her whole body trembling against the bonds that held her.

The girl begged, her voice breaking, "Please... no, please don’t... stay away from me... someone help me..."

She was one of the girls from the beach party, hanging four feet off the cavern floor. She was pinned to the black rock at her wrists, ankles, and torso by huge, thick globs of dark green biological glue, glossy and wet-looking in the green glow but hardened into a rigid shell she couldn’t escape. When she thrashed weakly against it, it didn’t give a single millimeter. She wasn’t alone. Five other college students were stuck to the cavern walls the same way, some unconscious with their heads hanging limp, others staring out into the dark with wide, traumatized eyes, and below them the murky, waist-deep saltwater churned.

Three fish men rose out of the water, grotesque, hulking, scaled humanoid things with webbed claws and sharp dorsal fins jutting from their backs. They walked right past the pleading girl without the slightest interest in her screams, moving with a cold, predatory indifference, and then they stopped and parted, bowing their heads slightly in a crude show of deference.

Something huge rose from the deepest part of the cavern pool.

It was the Alpha. The White Alpha stepped out onto the rocky shore, much bigger than the others and standing nearly seven feet tall. His scales weren’t murky green but a stark bone-white that caught what little light the cave had. His face was heavily mutated, filled with rows of razor-sharp teeth, but the most terrifying thing about him was his eyes. They weren’t dead fish eyes. They were a piercing, intelligent, glowing crimson. He tilted his head slowly as he looked over the trapped students one by one, and a low, clicking purr rumbled in his chest while the rest of him held perfectly still, like a hunter sizing up his prey.

CHAPTER 13 The Crash and the Call

In the Summers living room, the front door stuck a little in its crooked frame before it swung open, and Buffy, Xander, Dawn, and Willow walked in. Anya and Tara were sitting at the dining room table, surrounded by colorful brochures, seating charts, and a big binder labeled WEDDING/PARTY LOGISTICS. Willow was already talking before the door had shut behind her, pacing a line across the rug.

Willow said, "Okay, so the cellular degradation wasn’t actually degrading. It’s synthesizing. The DNA strand I looked at is secreting a mutagenic enzyme. In plain English: their leader isn’t just killing those college kids. It’s a hive leader. It is actively converting them to multiply the brood."

Tara stood up from the table, her brow furrowing with worry as she watched Willow’s frantic pacing.

Tara said, "Will? Are you okay? You look incredibly pale..."

Willow waved a hand to brush it off, but her face was pale and strained and her eyes were glassy, her body swaying as the exhaustion finally came crashing down over her, and she tried to push right through it.

Willow said, "I’m fine, totally fine, just doing the science thing. The point is, the beach isn’t just a crime scene. It’s a nursery. We have to lock down the entire coastline before—"

She stopped in the middle of the sentence. Her eyes fluttered shut, and without any dramatic flair at all her knees simply buckled and she folded sideways onto the floral sofa, her head bouncing softly against the armrest. She was out cold.

Buffy yelled, lunging forward, "Willow!"

Buffy and Tara reached the couch at the same time, and Buffy grabbed Willow’s shoulder and shook her firmly.

Buffy said, "Will! Hey, wake up!"

Tara pressed two fingers to the side of Willow’s neck, and the fear on her face slowly melted into a deep, confused relief.

Tara said, "Her pulse is steady. She’s not... she’s not in a coma. She is just completely, impossibly asleep. Like her battery just hit absolute zero."

Xander came around the end of the couch and looked down at Willow.

Xander said, "She’s been running on fumes since the siren attack. Let her sleep. The coastline isn’t going anywhere."

Without waiting for anybody’s permission, Xander gently scooped Willow up into his arms and carried her easily against his chest, up the stairs and into the quiet of the master bedroom she shared with Tara. He laid her down gently on the mattress and pulled the heavy quilt up over her shoulders. He sat on the edge of the bed for a moment, reached out and brushed a stray lock of red hair off Willow’s face, his expression softening into pure brotherly affection. Then he leaned down and kissed her forehead.

Xander whispered, "Sleep tight, Supergirl. I’ll hold the fort tonight."

He stood up and quietly left the room, and Tara took his place at once. She crawled up onto the mattress with quiet urgency and lay down right behind Willow. Her own breathing slowed as she wrapped both arms protectively around Willow’s waist and buried her face softly in the back of Willow’s neck, holding her close with deep, steady care.

Tara whispered into the quiet room, "I’ve got you. You’re safe."

Willow stirred once against her, not quite awake. In a slurred, drowsy murmur she told Tara about the little corked vial of glowing orange liquid she had knocked back in the police station basement, the pick-me-up that had carried her through the whole afternoon.

Tara’s face fell. She didn’t say a word. She held Willow until her breathing went deep and even again, then slipped carefully off the bed and went downstairs without looking at anyone. In the kitchen she took the cast iron skillet off the stove, turned the hot water on hard, and pretended to wash it, scrubbing at the black iron with short, angry strokes in complete silence.

Down in the living room, Xander came back to find Buffy standing in the middle of the floor, holding Dawn’s translucent flip phone to her ear. Anya was standing by the dining table clutching her binder to her chest with both arms, looking completely bewildered.

Anya said, "I don’t understand. If the beach is a nursery for mutating fish-monsters, what happens to the sunset bachelor cruise? And the tiki bar? The deposits are non-refundable, Buffy. Does a mutant invasion count as an act of God for insurance purposes?"

Buffy held up one finger to silence Anya as the call connected, and her free hand was already drifting toward the weapons chest in the corner, muscle memory moving before the words ever left her mouth. She smirked a little into the phone.

Buffy said, "Hey, Lucian. I hope you don’t have any pressing plans for the evening."

Lucian’s voice came through the phone, low and calm.

Lucian said, "Just reading. Do we have a situation?"

Buffy said, "We have a major situation. Mutated fish-men are currently trying to repopulate Sunnydale via the local college student body, and my resident witch is currently down for the count. We need muscle."

Lucian said without hesitating, "I’ll be there in ten minutes."

A genuine, witty spark lit up in Buffy’s eyes.

Buffy said, "Good. Wear something you don’t mind getting completely ruined. It’s your first official monster hunt... time to get your feet wet."

She snapped the phone shut with a sharp click and looked at Xander, and then over at the heavily armed weapons chest sitting in the corner of the room.

Buffy said, "Alright. Let’s go fishing."
