I Choose Life

Download this script (.txt)

Buffy the Vampire Slayer. Season seven, episode 11. I Choose Life.

CHAPTER 1 The Crystal

The next afternoon at the Summers house, a day after they had carried Spike out from under the school, the mid-afternoon light came in softly through the curtains of Willow and Tara's bedroom on the shaded side of the house and lay in warm pools of gold across the quilt and the floorboards, the shadows gentle in the corners, while down in the living room the antique clock ticked slowly and faintly and birds called back and forth in the yard outside. A six-inch cyan crystal glowed softly against Willow's skin, and the pages of their books lay still between them as the two of them took up the weapon research again where they had left it.

Willow and Tara sat up side by side against the headboard in light, comfortable clothes for lounging in bed, soft jogging shorts and thin cotton tank tops, each with a book open across her lap and their shoulders pressed close together in the quiet, and the red of Willow's hair was still darker at the roots where the stone-teeth spell had drained out of her yesterday, a reminder that her magic was still damaged and that no amount of research was going to mend it.

Willow turned the cyan crystal slowly between the fingers of her left hand with her eyes on a page of Latin text, while her right hand rested on Tara's forearm, her thumb moving in that slow, absent, grounding stroke she did without thinking. Beside her, Tara read from a thick Watchers' Council textbook on the Slayer line, tracing one line at a time with her fingertip, and when she leaned over to turn a page, the healing bandage low on her left side near her belly button pulled faintly under the thin cotton of her tank top.

Tara looked up from the page, her voice soft and focused, quietly excited. "Whatever this weapon is… we have to find it. I'm excited for Buffy."

Willow said, a little distracted, "Me too, honey."

Her thumb kept up its slow stroke along Tara's forearm, and she never lifted her eyes from the Latin.

CHAPTER 2 Positive

Out in the upstairs hallway, in the same soft afternoon light, Buffy suddenly sprinted past the open bedroom doorway, her bare feet slapping hard on the hardwood as she ran toward the hall bathroom across from her own bedroom door. She ducked into the laundry closet for a moment and came back out with an armful of towels and her small toiletry bag clutched against her chest, then turned and ran into the bathroom and slammed the door shut behind her with a loud, final crack that echoed down the hall.

For a long, breathless moment after the slam, the closed bathroom door sat silent at the end of the hall.

Then Dawn came down the hall from the far end, home from school, and walked slowly into the bedroom and sat down on the foot of the bed facing the two of them, her legs dangling over the edge and her right hand held low against her thigh, closed around something small and white.

Tara and Willow looked up from their books at the same moment.

Tara looked up, soft and immediately concerned. "What's wrong, sweetie?"

Dawn lifted her hand and slowly turned what she was holding so they could see it: a pregnancy test, with a clear plus sign in its little result window.

Willow swallowed hard, and Tara's eyes went wide with genuine shock, and for a long moment none of them said anything at all. Dawn sat at the foot of the bed holding the test up, Tara and Willow stared back at it from the headboard, and the only sounds were the faint tick of the antique clock drifting up from the living room and the muffled noises of Buffy behind the bathroom door, while a heavy silence settled over the three of them.

CHAPTER 3 Not Mine

From the far end of the hall came the muffled but unmistakable sound of Buffy throwing up into the toilet, a wet, forceful retching and then the rush of the flush, and it carried clearly through the open door into the bedroom.

Dawn's eyes flicked toward the doorway at the sound and stayed there for a second before she turned back to them and held the positive test a little higher.

Dawn said, quiet and direct, "It's not mine."

Dawn said, "I found it in the trash in the other bathroom."

Willow immediately pushed herself up off the bed with her mouth already opening to scream Buffy's name, and Tara's hand shot up and clamped firmly over Willow's mouth, her eyes never leaving Dawn.

Tara said, steady, low and firm, without a trace of her stutter, "Throw that away in our trash can in our bathroom."

With her free hand she pointed to the small bathroom attached to their bedroom.

Tara said, "You will not tell anyone about this. Do you understand me?"

Dawn nodded slowly, her voice quiet and serious. "Yes, ma'am."

Dawn stood up without another word, crossed the bedroom into the little attached bathroom, and dropped the test into the small trash can beside the toilet, then tore off a few squares of toilet paper and laid them carefully over it until it was completely covered, and stepped back out to stand near the bed.

Tara slowly took her hand away from Willow's mouth, both of them breathing harder now, and they shared a long look that carried everything neither of them was saying.

Tara said, soft but resolute, "We cannot tell her we know. We have to let her tell us on her own terms. She is going through so much right now."

Then she leaned close to Willow and dropped her voice to a whisper.

Tara whispered, "Did Lucian spend the night again?"

She looked back up at Dawn, who stood close at the side of the bed beside Willow, her hands knotted together in front of her.

Tara said, "Have you seen him this morning?"

Dawn turned her head and looked across the hall through Buffy's open door at the part of her sister's bedroom she could see from there, the bed empty, and shook her head.

Dawn said, "Nope. Not today. He must be at work or something."

Tara slid out of bed and took Dawn's hand in hers, gently but firmly, and looked back at Willow, and Willow climbed off the bed without a word, and the three of them walked out of the bedroom, along the upstairs hall past the closed bathroom door, and down the stairs together.

CHAPTER 4 Chocolate Milk

A few minutes later in the kitchen, the mid-afternoon light spilled in through the window over the sink and mixed with the warm glow of the kitchen lights, and the secret sat heavy over all three of them.

Tara moved around the kitchen island with a quiet, deliberate purpose, pulling out pans and eggs and butter as though it were breakfast time even though it was the middle of the afternoon, her movements calm and practiced and the tension clear underneath them, and she kept glancing through the doorway at the dining-room table where Willow and Dawn sat.

Willow sat with her arms wrapped loosely around herself, visibly rattled, and Dawn sat quietly watching Tara, and Tara kept herself turned at the stove so she could see both of them while she worked. For a long while the only sounds were the soft clink of the pans and the scrape of a spatula, and upstairs the muffled sounds from the bathroom had finally stopped.

Buffy walked in freshly showered, in a simple white bathrobe tied loosely at the waist, with light new makeup on, a little concealer under her tired eyes and a touch of blush, her wet hair slicked back and clipped up so she could style it later, drained but put together, and the bandage on her left forearm where she had fed Spike yesterday at the Magic Box was healing quietly under the cuff of her sleeve.

Tara, Willow and Dawn all froze and watched her in a complete, heavy silence as Buffy went straight to the fridge, pulled out the big jug of chocolate milk, unscrewed the cap and drank straight from it in long, thirsty, desperate gulps, four of them, and then she lowered the jug, wiped her mouth with the back of her hand, screwed the cap back on and put the jug back on its shelf.

She shut the fridge and turned slowly, and when she saw their faces she let out a heavy, tired sigh, walked through to the dining room and sat down at the table across from them.

Buffy said, quiet and resigned, "I guess you found out…"

She looked down at her hands on the tabletop, her fingers trembling visibly with nerves, and when Willow reached across for one of them, Buffy drew both hands back just out of reach, and Willow froze in mid-motion with her hand hovering over the table.

Buffy's voice came soft and steady, full of an emotion she wasn't trying to hide. "I told Lucian… We have been together now for over five months… This is life. Despite everything that happens around us and this town… life still happens."

She raised a single finger toward Willow, gently silencing her before she could speak.

Buffy said, "The last two weeks… we were not careful."

Willow's eyes dropped, before she could stop them, to the front of Buffy's bathrobe, where the white terrycloth lay perfectly flat over a stomach that showed nothing at all, and then she lifted them back up to Buffy's face with a small, helpless, crooked smile wobbling at one corner of her mouth, half shock and half pure love, and Willow said, very softly, "Two weeks? Nothing's ever normal with you, is it?"

Buffy looked Willow dead in the eyes, firm and clear.

Buffy said, "I am keeping it. Lucian did not manipulate me. He did not threaten to leave me or give me any bad vibes. He said you can choose to keep it, or not. I will still love you and marry you. You know him."

She drew one long, shaky breath, and then her face crumpled and she broke into real, ugly, body-shaking sobs, her nose running.

Buffy said through heavy sobs, fierce, "I want this… I want life… and I will be damned if I let whatever this is take it from me. This is mine… and it belongs to me for once…"

Tara turned off the stove and came through the doorway, and without a word all three of them closed in around the table at once and folded Buffy into one big, tight, overlapping hug, the four of them huddled together with their arms wrapped fiercely around each other while Buffy sobbed loudly in the middle. Tara stroked her wet hair, Willow rubbed slow circles on her back and Dawn clung on tight, all of them messy and warm and protective, their hands gripping fistfuls of bathrobe and t-shirt, and they held on for a long time before anyone let go.

CHAPTER 5 For Once

When the hug finally loosened, Buffy wiped her face on her bathrobe sleeve and went down the hall to the downstairs bathroom, and the three of them were left at the dining-room table in a charged silence, their eyes still glistening, looking at her empty chair and the hallway where she had gone.

Tara's eyes were wet, and she stood where she had been holding Buffy with one hand resting without thinking over the healing wound on her lower left side, staring at the empty chair for a long moment.

Tara said, quiet, almost to herself, "For once. That's what got me."

Willow sat back down slowly, picked up a napkin and started twisting it between her fingers until it tore, without looking at anyone.

Willow said, soft and a little raw, "She pulled her hand back when I reached for it. I thought I'd fucked it up. And then she just… said it. Like it was already decided. Like it was hers."

Dawn hadn't moved, and she sat staring at the tabletop with one knee bouncing underneath it, and when she spoke her voice came out smaller than usual.

Dawn said, "She's pregnant. Like… actually pregnant. My sister's gonna have a baby."

After a moment Tara finally sat down beside Willow and reached over and took her hand, stopping her before she could destroy another napkin.

Tara said, "She's scared. And she's happy. Both at the same time. I could feel it when we were holding her."

Willow let out a shaky breath that was almost a laugh.

Willow said, "I'm gonna be an aunt. That's… that's insane. I don't even know how to—"

She stopped and looked toward the hallway where Buffy had gone.

Willow said, the words tumbling out faster and faster the way they always did once her brain caught hold of something, her hands lifting off the table to shape the idea in the air, "Okay, but think about it. A Slayer's body isn't just a regular human body with extra upper-body strength. The Slayer energy is in her all the way down, like, on a chemical level, which is why she heals in days instead of weeks. So if that energy is interacting with her hormones, then the whole hormonal structure of the pregnancy could be totally different from a human one. Everything sped up. Earlier hormone levels, earlier morning sickness, earlier everything. It's not weird. Well, okay, it's weird. But it's Slayer weird, which for us is basically normal. Right?"

Tara nodded, and Dawn wiped her eyes with the sleeve of her hoodie and folded her arms on the table.

Dawn said, quiet, "Lucian's not going anywhere. And we're still here. So… that's something."

CHAPTER 6 Family Circle

The house had gone quiet in a long silence, the warm afternoon light lying across the dining-room table, when they heard Lucian at the front entry.

He eased aside the sheet of plywood that had stood in for the front door since Caleb had torn it off, stepped in and set the plywood back in place, then paused just inside and took off his boots one at a time, methodically, standing them side by side against the wall. He was still in his work-day clothes from Daniels Technologies, and he walked through to the dining room and sat down at the table, let out a slow breath through his nose with both hands flat on the tabletop, and looked up at the women watching him.

Lucian said, quiet and steady, with no defensiveness in it at all, "So you know…"

Tara leaned in the kitchen doorway, soft and matter-of-fact and warm. "Well… it's a small house."

Willow leaned forward, gentle and direct. "Buffy never gets sick because of what she is. I mean really sick, you know what I mean? So… morning sickness? Dead giveaway."

Willow reached across the table and laid both of her hands over Lucian's, deliberately, a quiet and grounding gesture of trust.

Willow said, earnest, "We trust you enough to be with her. We trust you enough to be here in our lives… and to raise a child from our bestest friend in the entire world."

Dawn stood up without a word, walked around the table and hugged Lucian from the side, then climbed into his lap as naturally as if she had done it a hundred times and looked up at him with big, searching eyes.

Dawn looked up at him, soft and vulnerable and direct. "Do you know if it's…?"

Willow blurted out, excited, "It's a girl. I looked."

Tara gave Willow's arm a gentle but firm slap and a look that said *really?* as plainly as words.

Willow flushed pink, flustered and embarrassed. "Oh… sorry. I didn't mean to… I mean… sorry."

Lucian let out a small, genuine laugh, the first real release of the afternoon, and reached up to rub the back of his neck with one hand as a smile spread across his face.

Down the hall the bathroom door opened, and footsteps came toward the dining room along with the sound of Buffy sniffling hard and clearing her nose and her raw throat.

Buffy came in still in her bathrobe and stopped when she saw Dawn sitting in Lucian's lap, giving her a look that was half exhausted and half fond.

Buffy said, her voice thick but steady, "You're in my seat, squirt. Get up."

Dawn rolled her eyes with a smile as the tension in the room cracked, slid off his lap and went back to her own chair, and Buffy took her place, wrapping both arms around Lucian's neck from the side and claiming her seat and him in front of everyone, her engagement ring catching the light on her finger.

Dawn's voice came out quiet and honest. "I don't know what to say… how to feel. It's such a shock."

Buffy said, low and clear, her head resting against Lucian's, "Tell me about it. I found out three days ago… so I know."

Through all of it Tara had gone quietly back to the stove in the kitchen to start a second round of breakfast, cracking eggs into the pan and tapping the wooden spoon on its rim, listening through the doorway and giving them space, while at the table Lucian laid his hand over Buffy's arm where it wrapped around him and held it there, the healing wound on his left side quiet under his work shirt.

CHAPTER 7 Joyce

A few minutes later the warm golden light over the dining room was suddenly undercut by a cold blue glow spreading in from the living room.

They were gathered around the table in a warm cluster with Lucian's arm around Buffy when a low hum rose in the house and a cold blue light came spilling across the floor from the living room, and as the hum settled into a low, steady drone, the light drew itself up into the shape of Joyce Summers standing at the edge of the dining room, her face exactly right except for the eyes, which were slightly off, colder, wrong in a predatory way.

Dawn turned her head away with her eyes squeezed shut and both hands pressed over her ears, a raw, instinctive flinch, while Tara froze at the stove with the wooden spoon still in her hand, Willow went very still in her chair, and Lucian let out a long, tired sigh.

Buffy slowly stood up from the table without flinching and looked the First dead in its stolen eyes.

Buffy's voice stayed low and steady, a quiet fury burning underneath it. "You could never be my mother. You could never have the strength… the fortitude… to raise two daughters like us alone. To help care for all my friends… and never turn anyone away."

Then she turned her back on it and walked toward the stairs, and while its eyes followed her, Willow and Tara slipped quietly out of the dining room and up the stairs ahead of her, and the First's borrowed face twisted with rage.

The First said in Joyce's voice, sharper and angrier, "You don't get to walk away from me, young lady!"

Buffy climbed the stairs, turned right at the top and stopped in the hallway outside Willow and Tara's bedroom, where she folded her arms and waited, and the First came up the stairs after her still wearing Joyce's face.

The First said in Joyce's voice, dangerous, "Oh, don't you look at me like that young…"

Buffy said, cold and clear, "WILLOW. NOW."

From inside the bedroom Willow, kneeling on the floorboards with Tara behind her, rolled the cyan crystal across the floor toward the doorway where the First had come to a stop, and it spun to rest right in front of its feet, and in a split second it went off in a violent detonation of hard white-blue light that ripped the Joyce shape apart and left nothing of it behind but a perfect circle of floorboards scorched black just inside the bedroom doorway.

The burned circle lay there like a permanent scar, and Buffy stepped into the bedroom doorway, where Willow was still down on her knees because they had buckled under her the moment the crystal went off, breathing hard, with a thin line of blood already running from her nose and the red of her hair darkening again at the roots, the same price her damaged magic had made her pay for the stone teeth under the school yesterday.

Buffy said, quiet and serious, "You're sure it can't come back in here again? That it worked?"

Willow said, still kneeling as she caught her breath, "Yeah… but Buffy… it also disrupts my and Tara's magic too. For about three houses' distance from where the spell went off. It should only last around three moons. But… it'll be hard for me inside the space to do anything."

She could already feel what she meant, because the magic in the room had not gone quiet but had turned jagged, running through her in uneven, static spikes like a graph gone wild instead of the gentle river it used to be, so that small things would still answer her and Tara but anything that needed a steady, constant flow would fight them every step of the way, and the First, which needed that same steady flow just to hold its borrowed shape and voice, could still push itself inside the zone, but only broken and glitching, and it could no longer listen in on the house or spy on anyone in it.

Buffy said, nodding with her arms crossed, thoughtful, "There's always a cost, isn't there?"

She let that sit for a moment, her eyes resting on the black ring scorched into the floorboards.

Buffy said, "We'll have to roll with it then. Thanks, Will. You did great."

Then the entire house suddenly lurched in a violent shake, and pictures fell from the walls and glass rattled in every window frame, and downstairs Lucian pulled Dawn under the dining-room table while upstairs Buffy braced herself in the bedroom doorway and Tara dropped down beside Willow to hold her, and for a few long seconds the whole house shook around them before it went still again with nothing broken but the fallen frames, though thin new cracks had run up through the plaster of the walls in three of the rooms.

Buffy said, low and satisfied, almost smiling, "I guess we pissed it off."

She paused just inside the doorway with her arms still crossed, looking down at the perfect black circle scorched into the floorboards at her feet.

Buffy said, "Good."

CHAPTER 8 Aftershocks

After the earthquake the house sat in an eerie quiet, the soft afternoon light beginning to fade toward evening, and little by little the ordinary sounds of the household came back.

Buffy stood in the bedroom doorway and gave Willow a small, tired, warm look.

Buffy said, quiet and steady, "I need to get ready…"

Willow nodded once from where she still knelt on the floorboards, the back of her hand pressed under her bleeding nose.

Tara said softly from behind her, out of sight in the room, "If you need anything…"

Buffy said, smiling gently, "I know."

Buffy walked down the hall to her own bedroom and closed the door behind her, and downstairs Lucian and Dawn were still sitting at the dining-room table.

Lucian's voice was low and sincere. "You were very brave with how you handled that."

Dawn's answer came soft and honest. "Thanks… I know it wasn't my mother. It was just… her face."

Lucian gently took Dawn's hand, stood up from the table and led her through into the front living room.

Lucian knelt in front of the TV and switched on Dawn's PS2, peeled the plastic off the copy of Final Fantasy X still sealed from the SunnyMart sale and slid the disc into the tray, then handed her the controller and sat down beside her on the couch, quietly taking care of her, his right hand resting easy on his own knee.

Dawn said, "I was looking forward to playing this so bad… I paid for half of it too. It was on sale."

She looked up at him, vulnerable, the controller resting forgotten in her lap.

Dawn said, "I've never seen my sister love anyone as much as you… except Angel."

Lucian said with a small, thoughtful sigh, "Yes… as you know, I have met him. He is very strong. And in personality… it's easy to see why she loved him so much."

Dawn's voice turned quiet and sincere. "He reminds me of you in a way… but not… like… without the brooding stuff."

She turned back to the game as the Square logo came up on the screen and the soft title music began to play, and Lucian sat quietly beside her with his right hand resting under his chin, thoughtful, while the music played on.

Before the music had finished its first slow loop, the old yellow plastic phone on the kitchen wall rang, shrill and insistent, and Tara came down the stairs at a quick barefoot trot, one hand trailing along the banister, and crossed through the dining room past the fallen picture frames into the kitchen. She lifted the receiver off its hook on the wall beside the fridge on the third ring and leaned one shoulder against the wall, and the long coiled cord swung loose beneath it as she brought the phone to her ear.

Tara said, a little breathless from the stairs, "Summers residence, this is Tara."

Anya's voice came through the line loud and brisk over the rumble of an engine and the hiss of tires, as though she had never once in her life believed a telephone needed to be talked into quietly. "Tara. It's Anya. We're at LAX. Well, we were at LAX, and now we're in a shuttle leaving LAX, which cost twenty-two dollars each, which is robbery, but Xander says we're not allowed to walk to Sunnydale in our good shoes."

Tara smiled into the receiver, and her free hand drifted down to the cord, and she began winding one of its yellow coils slowly around her middle finger, around and around, the plastic curl snug and cool against her skin. "Hi, Anya… it's so good to hear your voice. You made it."

Anya said, "Of course we made it. I told Buffy we'd make it. I don't break promises, they're basically contracts with feelings in them. The shuttle driver says two hours if the freeway behaves, so we'll be there soon. Before dark, he says, but he also said his name was Rico and his name tag says Dale, so I don't fully trust him."

Tara laughed softly, the sound low and warm, and let the coil slip off her finger and caught the next one, winding it around the same finger as she turned to look out the kitchen window at the backyard, where the string lights hung waiting in the trees. "We'll be ready for you. Willow and I hung the lights this morning. It looks… it looks really beautiful out there, Anya."

Anya said, "Good. Beautiful is good. Weddings should be beautiful, and also affordable, and I am bringing a gift that is both. And how is my store? Are you still writing the receipts in the green book? The green book, Tara, not the blue book. The blue book is for incense."

Tara pressed her lips together to hold back a smile and nodded as though Anya could see her, the cord wrapped twice around her middle finger now and her thumb running gently over the coils. "The green book. Every receipt. I promise."

Anya said, and her voice went suddenly softer, almost shy under the engine noise, "Good. Thank you. I mean it. I worry about it, and I worry about all of you, which is very inconvenient from Texas. Tell Buffy we're coming. Tell her Xander's already crying a little and we haven't even gotten off the freeway."

Tara's face softened all the way, and she leaned her head back against the wall beside the phone with her eyes half closed. "I'll tell her. Travel safe, both of you… we can't wait to see you."

Anya said, "We will. Goodbye, Tara," and the line clicked off without any more ceremony than that.

Tara stood a moment longer with the quiet receiver against her ear and the coiled cord still wound around her middle finger, then unwound it slowly, one loop at a time, and hung the phone back on its hook on the wall, and went back up the stairs with a small smile still on her face.

Upstairs in their bedroom, Willow still knelt near the scorched circle in the floorboards when Tara came back in and settled close beside her, both of them drained and relieved.

Willow said, "Well… at least it worked."

Tara said, quiet, "Yeah. Finally."

Willow said, "At least it can't hear us or spy on us anymore within the bubble… that's why it was so angry. We better get ready to…"

Tara nodded and turned her back to the room, pulled her tank top off over her head and began to change, her wedding clothes laid out waiting on the bed beside her.

In her own bedroom Buffy stood at her white vanity in the quiet, staring at her reflection in the oval mirror, and for several long seconds she did not move at all, the baby and her mother's stolen face and the price Willow had just paid all settling over her at once, with the whole business of getting ready still ahead of her. Mr. Gordo sat propped against the mirror, and without looking down she found his ear with her fingers and picked at it.

CHAPTER 9 Aisle

That same evening, after the sun had gone fully down behind the houses and only the last of the dusk lingered in the sky, string lights glowed soft and golden through the trees of the Summers backyard, and paper streamers in white and soft gold fluttered in the evening breeze, the whole yard dressed by hand for something small and personal and a little bit pagan, on the same day the house had learned about the baby and driven the First out of its halls.

Chopin's Nocturne in E-flat major, Opus 9, Number 2, played softly from a simple CD player set beside the vine-and-rose archway, the breeze moved gently through the leaves, a four-foot altar draped in red cloth stood waiting at the front, and a purple-and-gold carpet runner ran down the short aisle, while off to one side, among the tomato stakes, Amy's kneeling stone figure watched with blank stone eyes, someone having hung a small wreath of white flowers around her neck, and somewhere in the front row the happy sniffles had already started.

Tara and Willow stood at the altar as officiants, dressed in an elegant Celtic Irish Wiccan style in flowing natural cotton lace and linen. Tara wore ivory with knotwork embroidery at the neckline and hem, a light shawl over her shoulders, the handfasting cord tied at her belt and flowers woven into her golden dirty-blonde hair, and Willow wore a similar cut with blue-green embroidery accents and flowers in her red hair, the two of them looking grounded and quietly magical.

Xander and Anya sat in the front row of wooden chairs, and Clem sat beside Xander in the chair nearest the aisle with an old, boxy VHS camcorder propped on his right shoulder, the strap wrapped around his loose-skinned hand and his left eye squeezed shut while his right one pressed eagerly to the rubber eyecup, its little red record light glowing steadily as he swung it slowly from the archway to the guests and back again, so that nobody's big night would go unrecorded. Dawn sat on the grass in front of the first row with her hands clasped and her eyes wet and focused, and a handful of other guests were scattered through the chairs behind them. Far at the back, alone, Spike leaned against a tree in his own signature coat, fetched back from his crypt since the Seal, recovered just enough after the Slayer's blood to stand there for this, a quiet, watchful presence at the edge of the string lights.

Lucian stood at the far end of the short aisle with the purple-and-gold runner beneath his feet, in a formal black suit with a crisp white dress shirt and black tie, a tailored suede vest with silver buttons and a tailed over-jacket, his long black hair flowing loose and formal down his back. His posture was perfect, calm and focused with a model's precision, both hands relaxed at his sides, and nothing in the way he moved showed the healing wound on his left side.

He walked up the aisle one smooth, deliberate step at a time with a quiet confidence, his hair trailing behind him in the glow of the string lights, and when Clem lowered the camcorder from his eye just long enough to grin and wave his free, loose-skinned arm at him from his aisle chair, the red light still glowing as he swung the lens back up to follow him, Lucian gave him a single respectful nod without breaking his stride.

When he reached the altar, Willow and Tara turned to him with warm, genuine smiles full of love and acceptance and a quiet pride.

CHAPTER 10 Prophecy Girl

Then Buffy stepped into view from behind the lush curtain of vines at the back of the yard, holding the sides of her skirt delicately in both hands. She wore the white dress her mother had bought her for the Spring Fling all those years ago, the night the Master drowned her, subtly altered now and shimmering with a radiant champagne glitter across the fabric that caught every one of the string lights, with soft pink blush, black eyeliner, a soft grey-blue shadow and deep red lipstick, her hair styled simply and beautifully, and her engagement ring on her finger, waiting to become a wedding band.

She walked slowly and gracefully down the short aisle and stopped at the front row beside Xander and Anya with a warm, emotional smile, holding back tears of joy, and Xander looked up at her with a quiet pride and love while Anya was moved in her own particular way.

As she moved on toward the altar, Xander caught his breath out loud, and Willow pressed her fingers to her lips, the two of them who had been there that night recognizing the dress, while Dawn stared with wet eyes and the Chopin played on softly underneath. Then Buffy and Lucian stood together before Tara, both legal officiant and priestess tonight, with Willow beside her smiling softly.

CHAPTER 11 I Choose This

At the altar the string lights and the white roses of the archway framed the two of them in the warm, reverent last of the dusk.

Tara's voice came soft, warm and reverent, with a quiet strength beneath it. "We stand here tonight not just as two people, but as two souls who have chosen life in the face of darkness. Lucian and Buffy, you have walked through fire and still reached for each other's hand."

She gently took their hands and bound them together with a simple handfasting cord of soft gold and deep red.

Tara said, "Do you, Lucian, promise to be her safe harbor in the storm, to choose her every day, to build a home with her even when the world tries to take it away?"

Lucian's voice came deep and steady and full of love, his eyes only for Buffy. "I do. I will stand with you, fight for you, and love you — through every shadow and every sunrise."

Tara turned to Buffy, a maternal pride shining in her face. "And do you, Buffy, promise to let him love you, to keep choosing life with him, to let this family — all of us — stand beside you as you build something beautiful and real?"

Buffy's voice was thick with emotion but strong, and she smiled through her tears. "I do. I choose this. I choose him. I choose us."

Willow stepped forward then with a thin gold band cupped in her palm, and Lucian took it from her with his free fingers and slid it slowly onto Buffy's ring finger, inside the loops of the gold-and-red cord, until it sat snug above her engagement ring and caught the string lights.

Tara smiled warmly, a joyful strength in her voice. "Then by the power vested in me by the State of California, and by the love and the will of everyone gathered here who has fought for this moment…"

She paused, and the whole yard held its breath with her.

Tara said, "I now pronounce you husband and wife."

The string lights glittered across Buffy's dress, and quiet joy spread over every face in the yard.

CHAPTER 12 Blessed Be

Under the golden string lights Buffy grabbed the front of Lucian's jacket in both hands and pulled him in, and Lucian smiled, raised one index finger and waved it slowly side to side in a playful *no, no*, and Buffy's smile burst wide open in radiant, delighted surprise.

Lucian dipped her smoothly backward in one perfect, confident motion and kissed her long and deep in front of everyone, passionate and respectful, a full public declaration, and he held her there in the dip for a long moment while the string lights sparkled across the champagne glitter of her dress.

Applause and cheers erupted all around them, with happy sniffles from Dawn and Anya underneath, and Clem waved his free, loose-skinned arm wildly from his chair while keeping the camcorder held steady on the couple with the other, while far at the back Spike still leaned against his tree, quiet and watching, keeping the word he had given her under the school by being there without claiming anything at all.

Lucian lifted her back upright, and Buffy gave his arm a playful punch, not hard at all, and laughed, and he grinned down at her.

Together they turned to Tara and Willow, and Buffy and Lucian each leaned in and kissed one of them on the cheek, and Tara and Willow kissed them back on theirs, the four of them sharing a quiet, emotional moment at the altar.

Tara's voice was soft and warm and full of love. "Blessed be."

Lucian and Buffy hooked their arms together and walked back down the short aisle as husband and wife, toward the people who loved them.

They stopped at the front row beside Xander and Anya, with Dawn on the grass in front of them and Clem waving his free, loose-skinned arm happily while the camcorder on his shoulder kept on rolling.

Buffy's voice was thick with emotion, happy tears spilling over. "I'm so glad you could make it from Texas. Thank you, Xander."

Xander rose from his chair and said, his voice cracking with genuine love, "Buff… I would never miss this for you. Ever."

He shook Lucian's hand firmly and sat back down, and beside him Anya dabbed at her eyes with a tissue.

Anya said, "Halfrek couldn't make it… but at least I'm here."

Buffy said, leaning down and softly kissing Anya's cheek, "I love you, Anya."

Then she and Lucian hooked their arms together again and walked on across the grass, arm in arm, toward the back door of the house and the warm light of the kitchen waiting there.

Above the backyard, past the string lights and the trees and the vine-and-rose archway, the warm glow of the wedding faded slowly up into the dark, star-filled sky.
