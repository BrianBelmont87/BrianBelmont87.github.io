We (Too) Shall Rest

Download this script (.txt)

Buffy the Vampire Slayer. Season seven, episode 08. We (Too) Shall Rest.

CHAPTER 1 Morning Hallway

That morning soft sun cut through the banister rails of the Summers house's upstairs hallway in moving bars of light and shadow that walked the walls like they belonged there, lived-in and familial, dust motes drifting slow in the beams. Across from Buffy's bedroom the bathroom door was shut with a shower still running faint behind it, steam ghosting under the crack, and the house smelled faintly of soap and last night's coffee.

Buffy had not slept at all; somewhere past three she had sat at her white vanity with the lamp on and Lucian asleep behind her, one arm across the empty space where she was supposed to be, and she had carried what the group still did not know. When morning finally found the hallway her eyes still held that watch — bruised soft under the lids, jaw a little tight, body upright because the Slayer did not get to fold in front of her sister.

The shower cut off, and Dawn came out wrapped in a towel with another towel piled on her wet hair, skin still damp, steam curling behind her into the hall. Buffy and Lucian were waiting right outside, Buffy still in the soft tank and lounge pants she had never changed out of, her hair a mess, and Lucian beside her with one hand resting lightly at the small of her back like he had been holding that spot for hours without needing thanks, steady and quiet, matching her silence with presence instead of questions.

Dawn grinned wide with that little-sister defiance sparking all the way up into her eyes. She pointed an index finger at her own eye in the classic I'm-watching-you, then turned it on the two of them, stuck her tongue out, and sauntered past them into her room, brushing her wet hair in long strokes that flicked water onto the floorboards.

Buffy rolled her eyes, fond under the fatigue, her voice warm despite everything she had held through the night.

Buffy said, "Finally… Dawn Summers, you are officially banned from hogging the hot water until you're thirty."

A smirk tugged at the corner of Buffy's mouth and deepened into real affection. Lucian's hand stayed where it was as the two of them stepped into the bathroom together. The door closed firmly behind them with the soft click of the latch, and faint water started again inside while the hallway stayed outside their private space, the morning light still walking the walls alone.

CHAPTER 2 Pancakes

Down in the Summers kitchen that morning, warm sun hit the stove and the steam rising off the cast-iron skillet, and the whole room smelled like batter and butter and something almost like peace.

Tara stood at the stove teaching Faith with the patience of someone who had already decided the house needed this morning, and Faith was actually smiling — rare, genuine, almost childlike — laughing under her breath while she flipped pancakes, flour smudged on her cheek and across the black tank she had slept in.

Tara gestured with the spatula toward the skillet, calm and detailed as she walked Faith through it, and then handed it over.

Tara said, "Okay, loosen the edge first… wrist flick right at the peak… commit like you mean it. You've got this, Faith."

Faith raised the spatula like a weapon, cocky joy flooding her face.

Faith said, "Flipping knives, anything — this is my time to shine. Watch and learn, Blondie — I'm about to school this pan!"

She went for the flip with big energy, and the pancake folded halfway over into a lopsided mess that landed back in the skillet with a wet slap. Faith froze, frustration flashing through her shoulders and twisting her mouth.

Faith muttered under her breath, scowling, "Son of a —"

Tara reached around in front of her and gently placed her hand over Faith's on the spatula handle, guiding her hand through the flip, warm and steady and in no rush, the two of them standing close at the stove, soft and intimate without asking for anything.

Faith went completely still while a flush rose in her cheeks and neck like someone had lit a match under her skin. Her throat worked, and words formed and got swallowed hard. The spatula handle suddenly felt like the most intimate thing she had ever held, and she hated that she noticed, and she hated more that she did not pull away.

Faith said, "…Uh… that's… not how I usually hold a handle…"

Tara did not pull away immediately, and her voice stayed warm and knowing, a soft smile at the edge of it.

Tara said, "You are learning… a lot more than cooking. And you're doing beautifully."

She leaned in and placed a single gentle kiss on Faith's cheek, soft and lingering just a heartbeat, then released the hand with a small reassuring pat and turned back to her scrambled eggs, humming quietly like she had not just rearranged something inside Faith's chest.

Faith stayed frozen one more second, fingers still shaped around the handle where Tara's hand had been, then let out a triumphant little exhale and tried again. The pancake arced perfectly and landed clean, golden and beautiful, and for half a second Faith looked like someone who had been handed a kindness she did not know how to name.

Faith set the spatula down on the edge of the stove and looked over at Tara, her grin going almost shy.

Faith said, "…Damn. Okay. That one felt… different. Good different. Thanks, T."

Through the open doorway into the garage, Angel slept deeply on the floor mattress in the far corner, still in his coat, curled slightly on his side, his face peaceful in the dim safe shadows and one arm draped protectively over an open grimoire beside him — the books he had been reading past three in the morning still open, still unfinished.

At the kitchen island behind Tara and Faith, Willow turned a single page of a thick old book with a soft dry rustle, deeply focused, exhaustion sitting under the small supportive smile she aimed at the stove.

Willow said, quiet and concentrated, "You two are adorable… and that pancake actually looks edible. Progress. Just don't burn the house down before we finish translating this next ward."

CHAPTER 3 The Table

A little later, when the last pancake was off the skillet, Tara carried the tall golden stack out of the kitchen in both hands, through the doorway and into the dining room, and set it down in the middle of the long wooden dining-room table, then went back for the scrambled eggs and brought the skillet through on a folded dish towel so the hot iron would not mark the wood. Faith followed her with the platter of sausage in her right hand and a cold can of cola in her left, and Dawn padded in behind them with the carton of juice and a stack of glasses hugged against her chest. Soon everyone was gathered around the dining-room table under the warm morning light coming through the windows, with plates of pancakes and eggs and sausage, cola for Faith, juice for Dawn, and the ordinary clatter of a house that had decided to pretend for one meal that nothing was coming.

Tara and Willow sat at the head, close enough that their shoulders almost touched. Dawn sat next to Tara in a hoodie and shorts, her wet hair pulled back. Buffy sat between Lucian and Faith, still carrying the night in her eyes, and Faith still had flour on her cheek and a cocky grin fighting a surprised smirk.

Willow forked a bite of scrambled eggs and held it to Tara's mouth with her other hand cupped under Tara's chin to catch a drip, her eyes sparkling with playful love. Tara leaned forward and accepted the bite with a soft grateful smile, chewing slowly. Willow leaned in for a quick playful peck on the lips with a small affectionate laugh, her hand lingering on Tara's cheek.

Buffy and Faith both reached for the same sausage link, and their forks clinked loud as they slowly lifted their eyes and locked on each other. Buffy raised her eyebrows in challenge, then deliberately pulled her fork back with a small nod, let Faith have it, and speared another. Faith picked the sausage up with her fingers, popped it in her mouth, chewed casually, and sipped her cola with her eyes half-smirking on Buffy.

Buffy looked at Dawn, firm but caring, with a slight tilt of her head.

Buffy said, "Make sure you are all caught up on homework. We are going to solve this preacher problem soon, so you will go back to school in a couple of days."

Dawn rolled her eyes a little, shoulders slumping, then nodded, resigned and accepting.

Dawn said, quiet, slightly pouty but compliant, "Yeah okay…"

Lucian picked up a sausage with his fork, his other hand cupped underneath for grease, and lifted it toward Buffy's mouth. The entire table stopped mid-bite, forks pausing and eyes widening. Buffy gave a soft surprised smile mixed with fondness, shrugged lightly, leaned forward, and took the sausage from the fork with her mouth, chewing slowly while she held his eyes. Lucian leaned in and kissed her gently on the cheek, and Buffy reached under the table and squeezed his leg, a warm private expression on her face that belonged only to them.

Willow turned toward Buffy, earnest and supportive, still holding Tara's hand on the table.

Willow said, "Me and Tara have been doing healing stuff upstairs on me last night. When you need me, I have some juice now."

Tara reached over between bites and squeezed Willow's hand on the table, her face soft and proud.

Tara said, with the stutter light and warm, "Sh sh she has been coming along nicely…"

Buffy said, "Good. We are going to need her."

Then Buffy suddenly pushed her chair back and stood, looking around the table with tired, determined eyes and a soft grateful smile, her voice warm and sincere.

Buffy said, "Thank you, Tara, for cooking, doing our laundry, cleaning the bathrooms, and the house. Being the home mother that Joyce… my mother once was… You have no idea how much what you do matters to me inside our home."

Everyone stopped eating mid-bite and lowered their forks. All eyes shifted first to Tara, then to Buffy, and the table fell completely silent.

Buffy said, her voice steady but heavy, "That is why the hard part comes next… We are going to use you as bait, to lure out the preacher…"

Faith stopped chewing and folded her arms tightly across her chest, jaw set, eyes narrowing. Dawn's eyes went wide with shock, her mouth slightly open, her hoodie sleeves pulled down over her hands. Willow's expression darkened, her eyes narrowing protectively, her free hand clenched in a fist around the cuff of her sleeve. Tara slowly stood, her face calm and resolute, her posture straight and steady.

Tara said, soft and clear, the stutter gone, quiet love in her eyes, "I understand… and everything I have done for you all was from my heart. Because I love each and every one of you."

Willow stood abruptly beside her, facing straight at Buffy, her face flushed with anger and her free hand balled at her side.

Willow's voice turned sharp and angry, a protective edge cutting through every word. "Buffy, you can't be serious… If you do this, I am going with her…"

Buffy met Willow's gaze, firm but strained.

Buffy said, low and decisive, "No, you're not. You're staying with us in the back waiting to spring the trap. Saving your juice for when we need it. As far as he can tell, she's alone. We'll be close the whole time."

Faith leaned forward slightly, arms still folded, and jerked her thumb toward herself and Buffy, low and serious and concerned.

Faith said, "This is risky, B… that guy is as strong as one of us."

Her thumb jabbed between the two of them once more, and her voice dropped quieter, almost grudging.

Faith said, "I said I'm in, B. Still don't love it."

Buffy slammed her hand down hard on the table, the sound sharp enough to make Dawn jump. She stood taller, her body shaking slightly, her eyes glistening, her voice cracking with emotion.

Buffy said, "My mind is made up on this. That is the plan."

For a second nobody breathed, and then she turned sharply and walked out of the dining room, her feet pounding heavily up the stairs like if she stayed one more heartbeat she might take it back, and the bedroom door slammed loud upstairs. Everyone froze, exchanging silent heavy glances — Tara worried and still standing, Willow dark with anger, Faith with her arms folded tight, Dawn pale and shocked, Lucian quietly concerned and already half-turned toward the stairs, and Willow's hand still loosely holding Tara's across the space between them. The quiet sat heavy over the half-eaten plates and the sausage going cold and the morning that had started with pancakes and a kiss on Faith's cheek.

CHAPTER 4 The Aftermath at the Table

In the same warm dining room a moment later, the tension had cooled but still hung in the air like something that had not finished landing.

Everyone except Faith was on their feet, and with Buffy gone upstairs and Lucian already turning toward the stairs after her, Willow stood flushed with anger and fear, her eyes wide and protective, and Dawn stood close with her sleeves over her hands, her eyes filling and her lip trembling. Faith stayed seated, casually chewing sausage with her elbow on the table, half-bored and fully alert.

Willow shook her head hard, her eyes locked on Tara, her voice rising.

Willow said, "No, no way, Tara… I can't stand there like that… while you… you vs the preacher? That is insane!"

Tara stepped forward and pulled Willow into a tight hug, gently pressing Willow's face against her collarbone, one hand cradling the back of Willow's head and the other steady at Willow's spine, quiet love in her eyes that did not flinch from the fear.

Tara's voice stayed soft and soothing, the stutter light, a calm certainty under every word. "It's for the right reason, Will… I understand what she is doing. Sh sh she is Buffy, sh she knows what she is doing."

Lucian quietly turned and headed upstairs without a word, concerned and determined, one hand on the banister as he climbed.

Faith stayed seated and slowly cracked her knuckles, right then left, one at a time, the sound sharp in the quiet room while she kept a low serious gaze on the group.

Faith's voice came low and rough, completely matter-of-fact. "Tonight it's going to be the preacher… or us. That's how this is. My blood is boiling for his payback too."

Tara looked across the table directly at Faith, her eyes warm and knowing, and Faith looked back. The cocky mask slipped as she remembered the backyard hug and the kitchen sink, and her expression softened, her eyes a little wet with no tears falling, until she looked away and stared hard at the wall with her jaw tight.

Dawn's voice cracked as she looked from Tara to the stairs and back, her eyes wide and scared.

Dawn's eyes filled with tears, and her voice came out small and pleading. "I don't want you to go… I have a bad feeling inside… I love you so much, Tara… Please don't go…"

Tara sat back down and gently pulled Dawn onto her right leg, cuddling her close against her chest and rocking slowly in the chair the way Joyce used to when the house still had a mother in it. One hand stroked Dawn's hair in long soothing strokes while the other rubbed her back in small circles, and she pressed a soft kiss to Dawn's forehead. Dawn's fists stayed knotted in Tara's shirt while her breathing hitched and slowly started to match Tara's, and for a minute the dining room was only that — Dawn catching her breath against Tara, and Tara not letting go while everything else frayed around them.

Willow remained standing, slowly turning her head toward the upstairs as if she could stare through the entire house at Buffy, her eyes burning with quiet anger, her bottom lip quivering, her hands clenched at her sides. She did not follow Lucian up, and she did not sit. She just stood there with Dawn's soft crying in her ears and Tara's calm in front of her and Buffy's slammed door still echoing in the stairwell, and the morning light on the table looked suddenly wrong for what they were about to do.

CHAPTER 5 Garage Planning

At sunset the Summers garage sat dim, the last warm orange light leaking around the edges of the closed door and cool interior shadow lying across their faces. Angel's mattress and the open books still claimed the far corner like unfinished work that would wait.

Buffy stood straight and commanding under the fatigue with Lucian close beside her, quietly worried. Angel leaned against the far wall in his dark overcoat, arms crossed, his face shadowed with concern.

Buffy said, firm, tired but resolute, "We're not waiting to find the weapon in the Slayer diaries. We are going to take down the preacher tonight before he comes back into the house. We cannot keep going like this anymore, waiting for him to come to us."

She looked directly at Angel, the plan she had built with Faith late last night still sitting sharp behind her eyes.

Buffy continued, "I know the preacher is targeting Tara specifically for some reason… we are using her as bait. I will hold Willow and her magic in reserve until the last second to hit him with everything she has literally, then we all swoop in together coordinated and finish him off."

Angel rubbed the back of his head gently, his brow furrowed, concerned and hesitant.

Angel said, low and careful, "Buffy, that preacher is as strong as—"

Buffy interrupted quickly, her voice sharp and decisive, eyes flashing, "I know — a Slayer. That is exactly why we are not waiting anymore. We are taking the fight straight to him and ending this once and for all."

She turned to Lucian beside her in the dim orange light, her tone softening.

Buffy said, "Lucian will take Dawn in his car just outside of town and wait for me to call him to come back… if we make it… if we win…"

She stepped close to him and interlocked their fingers, looking up at him with vulnerable eyes, then pulled him in and kissed him deeply, slow and full of emotion, before she drew back just enough to rest her forehead against his.

Buffy said, her voice soft and cracking slightly, her eyes glistening, "This is always the hardest part, Lucian… the waiting to see if I come back to you or not… to see if the evil I am fighting finally wins. Thank you for loving me enough, for being brave enough to handle this… I love you so much…"

Lucian's face tightened with emotion, his eyes shining, and he pulled her tightly against him, arms around her back, holding her close and protective, his breathing shaky and his face buried in her hair, and for a long moment neither of them moved while the garage around them went quiet enough to hear the faint tick of the metal door cooling as the sun went down, and Angel's stillness by the wall. Buffy breathed him in, and Lucian's hand spanned her shoulder blades like he was memorizing the shape of her coming back.

Behind them Angel winced slightly, jaw tight, and looked away, pained to watch the exchange — old history sitting in a room that was not his anymore, love he used to hold now belonging to someone else's arms, and he took it without making it theirs to carry.

CHAPTER 6 Tara Prepares

Upstairs at dusk, while the meeting went on down in the garage, the last of the light slanted across the mirror in Willow and Tara's bedroom, and at first Willow was only a pleading blur at the edge of Tara's resolved calm.

Tara stood in a simple black bra and black panties, and whatever fear lived in the room did not make it into her hands. Her face was calm and her eyes were steady, and she ignored Willow's pleas with quiet determination — not cruelty, just the kind of love that had already decided. She turned to the mirror and tied her long golden dirty-blonde hair into a tight ponytail, her posture straight and purposeful like she was dressing for work she alone could do.

Willow stood nearby in an oversized linen shirt, her face pleading and worried, her voice rising but reaching Tara distant and muffled, as if Tara had already stepped halfway out of the room.

Tara reached for a tight long-sleeve dark red shirt and pulled it on carefully, wincing slightly as she tugged it down over the fresh bandage on her lower left side near the belly button, smoothing the fabric with steady hands. She took tight-fitting black yoga pants from the top of the dresser and slipped them on one leg at a time, bending smoothly. Then she knelt, put on her running shoes, and laced them tightly, her face calm and resolved while the sound of her own fast heartbeat went loud in her head.

She turned to face Willow, and Willow's voice snapped suddenly back into focus, raw and desperate.

Willow said, her voice breaking, her eyes wide with fear, "Tara, please, baby, think about this…"

Tara held Willow gently by the upper arms, looking directly into her eyes with quiet strength and love, and said nothing. She closed her eyes, leaned in, and softly kissed Willow's forehead, lingering there with her hands steady on Willow's arms.

Then she released her, picked up a large canvas bag with its contents hidden inside, shouldered it, and walked out without another word.

Willow's face crumpled, and she dropped hard to her knees, her body folding forward like something vital had walked out of the room with Tara. Tears streamed down, soaking the front of her shirt, her shoulders shaking violently and her chest heaving with shattered gasping breaths as she cried uncontrollably on the bedroom floor, while the dusk light kept fading across the empty mirror and the salt circle from last night's healing still faintly marked the floorboards where they had sat with hands clasped, knitting back together what had been torn. Magic sat thin and fragile inside her — threads of light across a black void — and she needed every one of them for tonight, and right now all she could do was break on the floor and try to breathe.

CHAPTER 7 Departure

Later that night the front entryway of the Summers house sat dim, and from the living room the antique clock ticked steady against the quiet.

Tara came down the stairs quickly, her feet steady with purpose, in the dark red long-sleeve shirt, black yoga pants, and running shoes, the canvas bag over her shoulder and her ponytail swinging, her face calm and resolved and her breathing steady. She reached the boarded-up front entry, the big plywood sheet Buffy and Lucian had fitted into the doorway, where Buffy, Angel, and Faith were waiting — Buffy serious and protective, Angel tense with his arms crossed, Faith leaning against the wall with her arms folded, grim and alert.

Buffy said, low and firm, her eyes locked on Tara, "No matter what, remember the plan, where to go, and where to make him circle around. If you get caught during the circle around, Tara…"

Tara looked directly at Buffy and nodded once, her face strong and steady.

Tara said, quiet and resolute, "I know… I would be alone with him…"

Angel said, low and concerned, his jaw tight, "You would be alone with him for too long…"

Tara nodded again and took a few deep breaths. Then she shouldered the plywood panel aside and jogged out into the dark street away from the house, her ponytail whipping, her arms pumping.

The only sound left inside was the steady ticking of the antique clock in the living room as Tara's figure got smaller and smaller, jogging down the middle of the empty street into the night.

As soon as Tara was gone, Faith went upstairs and appeared in the doorway of Willow and Tara's bedroom, leaning casually against the frame in a black tank top and cargo shorts with her arms folded, serious and steady, looking down at Willow, who was still on her knees crying.

Faith said, low, rough, and direct, "That sadness? Turn it into anger. Rage into passion and fire. Love won't help you this time, not against that thing out there."

Willow slowly stood, her face wet with tears, her shirt damp on her chest, her eyes red and swollen, and walked toward Faith on shaky legs. Faith stepped forward and stopped her gently but firmly with both hands on her shoulders, point blank in her face.

Faith said, her voice low and steady, almost soft — not her usual armor — "You want me to carry you downstairs? It's show time, Red. Let's go…"

Then her eyes locked on Willow's, sincere and intense, and something from the kitchen sink and the backyard hug and Tara's kiss on her cheek sat open in her face for once without a joke to hide it.

Faith said, "Your girl… she taught me something since I've been here, Red. She taught me this feeling… this feeling that I have inside of me now. I don't know what it is, but it feels gentle, powerful and good… I promise you… I would die for her too."

They went downstairs, out past the plywood, and piled into Joyce's silver sedan with the others. Willow sat in the back seat next to Faith with her eyes closed and her hands on her knees, breathing slowly and deeply as she meditated and gathered her magic, her face shifting from grief to focused resolve. On the driver's side, the door Caleb had torn off in the alley yesterday was still nothing but an open gap. Angel sat in the driver's seat in his heavy overcoat, grim and focused, and Buffy rode in the passenger seat with her jaw set and her eyes forward.

At the same time Lucian and Dawn drove off in the opposite direction in Lucian's black 1981 Trans Am SE, gold trim along its sides and the large eagle spread across its hood, Dawn small and scared in the passenger seat, and Lucian kept his hand on the wheel and didn't argue about being sent away, even though every mile took him further from Buffy.

Angel started the engine, and the sedan pulled away slowly, following Tara's direction into the night.

CHAPTER 8 The Chase and the Trap

Out on the Sunnydale streets that night the street lamps burned quiet in the cool air, and ahead the construction site waited with its exposed skeleton frames, sandy ground and crane shadows, harsh work-light spilling out near the site perimeter.

Tara kept jogging steadily, the canvas bag bouncing lightly against her back with every stride and her ponytail whipping behind her. Her breathing stayed controlled but grew heavier as the streets emptied around her, and light sweat glistened on her forehead and neck. Her shoes found the familiar cracks in the Sunnydale pavement and kept going anyway.

She moved closer to downtown, passing near the closed Magic Box with its windows dark, then turned right past the dark Espresso Pump, with almost no cars on the road. She kept on toward the distant construction site, arms pumping, heart loud, bait doing exactly what bait does — drawing the thing that hunts.

Far behind her, Caleb stepped from the shadows, tall and imposing in a long dark coat thrown over his charcoal suit, heavy black boots on his feet, fully corporeal, with real weight and real breath and a real wrongness to him. He tilted his head slightly and whispered into the night, his voice low and cold.

Caleb whispered, eerily calm, "The filthy nest is going to be cleansed tonight…"

His right hand twitched once, and then he broke into a steady powerful run after her.

Tara reached the construction site and ran past rows of wooden skeleton homes with no walls yet, exposed pipes rising from the ground. She was laboring now, her brow sweat-covered, her ponytail sticking to her damp neck. She ducked into the shadows of the drywall racks and large pipes and sprinted quickly left, her feet almost silent on the wet muddy patches, avoiding any dust kick-up.

She reached the back of the site, ducked beside a large concrete sewer pipe resting on blocks beside the crane, crawled inside, and curled her body small. She closed her eyes tight and focused on slowing her breathing, her chest rising and falling deliberately and her hands pressed lightly to the cold concrete, quieting her thundering heartbeat, her face concentrated and calm under the tension.

In the middle of the site Caleb stopped, scanning the sandy ground for tracks, his heavy coat swaying. He saw faint prints mixing with the workers' footprints, and his face twisted with frustration. He pounded his fist hard into a stacked drywall rack, smashing pieces, dust exploding around his knuckles. Then he guessed a direction and charged, suddenly turning right — the opposite direction from the one Tara had gone.

Inside the sewer pipe Tara stayed completely still and silent, curled tight, eyes wide and focused, breathing shallow and controlled, sweat on her brow. She slid the canvas bag around in front of her, reached in, and pulled out a small jar of nails and a handful of pennies, watching Caleb head to the far opposite side of the site, missing her completely.

Once he turned right at the far end of the pipes and disappeared, Tara slowly crawled out with extreme care, planting one muddy running shoe at a time until she had fully emerged. She knelt quietly beside Caleb's large footprints, her face calm and concentrated, and placed several pennies into the footprints first, then one nail after another from the jar, jabbing them firmly into the prints.

Tara whispered in Latin, her voice low and steady, "Errans in tenebris, graves sint pedes tui ut cor tuum."

The words asked that his feet grow as heavy as his heart.

She kept jabbing the nails in one by one, her expression resolute.

Tara continued in Latin, her voice firm, "Tenebrae cordis tui te pungant omni gradu."

She was asking the darkness in his heart to sting him with every step he took.

In the distance Caleb suddenly screamed with rage and pain.

Caleb roared, furious, "I am going to PURIFY YOU SLOWLY WITCH… UNTIL YOU SHINE!"

His face twisted with pain, teeth gritted, eyes burning with fury. He dragged one foot at a time, moving slowly, his massive legs impossibly heavy despite his super strength, his body hunched forward fighting the weight, and inside his boots, where no one could see, his feet were bleeding. With great effort he power-walked back the other direction and rounded a corner.

Suddenly he spotted Tara in the distance, about sixty yards away, and pointed a thick finger at her, his face dark and hateful.

Caleb growled, low and venomous, "There you are, you filthy little witch… You are all going to burn! BURN IN HELL!"

Tara stood tall, her ponytail slightly messy from the run, her face calm, and called clearly across the distance.

Tara said, steady, almost casual, "Come and get me then, preacher…"

She turned and sprinted toward the front of the construction site, where Joyce's car was now silently parked, her arms pumping and her ponytail whipping behind her.

Caleb kept power-walking after her one heavy leg at a time, his face raw fury, the veins standing out in his neck.

Caleb roared through the pain, "You think this is going to stop me? It won't! Nothing can stop me… I am the only son!"

He grew louder as he dragged his heavy legs on, one after the other.

Tara slowed at the edge of the site, turned back, and watched him with her arms crossed over her chest, her expression almost bored, one eyebrow slightly raised, a small ironic smirk on her lips.

Tara said, low and dry, almost amused under her breath, "Only son… right."

CHAPTER 9 The Fight

At the construction site, the floodlights from the skeleton homes threw harsh white pools and long shadows across the gravel and grit, and at the front of the site Tara crouched behind Joyce's car with the canvas bag clutched to her chest, watching between the floodlit frames as the trap she had led him into finally closed.

Faith balanced on the very top edge of the cab roof of a big yellow excavator, twelve feet off the ground. She cracked her knuckles, right first with a sharp pop and a small grin, then left, her eyes locked down on Caleb below, her ponytail messy with strands stuck to her sweaty neck, her expression cocky and feral.

Faith said, low and taunting, her voice carrying across the site, "Hey preacher… ready for round three?"

She leapt straight off the cab roof with zero hesitation, body vertical, knees tucked, arms raised high and both fists clenched together into one solid hammer. Through the full twelve-foot drop her ponytail whipped up and then snapped down, her tank fabric stretching and her leg muscles firing, her joined fists aimed at Caleb's head.

She landed perfectly, both fists smashing into the crown of Caleb's skull with a meaty crack. The impact forced his head down hard, and his knees buckled instantly, his body dropping straight onto both knees in the gravel with a heavy thud, dust exploding outward in a six-foot radius and his coat flapping violently.

Before he could recover, Faith pivoted on her left foot and fired her right leg in a fast low arc, the underside of her boot connecting square on the left side of Caleb's jaw in a perfect roundhouse. The kick snapped his head hard to the right, his body twisting with the momentum, shoulders rolling, coat flaring as he crashed sideways into the gravel on his hip and elbow with a gritty scrape.

Caleb's head snapped back up almost immediately, his eyes wild and furious, blood at the corner of his mouth, his face twisted with dark delight.

Caleb growled, low and hungry, "Good… another dirty girl for me to clean…"

Buffy stepped in directly behind him, arms crossed tight, chin lifted, her expression cold and commanding and her stance wide and grounded.

Buffy said, flat and deadly calm, "Make that three."

She uncrossed her arms in one smooth motion, stepped forward, and drove her front right boot straight into Caleb's left ribs in a brutal front kick, her hip snapping forward and her knee extending fully, the boot sole landing with a deep meaty thud that lifted his entire torso slightly off the ground. The force sent him skidding sideways across the gravel almost eight feet, his coat dragging, a dust plume trailing behind him.

Caleb pushed up slowly, roaring pure rage, the veins bulging in his neck, his coat torn at the shoulder, his eyes blazing.

The two Slayers immediately flanked him, Buffy on the left and Faith on the right, both dropping into perfect fighting front stances, bodies coiled, shoulders slightly rounded, eyes locked.

Buffy glanced sideways at Faith without turning her head, her voice low and focused.

Buffy said, "Ready?"

Faith kept her eyes glued on Caleb and gave one sharp nod, a small predatory smirk at the corner of her mouth.

Faith said, quiet and deadly, "Ready."

The three of them exploded into motion in a tight triangle.

Faith opened first with a blazing left jab straight to Caleb's jaw, a sharp crack that snapped his head back half an inch, and almost simultaneously Buffy threw a right cross from the opposite side that slammed into his ribs with a meaty thud, his coat flaring.

Caleb absorbed both like a tank, snarling, and swung a heavy right backhand in a wide arc of his massive arm that caught Faith across the cheek, his knuckles grazing her jaw. Faith's head whipped sideways, her ponytail lashing and a small spray of spit flying, but she rolled with it and fired an immediate lightning left hook to his liver, and Caleb only grunted and stayed upright.

Buffy ducked low under a swinging arm and drove an uppercut straight up into his chin with a loud clack that snapped Caleb's head back. Immediately he grabbed Buffy by the front of her tank with his left hand, the fabric stretching tight, and effortlessly lifted her clean off the ground, her boots dangling, holding her suspended for a full second with her eyes wide in shock.

He roared and hurled her sideways like a ragdoll, and Buffy flew horizontally, arms flailing, hair whipping, straight into Faith. The two Slayers collided hard and crashed into the gravel in a tangled heap, dust exploding, both of them grunting on impact.

For one heartbeat they lay there, Faith on her back and Buffy half on top of her, both breathing hard, faces dirty, eyes burning with fury, and then they moved as one.

They exploded upward in perfect sync, Faith kipping up first and Buffy rolling sideways and springing up beside her, pure Slayer rage in both of them, teeth bared, eyes locked on Caleb, shoulders heaving, and they charged again with even more fury.

Faith threw a blinding six-punch combination — left jab, right cross, left hook, right uppercut, two fast body shots — her fists blurring, while Buffy mirrored her with rapid kicks — a front kick to the knee, a spinning back kick to the ribs, a jumping knee to the sternum.

Caleb took the barrage and refused to go down, answering with heavier, slower, devastating power shots: a massive right haymaker that Buffy barely ducked, the wind of it ruffling her hair, and a brutal left elbow to Faith's collarbone that lifted her slightly off her feet. He grabbed Faith by the throat for a split second, lifted her an inch, and slammed her down, but she twisted in mid-air and landed on her feet.

Both of them reset instantly, circling again, sweat flying, eyes blazing with adrenaline and rage, bodies coiled and ready for the next exchange.

Faith and Buffy suddenly broke away in perfect sync, sprinting to opposite sides, boots kicking up sprays of gravel, hair whipping, looping behind him in a wide arc that forced Caleb to spin in place.

Then the dark magic weight finally lifted from Caleb's legs, and his eyes flashed with savage joy as he exploded forward in a clean, terrifying sprint, long powerful strides eating the ground, coat flapping like a cape, arms pumping, heavy boots pounding the dirt.

Caleb roared as he ran, his voice echoing across the site, "Run all you want, little girls… I will still purify you both!"

Buffy and Faith instantly split, Buffy hard left and Faith hard right, their paths diverging like scissors opening. Caleb skidded to a brief stop in the gravel, head snapping left then right, eyes wild with indecision, chest heaving, and hesitated one heartbeat. Then he snarled, committed, and went straight after Buffy with renewed fury.

He barreled straight through a half-built wooden house frame like a freight train, his shoulders smashing two-by-fours into splinters, wood exploding in all directions, dust and nails flying. Without breaking stride he leapt onto the bed of a parked construction truck, his boots slamming the metal with a loud bang, and launched a full-body dive toward Buffy.

Buffy tried to dodge half a second too late, and Caleb crashed into her mid-run, his massive shoulder slamming her torso. Her body folded at the waist, arms flailing, hair whipping forward, and they tumbled hard across the gravel in a rolling heap until Caleb ended up on top, pinning her with his weight, his knees on either side of her hips, one huge hand gripping her tank collar and yanking her face up inches from his.

Caleb leaned down, his eyes inches from hers, his voice low and demonic.

Caleb said, "You think you can stop what is coming? I am what is coming!"

Buffy's eyes flashed with savage defiance, and she snapped her head forward and bit down hard into the meat of his cheek right under his right eye, her teeth sinking deep and her jaw locking, and blood immediately welled around her mouth.

Caleb roared with pain and rage and kicked her off with both legs in a violent thrust, launching Buffy backward to roll through the gravel, spitting blood, her chest heaving.

Caleb slowly stood, towering, one hand pressed to his bleeding cheek, blood running over his fingers and dripping onto his coat, his face a mask of pure hatred and disgust.

Caleb spat blood, his voice dripping venom, "You filthy whore…"

Ten feet away Willow stood completely still, eyes closed, her hair starting to lift and darken at the roots, her blouse sleeves fluttering, dark energy crackling faintly around her clenched fists. Her face was calm, almost serene, and terrifying for it. She was pushing her damaged magic past comfort again, and she did not stop.

Buffy, still on the ground with blood on her lips, screamed raw and desperate at the top of her lungs, "NOW, WILL!"

CHAPTER 10 Lightning and Stone

The floodlights over the construction site flickered wildly, strobing white pulses across the gravel and the wooden frames.

Willow stood ten feet behind Caleb with her eyes closed one long moment, her hair slowly lifting as it turned jet black from the roots outward, dark veins spiderwebbing her cheeks and running down her neck. Her blouse sleeves fluttered with the raw power building in her as she slowly extended both hands forward, palms open, fingers spread, wrists locked straight.

Buffy turned onto her side where she lay on the ground, arms crossed tight over her face, head tucked, body curled small.

Willow's eyes snapped open, pure black voids, and she whispered under her breath, barely moving her lips, “For Tara…”

Then she unleashed it.

A thick jagged bolt of crackling white-violet lightning, blazing pink and lavender at its edges, ripped from her right palm first and slammed Caleb in the upper back with a deafening crack, arcing his spine. His skeleton flashed bright white inside his flesh for a split second, every rib and vertebra glowing like an X-ray, and his body jerked violently upward, his coat smoking.

A second bolt came immediately from her left hand, square in his face, forking across his cheek and eye. His skull lit up again, jawbone and teeth glowing through the skin, his mouth open in a silent scream, black smoke pouring from his nostrils.

Willow kept pouring it into him in a relentless eight-second barrage, bolt after bolt: his chest, his ribcage glowing like a lantern; his left shoulder, the bones of his arm flashing; his right thigh, the bone lighting up through the muscle; the side of his head, his entire skull illuminating, the eye sockets like headlights. Each impact convulsed him in a different direction, shoulders jerking, knees buckling, back arching, arms flailing, his screams guttural and constant, his voice cracking, black smoke coming from his mouth and ears.

Finally she cut the power, and Caleb collapsed face-first into the gravel, his body smoking heavily, slow tendrils of black vapor curling upward, his coat charred at the edges.

Willow's knees buckled under her and she caught herself on one hand in the gravel, a thin line of blood running from her nose over her lip while the black drained out of her hair back to red everywhere but the roots and her chest heaved. Breathing hard, she said just one thing, quietly, more to herself than to anyone else.

Willow said, "That was for Tara."

Faith rushed over, grabbed Buffy's arm in a firm grip, and hauled her to her feet, and the two of them traded a hard satisfied high-five, faces dirty, small triumphant grins on their lips.

The group — Buffy, Faith, Willow, and Tara, who had rejoined them — walked together toward Joyce's car, veering far left near the front of the site, where Angel waited in the distance. Angel gave them a thumbs-up as they came, fresh wet cement streaking the left shoulder and sleeve of his heavy coat and grey smeared across both of his hands.

Suddenly Caleb's body jolted once… twice… then a third violent spasm. His black eyes snapped open, bleeding thick black tears down his cheeks like oil, and he pushed himself up slowly, turned, and walked toward them at a deliberate, terrifyingly calm pace.

Caleb said, low, raspy, completely confident, "You can never kill me… I cannot die…"

Buffy and Faith stepped to the front together, bodies low, hands up, shoulders squared, eyes locked on him in unified defiance, while Willow caught Tara by the arm and pulled her back behind Angel.

Suddenly Caleb broke into a blind roaring sprint straight at them, arms wide, coat flapping.

He never reached them, because his right foot plunged deep into a hidden pit of liquid cement, sinking him to the thigh. He tried to correct, but his momentum carried him forward and he crashed face-first into the wet grey slurry, thrashing wildly, arms flailing, trying to swim forward while the cement quickly swallowed him up to the neck, thick grey sludge coating his face and shoulders.

Willow stepped forward calmly, knelt at the edge of the pit, and gently placed both hands on the surface of the wet cement, her eyes focused and dark and fresh blood welling at her nostril as she pushed what was left of her magic down into the slurry.

Willow said, soft, almost gentle, commanding, "What was water… now become solid…"

The cement instantly began to harden with loud cracking sounds, the thick sludge turning to solid stone in rapid waves that locked Caleb inside. He screamed, then laughed maniacally, then screamed again as the rock climbed over his chest and neck and sealed his mouth, until only the top of his head and his black, bleeding eyes stayed above the stone, staring out at them with pure hatred.

CHAPTER 11 The Burden

Outside the pit the floodlights burned dimmer now, and the wind moved across the gravel while the stone encasement held the center of the site. The air had gone cold, and the only warmth left was on the faces of the people about to turn away from what came next.

Angel looked at Caleb's eyes and said, "We can't leave him like that."

Buffy took in a shaky breath, let it out, and said in a quiet voice, "I know…"

Buffy walked to a half-built wooden frame where a pickaxe leaned against a rebar stack. She gripped the long wooden handle with both hands, testing the weight, her knuckles white, her face hard and exhausted. Then she walked slowly toward the stone-encased Caleb, boots crunching gravel, her eyes locked on the visible face with cold intent.

Angel stepped directly into her path and put one hand gently but firmly on the pickaxe handle, stopping her, his expression ancient, tired, and protective.

Angel said, low, gentle but absolute, "No… I have to do it. You're not that kind of person. None of you should be."

Buffy stopped dead and looked down at the ground for a long moment, breathing shaky, then slowly released the pickaxe into his hands without a word, their fingers brushing as she let go, her eyes wet, and she nodded once with her lips pressed tight.

Angel took the pickaxe, gripping it firmly, and spoke quietly to the group.

Angel said, soft and heavy, "Don't look."

Everyone turned their backs — Tara pulled Willow gently into her chest, shielding her, and Buffy turned sideways with her arms wrapped around herself. Faith was the only one still facing forward, arms crossed, her eyes locked on Caleb, cold and unblinking, her jaw set.

Angel raised the pickaxe high above his head, his muscles straining under the coat, and brought it down with brutal finality three times, driving it down into his skull.

Still turned away, Buffy winced hard at the first sickening wet crunch of the pickaxe caving Caleb's skull, her eyes squeezed shut, her shoulders hiked, a small involuntary whimper caught in her throat behind gritted teeth. The second and third impacts were wetter, heavier crunches reverberating into the night, and her whole body flinched with each one, her head jerking slightly, a single tear slipping down her cheek as she forced herself to stay turned away.

After the final blow Angel tossed the pickaxe aside, and it clattered loudly onto the solid stone encasing Caleb with a metallic ring and a dull thud.

The group stood in heavy silence, with only the distant wind and their ragged breathing. Faith was still facing forward, her eyes on what Angel had finished, because somebody had to look. Buffy was still turned away with her arms around herself, the tear track drying on her cheek while her shoulders slowly came down from the flinch. Tara held Willow, and Willow held Tara. Angel stood over the stone with the pickaxe discarded and centuries of killing sitting quiet in his posture like a burden he had chosen so the rest of them would not have to learn its weight tonight.

Nobody spoke, and the night held what they had done, and for once the holding was enough.

When the group finally turned away from the stone and started back toward Joyce's car across the grit under the dimming floodlights, Angel thumbed his cell and called Lucian, telling him in a few low words that it was over and that he could bring Dawn home.

CHAPTER 12 Home Again

Back at the Summers house the driveway lay lit by the spill of the porch light, everything quiet and still with the muted exhaustion of people still processing what they had done.

Joyce's silver sedan pulled into the driveway with its driver's side door still completely missing and Angel at the wheel, cement drying in streaks down his coat. Tara sat in the front passenger seat with her seatbelt carefully positioned above the fresh stitches on her lower left abdomen, and Buffy, Faith, and Willow were squeezed into the back seat. It was quiet inside — no music, no talking, only the engine's rumble and the tires crunching the grit they had carried home from the site across the paved driveway while everyone processed.

Angel put the car in park and killed the engine, and one by one they climbed out. Tara moved carefully with a hand over her side. Buffy climbed out of the back seat first, then Faith, then Willow, and no one spoke. Even Faith was quiet, her eyes distant and her arms loose. Buffy's face was drawn and tired, and Willow looked pale and emotionally wrung out.

Only a minute behind them Lucian's black Trans Am pulled into the driveway, and Dawn threw the passenger door open before the car had fully stopped and ran straight across the driveway.

Dawn cried out, her voice breaking, "Tara!"

She crashed into Tara and wrapped both arms around her, sobbing into her shoulder. Tara winced slightly at the impact on her stitches but immediately folded Dawn into a gentle, steady hug, her hand stroking the back of Dawn's head.

Tara said, soft and warm, kissing the top of Dawn's head, "Hey… I'm okay. I'm right here, sweetie."

Willow stepped up beside them, and Tara kept one arm around Dawn and reached out with the other, pulling Willow into the hug. The three of them stood like that for a long moment, Willow and Dawn crying quietly and Tara holding them both with quiet strength.

Tara kissed Dawn's cheek once more and gently guided them both toward the house.

Tara said softly, "Come on. Let's go inside."

Willow, Tara, and Dawn walked up the porch steps and disappeared inside past the plywood, which knocked back into its frame behind them.

That left Lucian, Angel, Buffy, and Faith outside in the driveway in a loose cluster, still quiet, all four of them dirty and aching and too tired to talk.

Then a phantom shape flickered beside Buffy in a faint cold blue light, a low hum hanging in the air around it. The First Evil wore Buffy's face and the clothes she had died in, the white sweater and grey slacks from the night she dove from Glory's tower, spotless and untouched, while the real Buffy stood beside it in a dirt-streaked tank with dried blood on her face. It was non-corporeal, a flat unnatural presence that put no weight on the driveway and moved no air, and its expression was calm and knowing, like it thought it had the upper hand.

Faith looked at it for one second, then deliberately turned her head and looked at Angel instead.

The First said smugly, in Buffy's voice, "You think you won? You haven't won anything. I have an entire legion waiting to come out of the gr—"

Buffy talked right over it, her voice tired and steady, looking at Angel, "So Angel, I think you should spend the night again. We should keep working on those texts."

Lucian nodded from beside Buffy, motioning toward Angel with an open hand.

Lucian said, "Yeah. That's a good idea."

The First tried again, its voice sharpening, "You think you can ignore me? I am every—"

Faith interrupted, casual, almost thoughtful, still looking at Angel, "You know what? I kind of like being back here again. It's nice. I missed the air of this place."

The four of them — Buffy, Lucian, Angel, and Faith — turned their backs on the First Evil at the same time and walked toward the house together, and no one looked back.

The First Evil's face contorted with pure seething hate, its mouth twisting and its eyes burning as it watched them leave. It stood alone in the driveway a few seconds longer, touching nothing and disturbing nothing, and then the cold blue light guttered, the hum dropped to silence, and it was simply gone. The driveway lay quiet again except for the long scrape of plywood being dragged back across the entry behind the group inside.

CHAPTER 13 Rest

Inside, a warm low light spilled from the rooms above onto the staircase and the upstairs hallway, and the old wooden steps creaked softly underfoot in a house that had finally gone still.

Tara and Willow went slowly up the stairs, Tara moving carefully with one hand lightly over her stitches and Willow staying close with one hand at the small of Tara's back for support. Neither of them spoke, and there was only their slow breathing and the soft creak of the old wooden steps.

When they reached the bedroom, Willow opened the door, they stepped inside, and the door closed gently behind them.

Inside Willow and Tara's bedroom the lamp threw a warm low light across the bed, where Tara and Willow knelt facing each other and spent a long moment just looking — tired, relieved, quietly overwhelmed by tonight.

Slowly they reached for each other at the same time.

The kiss started very gentle and soft, their lips barely brushing at first, catching their breath from the night like they were checking that the other one was still real. It deepened gradually, slow at first and then growing with need and relief, the kind that comes after running toward something that could have taken you. Willow's hands cradled Tara's face, and Tara's fingers slid into Willow's hair and held on.

Willow gently took the hem of Tara's dark red long-sleeve shirt and lifted it over her head with care around the stitches, and Tara helped, arms rising, her breath hitching once when the fabric cleared. Underneath was the simple black bra, her stomach and shoulders bare, the bandage still there from the alley and the night — proof she had walked into the trap and come home. Willow let the shirt fall aside and slowly lowered Tara onto her back, following her down, kissing her deeply, forehead to forehead between kisses like she could not stop checking.

Tara's hands moved slowly up and down Willow's back, soothing, grounding, quiet love, and Willow trembled once and then settled into the touch. It was faces and hands and the way their bodies fit together in relief and safety, and none of it was hunger; it was healing, two people who had nearly lost each other tonight choosing each other again, freely and with their whole hearts. They held each other in the soft light and let the night finally loosen its hold, and for a while the bedroom was only breath and warmth and two heartbeats slowing together for two people who had almost lost this.

In Buffy's bedroom the only light was the faint blue-white of the streetlight coming through the window, and Buffy lay on her back completely passed out, one arm thrown above her head, breathing slow and deep for the first time since her long watch at the vanity — the kind of sleep that only hits after the body has spent everything and believes the house is safe. She was in a simple dark bra with the sheet pulled up to her waist, the dirt washed off and the blood rinsed away.

Lucian lay on his side next to her, propped on one elbow, watching her sleep, his face soft with quiet love and a relief so deep it almost hurt to look at. After a long moment he carefully, gently lowered himself and slid his arm across Buffy's bare stomach, resting his hand lightly just below her ribs where he could feel her breathe, very careful not to wake her, his own body finally unclenching after hours of waiting outside town with Dawn and a phone that might never have rung.

He closed his eyes and took a slow breath, finally allowing himself to rest, while the whole house lay quiet and steady around them like it was tucking them in.

In Dawn's bedroom Dawn lay curled in bed under blankets pulled up to her chin. She stirred slightly, her feet moving under the covers in a small sleepy shuffle, and then settled again, her breathing soft and even and her face peaceful, looking safe.

Below the quiet house, down the narrow wooden stairs, the single bulb in the garage was still burning, and under it Faith lay on the weight bench doing controlled bench presses with heavy weight, never letting the bar slam. Every rep came slow and deliberate on the way down, like if she rushed she might crack open whatever Tara's kiss on her cheek had started that morning, her face focused, her jaw tight, sweat on her brow. She was in a black tank top and cargo shorts, working the night out of her body one careful press at a time — the preacher, the pit, the wet crunch she had watched when nobody else would.

Angel stood nearby leaning on the workbench with his arms crossed, talking in a low calm voice too quiet to carry past the two of them, his posture relaxed and almost brotherly, the kind of company that does not ask you to explain why you cannot sleep yet. Faith listened while she lifted, nodding once with her eyes on the bar, and for once she did not fill the quiet with a joke.

The night wore on slowly around them, and Faith kept lifting while Angel kept her company.

Upstairs people slept and healed and held each other, and down here two fighters stayed awake a little longer, because that was how they rested.

END OF EPISODE 08 – WE (TOO) SHALL REST
