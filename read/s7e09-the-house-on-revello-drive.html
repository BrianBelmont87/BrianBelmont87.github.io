The House on Revello Drive

Download this script (.txt)

Buffy the Vampire Slayer. Season seven, episode 09. The House on Revello Drive.

CHAPTER 1 Morning Light

The morning after Caleb fell, Tara lay on her back in Joyce's old bed with her eyes half-lidded and one bare arm bent above her head, and Willow was already curled on her side facing her with her cheek on her hand and a private little smile waking on her mouth before either of them had decided to get up. Rich golden sunrise had pushed through the gauzy curtains and laid honeyed light across bare shoulders and collarbones, soft shadows pooling in the folds of the white sheet that had slid down to their hips, and somewhere outside a bird started up like it had forgotten yesterday entirely.

Both of them were warm under the crisp white sheets, and when Tara shifted, the quiet outline of the fresh stitches on her lower left side near her belly button showed for a moment without asking for attention, and Willow, who could still feel last night's nosebleed as a dull ache behind her eyes, knew her magic was still thin and damaged and that one quiet morning was not going to put it back.

Willow's right hand stroked Tara's face — cheekbone, jaw, the column of her throat — and then she leaned in and kissed her deep and slow the way morning loves to kiss when nobody is rushing it, and her hand slid under the edge of the sheet in one smooth loving motion.

Tara's eyes fluttered and she bit her lower lip, and a tiny involuntary sound broke out of her.

Tara's breath hitched, and her voice came out low and wrecked against Willow's mouth. "Will…"

She shuddered, her shoulders and collarbones going tight and then melting, and both her hands clutched Willow's upper arms, fingers digging in with desperate tenderness while their foreheads almost touched.

Willow smiled into the kiss and whispered against her lips, "I love you so much, baby…"

They stayed locked like that, breathing each other in, the sheets shifting gently around them while the morning held still for once.

CHAPTER 2 Garage Respect

Buffy threw a jab-cross across the mat and Faith forearm-parried it, and Buffy stepped hard onto Faith's right foot and drove a shoulder into her chest, so that Faith's legs flew up and she slammed down flat on her ass on the mat hard enough to rattle the tools on the workbench. The single hanging work light buzzed overhead, and the mid-morning sun leaked in thin bright blades around the edges of the closed garage door and across the mat, where both of them already had a sheen of sweat.

Buffy stood squared off in a black sports bra and grey workout shorts, bare feet planted, her blonde hair yanked into a tight ponytail, a faint bruise still sitting above one eye. Faith lay facing her in a brand-new charcoal grey racerback tank fresh out of Tara's laundry, black compression shorts, bare feet, and a messy high ponytail, the fresh scabs on her knuckles from the construction site catching the light.

Faith grinned wide, wiped sweat away with the back of her hand, and pushed up on her elbows. "Damn, B… you really do still fight dirty after all these years. Guess some things never change — you just got better at pinning me down."

Faith was still grinning on her back for half a beat when her eyes glazed and the memory hit hard and ugly: the rooftop outside her old apartment the night before graduation, her own knife, the one the Mayor had given her, in Buffy's hand and then buried in her stomach, and herself stepping back off the ledge onto the bed of a passing truck while Buffy watched, the whole thing gone grey and nightmare-edged in her head, and then half a heartbeat later it was gone.

Faith blinked hard and shook her head until her ponytail whipped, and a small flash of old pain crossed her face and left. Buffy saw it, and her guard slipped, her fists lowering like she had forgotten how to hold them for a second.

Faith exploded upward off the mat and onto her feet. She threw a double right jab, and Buffy slipped the first and ate the second on the cheek; Faith's front snap kick landed meaty in Buffy's midsection and drove her back a step. Faith went for a low sweep and Buffy hopped clean over it, then stepped inside so fast that her right fist stopped one inch from Faith's nose and held there, vibrating in the still air.

Buffy held the fist there, a small proud smirk tipping one corner of her mouth. "I have learned a lot since school."

Faith froze with Buffy's fist still hanging an inch from her nose, her eyes dropping to the knuckles and then lifting slowly back to Buffy's face, and then a slow grin climbed her mouth and she let out a breath through her nose, respect warming the cocky line of her jaw. "I noticed, B…"

Faith straightened out of her stance, her breathing falling into the same count as Buffy's, and glanced toward the corner. Angel was there in the shadow well clear of the sun, his heavy dark wool overcoat draped over him, boots off, arms crossed, and he gave them a silent nod with a faint half-smile, awake and watching like a man who had seen too many versions of this and still found this one worth staying up for. Faith turned back to Buffy across the sweat-damp mat. She dropped her hands to her sides, brought her heels together, and gave a short, deliberate bow from the waist, the way students bow to each other in a karate dojo.

Buffy returned it at the same clean angle, and they held the bow for two full beats while the garage went still around them, sweat shining, their breathing synced, and Angel watched from the shadows without interrupting.

When the bow broke, Angel settled deeper into the workbench chair, crossing his arms again, his eyelids already heavy. The spar was done, and he let the quiet take him toward sleep the way a soldier does when the watch finally ends.

CHAPTER 3 Grocery Run

The white Toyota Highlander, a rental Willow had picked up while Joyce's sedan sat in the driveway waiting on a new driver's door, crunched into a front-row spot between a red minivan and a silver sedan, and Willow killed the ignition and sat with both hands still on the wheel for half a second the way she always did when parking was finished and the real errand was about to start, red hair in a loose low ponytail, oversized soft lavender peasant blouse and dark jeans, her silver bracelet catching the light when she pulled the key. Tara rode shotgun with a handwritten list already folded once against her palm, a soft cream knit cardigan open over a sage-green blouse, light-wash jeans and ankle boots, her golden dirty-blonde hair loose and wind-tousled and a silver crescent necklace resting at her throat. Dawn bounced in the middle of the back seat in a bright pink hoodie over a tank, denim shorts and white sneakers, her hair in a messy ponytail and a crossbody bag at her hip, already halfway into inventing a story about nothing.

Tara stepped out, and her hand brushed her bandaged side once out of habit. She smiled at the store the way a woman smiles at a job she already knows how to finish.

She looked at the girls over the roof of the Highlander. "Okay, girls… we really do need quite a bit. Let's try to be quick but thorough."

Willow thumbed the key fob until the lock chirped, then slipped her hand into Tara's for a reassuring squeeze, while Dawn bounced on ahead toward the automatic doors, and the three of them walked in together with Tara in the center, Dawn on her right and Willow on her left.

The automatic doors whooshed open onto squeaking carts, a scanner beeping somewhere and the PA crackling about a cleanup on aisle seven, and the smell of cold cuts and fresh bread pulled them straight back toward the deli in the far corner.

Behind the counter Clem worked like he belonged there, a loose-skinned demon in a red SunnyMart apron over a faded Hawaiian shirt, his droopy neck folds and arm folds moving whenever he did, and the second he saw them his whole face lit up like the deli had been waiting for this exact order.

Clem flapped both loose-skinned arms in a big happy hello that made the apron strings bounce, smiling so wide the neck folds rearranged themselves into something almost like dimples. His voice came bright and friendly and a little high. "Oh hi guys!… I got a new cut of roast beef today. Real good. You should try it."

Dawn smiled and tipped her chin up at him. "Hello, Clem…"

Clem bustled down the length of the counter to meet them, wiping his hands on the apron as he came and spreading both arms wide over the glass like he was presenting the whole deli case to them personally, his shoulders bouncing with each step.

Clem reached over the glass and gently squeezed Dawn's cheek with affectionate uncle energy and nothing scary about it, and while he did it he played with the soft end of one of his own ears with his other hand, twisting it thoughtfully like a human fidgeting with a cuff. Dawn leaned into the cheek squeeze with her eyes half-closing, and then giggled when his ear flopped back into place.

Clem plopped both elbows down on top of the glass case and leaned way over it on his forearms, his chin propped in one loose-skinned palm and his whole body tipped toward Tara like a man settling in to hear the best news of his day, his eyebrows climbing high to say go ahead, I'm all ears.

Tara smiled at the counter, her fingers resting lightly on the edge of the glass. "We'll take the new roast beef, please. And… a huge block of cheddar cheese — the biggest one you've got. With thirty-two slices of black forest ham, sixteen slices of low fat smoked turkey, and three packages of smoked breakfast sausages."

Clem heaved the biggest block of cheddar in the case up onto the cutting board with both arms, his loose skin flapping once with the effort, then set the slicer humming, and every time Willow or Dawn glanced his way he lifted the hand he was feeding the roast beef through with and waggled it at them in a cheery little wave, his whole torso bobbing along to the rhythm of the blade, before he went right back to the meat. He sliced everything with the focused care of a man who had found the one job that fit his hands, bagged each cut with practiced perfection, and stickered them one by one while the prices climbed. Willow winced as the numbers started adding up, her shoulders pulling in, and Tara sighed gently beside her.

Tara tipped her head toward Willow and touched her wrist once. "We have no choice, sweetie…"

Willow nodded once and took two of the bags when Clem slid the order across. Clem came up off the case onto his toes and swung one long loose arm overhead in a broad, slow goodbye wave that rocked his whole body side to side like a man flagging down a ship, the folds along his arm swaying with every pass, while with his other hand he pinched the tip of one ear briefly between two fingers like a goodbye fidget, still smiling wide.

Clem said, "You three take care, okay? Tell the Slayer I said hello. Haven't seen Spike around in a while, either… if he turns up, tell him old Clem's still saving him a seat at kitten poker."

Tara tucked the stickered packages into the cart, and they walked out into the bright aisle with their backs to the deli and Clem still waving them off over the top of the glass.

Dawn spotted a PS2 display and bounced, grabbed a Final Fantasy X case with a bright yellow ON SALE thirty-nine ninety-nine sticker on it, and spun toward Tara, hopeful and sheepish, shifting her weight from foot to foot.

She held the case up in both hands, her eyes big and her voice careful around the hope. "Tara… look! Final Fantasy X is on sale for just thirty-nine ninety-nine… is it okay? I've got ten fifty-seven right now I can put in…"

Tara stopped the cart with her hip, a warm caretaker smile rising, and nodded.

Tara said, "Toss it into the cart, sweetie."

Dawn's face lit up with a big grin and bouncing shoulders, and the case clacked carefully into the cart while Willow rubbed Tara's back once with a tiny relieved exhale.

They moved through produce the way a family that intends to keep eating moves through produce — broccoli and baby spinach into the cart, a clutch of bananas, six red apples with one already in Dawn's hand, juice and a few sodas riding the bottom rack, more orange Fanta and ground coffee. Dawn sneaked an extra apple and Tara chuckled, and they pushed toward checkout with Tara centered and content between them, and at the register Tara paid in cash, counting the bills out carefully while the scanner beeped through everything it would take to keep a full house fed.

CHAPTER 4 Fred's Call

Back at the house, with the morning still young and the whole long day still stretched out ahead of sundown, Buffy sat on the edge of the couch folding a throw blanket, still in the sports bra and workout shorts from the spar, her ponytail swinging while she cooled down, and the cordless phone on the side table rang sharp and insistent before she had finished the fold.

She set the half-folded blanket down, crossed the rug in three purposeful barefoot steps, and picked up the cordless phone.

She settled into the familiar Slayer answer without thinking about it, her chin tipped toward the mouthpiece. "Summers residence, Buffy."

She listened with the receiver pressed hard to her ear, her brows knitting and her eyes widening, while Fred's voice came urgent and tinny through the line: a massive demonic attack in LA, Angel and Faith needed sooner, send Angel at sundown, and do not wait for anything softer than that.

Buffy's knuckles went pale on the phone, and her voice trembled even while it stayed resolute.

Buffy said, "Yeah… yeah, okay… I will do that right now. He's in the garage sleeping."

She thumbed the phone off with a soft click and set it back in its cradle, then stood motionless for one long beat with her eyes distant before she let out a sharp breath and strode through the kitchen toward the garage door at the top of the steps.

CHAPTER 5 Angel's Blessing

Angel sat upright in the workbench chair in the garage, his dark coat still draped over him and his boots off, arms crossed, in the light sleep that had come after the spar, the years sitting on him heavier than any fresh wound, and the door from the house creaked before Buffy's soft footfalls reached him. Buffy lowered herself into a folding chair beside him, knees angled and hands in her lap, her shoulders catching the thin blades of morning sun that slipped in under and around the big door.

Buffy leaned in a little, her voice dropping for him alone. "Angel… I need to talk to you. It was Fred."

Angel turned slowly as his eyes opened, and his gaze softened. He sat up and breathed in Buffy's familiar scent, and for a split second his face contorted, a pinch of the brow and a flicker in the jaw, before he checked his wristwatch like the numbers were the only honest thing left.

He kept his eyes on the watch face a moment longer, his voice gravel from sleep. "I have nine hours and eighteen minutes until I can leave… til sundown."

Buffy put her hands together and leaned forward, open vulnerability sitting plain on her.

She searched his face, soft and careful with the question. "Angel… now that we are finally all alone… I have to know what you think about me marrying Lucian."

The door from the house creaked open behind her, and Faith's silhouette filled the doorway. Angel's eyes flicked past Buffy, and his shoulders squared.

The protectiveness came out flat and final. "Not now, Faith!"

Faith paused half a beat at the top of the steps with one hand still on the knob, read the look on Angel's face, nodded once, and drew the door softly shut again, her bare feet padding away across the kitchen floor.

Angel sighed deeply, an old habit of breath for a man who didn't need it, stretched one leg out long across the cold concrete, and met Buffy's gaze with a hard-earned peace.

He held her eyes the way only years could teach a man to hold them. "Buffy. I left you back at graduation so you could have a life like this… with a person like that. You know that. This is what I hoped you would find."

Buffy gave a small nod, her eyes dropping to her hands in her lap and then coming back up to his, taking in every word of it.

Buffy said, "Yeah… I know…"

Angel prompted gently, "But?"

Buffy's voice caught, honest and raw. "But nothing. I love him, Angel… it's just that even with me training him, and him lifting weights…"

Angel interrupted, smooth, calm and certain. "It will barely be enough… I know…"

He tipped his chin toward the house beyond the garage walls, kind without flattening anybody who lived there. "He has to learn to hold the house the way Tara does then… steady on the home ground while you go out there. That strength is not waiting small. It is how she keeps everyone alive."

Buffy's voice trembled, and her hands shook on her knees. "I told him, Angel… that if I die… to pretend everything was just a dream and to move far away from here… from this town… and to just let go… and move on…"

Her hands and arms visibly shook, and Angel leaned forward, arms opening, and pulled her into a careful embrace. Buffy set her chin on his right shoulder and cried silently while Angel stroked the back of her hair, slow and soothing, old love transformed into a quiet blessing, and held her there in the deep shadow at the back of the garage, well away from the thin bright blades of sun around the edges of the big door.

CHAPTER 6 Motion in the Ocean

Faith stood outside the closed door to the garage at the top of the narrow wooden steps off the kitchen, her arms loosely crossed in a sentinel's posture and her ears tuned to what she was guarding, the kitchen warm around her and the garage cool and muffled behind the old wood. Lucian walked up from the front of the house in a black embroidered shirt with the sleeves pushed up, dark jeans, boots and a simple silver chain, his long jet-black hair past his shoulders and his aqua-blue eyes catching the kitchen light, the raven tattoo hidden under the shirt on his left shoulder and pec. He reached for the handle of the garage door.

Faith's forearm snapped up, and her palm landed flat against the wood.

She smirked with one eyebrow up, her voice casual and already in charge. "Boss needs a minute with your girl…"

She turned fully toward him, and her eyes scanned him, his jaw, his breathing, the set of his shoulders, with a testing half-smile and her eyebrow still arched.

She tipped her chin at him, probing without raising her voice. "You trust her, don't you?"

Lucian answered steady and calm, with no hesitation. "Yes, I do."

Faith gave a quick approving nod, her smirk softening.

Faith said, "Good… then it's the couch for us, buddy, til they're done in there."

She gave him three firm brotherly pats on the shoulder and a light guiding nudge on his upper back, and they walked to the living room couch, Faith sprawling with one arm along the back and Lucian sitting forward slightly, a respectful hush between them.

The antique clock in the living room ticked loud — tick… tick… tick… — and for a long stretch there was nothing else, Faith looking at Lucian with only the clock between them.

Then she sighed, stretched her arms overhead with a slight arch of her back that flexed the muscles under her tank, and dropped the posture.

She tipped a teasing smirk at him sideways. "So, Lucian… you can keep up with one of us, huh?"

Then she threw her palms out in a defensive whoa and shook her head, waving both hands once with a quick disclaimer already forming. "Hey, I am not offering or anything. Not trying to hurt B. I am just… I don't know… you're just a normal human, no offense…"

Lucian held calm eye contact with Faith. He pursed his lips once, wetting them with his tongue before he spoke, and his right hand came up palm-upward with the fingers out casually while his left stayed easy on his knee, a calm, smooth, knowing half-smile on his face, with no rush and no apology for being human in a room full of Slayers.

Lucian said, "It's not about the pound, or the grind, Faith… it's about the motion in the ocean… how the water loves the rocks."

Faith's eyes widened and her eyebrows shot up and her mouth fell open, and then a sharp, genuine bark of laughter broke out of her, shocked and delighted, and she slapped his thigh with a loud, meaty smack on the denim, harder than she meant to, the way a Slayer does when she forgets she is sitting next to a normal man.

Still chuckling, she pointed at him with one finger and shook her head. "Okay, Lucian… you can have that one. Just one. Don't push it."

She leaned back grinning and shaking her head while Lucian chuckled softly, and an unexpected common ground sat between them under the warm light while the clock kept its quiet count.

CHAPTER 7 Jerry Springer Chaos

Somewhere in that quiet Faith had reached for the remote, and by the time Buffy came out of the garage the TV was blaring "JERRY SPRINGER TIME! JERRY! JERRY! JERRY!" across the front living room, and Buffy, raw and emotional, walked into the kitchen sightline, saw Faith and Lucian on the couch, and approached with her whole body already braced.

Her voice came tight, her arms already folding across her chest. "I thought I told you to stay away from him while you are here… He is mine…"

Lucian started, "Buffy, noth…"

Buffy raised a finger and cut him off, her arms crossed tight again as she turned back to Faith.

Buffy's jaw locked, and Riley sat behind her eyes when she looked at Faith. Her voice went flat and specific. "He is not a toy, Faith… He is not a meat stick. He is my soon to be husband… I know that does not compute to you…"

Faith stood quickly, palms out, her eyes wide with genuine surprise.

She rocked back a half-step, palms still up, protective loyalty cutting through the defense. "Whoa, B! I was literally just playing bodyguard so your man didn't walk in on your cry sesh with Angel! We were talking how much he trusts you — chill! I didn't do any…"

Buffy yelled, furious and shaking, her finger stabbing down toward the floor in a full-arm motion that started at her chin, and the words came out hurt and specific, the sound of a woman who felt threatened in her own home and had been cracked wide open by the morning.

Buffy said, "You slept with Riley, in my body! You think, I would forget???"

For four long seconds nobody moved, and the tension in the living room was thick enough to taste while Buffy's lower lip quivered and her face went taut with anger and something close to rage.

Faith's face looked hurt and disarmed, her palms still half-raised between them.

Buffy said, "Go to the garage now…"

Faith's jaw set, and she planted her feet. "No…"

Buffy narrowed her eyes slowly, her left foot shifting slightly behind her body while her front right foot started to point at Faith.

Buffy said, "What did you say…"

Faith saw it, and her fists rose slowly, her arms trembling.

Faith said, "Don't… Don't do it, B…"

Buffy's eyes dropped to Faith's trembling fists and locked there, while the TV kept roaring "JERRY! JERRY!" and Lucian stood frozen between them like a man who had walked into the wrong weather.

CHAPTER 8 Tara Intervention

Grocery bags rustled and midday sunlight flooded into the front entry as Willow shouldered the plywood panel aside and dragged it wide, and Tara came in charged from the long store run, holding two large paper grocery bags against her upper body, looked left, saw the standoff — Buffy braced, Faith with her fists up, Lucian frozen, Jerry Springer still chanting — and walked into the front living room fast without setting anything down, the soft steel already rising in her before she had to decide what to say.

Tara's chin lifted, and love sat under the steel in her voice without needing to announce itself. She pointed straight at Faith. "I will not have that in this house! You!"

Faith visibly flinched at the sound of Tara's voice; it cut right through her.

Tara kept the point on Faith and tipped her head toward the bright gap where the plywood stood dragged aside. "Go help unload the car, NOW."

Then she turned a slow look on Buffy and held her eyes, soft and firm with no room to argue, and waited one beat before she spoke.

"Go upstairs to your room. Now…"

Buffy lowered her hands slowly, and great sadness filled her eyes. The room around her went huge and bright and judging — Willow in the entry with groceries still in her arms, Dawn behind her staring, Lucian half-risen and frozen, Faith still standing with her fists forgotten at her sides, Jerry Springer still chanting like a cruel joke — and Buffy felt herself shrink inside her own skin, small the way a little kid feels when every adult face in the room has decided she did something bad and wrong and there is no speech left that can fix it. A burst of air punched out of her mouth in a soft cry, her waist bent inward, and the tears broke. She saw Dawn's eyes on her — Dawn, who had just watched her sister shrink and break in front of the whole living room — and that was the last of it. Buffy ran upstairs like a child sent to her room, and the door slammed shut behind her.

Dawn stood still in the entry with the grocery bags still in her arms, her mouth open a fraction, watching the empty stairs like she had just seen something she wasn't supposed to see, and the power of Tara's soft steel sat in the quiet after the slam.

Lucian tried to rise, and Willow set her grocery bags down on the bottom stair, crossed the room to him, and shook her head gently as she laid a hand on his shoulder.

Willow squeezed once and kept her voice gentle at his ear. "Not now, Lucian… She needs to breathe first… trust me…"

Tara shifted the two bags higher against her chest and looked up toward Buffy's room through the ceiling, then looked back at Lucian and said calmly, "I don't want you thrown out the second story window. Better not do that."

She sighed, lowered her arms a little to settle the two paper bags against her body, and nodded toward the plywood entry and the driveway beyond it. "Why don't you help us unload the car and then help me wash veggies for dinner?"

Lucian got up and followed Faith out past the plywood and down the steps to the Highlander in the driveway to unload, and the moment the two of them were out of Tara's sight Faith started grumbling, hauling the first heavy bags out of the back.

Her voice floated in from the driveway, exasperated and half under her breath. "I didn't do anything this time… I didn't do anything…"

Tara, Willow, and Dawn went into the kitchen and unloaded the groceries onto the kitchen island, taking items out of the bags and putting them away in the fridge as a team, and for several minutes nobody said much while the bags emptied, Tara's calm still sitting on the room like a hand that had already settled the fight.

CHAPTER 9 Predator Talk

Tara breathed out deep and slow and set the final bag of vegetables on the counter, the produce and deli nearly put away, and Faith stepped closer from the direction of the living room with one hand cupped open at her side, sincerity sitting raw on her face.

She stopped a step short of Tara and worked the words out carefully. "Tara, I didn't do anything this time. I swear… I respect your home and your family… B's just… she's having…"

Tara raised a hand and placed it on Faith's shoulder, warm and steady, with quiet forgiveness in it.

A soft smile found Tara's eyes, not pity, just knowing. "I know, sweetie… It's okay."

Faith nodded once, relief and shame mixed in her face, and spotted the thick roast beef sub Tara had built for her out of Clem's fresh-sliced deli meat while the bags were being emptied, sitting on a paper towel on the counter.

She tipped her chin at the sub, quietly asking. "Mind if I have this?"

Tara laughed softly, reached into the fridge, and handed her a Coke.

Tara pressed the cold can into Faith's hand and nodded at the sandwich. "Of course, sweetie. You earned it."

Faith took both and leaned back against the kitchen island to take the first bite, and Tara turned to the last of the fridge items.

Lucian bent at the open refrigerator, set the last bottle on the shelf, and shut the door with his right palm, then turned and gave Tara a small tired nod.

Tara's shoulders eased, warm gratitude in the look she gave him. "Thank you, sweetie."

Lucian offered a brief smile and did not linger, walking through the doorway toward the couch in the front living room.

Willow and Dawn had homework spread open across the coffee table, a scuffed old pine one Willow and Tara had carried in from the garage to stand in for the one Faith went through, Willow sitting on the floor beside Dawn at eye level with a pencil tapping her lip, and Dawn focused and mature about it, with no pouting.

Willow tapped the pencil once against the edge of the notebook, matter-of-fact. "You will be going back to school tomorrow since we dealt with the preacher."

Dawn kept her eyes on the page a moment, then glanced up directly. "Can you help with it at least?"

Willow's mouth tipped, patient, with a clear boundary under the warmth. "Of course… But I won't do it for you. There's a difference."

Dawn nodded once, simple and accepting. "Deal."

Lucian reached the couch and sat, and his eyes lifted to the ceiling at the muffled sniffle coming from upstairs, where the crying after the fight was still going. He knew it, and he crossed his arms and let a frustrated breath out through his nose, his brow pinched, wanting to go to her and not letting himself move yet.

Faith came in from the kitchen with the roast beef sub in one hand and the Coke in the other and dropped onto the couch beside him with zero ceremony and a heavy bounce of the cushion. She glanced at Willow and Dawn at the coffee table, absorbed in their books and deliberately not looking, then took a big bite of the sub, chewed, took a long sip of cola, let out a small honest burp, and side-glanced at Lucian.

She talked around the bite, casual as anything. "You know Buffy ain't the same as me. But she's still a Slayer, right?"

Lucian turned toward her on the couch, and Willow's pencil paused, listening without her looking up.

Lucian's voice came quiet and careful. "…Yeah."

Faith swallowed and turned to face him fully, with no teasing smirk now, flat and serious.

She locked eyes with him, no soft landing. "I bet you've been a solid rock. Never saying what you want. Never putting your foot down in this relationship. Just letting her walk all over you with her wants."

She stared at him and waited, the sub forgotten in her hand.

Lucian held her gaze for several seconds and did not flinch.

He answered low and honest. "I was doing that to create less friction."

Faith rolled her eyes hard with a full, exasperated turn of her head, and leaned in sharp.

Faith said, "Lucian — you can't be that way with one of us. We're girls, sure. But we're Slayers. We're predators."

She pointed the sub up at the ceiling toward Buffy's room like a gavel, commanding without raising her voice. "You need to go up there right now and let her know that what you want matters to you."

For a moment the living room was completely still.

Willow did not look up from Dawn's notebook, and her pencil stayed still while she spoke barely above a whisper, quiet certainty in her throat.

Willow said, "I agree."

Dawn glanced back over her shoulder with a quick curious look at Lucian, then looked down at her papers again, and her pencil started moving.

Lucian looked at Faith for a long moment, and his shoulders squared under the black shirt, the look of a man who had finally stopped arguing with himself.

He stood, crossed the living room into the front entry where the staircase rose beside the boarded-up door, and looked up the steps.

He started up the stairs, deliberate and unhurried, not because Faith had pushed him or because anybody else had voted him upstairs, but because she had only said out loud what he already knew, and the choice was his.

Faith took another satisfied bite of the sub, and Willow almost smiled over the homework at the coffee table, and the muffled crying upstairs finally went quiet as his footsteps approached.

CHAPTER 10 What I Want

Buffy lay on her side in her bedroom with her knees pulled to her chest and Mr. Gordo, her old pink stuffed pig, tucked under her arm as she faced the door, her face puffy and red and sniffling, still in her sports bra and workout shorts from the fight, quiet tears still tracking down while the half-drawn curtains let a soft midday spill lay warm shadows across her tear-streaked skin.

There was a soft knock on the door, and Buffy did not move except to shut her eyes tighter.

A second knock came, harder and more insistent.

Buffy lifted her head with a wet, annoyed sigh, wiped her nose on the back of her hand, rolled off the bed, crossed to the door, and yanked it open.

Lucian stepped inside without waiting for an invitation and closed the door behind him with a firm click.

Buffy crossed her arms over her chest, her hair messy, her eyes swollen, and her chin lifted defensively.

Her voice came raw, her arms still a shield. "I need to be alone right now, Lucian—"

Lucian cut her off, quiet, with no apology and his eyes steady on hers. "I didn't come up here because anybody told me to wait, or told me to go. I came because Faith told me the truth and because I could hear you crying through the floor and I couldn't sit on that couch another second pretending that was fine."

Buffy opened her mouth to argue, her arms still locked across her chest, and nothing came out.

He stepped a half-pace closer, steady. "You ran upstairs. You slammed the door. You shut me out. And I let you — because I thought that was what you needed. Less friction. A rock that doesn't ask for anything."

He let it sit a moment, his eyes never leaving hers.

He kept it simple, firm without raising his volume. "What I want matters to me. And what I want right now is to be in this room with you — not downstairs being good and patient while you fall apart alone."

Buffy tried to speak, her chin trembling while an apology formed on her lips.

Her voice broke softly in the middle. "Lucian, I'm sorry, I just — I wasn't trying to—"

Lucian shook his head once, gentle and immovable. "I'm not asking you to explain Faith. Or Angel. Or Riley. Or any of it."

He stepped closer, his left hand rising but not touching her yet, while his right stayed lower at his side.

His voice dropped, certain. "I'm asking you to stop treating me like I'll leave if you have a bad day. I chose you. I'm still choosing you. But I am not going to disappear into what you need and call that love."

For a long moment neither of them moved, and then Buffy's arms loosened and her chin trembled.

She tilted her head, a slow smile rising through the wreckage, her eyes wet, and something in her shoulders finally dropped.

She almost laughed through the tears. "You know what, Lucian? All the showing is great, really, but sometimes a girl needs to actually hear it. Points for the hearing part."

Lucian said nothing back, and he did not have to, because Buffy was already moving. She had wanted him for more than two days, the whole time Caleb had held the town in his fist and she had pulled on commander mode like armor and kept Lucian at arm's length behind it, all orders and patrol plans and careful distance while the want sat banked and patient underneath, and now, with his words still standing in the room between them, that whole wall came down at once. She uncrossed her arms, closed the last of the space between them in two quick barefoot steps, slid her right hand flat up his chest and curled her left around the back of his neck, and rose onto her toes and pulled his mouth down to hers, and the kiss was deep and hungry and almost laughing at the edges, pure joy breaking through the last of her tears. Without letting go of him she hooked her right heel behind his ankle, leaned her weight forward into him, and took them both down to the carpet, Slayer-quick and careful all at once, one forearm sliding under his back to break the fall, so that he landed on his back with only a soft thud, and she came down straddling his chest with her weight kept high and well clear of the healing knife wound low on his left side, both hands framing his face, never once breaking the kiss.

Her hair fell around his face as her head moved, her fingers stroking his cheek and his jaw, and his hands found her waist, careful and then sure, while they shared breath and the ugly cry became something else entirely, something that belonged only to the two of them on the carpet with the door closed.

For a long while there were only faces and shoulders and hands, her sports bra and his embroidered shirt, a passion that had no audience and wanted none, and the bedroom kept what belonged to the two of them private behind its closed door, sealed off from every other room in the house.

Later Faith sat on the couch downstairs with the sub half-eaten and the Coke on the coffee table, examining her index fingernail, casual and unbothered. Upstairs had gone quiet again, and the whole house had the good manners of a home that knew what privacy looked like.

Faith did not look up as she buffed the edge of the nail with her thumb, landing a deadpan joke after the fact.

She tipped the nail toward the ceiling, dry and satisfied. "There it is. One foot down — whole house shook. You're welcome."

Willow pressed her lips together to suppress a smile, her eyes on Dawn's homework, and Dawn's eyebrows shot up before she went back to her pencil without a word.

Faith took another bite of the sub, chewed, and sipped her cola while the homework went on at the coffee table and the afternoon settled into the couch cushions, and the whole house seemed to breathe a little warmer than it had that morning.

CHAPTER 11 Laundry Closet

Tara had forgotten a load of wet laundry in the washer, and Faith helped her move it, the two of them shoulder to shoulder in the small upstairs laundry closet to the far left of Buffy's room, with the humid scent of detergent, a tight overhead bulb, and the machines side by side.

Faith looked down at her hands, then back up at Tara.

She swallowed once, vulnerability sitting plain on her face. "Angel said we got to go as soon as the sun goes down… Soon…"

Her voice went softer, and she kept her eyes on Tara's. "I don't know what it is you made me feel while I was here but… I think it's what everyone was trying to get me to feel, when I was hurting everyone and myself…"

Emotion climbed her throat, and she pressed a palm briefly to her own sternum. "It's like this warm glow in my chest. That started in the backyard when you just held me… it was so quiet... I am scared of it now that I feel it more…"

Faith pushed slightly closer until their faces were barely touching, and Tara froze, her cheeks rising red and a flush spreading across her chest around her collarbones as she breathed in with a shirt still in her hands.

Faith barely brushed her lips against Tara's in a very slow kiss that stroked across, and Tara very slowly and gently pulled back just a little.

Tara held Faith's eyes, steady and clear, the shirt still crumpled in her fingers. "Faith… I could never hurt Willow. She is a part of me… a part of my soul and my life…"

She tipped her chin a fraction, gentle and firm together. "That is the thing I am trying to show you… not the quick and easy thrill… but the lasting love that holds you inside…"

Faith slowly drew back, giving Tara the little bit of space she had made.

Her voice went almost childlike. "But if you weren't with Red?"

Tara's face softened with understanding as she looked at her.

She opened her arms a little, offering instead of answering the if. "Hug instead?"

Faith nodded slowly and put her arms around Tara's shoulders, and she started to cry — not the sharp kind she used in fights, but the quiet kind that snuck up on her when somebody offered her something that wasn't a thrill. Tara sank to her knees with her and held her, stroking her hair gently, and Faith broke completely against that hold the way armor breaks when it finally trusts the hands taking it off, letting it all go into the smell of detergent and the warm glow she had named and been scared of.

Just out of sight around the side wall, Willow stood listening to the entire exchange with her eyes closed.

Willow's mouth curved, soft and full of love, barely a breath. "That's my baby…"

The dryer ticked softly as it cooled beside them, and Tara stayed down on the floor of the laundry closet with Faith gathered in her arms, rocking her a little and smoothing her hair, while just around the corner, unseen, Willow stood with her back against the wall, her eyes closed and one hand pressed flat over her heart.

CHAPTER 12 Dinner Glow

Willow, Dawn, Tara, and Faith sat at the dining-room table under the rich golden sunset coming through the windows, warm oranges and yellows lying across the plates, soft chatter and taco crunch and the clink of silverware filling the room, while out past the glass the sun sank low and red behind the rooftops across the street.

Willow called, "BUFFY, LUCIAN, DINNER!"

Faith built a taco — cooked beef, a spoon of refried beans, lettuce, shredded cheddar — took a huge crunchy bite, went wide-eyed at Tara, and made a loud, satisfied "Mmmmmm."

Tara laughed, nodding, and tipped her fork at Faith. "You're welcome, sweetie…"

Buffy came down the stairs and turned the corner into the dining room with her hair in an elegant updo and a nice shoulder-bare one-piece dress on, a large hickey plainly visible on her left shoulder near her neck, her legs slightly wobbly as she walked to the table though she kept her grace the whole way, and she sat, smiled, and made herself a taco. Lucian sat down beside her in the same black embroidered shirt and jeans he had worn all day, proud and slightly embarrassed.

Everyone around the table stopped chewing and just looked at them, Willow's taco hovering halfway to her mouth, Dawn's eyebrows climbing toward her hairline, and Tara's fork paused over her plate.

Faith smirked wide, still chewing, and leaned back with cocky Slayer energy.

She grinned around the bite, blunt approval flashing. "Damn B... you came down here glowing like you just got properly slayed and that hickey is screaming louder than the fight earlier. Lucian finally grew a pair and handled you right, huh? Respect."

Buffy smirked right back at her across the table, settled deeper into her chair and lifted her chin, not bothering to cover the hickey.

Buffy said, "You should see what his back looks like… he might need stitches…"

Lucian laughed a little beside her, happy, a faint flush of embarrassment warming his face.

The table held its awkward, knowing, fond stares under the golden sunset, all family love and teasing and obvious evidence of the reunion upstairs, while the last red edge of the sun slipped down behind the rooftops and the garage door at the top of the steps stayed shut on Angel, waiting for it to go.

CHAPTER 13 Sunset Departure

Angel's black 1967 Plymouth GTX sat at the curb out front with its hood still buckled in a deep V, the windshield frame empty where Faith had kicked the shattered glass out, and one headlight dead from the wasp demon, battered but still drivable, its battle scars plain under the streetlights in the purple dusk after the sun had finally slipped below the rooftops. Everyone had finished dinner, and the moment the last sliver of sun was gone Angel came up from the garage with Fred's urgency already sitting in his shoulders — LA under attack, the clock run out, and no soft version of leaving left.

Angel looked at Faith, quiet and direct. "It's time to go."

Everyone pushed back from the table and went out past the plywood together, down the porch steps and across the cooling lawn to where the GTX waited at the curb.

Dawn hugged Angel and looked up at him, hope sitting honest in her face. "I hope we see you again soon, Angel…"

She looked at Faith, waved goodbye, and went back inside.

Willow stepped close to Angel first, polite and clear.

Willow said, "Goodbye, Angel. Travel safe."

Then she turned to Faith, soft and real. "Goodbye, Faith. Come back when you can."

Tara stepped forward and hugged Faith, not too long, warm and steady, and kissed her cheek once.

Tara held Faith's eyes a moment after the kiss, welcoming without pressure. "You are welcome in our home, Faith… anytime…"

Faith held her emotions down hard, her jaw tight and her eyes bright, her hands jammed deep in her jacket pockets so nobody would see them shake.

Faith said, "Thanks…"

Lucian shook Angel's hand, and Angel clapped him lightly on the right shoulder, careful to keep clear of the healing Bringer wound low on his left side, and Lucian did not flinch about it.

Angel's grip lingered, meaningful, on Lucian's hand. "Take care of her for us both…"

Lucian met his eyes, steady. "I will..."

Everyone else went back inside, leaving Faith, Angel, and Buffy alone out front.

Buffy looked at Faith in the dusk, the one working headlight of the GTX throwing a long pale stripe across the grass between them.

Buffy said, "Come back sometime, Faith…"

Faith nodded once, her eyes holding Buffy's.

Faith said, "You got it, B."

Buffy stepped toward Angel and looked like she would speak, but instead she hugged him and kissed his cheek. Then she turned and walked back to the house without looking back, slipped inside past the plywood, and dragged the panel back across the entry behind her.

Angel and Faith stood together by the damaged car under the streetlights in the deepening dusk, the sun gone at last, with the long night drive to LA waiting hard in front of them.
