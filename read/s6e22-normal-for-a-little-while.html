Normal for a Little While

Buffy the Vampire Slayer. Season six, episode 22. Normal for a Little While.

CHAPTER 1 Early Afternoon

In Sunnydale Main Park, south of UC Sunnydale, warm early-afternoon sunlight filtered through the trees in soft dappled patches across the grass and the cement walking path. The air carried the faint scent of cut grass and distant barbecue smoke. Kids played nearby, a couple of people sat against tree trunks reading paperbacks, a woman in her early twenties jogged steadily along the path with white wired headphones on, and two dog walkers ambled farther down. The park breathed ordinary life around the picnic table where the group was gathered.

Willow stood at a small portable charcoal grill on the grass near the table, the sleeves of her light green button-up rolled to her elbows. She wore brown slides, and a small white towel was tucked into the back pocket of her jeans. A tiny private smile touched her mouth as she worked.

At the wooden picnic table a few feet away sat Buffy, Lucian, Tara and Dawn. Buffy, in a simple white tank top and light-wash jeans with a small rip at one knee, was half in Lucian’s lap, turned sideways on the bench with one leg tucked under her, her hair loose and wind-tousled. Lucian wore a black shirt with a dark embroidered collar and cuffs, open at the throat, sleeves pushed up, and his right hand rested on Buffy’s lower back. Tara sat across from them in a soft lavender cardigan and a long flowy skirt, her golden dirty-blonde hair down and slightly wavy. Dawn sat at the end of the table, legs swinging, in an oversized faded grey t-shirt and blue jean shorts, her hair up in a messy ponytail. A bright neon yellow Frisbee lay on the grass nearby, damp from an earlier game of toss.

Willow extended her right hand toward the grill, palm facing the coals, eyes closing for just a moment. A very small, almost invisible ripple of heat shimmered in the air as she gave the tiniest wave of her fingers. The coals caught and began to glow bright orange-red within seconds, faster than they should have, but not dramatically so. Just convenient.

She opened her eyes, that tiny private smile still on her mouth, picked up the metal spatula, and carefully placed four hamburger patties onto the grill one at a time, the meat making that first soft sizzle. She closed her eyes again briefly, leaned in just enough to smell the cooking meat, and let out a small content sigh through her nose.

Willow said softly, almost to herself, peaceful and quietly pleased, "Mmm. That first real sizzle... always smells like things are going to be okay for a little while."

She sprinkled Lawry’s Seasoned Salt evenly across the tops of the patties with gentle practiced flicks of her wrist, then set the shaker down and wiped her hands on the towel in her back pocket. A small smudge of ash dotted one cheekbone; she didn’t notice.

At the table, Buffy was tickling Lucian’s ribs lightly with her left hand while laughing under her breath. Lucian’s right hand stroked slowly and smoothly up and down her spine in long grounding passes. When she tickled him again he let out a quiet laugh and caught her wrist gently, his thumb brushing the inside of her wrist, his eyes crinkling at the corners.

He slid his hand up from her back to the side of her face, thumb brushing her cheekbone, and leaned in to kiss her, soft, unhurried, gentle. Buffy’s free hand came up to rest lightly on his chest, fingers curling into the embroidered fabric.

Buffy didn’t stay gentle.

She pushed forward into the kiss, turning it into something deeper, hungrier for a few long seconds. Her hand slid up from his chest to the back of his neck, fingers threading into the long black hair tied at his nape and giving a tiny possessive tug. When she finally pulled back, she did it slowly, catching his bottom lip between hers for a moment before letting it go with a soft audible little snap. She stayed close, looking him straight in the eyes, smiling, small, private, a little bit wicked. She bit her own lower lip once, still smiling, a flush creeping up her neck, then turned her head toward Tara. A few strands of her wind-tousled hair stuck to her damp lower lip; she brushed them away with the back of her hand.

Buffy said, her voice warm and genuine, a little playful, "You’re doing really good with everything, you know. The store, school... all of it. I’m proud of you."

Tara watched the two of them with a gentle, steady smile, her eyes soft, the fine crinkles at the corners showing quiet amusement and affection, a paper napkin twisted lightly between her fingers.

Dawn was absorbed in a chunky black Sega Game Gear, a hand-me-down relic, playing Sonic the Hedgehog, the entire little console tilting and moving with her thumbs and slight wrist motions as she navigated, her ponytail swaying with the small movements. A small furrow appeared between her brows as she concentrated, and she didn’t look up when she spoke.

Dawn said, without looking away from the screen, casual but sincere, "I’m starving. Like... actually dying. Those smell so good, Willow."

Tara opened her mouth to answer Buffy, but before she could speak, the make-out moment between Buffy and Lucian caught her attention again for half a second. A faint pink rose on her cheeks. She cleared her throat politely and glanced at the six-pack of Fanta sitting on the table, just out of her reach.

Tara said, amused but gentle, "Buffy... pass me the Fanta?"

CHAPTER 2 The Mean Girls

The warm early-afternoon light held. A light breeze moved the leaves overhead, casting shifting patterns across the table.

Buffy was still close to Lucian, one hand resting on his chest, her thumb tracing a small absent circle on the embroidered cotton. Tara had the can of Fanta in front of her, fingers wrapped around the cold aluminum. Dawn was back to her Game Gear, though her thumbs had slowed; she glanced up once with a small frown. Willow was at the grill, spatula in her right hand, casually flipping one of the patties.

Three girls walked up the cement path toward the group with that synchronized, practiced mean-girl strut. All three had long, carefully styled blonde hair with perfect highlights, not a strand out of place despite the breeze, and expensive designer handbags hung off their shoulders. They chewed bright pink bubble gum loudly, blowing and popping small bubbles as they walked, and wore low-rise jeans with visible designer waistbands, tight sparkly tops and platform sandals that clicked on the path.

The middle girl, the tallest, with the sharpest features, clearly the leader, spotted Lucian first. She slowed, then stopped completely, the other two flanking her. She tilted her head, eyes narrowing as she looked him up and down, one perfectly plucked eyebrow arching, and her gum popped loudly.

The leader said, loud enough for the whole table to hear, her voice mocking and cruel, "Okay, no — is that her boyfriend or her RA? Because he looks old enough to be grading her midterms. Very late-nineties Hot Topic who never got the memo."

The other two girls giggled and popped their gum in unison, then turned their attention to Dawn. The one on the right smirked, shifting her weight so one hip jutted out.

The girl on the right said, "Dayum. How tall are you? Freshman year and you’re already built like a volleyball scholarship. Death by snu-snu, right?"

The three of them burst into mean high-pitched laughter, leaning into each other, shoulders shaking. One of them flipped her hair over her shoulder with a practiced flick.

Dawn’s face went red, and she lowered the Game Gear slightly but didn’t look up, her shoulders curling inward a fraction. Tara’s posture stiffened, her fingers tightening around the Fanta can until the aluminum dented slightly.

Tara said softly, stammering a little from nerves, "P-please... don’t talk like that."

The middle girl’s head snapped toward Tara. She mimicked the stutter with exaggerated cruelty, leaning forward, eyes glittering.

The leader said, "Wh-wh-wh-wha? Are you retarded or something?"

Her lip curled in a sneer as she straightened up and flicked her hair back over her shoulder.

The leader said, "God, this is why I don’t do the public parks. Come on — we’re going to be late for rush."

They turned to leave, still laughing and popping their gum. The sound of their sandals faded down the path.

Behind the grill, Willow had gone completely still. The spatula in her right hand now hung straight down at her side, tip pointing at the grass, the metal visibly softening and dripping, thick silvery droplets of melted steel falling onto the ground with soft plip sounds. Her hand was shaking, knuckles white around the handle, and her eyes were locked on the backs of the three girls, a muscle ticking once in her cheek, her breathing shallow and fast.

Buffy saw it immediately. Her body went still in Lucian’s lap, the playful energy gone in an instant. Her eyes sharpened, tension pulling at the corners of her mouth.

Buffy said in a low, urgent warning, "Will. No. Don’t."

Tara said softly, worried, reaching toward her, "No, sweetie..."

Dawn said, without looking up from the Game Gear, her voice flat and angry, "Should let her do it."

Everyone at the table turned to look at Dawn. She finally lifted her head, eyes hard, lower lip set in a stubborn line.

Tara reached across the table and placed her hand gently on Dawn’s forearm, her thumb making a small soothing stroke.

Tara said quietly, steady and kind, "It’s not right. They’re just hurting inside. That’s why they hurt other people."

Dawn rolled her eyes hard, scoffed, and yanked her arm away. She stood up abruptly, jammed the Game Gear under her arm with more force than necessary, and walked off toward the public bathrooms about fifteen yards away without another word, her shoulders rigid, ponytail swinging angrily.

Buffy sighed heavily, one hand coming up to rub her forehead, fingers pressing into the skin. Lucian’s hand stayed on her back, steady, his thumb continuing the slow grounding passes.

Willow’s breathing slowly evened out, and the shaking in her hand stopped. The last drop of melted metal fell from the ruined spatula onto the grass with a final plip. She stared at it for a second, then walked over to the nearby trash bin and dropped the destroyed spatula inside with a dull metallic clank.

She returned to the grill, picked up a regular metal fork from the small bag of supplies, and calmly started turning the burgers. She held her bare hand very close to the hot coals while she worked, close enough that it should have burned, but she didn’t flinch or react at all; the heat didn’t seem to touch her. Her expression was carefully blank, but her eyes were still a little too wide.

The park around them continued like nothing had happened. The jogger passed by on the path. A dog barked in the distance. The sunlight kept falling in soft patches across the grass. A blue and yellow butterfly drifted past the table, wings catching the light.

CHAPTER 3 The Little Strawberry

Moments later, the mean girls were gone. The park had returned to its quiet, ordinary rhythm, distant dog barks, the soft sound of the jogger’s footsteps fading down the path. Willow was at the grill, still holding the metal fork, turning the burgers with slow deliberate movements. Her breathing was steady now, but her back was rigid.

Tara stood up from the table without a word and walked the few steps over to Willow, calm and unhurried, the flowy skirt brushing the grass. She gently slid her left arm around the back of Willow’s head, fingers threading softly into her hair at the nape, and pulled her in until Willow’s forehead rested against her chest. Tara leaned down and pressed a slow tender kiss to the top of Willow’s head, lingering there for a second. Her free hand rested lightly on Willow’s upper back, rubbing small circles.

Tara said softly, warm with quiet pride, "Thank you, my little strawberry. I know that was hard. You didn’t do what you wanted to do. You’re getting better at this... at choosing. I’m proud of you."

Willow closed her eyes and let herself be held for a moment. Her free hand came up and rested lightly on Tara’s waist, fingers curling into the cardigan fabric. She didn’t speak; she just nodded once against Tara’s chest, breathing her in, and a small, shaky exhale left her.

Meanwhile, in the park’s public bathroom, fluorescent lights buzzed overhead with a low, flickering hum. The bathroom was clean but dated, with white tiled walls with a few hairline cracks, grey stalls, slightly yellowed sinks with dripping faucets.

Inside one of the stalls, Dawn sat on the toilet, arms wrapped around herself, staring at the stall door with a hard angry expression. Her eyes were glassy, but she refused to let the tears fall; a single tear clung to her lower lashes, and she blinked it away furiously.

The bathroom door opened, and the three blonde mean girls walked in, still chewing gum and laughing, and headed straight for the sinks, hips swaying. One of them checked her lipstick in the mirror.

The leader said, "God, did you see that guy? He looked like he crawled out of Hot Topic in ninety-eight and never got the update. Like, sir, the dorms have an age limit."

The third girl said, "And the soft little speech one? ‘P-p-please don’t t-t-talk like that.’ Jesus. How does someone like that even get into UC Sunnydale with half a brain?"

They laughed. One of them glanced down and noticed Dawn’s sneakers visible under the stall door. Her eyes widened, then narrowed with mean delight.

The leader said, "Oh my god. The tall one is in here."

The girl on the left turned on the sink, cupped her hands under the running water, and quickly threw it up and over the top of the stall. Cold water splashed down onto Dawn’s head. Dawn flinched hard, gasping, eyes squeezing shut as water ran down her face and soaked into her t-shirt. Her ponytail drooped, strands plastered to her forehead and cheeks. She wiped at her eyes with the back of one hand.

Dawn said sharply, freaked out, "Hey—!"

Before she could say anything else, Buffy was there.

She was leaning against the tiled wall inside the bathroom, arms crossed over her chest, watching them with a cold sideways glance. None of the mean girls saw her come in. She pushed off the wall and walked straight toward the middle girl, steps silent and deliberate.

In one fast motion Buffy grabbed the front of the girl’s shirt with her right hand and lifted her clean off the ground like she weighed nothing. The mean girl’s feet kicked in the air for a second before Buffy dropped her hard onto her ass on the tile floor. The impact made a dull thud, and the girl’s perfect hair flew forward into her face.

The girl on the left immediately started reaching into her purse, fingers fumbling. Buffy didn’t even look at her.

Buffy said, her voice flat and dangerous, "Do it. I dare you."

She extended her left index finger and casually pushed it straight into the tiled wall beside her. The tile cracked and crumbled around her finger like it was made of chalk. She pulled her finger out, and a small cloud of white dust drifted down onto her white tank top. She didn’t brush it away.

Buffy looked down at the girl she had dropped and leaned in, her voice low and steady.

Buffy said, "Now you know what it feels like to be helpless. To be a victim. Touch my sister again... I dare you."

The three mean girls were backed into the corner near the sinks. The middle one was still on the floor, breathing fast, mascara starting to smudge at the corners of her eyes. The other two were crying quietly, soft, shocked sobs, not dramatic. Real fear. One of them had her arms wrapped tight around herself.

Dawn slowly opened the stall door. Water still dripped from her hair and chin onto her already-wet t-shirt. She stepped out, wiped her wet face with her sleeve, walked to the sink, and rinsed her hands without looking at any of them. There was a flicker of something like grim satisfaction in her eyes. Buffy waited by the door, arms crossed again, watching the girls like a hawk, her posture solid and protective.

The two of them left the bathroom together without another word. The door swung shut behind them with a soft creak. The mean girls remained huddled in the corner, the only sound their quiet crying and the steady drip of the faucet.

CHAPTER 4 After the Storm

Back at the picnic table, the afternoon light had softened, and the first high clouds were drifting in from the ocean to dim the sun. Shadows slid across the grass and the wooden table, and a light breeze had picked up, carrying a cooler edge and the faint smell of rain.

Buffy and Dawn walked back from the bathrooms in silence. Buffy’s hand rested lightly on Dawn’s shoulder for a moment before dropping away. Dawn’s wet hair was starting to dry in messy waves.

Buffy dropped into her seat next to Lucian, and his hand found the small of her back as she sat, one grounding press before he let go.

She looked across at Tara with a half-joking, half-serious expression, one eyebrow raised.

Buffy said, "The police might be showing up later."

Tara just nodded slowly with that tired knowing look. She didn’t even seem surprised. Her cardigan had slipped slightly off one shoulder; she pulled it back up with a small automatic motion.

Willow brought over the tray of burgers, loaded with lettuce, cheese, tomatoes, and all the fixings. She set it down in the middle of the table, and everyone started reaching for food.

Dawn hesitated, her hand hovering over her burger for a second without picking it up. Instead, she sat up straighter, looked directly at Buffy across the table, and spoke, her voice steady but carrying real emotion, her eyes bright, a little red-rimmed but dry now.

Dawn said, "I want to be able to fight back when I’m cornered. I want you to teach me self-defense. For real this time. I’m not gonna quit like I did before."

She paused and glanced down at her hands once, then back up.

Dawn said, "I’m not a bully. But I don’t want to be saved anymore. Not by you. Not by anyone. I want to save myself."

The table went quiet for a moment as Buffy studied her little sister, her expression softening, eyes crinkling with a mix of pride and ache. Then she reached across and rested her hand on Dawn’s shoulder, firm but gentle.

Buffy said, quiet and honest, "I get it. You shouldn’t have to feel like you’re always the one who needs protecting. We’ll do it right this time."

She gave Dawn’s shoulder a small squeeze before letting go.

Buffy said, "One hour after school, three days a week. Garage or the back room at the Magic Box. No weapons. Just technique. You show up, we train. Deal?"

Dawn rolled her eyes, but there was a small reluctant smile at the corner of her mouth.

Dawn said, "Fine."

She finally picked up her burger and took a bite, chewing with exaggerated relief.

Tara lifted her burger, and just as she took a bite, a butterfly landed on her index finger, deep blue and black wings with bright cyan running through them like veins of light. It slowly opened and closed its wings in a calm rhythm, antennae twitching. Tara froze mid-bite, eyes widening just slightly in delighted surprise, then smiled softly at it, a pure, unguarded expression, the crinkles at her eyes deepening. A few strands of her wavy hair drifted across her cheek in the breeze; she didn’t move to brush them away.

Dawn noticed and quickly put her burger down, pulling a yellow disposable camera from her pocket with eager fingers. Her tongue poking out slightly between her lips in concentration, she took a photo of Tara holding the burger with the butterfly resting on her finger, the shutter clicking, then wound the film on with her thumb. Tara stayed perfectly still, smiling gently while Dawn captured the moment. The butterfly’s wings caught the softened light.

Willow watched them with a small grateful smile, clearly relieved the tension had eased. She tried to keep the mood light, one hand resting on the table, fingers relaxed now.

Willow said, "Hey... remember when we used to just do normal stuff like this? Burgers in the park? No drama?"

Lucian paused, set his burger down, and turned toward Willow with genuine curiosity, not lecturing, just thinking aloud.

Lucian said, "I’ve been thinking about that electromagnetic energy that overlaps our reality. The one that fuels magic. Where does it actually come from? In the bigger sense?"

Willow thought for a moment, then answered honestly, her head tilting slightly, eyes distant but warm.

Willow said, "It comes from in between the stars. All of them. That’s as far as I can see. I don’t know what’s past that... because I can’t see any further. But that is a really good question because it has to come from somewhere."

Lucian raised his eyebrows, looking at Willow thoughtfully.

Lucian said, "You mean like God?"

Willow nodded softly.

Willow said quietly, almost dreamily, "Yeah. A being of some kind."

The butterfly on Tara’s finger slowly lifted off and drifted away into the afternoon light, wings flashing once.

For a little while, the group just ate. There was the sound of chewing, the occasional rustle of the trees, and the quiet comfort of still being together after everything. A small smile lingered on Dawn’s face.

CHAPTER 5 Barbie Girl

Inside Joyce’s silver sedan on the drive to the Magic Box, gray overcast light came through the rain-streaked windows. The inside of the car felt cramped and slightly humid. The windshield wipers moved steadily across the glass with a rhythmic squeak-squeak. Rain pattered on the roof.

The group was still in their park clothes, a little rumpled and damp from the sudden rain that had started as they left. With five of them crammed into Joyce’s sedan, Lucian was squished in the middle of the back seat between Buffy and Dawn, looking deeply uncomfortable. His arms were pinned close to his sides, hands resting awkwardly in his lap, and he was hunched forward slightly, trying and failing to make himself smaller. Buffy, on his left, had pulled on a light denim jacket against the rain, the sleeves pushed up. Dawn was on his right, a light hoodie tied around her waist, her hair still half-damp from the bathroom. The neon yellow Frisbee was wedged on the floor near Dawn’s feet.

Willow drove, with Tara in the passenger seat, her cardigan pulled tight against the chill.

Lucian shifted again, his knees pressed together, elbows tucked in.

Lucian said under his breath, low and resigned, "At least there will be dessert later..."

Willow glanced at him in the rearview mirror with a small smile, eyes crinkling, then reached over and turned on the radio, her thumb tapping the knob once.

Aqua’s "Barbie Girl" came through the speakers.

Dawn immediately lit up and started singing at full volume, pointing dramatically at nothing in particular as she leaned forward, eyes bright.

Dawn sang loudly, "I’m a Barbie girl, in a Barbie world"

Buffy grinned and jumped in without hesitation, also singing loud and proud while doing little seated dance moves, shoulders shimmying. She pointed at Dawn with one finger, the other hand resting on Lucian’s knee.

Buffy sang, "Life in plastic, it’s fantastic"

Tara turned in her seat, smiling, and joined in, her voice softer than the other two but still enthusiastic, a little breathy from laughter, swaying as one hand tapped the armrest.

Tara sang along, "You can brush my hair, undress me everywhere"

Dawn sang, pointing at Buffy, "Imagination, life is your creation"

Buffy sang, laughing, "Come on Barbie, let’s go party"

The three girls kept going, trading lines back and forth, pointing at each other, laughing, and singing with zero shame. Dawn did a little head-bob while Tara swayed in her seat. Buffy was fully committed, belting the lines out between laughs, her head thrown back for one high note. While singing the next line, Buffy turned toward Lucian, teasingly shimmied her shoulders at him a couple of times with a playful grin, then gave him a very deliberate wink before continuing to sing.

Lucian’s face turned noticeably pinker, the flush creeping up from his collar. He let out a short surprised laugh and shook his head, looking down at his lap for a second before glancing back up at her, his ears bright red. He shifted in the cramped space, one knee bumping Dawn’s.

Lucian said, still pink, quietly amused, "Thank god for the little things..."

In the front seat, Willow just bobbed her head to the beat and tapped her thumbs lightly on the steering wheel. She didn’t sing; she just kept her eyes on the wet road, a small private smile playing at the corners of her mouth at the chaos happening behind her.

The girls continued singing loudly as the car drove through the rain toward the Magic Box.

CHAPTER 6 Back to Work

Outside the Magic Box, the sky was still overcast, but the rain had eased into a light drizzle. A few scattered drops fell here and there, soft gray light diffused through the clouds, and puddles reflected the awning lights.

A small line of customers, six or seven people, was waiting under the awning, looking mildly impatient. One person near the back of the line checked their watch, shook their head, and started walking away. Rain droplets beaded on the shop windows.

Joyce’s sedan pulled up and parked, and everyone got out, doors slamming. The group moved with the tired but determined energy of people who had had a full day and still had work to do.

Tara was the first one to move, jogging quickly toward the front door with her keys already in her hand, her cardigan dotted with rain spots. She fumbled with them for a second, her fingers slightly clumsy from the rush and the damp, before finally getting the right key into the lock. She pushed the door open, then turned back toward the group and the waiting customers.

Tara said, gentle and clearly apologetic, stuttering softly, "I’m so so so so sorry we’re four minutes late. I was having lun lunch with my family. I’m so sorry... please, come inside everyone."

She held the door open as the customers began filing in, offering a small apologetic smile to each. Once they were moving, she turned to the others and started giving calm clear directions.

Tara said to Willow, "Please start the teapot and the coffee maker."

Tara said to Buffy, "Can you put out the cookies and biscuits on the guest table?"

Tara then followed a middle-aged woman who was already asking about tarot cards. She led her toward the display case, her voice soft and helpful as she began explaining the different decks, one hand gesturing gently.

Inside, the shop was warm and familiar, smelling of old books, herbs, and now fresh coffee. Everyone moved with practiced ease. The bell above the door chimed softly as customers entered.

Willow headed straight behind the counter and started prepping the tea and coffee station. She filled the kettle, turned on the coffee maker, and moved with quiet efficiency. Her green button-up was still a little damp at the cuffs; she pushed the sleeves up again.

Buffy and Lucian walked over to the guest table near the front window, Lucian rolling his shoulders once after the long day.

Buffy grabbed a tray of cookies and biscuits from behind the counter while Lucian reached for the nearest display plate without being asked and helped arrange them neatly. Buffy had shrugged off her denim jacket and draped it over a nearby chair.

Lucian said quietly as he placed cookies, "You know... this is kind of nice. The whole ‘normal couple running errands’ thing."

Buffy said, smiling as she set down a plate, "Yeah? You like playing house?"

Lucian said, "I didn’t say I was good at it. Just that it’s nice."

Buffy leaned in a little closer, her voice dropping into a playful, teasing tone as she glanced at him, and bumped his hip with hers, a small private smile on her lips.

Buffy said, "Well, if you’re good tonight... maybe I’ll let you play house a little more. In bed."

She gave him a soft wink.

Buffy said, "Hope you’ve got some energy saved up for me."

Lucian’s ears turned slightly pink, but he smiled and shook his head, the corners of his eyes crinkling. He reached up and tucked one of her damp strands of hair behind her ear with a gentle finger.

Lucian said, "You’re dangerous when you’re in a good mood."

Buffy laughed under her breath and bumped her shoulder against his before going back to arranging the last of the cookies.

Meanwhile, Dawn had taken her place behind the register. She stood up straight, shoulders back, looking focused and mature. Her damp hair was pulled back into a neater ponytail now; she had a small smudge of something, dirt or old mascara, on one cheek that she hadn’t noticed. A customer approached with a small bag of herbs and a book. Dawn rang them up cleanly, counted the change correctly, and handed it back with a polite smile, her posture proud, chin lifted just a fraction.

Dawn said, "That’ll be fourteen seventy-five. Here’s your change... and your receipt. Thank you for coming in."

The customer thanked her and left. Dawn watched them go for a second, then glanced around the shop with a quiet sense of pride. She was clearly taking this seriously, no slacking, no attitude, just steady, responsible work. She wiped the smudge from her cheek with the back of her hand, a small satisfied smile touching her mouth.

Tara was still helping the tarot customer, patiently explaining the difference between two decks while the woman listened intently. Tara’s cardigan sleeves were pushed up; she gestured with calm open hands.

The shop slowly came back to life around them. The bell above the door chimed as more customers entered. The smell of fresh coffee and tea began to fill the air. For a little while, everything felt almost normal again.

CHAPTER 7 The Light

Later that evening, the back training room was dimly lit, a single overhead bulb casting a soft, warm glow and shadows pooling in the corners. The room was quiet, away from the front of the shop, and the distant sounds of customers and the occasional chime of the front door bell felt far away.

Buffy was still in her white tank top and jeans from the day, her denim jacket left behind in the shop, her hair mostly dry now. Lucian’s sleeves were still pushed up. Both looked comfortably rumpled from the long day.

Buffy led Lucian into the room and closed the door behind them. She didn’t let go of his hand right away. For a moment she just stood there, looking at him, clearly working up to something. When she finally spoke, her voice was quieter than usual, honest and a little raw, her eyes flicking down once, then back to his face.

Buffy said, "I want to tell you something. About the first time I died."

She let go of his hand and leaned back against the wall, arms loosely crossed. A small furrow appeared between her brows; she smoothed it with two fingers.

Buffy said, "It was the Master. He bit me from behind, made me feel helpless. I could not stop him. I fell into a puddle of muddy water and was drowning when he discarded me. Xander brought me back with CPR, but... for those few minutes, I was gone. And when I came back, something in me changed. I think a part of me never really came all the way back."

She paused, then continued, her voice still low, her eyes distant for a moment, seeing something else.

Buffy said, "Then there was Angel. And that whole thing... you already know most of it. The curse, the Angelus stuff, sending him to hell. I loved him. I really did. But it was always wrapped up in pain and prophecy and darkness. It felt like that was just how it had to be for me."

She looked at him directly now, her expression open, vulnerable in a way she rarely allowed, a tiny sad smile touching one corner of her mouth.

Buffy said, "Are you seeing a pattern yet?"

Lucian didn’t rush to fill the silence. He held her eyes, gave one slow exhale through his nose, then nodded, the answer earned, not automatic.

Lucian said quietly, "Yeah. I see it."

Buffy nodded, then took a slow breath. This next part was harder for her. She pushed off the wall and took a small step closer, her arms uncrossing, her fingers twisting together in front of her for a second before she stilled them.

Buffy said, "When I first met you... I was dead inside. Not in a dramatic way. Just... numb. Like someone had turned the volume all the way down on everything. I could go through the motions. I could fight, I could talk, I could smile when I needed to. But nothing actually reached me. It was like I was watching my own life from behind glass. I kept thinking... this is it. This is what I get. This is what being the Slayer means. Everything good eventually gets taken or twisted or destroyed. Even love."

She swallowed once, her voice getting softer, but she didn’t look away.

Buffy said, "And then you showed up. And you didn’t try to fix me. You didn’t try to be some big hero or tell me everything was going to be okay. You just... sat with me. In the noise. You made me feel like I was allowed to be tired. Like I didn’t have to be the Slayer every second of the day. And slowly... one moment at a time... you started bringing me back. Not to the Slayer. To me. To being a person again."

She looked at him, eyes a little glassy but steady. Her hands had come up to rest lightly on his chest, fingers splaying over the embroidered fabric.

Buffy said, "You’re the light, Lucian. You’re the first thing in my life that didn’t come wrapped in darkness. And I didn’t even know how much I needed that until you were already here."

She fell quiet, waiting. Her thumbs made small unconscious circles on his chest.

Lucian was quiet for a long moment, not rushing to answer. He just looked at her, really looked at her, like he was letting every word settle, and his hands came up to cover hers on his chest, warm and steady. When he finally spoke, his voice was low and deeply sincere, a small, genuine smile touching his mouth.

Lucian said, "I don’t think I’ve ever been anyone’s light before. I don’t know if I even know how to be that for someone. But... if that’s what I am to you, then I’m not going anywhere. I’m not going to try to be some perfect savior or pretend I understand everything you’ve been through. I’m just going to keep showing up. Every day. Even on the days when the noise is loud. Especially then."

He gently took one of her hands from his chest and lifted it to press a kiss to her knuckles, his eyes never leaving hers.

Lucian said, "You don’t have to carry it all alone anymore. Not with me."

Buffy didn’t say anything right away. She just squeezed his hand and nodded once, her eyes still on his. The weight she’d been carrying seemed a little lighter in this moment. She leaned in and rested her forehead against his chest, breathing him in, her hair falling forward to hide her face for a second.

They stood there together in the quiet of the back room, the distant sounds of the shop continuing on without them. The single bulb hummed softly overhead.

CHAPTER 8 It’s Working

The shop had quieted down after the afternoon rush, and most customers had left. Warm soft lamplight pooled from a single standing lamp near the front window and the low overheads in the back. The air carried the faint comforting scent of steeped tea, old paper, and a hint of the cookies from earlier. Shadows were soft and deep in the corners.

Willow sat on the small couch near the front window. She had a chipped ceramic mug of tea in one hand and a worn paperback in her lap. She looked calm on the surface, but there was a quiet watchfulness in her eyes, a tension in her shoulders, as she occasionally glanced toward the closed door of the back training room. She took a slow sip of tea, then gently closed the book and set it aside on the cushion beside her. Her gaze lingered on the door for a few seconds.

Willow stood up with her mug, moving with deliberate, quiet care, her slides making almost no sound on the wooden floor. She walked slowly toward the back of the shop, past the counter, her free hand lightly trailing along a shelf for a moment as if grounding herself. She paused outside the back training room door, listening, her head tilted slightly, then reached for the handle with slow, careful fingers, turning it with almost no sound, and cracked the door open just a few inches, just enough to see inside.

Through the crack, Willow saw Buffy bent forward over the sturdy old wooden training table. She was still in her day clothes from the shop, the simple white tank top now slightly rumpled and pulled up in the front from movement, light-wash jeans unbuttoned and lowered just enough at the hips for the moment. Her blonde hair was loose and wild, cascading forward and moving in a steady rhythmic back-and-forth motion with each breath and shift of her body. Her breathing was heavy, unrestrained, and deeply present, soft, throaty feminine sounds escaping with every movement, raw and alive. Lucian stood behind her, his body close, one strong hand gripping her hip with clear grounding pressure, fingers splayed, thumb visible against the denim. He was still in his black shirt, sleeves pushed up, dark jeans low on his hips. His other hand was braced on the table beside her for support. From where Willow stood she could see mostly Buffy’s back, the subtle arch of her spine, the way her shoulders moved with each breath, and part of Lucian’s arm and torso.

Willow’s eyes widened for just a fraction of a second in a quick flash of surprise, her eyebrows lifting, and a faint flush touched her cheeks. Then her entire expression softened into a slow, warm, deeply relieved smile, the tension in her shoulders visibly melting, her eyes crinkling at the corners with quiet affection and something like pride. She watched for only another moment or two, long enough to take in the rhythm of Buffy’s hair, the soft sounds of life and connection, the way Lucian’s hand held her with such steady care.

From inside the room came Buffy’s heavier, throaty breathing and soft unrestrained sounds of pleasure and release with each movement. There was the subtle rustle of fabric, Lucian’s low quiet breaths, and the faint creak of the old wooden table under their weight, just the raw human sounds of two people connecting in a private moment, while the shop itself stayed quiet.

Willow’s smile lingered, soft and genuine, as she very gently pulled the door closed again, easing it shut with her free hand, fingers careful on the old handle, never letting it click or make any sound that would interrupt. She stood there with her back to the door for a second, still smiling, one hand resting lightly on the wood. A quiet breath of relief left her, soft, almost a small laugh of pure fondness.

She turned and walked back to the front of the shop, her steps lighter now, the mug held close to her chest.

Willow sat back down on the couch, took a slow, deliberate sip of her tea, and stared out the window at the darkening street with that same small, satisfied, deeply relieved smile, her eyes soft and a little glassy with emotion.

Willow said softly to herself, her voice warm and full of quiet wonder, "It’s working..."

She leaned back into the couch, still smiling. A single tear of happiness slipped down one cheek; she didn’t wipe it away. She just sat there, letting the moment settle.

CHAPTER 9 Home — Night

Back at the Summers house, it was deep night, and the street was quiet, the only light coming from the porch light above the front door and the warm yellow glow spilling from the bay window. The car’s headlights cut across the driveway as it pulled in and stopped, and the engine ticked as it cooled while crickets chirped in the background.

The car doors opened, and everyone climbed out looking tired in that good full-day-out kind of way, sun and breeze and rain and work all layered into them, their hair messy, their clothes rumpled, the faint scent of park grass, soap, and summer night air still on their skin.

Tara and Willow moved to the back of the car first, and when Willow popped the trunk, Tara reached in and pulled out a white plastic bag with leftover burgers and fixings, holding it carefully against her hip as if the leftover tomatoes were something precious.

Tara said, soft and practical, "Burgers and stuff. Lettuce, tomatoes, and the sliced cheese are still good. I’ll put them in the fridge before they go bad."

Willow nodded, closing the trunk with a soft thud, and they walked toward the house together, their steps slow, a little heavy with the end of a long day, Tara’s free hand brushing Willow’s as they walked.

The crooked front door stuck in its frame before it opened. Warm light from inside spilled across the porch, and they disappeared inside.

In the living room, the lamplight was warm and low, a single standing lamp in the corner casting soft pools of light. The TV was off, and the room felt lived-in and quiet, a faint smell of old books and laundry lingering.

Dawn, Buffy, and Lucian came in after them, Dawn dragging her feet slightly, shoulders slumped, Buffy walking beside Lucian, her hand loosely in his.

Buffy had kicked off her shoes at the door and padded barefoot across the carpet. Dawn still had her hoodie tied around her waist, her hair a mess of waves escaping the ponytail.

Dawn headed straight for the big couch but didn’t sit yet. She stood there for a second, rubbing her eyes with the back of her hand, and a yawn escaped her.

Dawn said, a little whiny but affectionate, "I’m wiped. I’m going upstairs."

She leaned in and gave Buffy a quick tired hug, then did the same to Lucian, a little more awkward but real, her arms going around his middle briefly before letting go.

Dawn said, "Night, you guys."

She turned and headed for the stairs, her footsteps slow on the wooden steps, rubbing the back of her neck once, ponytail sagging.

Upstairs, Dawn flicked on the bathroom light, bright and a little harsh after the soft living room, and stood in front of the mirror, staring at herself for a second with heavy eyes: dark circles, and hair dried into a mess of crooked waves from the park sink. She grabbed her toothbrush, put toothpaste on it, and brushed slowly, eyes half-closed. She spat, rinsed, spat again. The water ran. She wiped her mouth with the back of her hand, turned the light off, and walked down the short hallway to her room. The door closed with a soft click.

Downstairs, Lucian and Buffy had settled on the couch. Buffy was curled into his side, one leg tucked under her. She had taken his right hand in both of hers and was slowly playing with his thumb, stroking her own thumb gently across the pad of his, back and forth in a quiet, absent rhythm, her head resting against his shoulder, her hair spilling over his chest. Her toes curled into the couch cushion.

Across the room, in the large armchair, Tara sat with her legs tucked up. She looked peaceful, eyes half-lidded, cardigan wrapped around her.

Willow entered from the kitchen, drying her hands on a dish towel. She saw Lucian and Buffy on the couch and smiled, soft, warm, a little knowing.

She walked over to the back of the couch, reached down, and pulled the big throw blanket from where it was draped. Without saying anything, she gently laid it over both of them, tucking it lightly around Buffy’s shoulders. Her fingers brushed Buffy’s hair once in a small affectionate gesture.

Willow said, quiet and fond, "There."

She moved to the large chair, where Tara shifted slightly to make room, and climbed in to lie across Tara’s lap, her head resting on Tara’s thigh, body stretched out, feet dangling off the right side of the chair. Her eyes closed almost immediately.

Tara looked down at her with that soft private smile and slowly ran her fingers through Willow’s hair in gentle, repetitive strokes. Willow made a small content sound in her throat and shifted closer. Tara’s other hand rested on Willow’s shoulder, thumb making small circles.

Tara leaned down and kissed her, soft, slow, and intentional. Willow’s hand came up and cupped Tara’s cheek, thumb brushing lightly along her jaw as she kissed her back. Their hair mingled, red and golden blonde, in the low light.

The quiet domestic picture held all four of them. No one was speaking much, and the only sounds were the faint creak of the chair and the soft rustle of the blanket.

On the couch, Buffy’s thumb kept stroking Lucian’s. Then, without looking up, she hooked her thumb under his and slowly, deliberately, guided his hand down under the blanket... toward the top of her pants. Her breathing had already changed, just a little deeper. A few strands of her hair brushed against Lucian’s neck as she leaned into him.

She glanced at him sideways, eyes half-lidded, a small private smile on her lips. She leaned into him a little more, her free hand resting on his chest, fingers curling lightly into the embroidered fabric.

Lucian’s eyebrows lifted just slightly. Surprise flickered across his face for half a second, eyes widening a fraction, mouth parting, but he didn’t pull away. His hand disappeared under the blanket. His other arm tightened around her shoulders, pulling her closer. A tiny, almost inaudible exhale left him.

The blanket moved, subtle, slow, and rhythmic.

Buffy’s eyes fluttered. Her lips parted. A very low, quiet sigh escaped her, barely audible, almost just breath. Her fingers tightened gently around his wrist under the blanket for a moment, then relaxed again. She bit her lower lip to keep any sound in, a small tremor running through her shoulders. Her head stayed on his shoulder, and a faint flush crept up her neck.

Across the room, Tara and Willow were still in their own quiet world. Tara’s fingers kept moving gently through Willow’s hair. Neither of them looked over. The moment felt private, safe, stolen.

The room stayed warm, quiet, and lived-in, and the blanket continued its subtle rhythm. Buffy’s eyes stayed half-lidded, a tiny smile ghosting across her lips even as her breath caught once. Lucian’s free hand stroked her hair, slow and soothing, fingers threading through the strands.

CHAPTER 10 Glory Returns

The room was still warm and low-lit from the single standing lamp. Everything felt soft and safe, right up until the moment it didn’t. The shadows in the corners seemed to deepen.

Buffy was still on the couch under the blanket, body angled toward Lucian, one hand gripping his wrist beneath it as the blanket moved in slow, subtle rhythms. Her jaw was tight, her brow furrowed in deep concentration. A thin sheen of sweat had formed along her brow and upper lip, her hair damp at the temples, strands clinging to her skin. Her teeth were sunk into her bottom lip, holding back any sound, her breathing controlled but heavier than before, her eyes fluttering every few seconds as she fought to stay quiet. A tiny private smile kept trying to form at the corners of her mouth before she bit it back. She looked like she was holding her entire body in check, every muscle locked down except for the ones moving under the blanket, her free hand resting on Lucian’s chest, fingers flexing once.

Across the room, in the large chair, Willow’s eyes were closed, a small smile on her lips as Tara gently stroked her hair, Tara’s head tilted down, her own hair falling forward like a curtain.

Then a low hum rose out of nowhere under the quiet of the room, and a cold blue light gathered in the middle of the living room. As the hum cut off into dead silence, Glory was simply there, as if she had always been standing on that carpet. It was the First, wearing Glory’s face.

She stood in that faint cold blue light with her hands on her hips and her head tilted, that familiar cruel little smile already in place. She looked exactly like she had in life, blonde hair perfect and shiny, a short pink dress that somehow looked both designer and cheap, and an air of pure arrogance. Her nails were long and painted bright pink, and her presence made the air feel thick.

Glory said, pure valley girl, casual and already mocking, "Oh my god. Are you guys serious right now?"

For one heartbeat, nobody in the room so much as breathed.

Tara’s head snapped toward the voice. The second she saw Glory, her face went white with pure terror. A raw broken scream tore out of her throat, the kind of sound that came from somewhere deep and old. Her whole body jerked backward in the chair so hard she almost tipped it over. Her eyes were wide, pupils blown, mouth still open on the scream. Willow’s eyes flew open in confusion and fear, her body going rigid.

Buffy reacted instantly, yanking her hand out from under the blanket and pushing herself up off the couch in one sharp movement. Her jeans were unbuttoned at the top, the zipper halfway down, but they stayed up, and she didn’t even notice. Her hair was wild around her face, damp strands sticking to her flushed cheeks. Sweat glistened on her collarbones.

Lucian started to rise with her, protective instinct kicking in. His hand reached for her automatically, fingers brushing her elbow.

Buffy slammed her hand flat against his chest and shoved him back down onto the couch, hard, her eyes never leaving Glory.

Buffy said, low and deadly serious, "Stay back. She’s a god."

Buffy pointed at Glory and said, "I put you down once before... I will do it again!"

She raised both fists, sliding into a fighting stance without hesitation. Her breathing was still a little uneven, but her eyes were locked on Glory like a predator. A drop of sweat rolled down her temple; she ignored it.

Glory just laughed, that bright, mean, delighted laugh. She took one slow deliberate step forward, hips swaying, the pink dress swishing. She looked the group up and down like they were a particularly amusing zoo exhibit.

Glory said, tilting her head, grinning wide, "Aww, look at you. Pants half undone, all flushed and sweaty, and you’re still trying to play the big bad Slayer. That’s actually adorable. Did I interrupt something? Were you two about to get all gross on the family couch? In front of the witch and her broken little girlfriend? Classy."

She took another step, still smiling like this was the funniest thing she’d seen all year. Her eyes glittered with malice.

Glory said, "And this house? Still looks like a dump. I mean, seriously. You’d think after everything you’d at least get some decent furniture. But nope. Same sad little living room. Same sad little life."

Then Glory’s gaze locked onto Tara, and she stopped. Her smile sharpened into something colder. She slowly raised both hands in front of her, fingers splayed wide and pointed directly at Tara’s head like claws. Her long pink nails caught the lamplight. She gave a quick vicious little snap of her head and wrists, a sharp, deliberate motion like she was plunging her fingers straight back into Tara’s skull.

Glory said, her voice dropping into something cruel and intimate, "I can stick my fingers right back into that head of yours again! Just like I did before!"

Tara let out a broken animal whimper and curled tighter into herself, arms wrapping around her knees, rocking. Her eyes were huge and glassy with pure terror.

Willow was already on her feet, her face pale but determined. She threw both hands out in front of her, fingers spread, and started chanting under her breath, fast, trying to pull something big and powerful. Her red hair flew back from her face as the air around her hands began to shimmer... but then it snapped in a visible crackle of misfiring magic.

Willow cried out and grabbed the center of her chest with both hands like something had just punched her from the inside. Her legs gave out and she dropped hard to her knees on the carpet, gasping, eyes squeezed shut in pain, one hand still clutched to her chest, the other braced on the floor.

Glory watched this with pure delight, one eyebrow raised, head tilting the other way.

Glory said, "Awww. Your little witch is having a mid-life crisis? That’s so sad. Not exactly up to par these days, are you, little red? What happened? Did all that big bad magic finally catch up with you?"

She looked back at Buffy, still grinning, taking another casual step closer. Her blonde hair bounced perfectly.

Glory said, "And you... you really think you can take me again? This time I know all your moves. Everything about you. Hell, you died fighting me last time, and I am going to kill you again."

Buffy quickly bent to the side table, snatched up a long letter opener, and threw it straight at Glory’s head. It passed right between her eyes and stuck into the drywall behind her with a thump.

Buffy stared at the quivering handle in the drywall and then back at the unmarked face in front of her, her fists dropping an inch in pure confusion.

Buffy said, "What?"

Glory’s smile sharpened. The playful valley-girl tone dropped just enough to show the monster underneath. Her eyes went cold for a split second before the grin returned.

Glory said, "From beneath you... I am coming!"

Glory mock-stomped her right foot on the carpet without making a sound, and somewhere far beneath the house something answered her. The floor gave a low, grinding shudder, and the whole house shook and rattled for four long seconds, picture frames buzzing against the walls, before it settled again.

Then the cold blue light went out like a breath, and Glory vanished as suddenly as she had appeared, leaving soft laughter behind that slowly faded.

Everyone slowly looked at one another. The room was silent except for the ticking of the antique clock on the wall.
