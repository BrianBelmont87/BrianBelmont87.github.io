Buffy the Vampire Slayer. Season six, episode 11. Dawn of Love.

CHAPTER 1 The Morning Routine

That morning in Dawn’s bedroom, the little alarm-clock radio came on with some bright, tinny pop-rock song. The sun was already streaming through the window in golden shafts, and the whole room felt warm and alive and normal after the darkness of the night before. The alarm clicked off with a soft snap, the music playing on quietly underneath while the morning birds chattered outside her window.

Dawn popped up from under the covers like she had been shot out of a cannon, buzzing with teenage energy, her hair a mess from sleep and a pillow crease still pressed into one cheek. She grabbed her translucent flip phone off the nightstand. There was a new text message from Josh: “Coming over after school. Can I snag a ride with Willow to your place and a ride home later? Movie night still on?”

It was the movie night Willow had said yes to at breakfast almost a week ago, the one that had already been pushed back once, and Dawn was not about to let it slip again.

Her face lit up at once. She bit down hard on her lower lip, her eyes crinkling at the corners, and a small breathy laugh slipped out of her as she typed back with both thumbs, fast and a little clumsy with excitement: “Yes! Cool beans. See you at school.”

She practically fluttered around the room after that. She tore through her closet with hangers clacking and fabric rustling, until she settled on a faded denim jacket over a soft lavender scoop-neck top, her well-worn blue jeans, and white sneakers. She held it all up against herself in the mirror and did a small spin so the jacket flared out, then gave her reflection a satisfied little nod.

A few minutes later Dawn tiptoed down the upstairs hallway in her socks like a ninja on a mission. She stopped outside Buffy’s bedroom door, tilted her head to listen for a second, then eased the door open just wide enough to peek inside.

Morning light lay across the bed in wide golden bands. Buffy was deeply asleep, tangled up in the duvet and sheets with her face turned toward the light, looking more peaceful and unguarded than she had in months, nothing hard in her face at all. A few strands of blonde hair had fallen across her cheek, and one bare shoulder showed above a peach silk strap.

Moving with exaggerated stealth, Dawn sneaked across to Buffy’s vanity, glancing back at the bed every few steps to make sure her sister didn’t stir. Her eyes caught on the delicate silver clam shell hairpin resting near the mirror, and a slow, mischievous grin spread across her face.

She picked it up carefully and turned it in her fingers, so the tiny pearl inside the silver shell caught the light with a soft, innocent gleam. Then she slid the pin smoothly into her own hair, tucking a loose strand behind her ear with it. Her fingers lingered for a second to make sure it sat just right, the metal cool against her scalp.

Then she silently unscrewed a small pot of Buffy’s favorite expensive shimmering eyeshadow, dipped her pinky in, and brushed a quick, careful coat onto her eyelids while she watched herself in the mirror, before screwing the lid shut again with the softest twist.

She slipped out of the room without a sound and closed the door with the same careful control she had used to open it, hunched up with suppressed glee as she padded down the hall in her socks, while behind the closed door Buffy slept on, peaceful and still.

CHAPTER 2 Breakfast and the Predator

In the Summers kitchen, the morning sun poured through the windows and caught the steam rising off the stove. Bacon sizzled and pancake batter hissed on the hot griddle, and the whole room smelled of butter and maple syrup.

It was Tuesday morning, a fresh day after Monday’s chaos. Lucian and Tara stood side by side at the stove like a perfectly practiced team. Lucian wore a faded black Metallica shirt with Ride the Lightning cracked and peeling across the chest above the lightning-struck electric chair, and he was managing a skillet of bacon. Tara, in a soft earthy cardigan over a simple blouse and a long bohemian skirt, was flipping pancakes on the griddle.

Willow sat on a stool at the kitchen island in a soft green top and lounge pants, sipping orange juice and looking refreshed, her posture loose and easy. She had no idea at all that she had killed three people only hours earlier.

Willow leaned back on her stool with a long, contented sigh, her eyes going half-lidded, and she smiled brightly across the kitchen.

Willow said, "I don't know what kind of magic you're doing over that skillet, Lucian, but it smells infinitely better than anything Xander has ever tried to microwave in this house."

Lucian used a pair of tongs to lift the bacon out of the skillet onto a paper towel without looking up, his hands precise and calm. At Willow’s praise he gave the island the faintest sideways glance and a small, genuine half-smile before his eyes went back to the pan.

Lucian said, "It’s just butter, low heat, and patience, Willow. No incantations required."

Tara laughed softly under her breath as she slid three golden-brown pancakes off the griddle onto a waiting plate, the spatula scraping softly.

Tara said, "Don't give away our secrets."

Willow leaned back on her stool again and let out another long, happy sigh, her eyes drifting closed for a moment in the warmth of the room.

Willow said, "I wouldn't complain. I don't know why, but today just feels... lighter. You know? Just a really good, normal Tuesday."

Tara smiled over at her, soft and affectionate, with no idea of the horrible irony in it.

Lucian loaded a wooden bed tray with pancakes, bacon, a small pitcher of syrup, and two mugs of black coffee. He lifted it with both hands to test the balance and nudged the syrup pitcher over an inch so it wouldn’t slide. Then he gave Tara a respectful nod of thanks and carried it out of the kitchen and up the stairs with slow, steady steps, so that nothing spilled.

He pushed Buffy’s bedroom door open with his hip, the tray held level in front of him. The sunlight coming through the window lay warm across the bed, where Buffy was buried under the heavy duvet with only the top of her head showing.

As the door clicked shut behind him, she began to stir. She blinked slowly against the bright light and squinted at the tall shape standing near the bed. Then she pushed herself up onto her elbows, her hair a tangled halo around her face, and sniffed the air with her eyes still half closed. Her voice was thick with sleep and deeply suspicious.

Buffy said, "Smells like bacon... Am I in the hospital again?"

Lucian stopped at the edge of the bed, a small wry smile crossing his face as he watched her wake up.

Lucian said, "The hospital?"

Buffy rubbed one eye with the heel of her hand.

Buffy said, "Yeah. Because usually, when people bring me breakfast in bed, it means I've just been thrown head-first through a crypt wall and I'm currently bleeding out on a mattress."

Lucian chuckled softly, the sound low and warm in his chest.

Lucian said, "No crypts today. Just my own personal attempt at domestic bribery."

He set the tray down carefully on the nightstand, then sat on the edge of the mattress facing her. He picked up the fork, cut off a perfectly syrup-soaked piece of pancake, and held it up toward her mouth.

Buffy looked at the fork, and then at him, and then she leaned forward slowly and took the bite. She chewed with deliberate slowness while her eyes widened just a fraction as the taste hit her, and a small appreciative breath came out through her nose before she swallowed.

Buffy said, "Okay. Bribery accepted. What do you want?"

Lucian’s eyes stayed on hers, and he answered quietly.

Lucian said, "Just to see you awake in the daylight."

He reached back toward the tray, casually, for a piece of bacon, and Buffy’s hand shot out from under the covers so fast it was a blur. She caught his wrist in a firm grip before he had any time to react and pulled hard, dragging his whole body down onto the mattress in one fluid motion. In the same practiced movement she rolled and pinned him flat on his back and straddled his waist, while the heavy sheets tangled up around them both.

She was wearing peach silk panties and a matching peach silk top with thin shoulder straps, the silk catching the morning light on her shoulders and hips as she settled her weight over him. Lucian lay looking up at her, not the least bit bothered at being manhandled, only deeply intrigued by the sudden change in her.

Buffy leaned down until her face was inches from his. She tilted her head slowly, took the piece of bacon out of his fingers with her teeth, and chewed once, then twice. And then she kissed him, deep and demanding and hungry, a low, dangerous growl rumbling up from the back of her throat as she pushed the kiss deeper and poured all of that Slayer energy into it.

Lucian’s hands slid up to her waist and held her there, his fingers pressing into the silk at her sides, and the sheets shifted and bunched under them as their bodies found each other and their breath mixed. She rolled her hips once against him, slow and deliberate, and his breath caught hard in his throat.

The kiss went on and on, heavy and close, until the room held nothing but the muffled sound of their bodies moving in the tangled sheets, the soft, rhythmic creak of the mattress, and Buffy’s low, satisfied breath against his mouth.

CHAPTER 3 The First Date Talk

Back in her own bedroom, Dawn stood in front of the mirror practically vibrating. She checked her flip phone one more time, the little screen lighting up her face with Josh’s text still sitting there, and bit her lower lip with a wide grin.

She leaned in close to the mirror and added another tiny layer of the stolen eyeshadow with the tip of her pinky, quick but careful, then tilted her head one way and the other to study the shimmer. She grabbed her canvas backpack off the floor, swung it up over one shoulder, and bounded out into the hallway, never giving Buffy’s closed bedroom door a second look.

A moment later she bounced into the kitchen and dropped her backpack by the counter with a soft thud. Willow and Tara were sitting side by side at the island now, waiting to go over the movie-night rules one more time, and they had shifted into full serious-parent mode, Willow’s hands folded neatly on the countertop and Tara sitting up straighter, her face warm but resolute.

Willow squared herself on the stool, keeping her eyes steady on Dawn, and tapped her folded hands once on the counter.

Willow said, "Okay, Miss Summers. Before you run off to homeroom, let’s talk about Josh and this movie night."

Tara leaned forward a little with a gentle smile that didn’t take any of the firmness out of her voice.

Tara said, "We just want to set some ground rules, Dawnie. No hands in pants on the first date, okay?"

Dawn let out a long, loud, dramatic groan and dropped her head all the way back as though she had been mortally wounded. Her face flushed bright red from her neck to her hairline, and she flung both hands up in the air.

Dawn said, "Oh my god, Tara! Gross! We're just sitting on the couch watching a movie!"

Willow kept her voice calm and steady even while Dawn melted down, and she didn’t break eye contact for a second.

Willow said, "We know, we know. But it's important. You only do what you are comfortable with. Never let anyone push you into anything, okay? Also you're not allowed to close your bedroom door while he is here."

Dawn sighed and rolled her eyes so hard it was almost audible. But underneath the embarrassment she was grateful for the safety net they were throwing her, and the fight went out of her as the moment passed.

Dawn said, "I know. I promise. I've got it completely under control."

She grabbed an apple out of the fruit bowl and took a big defiant bite that crunched loudly through the quiet kitchen. Then she headed straight for the front door without another word, leaving the room behind her in a swirl of teenage energy.

CHAPTER 4 Structural Integrity

The front door clicked shut behind Dawn, and the kitchen fell silent for a moment. Willow and Tara both let their breath out at the same time and let go of their serious-parent postures, their shoulders sinking.

Willow slumped on her stool with a sigh. She said, "Well, that went okay, right? Not too traumatic?"

Before Tara could answer, a heavy, rhythmic thud, thud, thud sounded directly over their heads from Buffy’s bedroom, followed by a very muffled, very breathless groan from Buffy. The thudding picked up speed, steady and rhythmic and unmistakably physical, until the countertop began to vibrate under their elbows.

Right over the island, a small crack split through the ceiling paint with a soft splitting sound. A few white flakes of drywall drifted down like slow snow and landed on the countertop right next to Willow’s glass of orange juice, while the glass rattled again under the next thud and Buffy’s groan carried faintly through the plaster.

Willow tilted her head back slowly and looked up at the ceiling with a deadpan face, not even blinking at first, the orange juice forgotten in her hand. After a long second she tipped her head a little to one side, still staring up.

Willow said, "Again?"

Tara’s eyes went wide and she clapped both hands over her mouth. Her face flushed a bright crimson that spread all the way down her neck, her shoulders hunching forward as a small embarrassed breath puffed against her palms. She stared up at the ceiling with real worry mixed into the mortification.

Tara said from behind her hands, "Oh my... I hope he is okay up there."

Willow calmly brushed the drywall dust off the countertop with the back of her hand and took a slow sip of her orange juice, as if nothing unusual were happening at all.

Willow said, "She won't kill him."

Then her brow furrowed slightly, and after a moment she added, "..I think..."

CHAPTER 5 The Xander Talk

Outside the Summers house, Xander’s beat-up construction truck sat idling at the curb with a low rumble, back from the impound lot first thing that morning, its front bumper crumpled and its grille cracked from a wreck Xander still couldn’t remember. Xander sat behind the wheel in his grey work shirt, drumming his fingers on the steering wheel in a rapid, anxious rhythm, looking like a man getting himself ready for battle.

The front door opened, and Dawn bounded down the steps. She pulled open the heavy passenger door and climbed up into the cab with her usual energy.

Dawn said, "Hey! Thanks for the ride."

Xander drummed his fingers harder for a second, then grabbed the wheel at ten and two like he was about to take his driving test. His voice came out slightly too loud and far too serious.

Xander said, "Morning, Dawnie. Seatbelt."

Dawn rolled her eyes but buckled in without a word of complaint. Xander put the truck in drive and pulled away from the curb, turning left toward the main road to school, both hands still locked at ten and two and his knuckles pale on the wheel.

Xander said, "So. Movie night. With Josh."

Dawn kept looking out the window. She said, "Yep."

Xander nodded to himself a few times too many. He said, "Right. Good. Look, Dawn... as the primary male figure in this dynamic, I feel it is my sworn duty to explain a few things to you about the teenage boy."

Dawn turned her head slowly to look at him, her face flat and unimpressed, and raised one eyebrow.

Dawn said, "Oh, please don't."

Xander barreled right on, ignoring her, rambling, with one hand coming off the wheel to wave through the air.

Xander said, "No, it's important. See, the male teenage brain is basically a toxic wasteland of hormones, bad decisions, and a total lack of impulse control. They are like... like simple machines. They have one setting. And as a man, I know exactly what kind of maneuvers they try to pull on a couch in the dark—"

Dawn cut him off, entirely unbothered. She said, "Xander, I know what sex is. I have a cell phone and the internet."

Xander had just taken a sip from his metal coffee thermos as they rolled up to a red light. He froze in the middle of it, his eyes going wide, and stomped hard on the brakes, and a loud, violent spray of hot black coffee burst out of his mouth across the dashboard and the windshield.

He started coughing and hacking, pounding on his own chest with one fist, coffee dripping off his chin and soaking into the front of his shirt. Dawn just sat there and watched him with a calm, faintly amused look, waiting patiently for the light to turn green.

Then she said casually, "Do you have a napkin? You got some on the radio."

CHAPTER 6 The Edge and the Copyright

Later, in Buffy’s bedroom, the thudding against the floorboards had finally stopped. Buffy and Lucian lay tangled together in the heavy duvet with the sheets twisted and bunched around them, both still catching their breath while the morning sun lay warm over the bed.

Buffy rested her head near his chest and traced the lines of the raven tattoo across it with slow, gentle fingers. Her breathing settled into a steady, contented rhythm as the frantic energy from before eased into something quieter and more comfortable. After a while she spoke in a soft, thoughtful voice.

Buffy said, "You know... Riley used to treat me like a porcelain doll."

Lucian didn’t tense. He simply turned his head a little toward her and listened, his hand resting lightly on her back, giving her room to go on. And she did.

Buffy said, "Like if things got too rough, or if I showed too much of the dark stuff, I would just shatter. Or he would. But you... you don't do that. You don't treat me like I'm made of glass. You actually have the edge a girl like me needs."

She paused and bit her lower lip gently, a flicker of self-consciousness crossing her face as she realized what she had just said.

Buffy said, "Sorry. I didn't mean to bring up the ex. It just... kind of came up inside of me."

Lucian looked at her for a second. Then, without missing a beat, he answered in perfect, deadpan Buffy-speak.

Lucian said, "Hey. Ex-talk is just past-tense babble. No big."

Buffy blinked. Her eyes widened in real surprise, and then a bright, musical laugh broke out of her, a sound that room hadn’t heard in a very long time. She slapped his arm lightly, still laughing, and smiled wide.

Buffy said, "Okay. You can have that one without copyright infringement."

Lucian grinned back at her. He said, "Good. Because my legal team is terrible."

He shifted, and his fingers suddenly found the ticklish spot along her sides. He tickled her ribs just enough to make her squirm and laugh all over again, breaking up whatever tension was left, until she twisted away from his hand, still smiling.

Then the playfulness softened. Lucian leaned down and kissed her, and this time it wasn’t predatory or frantic but incredibly gentle, his lips moving slowly over hers while his big hands moved over her with practiced, easy grace. His palm slid along her ribcage, his thumb brushed the edge of the peach silk, and his fingers spread warm across her back.

Goosebumps rippled up Buffy’s bare arms and her breath hitched sharply in her throat. Her eyes fluttered shut as she gave herself over completely to the feeling of being genuinely cared for, arching a little into his hands, her bare legs shifting under the sheet and her toes curling against the mattress.

After a long moment Lucian slowly drew back. He pressed a soft kiss to her forehead, and another to the corner of her mouth. Then he rolled out of bed, planted his feet on the floor, and pulled on the faded Metallica shirt and trousers he had worn downstairs, rumpled now from the morning.

He reached under Buffy’s bed, pulled out a dark canvas duffel bag holding a few fresh sets of his clothes, and unzipped it. Buffy slid up to rest her back against the wooden headboard, pulling the sheet up over her chest with one hand, and arched an eyebrow at the bag.

Buffy said, "Moving in?"

Lucian pulled a clean shirt out of the bag and threw it over his shoulder. Beneath the blankets, Buffy deliberately let her legs fall open a little wider, a wicked, inviting smirk playing on her lips, the invitation plain and physical and unmistakable.

Buffy said, "You can move in if you want to. Again?"

Lucian stopped and looked back at her, then briefly closed his eyes and winced, one hand going to the small of his back, because the Slayer had thoroughly and repeatedly worn him out. Buffy’s smirk turned into a deeply satisfied grin, a faint sheen of sweat still cooling along her collarbone, and she nodded, looking extremely pleased with herself.

Buffy said, "Shower it is then."

CHAPTER 7 Pieces of Heaven

In the Summers kitchen, the thudding from upstairs had finally stopped. Willow, still sitting on her stool at the island, slowly lowered her glass of orange juice, and a small satisfied smirk tugged at her lips.

Willow said quietly, almost reverently, "Finally..."

Tara was leaning back against the counter by the kitchen sink, with the bright morning sun pouring through the window behind her and turning her long dirty blonde hair to a golden glow. She bit her lower lip as a distinctly mischievous little smile spread across her face, tilting her head with a playful challenge in her eyes.

Willow raised an eyebrow, reading her instantly. She slid off her stool and walked toward her with slow, deliberate steps.

Willow said, "Now? Right here?"

Tara’s smile deepened, soft and shy and bold all at once. She gave the tiniest nod.

Tara whispered, "Right here."

Willow stopped right in front of her, and the playfulness faded as something much deeper came up to the surface. Her voice went soft and intense.

Willow said, "You know… sometimes I look at you and I can’t breathe. Not because of magic. Not because of power. Just… you. You complete every broken piece of me, Tara. You make the noise in my head go quiet. You make me feel like I’m allowed to be both the girl who’s terrified of losing everything… and the woman who’s strong enough to hold it all. I don’t know how I existed before you. I don’t want to know."

Tara’s eyes filled with tears at once, and her breath caught in her throat, overwhelmed by the weight of what Willow had said. Her lips parted as she tried to speak, her voice thick and stumbling over the words.

Tara said, "W-Willow… you… you feel like the other half of my soul. Like… like God took one piece of heaven and split it perfectly between us. Every time you touch me, I remember where I came from. You… you make me feel like I’m home."

Willow lifted both hands slowly and cupped Tara’s face in her palms, her thumbs brushing the tears off Tara’s cheeks. Tara leaned into the touch immediately, her eyes fluttering half closed and a soft breath slipping out between her parted lips as she pressed her face into Willow’s hands.

They leaned in at the same moment. The first kiss was slow and familiar, their lips brushing softly and then pressing with tender depth. Willow’s thumbs stroked along Tara’s cheekbones while Tara’s hands came up to Willow’s waist and her fingers curled into the fabric of her top to pull her closer.

Tara answered by tangling one hand in Willow’s red hair while the other slid up Willow’s back, stroking along her spine.

She bit gently there while her right hand spread flat over the front of Tara’s blouse and pressed Tara firmly against her own body, and Tara’s back arched into the press with a soft gasp as her free hand gripped Willow’s shoulder.

She lifted it slowly, inch by inch, as they went on kissing, the soft earthy fabric bunching against Tara’s thighs.

CHAPTER 8 The Blood and the Shimmer

A harsh school bell rang out over the Sunnydale High cafeteria. The midday light through the windows mixed with the hard fluorescent glare overhead, and the room was a deafening mess of clattering trays, squeaking sneakers, and a hundred teenage conversations talking over one another.

Dawn stood near the end of the lunch line holding a bright plastic tray with a very unappealing slice of square pizza on it, the stolen silver clam shell hairpin still tucked behind her ear. She looked bored, while her eyes kept going back to the double doors at the entrance.

Finally the doors swung open, and Josh walked in wearing his letterman jacket, laughing easily with three of his friends from the football team. Dawn’s whole posture changed. She perked up with a bright, excited smile, the tiny pearl in her hair winking once as she moved.

She ditched her spot in the line and her tray along with it, and wove through the sea of tables with laser focus, stepping past backpacks and sneakers. She intercepted the boys just as they sat down at a central table and slid smoothly into the empty chair right next to Josh.

Dawn said, "Hey!"

Josh smiled at her, casual and easy. He said, "Hey, Dawnie. We still on for tonight?"

Dawn had already told him, in a breathless rush between classes that morning, that he could probably just crash on the couch afterward, which was something she had not mentioned to Willow or Tara at all.

Dawn beamed back at him. She said, "Totally. Everyone at the house knows about it and it's cool."

She reached up to tuck a stray lock of hair behind her ear, wanting to look perfect. As her fingers brushed against the silver clam shell hairpin she had taken from Buffy’s vanity, the sharp metal edge of the clasp caught the pad of her index finger and sliced the skin open, just a tiny, paper-cut nick.

Dawn didn’t even wince, she was so focused on Josh’s eyes. But a single drop of deep red blood welled up on her fingertip, and as she drew her hand away, it smeared right across the tiny pearl hidden inside the silver shell.

The pearl pulsed, for it was a Siren’s Tear, though Dawn had no way of knowing that. A faint green and purple glow flickered through the hair around the pin, iridescent and wrong and almost pretty, and then it vanished. An invisible wave of heat burst out of the hairpin, bending the air into a rippling shimmer like heat coming off asphalt. It spread outward in a perfect circle, rolling over trays and tables and bodies until it had washed across the entire cafeteria, and the roar of conversation cut off in the middle of a syllable.

The effect was terrifying. At the table, Josh stopped mid-sentence and his eyes locked onto Dawn, going wide as his pupils swelled. At the next table, two boys arguing over a comic book stopped talking, and their heads snapped toward her at exactly the same moment. Across the room a jock froze in the middle of a laugh, and a nerd bent over his homework looked up.

Behind the counter, the lunch ladies only watched, entirely unaffected and entirely unbothered. One of them went right on scooping corn, as if she had seen far worse in this building.

Within three seconds the din of the cafeteria had died into a heavy, dead silence, because every single boy in the place, dozens of them, was staring at Dawn with unblinking, obsessive focus.

The girls in the cafeteria looked around in total confusion, and the silence held for two more seconds before outrage set in. At a table near the windows, a girl smacked her boyfriend on the arm, and he didn’t even flinch, only kept on staring. At the center table the head cheerleader, a thin, vicious blonde in a sporty track jacket, snapped her fingers hard in front of her linebacker boyfriend’s face, livid.

The cheerleader said, "Hey, meat bag! I am right here! Hello?! Are you having a stroke?"

He ignored her completely and slowly stood up from the table, his eyes locked on Dawn. Dawn shrank back in her seat and patted at her arms and her chest and touched her face, desperately checking whether she had grown a second head or her clothes had fallen off.

Dawn said, "What's going on? Guys... you're creeping me out."

Beside her, Josh slowly reached out and took hold of her hand. Dawn flinched, but he didn’t let go. He brought her hand to his chest and pressed her palm flat over his heart, his eyes wide and glassy, and his voice came out breathless and intensely serious.

Josh said, "I never realized it before, Dawn. You are the sun. You are the only thing that actually exists."

Dawn tried to pull her hand back, but his grip was iron-tight. She said, "Josh, seriously, let go—"

Josh leaned in closer and whispered fiercely, "Tell me what you want. I'll do anything. I'll tear this entire school down to the foundation if you ask me to. I'll kill for you."

All around the cafeteria, dozens of chairs scraped back against the linoleum at once, a wave of sound like a flock of birds taking off wrong. Every teenage boy in the room was on his feet, trays forgotten and homework abandoned, while behind the counter the lunch ladies watched with folded arms. The boys all began walking slowly, methodically, and silently toward Dawn’s table like a horde of lovestruck zombies, their sneakers squeaking almost in unison on the waxed floor.

CHAPTER 9 The Brawl and the Promise

The mob of boys was closing in with blank, obsessive eyes when a hand grabbed a fistful of Dawn’s hair from behind. It was the blonde cheerleader, absolutely furious. With one vicious yank she pulled Dawn’s chair straight over backward, and Dawn screamed as she tumbled out of it and hit the hard linoleum floor.

The moment she hit the ground, the boys snapped. The sight of the object of their devotion being attacked set off a primal, violent rage. Josh roared and tackled the linebacker boyfriend over a table while trays of food flew up into the air, and total, unhinged chaos broke loose. The boys weren’t just fighting the girls but brutally fighting each other to get closer to where Dawn had fallen, fists flying and tables flipping and girls screaming as sneakers stomped all around her.

Dawn scrambled onto her hands and knees, her face pale with terror, and crawled frantically through the forest of stomping sneakers and shattered plastic trays, her heart pounding in her ears.

She spotted a small, heavy metal maintenance hatch set low in the cinderblock wall, beside a red fire hose station and the fire alarm pull. She lunged for it, grabbed the heavy latch, and yanked with everything she had.

The hatch flew open, but not because of her. Someone had pulled it from the inside. A black combat boot stepped out of the darkness and planted itself on the bottom lip of the hatch, and the familiar worn leather of a black duster came into view. A pale hand reached out of the shadows of the boiler room toward her, while a dry, amused voice drawled out of the dark.

Spike said, "Well, that didn’t take long. School's back in session at the Hellmouth, I see. Come on, Little Bit."

Dawn didn’t hesitate. She grabbed Spike’s hand, cool fingers closing firm around hers, and he hauled her in through the narrow hatch as if she weighed nothing. He slammed the heavy iron door shut just as three bloodied boys crashed into it from the outside, with a triple metallic boom that shook dust down off the boiler pipes.

Inside, the boiler room was dark except for the faint orange glow of a pilot light and the thin light creeping through the ventilation grates. It was hot and humid and loud with rushing water and hissing steam pipes. The boys outside pounded frantically on the metal hatch, bang, bang, bang, and Spike didn’t even flinch.

He reached up and grabbed two thick steel plumbing brackets bolted to the wall beside the door. With one sharp grunt he bent the bars down and twisted them over the hatch’s locking wheel, making a barricade that would hold, at least for a while.

Then he turned to Dawn, and the cool, mocking look dropped off his face all at once. He stepped in close, and his sharp eyes went over her face, her arms, the way she was standing, his nostrils flaring as his vampire senses caught something, while the steam hissed behind him and the hatch kept booming under the boys’ fists.

Spike said, "I smell blood. Are you hurt? Where?"

His hands hovered over her shoulders and arms without quite taking hold, checking her for injuries as if she might break. Dawn was overwhelmed by the chaos outside and the magic of the pin and now Spike’s fierce attention. She stepped back away from him and threw her hands up.

Dawn said, "Stop! I'm fine! It was just my finger! I just cut my finger!"

She held up her trembling hand. Spike looked at the tiny smeared cut on her index finger, and the tension went out of him a fraction of an inch. Dawn looked around the cramped, filthy boiler room and then back at him, her chest still heaving.

Dawn said, "What are you even doing down here? Are you following me?"

Spike stared at her in the dim light. The cruel sarcasm he usually wore like armor was gone completely. He looked tired and ancient and deeply serious, and his voice came out low and rough.

Spike said, "You think I am ever really away from you? I'm not."

He took one slow step toward her, careful not to make her feel trapped, but needing her to hear him.

Spike said, "I promised I would always be there to protect you. To never let anything... happen to you again. Like before. With Glory."

He looked down for a second as a shadow of guilt crossed his face, and then he met her eyes again with absolute conviction burning in his.

Spike said, "I may be evil, Dawn. But I am still a man. And a man keeps his word."

Dawn stared at him, and the fear slowly drained out of her, replaced by the aching realization of just how much this soulless vampire cared about her. Her eyes softened as tears pricked at their corners, while outside the pounding on the hatch went on, and inside the only sound was the steady hiss of the steam pipes.

CHAPTER 10 Techmo and the Texas Ticket

The Sunnydale commercial construction site sat under a dusty midday sun, earthmovers rumbling in the background and the air thick with the smell of hot asphalt and sawdust. A sleek convertible that looked completely out of place pulled up to the chain-link fence.

Anya stepped out of it, fully committed to what she believed was proper construction-wife attire: tight denim cut-off shorts, heavy scuffed leather work boots, a bright orange crop top, dark sunglasses, and a strand of red pearl-like beads around her neck, her lips painted a fierce deep crimson. She grabbed a brown paper bag and a thermos off the passenger seat, ignoring the dust blowing against her legs, and strutted in through the open gate.

The whole site seemed to pause at once. Half a dozen guys in hard hats stopped what they were doing. A loud, appreciative wolf-whistle cut through the noise of the machines as they watched her long legs pick their way across the uneven dirt, the red beads bouncing at her throat.

Across the site, Xander stood near the foreman’s trailer going over a clipboard, with a plastic cup of ice water tipped against his mouth. When he spotted Anya, he completely forgot how swallowing worked. The ice water poured straight back out of his mouth, dribbling down his chin and soaking the front of his grey work shirt in a spreading dark stain until the cup was empty. He just kept staring.

A big, burly framer named Mac walked past and slapped him hard on the back, laughing.

Mac said, "You're a lucky son of a bitch, Harris. Don't drown yourself before lunch."

Xander coughed and wiped his chin with the back of his hand, completely mesmerized, as Anya marched right up to him. She announced it loudly over the roar of a bulldozer.

Anya said, "I have brought you sustenance! And I am wearing boots, so I comply with the Occupational Safety and Health Administration regulations!"

Xander didn’t say a word. He grabbed her hand, yanked open the door of the mobile office trailer, and pulled her inside. The door slammed shut behind them and cut off the noise of the site, leaving the dim, cluttered little trailer lit only by its small windows. Before Anya could even hold out the paper bag, Xander backed her up against the flimsy paneled wall and crashed his lips down onto hers, tasting dust and her lipstick.

It was fast and hard and desperate. The lunch bag slipped out of Anya’s hands and hit the linoleum with a heavy thud while the thermos rolled away under the desk. Her sunglasses got knocked crooked, so she shoved them up on top of her head and matched him kiss for kiss, her hands diving into his dusty hair to pull him closer.

She jumped up and wrapped her long legs tight around his waist, her boots locking at the ankles behind him and the red beads pressed between them. Xander groaned and gripped her thighs and pushed her harder against the wall until the paneling flexed, the two of them heated and breathless and completely out of control, mouths hungry and hips pressed together, lost in it.

Three hard knocks rattled the trailer door, and they both froze. The foreman’s voice came muffled through it.

The foreman said, "Harris! Line four! It's Techmo from Texas! Sounds important!"

Xander’s eyes snapped wide open, and the haze was gone in an instant, replaced by sheer panic, because Techmo was the huge, top-tier construction conglomerate.

Anya whispered urgently, still clinging to him, "Texas? Who is Texas?!"

Xander was hyperventilating a little. Anya slid down off him, grabbed a napkin off the messy desk, and frantically scrubbed the smeared red lipstick off his mouth and chin, then smoothed down his collar with both hands.

He stumbled over to the cluttered desk, his hand shaking slightly as he picked up the heavy desk phone and punched the blinking button for line four. He cleared his throat, trying to make his voice sound deep and professional.

Xander said, "Xander Harris speaking."

There was a long pause while he listened to the voice on the other end, nodding slowly. A bead of sweat ran down his temple as his eyes widened in absolute terror.

Xander said, "Sir... I... I don't know. That's... that's so far away. That would basically be starting a whole new life."

Anya stopped straightening her crop top and stared at him, perfectly still. Xander swallowed hard.

Xander said, "Yes, sir. I understand, sir. I... I will let you know before Monday morning. Thank you."

He lowered the receiver slowly back into the cradle, and the click sounded as loud and final as a gavel in the small trailer. He turned to look at Anya as if he had just seen a ghost, his work shirt still damp from the ice water and a faint smear of lipstick left at the corner of his mouth. His voice came out hollow.

Xander said, "That was Techmo. They... they want to hire me. An Assistant Supervisor position. For a massive project in Abilene, Texas. It's called... Green Glass."

Anya stared at him. For a woman who had spent a thousand years thriving on the chaos of vengeance, the crushing, ordinary reality of a human move across the country hit her like a freight train. All the color drained out of her face, her red lips parted, and for the first time in her life she had nothing at all to say.

CHAPTER 11 Chivalry and Sewage

Back in the boiler room, only minutes later, the heavy metal hatch was buckling inward under the lovestruck boys slamming against it again and again, and Spike’s bent brackets were starting to give. Dawn was backed up against the wall in a panic.

Spike looked down, and his eyes locked onto a rusted iron drainage grate set into the concrete floor. He kicked it hard with his combat boot, shattering the rusted bolts, and heaved the heavy iron square out of the way. A foul, dank smell rose up out of the darkness below. He swept one hand toward the hole.

Spike said, "Ladies first."

Dawn looked down into the pitch-black hole, then back at the buckling hatch. Then she held her breath and dropped down into the dark.

She landed on a narrow, dry brick ledge that ran along the tunnel wall, just above the sludge. Spike followed right behind her, splashing down into the muck beside the ledge, and reached up to drag the grate back over the opening just as the boiler room hatch finally gave way above them.

They were in a dim sewer tunnel, with faint light filtering down through the grates overhead and water dripping and echoing somewhere along the pipes. Ankle-deep sludge rolled slowly past Spike’s boots. The smell hit like a wall, rot and rust and wet earth.

Dawn gagged at once and pinched her nose, trying to keep her lavender top and denim jacket away from the dripping, moss-covered brick, her white sneakers one wrong step from ruined on the slimy ledge.

Dawn said through her pinched nose, "Oh my god. It smells like actual death down here! My clothes are going to be ruined!"

Spike looked at the murky water flowing ahead of them, then at Dawn’s clean sneakers. Without a word he stepped up to the ledge, scooped her up into his arms, and carried her bridal-style against his leather duster, so her sneakers stayed well clear of the sludge while his own boots sank deeper. Dawn yelped in surprise and threw her arms around his neck so she wouldn’t drop into the muck.

Dawn said, "Hey! What are you doing?"

Spike waded forward into the water, completely unbothered. He said, "Can't have you smelling like the Hellmouth's plumbing for your big date, can we, Little Bit? Besides, these boots have seen worse. Keep your feet up."

He waded deeper, with the sludge sucking at his boots. Dawn relaxed a little against his shoulder, her face scrunched up against the smell but her eyes soft with gratitude. She was deeply touched that he was trudging through the city’s sewage just to keep her outfit clean.

It was a long way home under Sunnydale. Above them the afternoon burned on, bright and deadly, and Spike kept to the tunnels the whole way, turning down one dark passage after another, the only road through town that kept him out of the daylight. Hours slipped by in the dripping dark.

Late in the afternoon, the sun hung low over a quiet suburban street a few blocks from Revello Drive, stretching long orange shadows across the pavement. Beneath it, a heavy manhole cover lifted and slid aside under one pale hand.

Spike stayed down in the shaft, well back in the shadow, where the low slanting light couldn’t reach him. He lifted Dawn up through the opening with both arms, and she scrambled out onto the warm pavement. The sunlight fell across her, and not an inch of it touched him.

She checked her clothes and found herself spotless. Below her, Spike was covered in sewer muck from the knees down. He jerked his chin toward the road.

Spike said, "Right then. Off you go."

Dawn looked down at his ruined boots and back up at him, genuinely grateful.

Dawn said, "Spike... thank you."

Spike just gave her a sharp two-finger salute from the shadows. Then he reached up, dragged the heavy cover back over the hole above his head, and the manhole scraped shut, closing him safely into the dark before the last of the sun could find him.

Dawn took the long way home, slipping through back streets and alleys and cutting across quiet yards to stay clear of any boys who might still be looking for her. But the silver clam shell hairpin was still tucked behind her ear, and by the time the sun had set, they had caught her scent like hounds, a block from Revello Drive.

CHAPTER 12 The Normal Penis Protocol

At dusk, Lucian was stepping out the front door onto the Summers front porch, adjusting the collar of a dark structured jacket he had pulled on over a clean black shirt from his duffel, dark embroidery winding along its open collar and cuffs. His long dark hair was still damp, and the silver raven buckle caught the last of the light. The two of them had only just come out of their second shower of the day.

Buffy followed him out onto the porch in a fresh fitted grey t-shirt and dark jeans, looking incredibly relaxed, her own hair wet too and hanging in damp waves over her shoulders. She leaned against the doorframe.

Buffy said, "You're sure you have to go to work? I could probably convince you to call in sick."

Lucian smiled and leaned in to kiss her. He said, "As tempting as that is, my boss is an artificial intelligence mainframe. It doesn't really understand the concept of sick days. I'll see you tomorrow."

Just as they broke the kiss, Xander’s beat-up construction truck came screeching up to the curb. Xander killed the engine and sat there looking pale and shell-shocked from the Techmo call, while Anya, who had left her convertible back at the site, hopped out of the passenger side, just as tense, and marched up the front walk. Hearing the squealing tires, Willow and Tara stepped out onto the porch behind Buffy to see what was going on.

Anya stopped at the bottom of the porch stairs and looked up. She looked at Lucian, and she looked at Lucian’s wet hair, and then she looked at Buffy’s wet hair hanging over her fresh t-shirt, and the math put itself together with devastating Anya clarity. The Texas panic disappeared from her face, replaced by pure, unfiltered triumph. She flung her arm out and pointed straight at Lucian, the red beads swinging, thrilled.

Anya said, "See! A normal penis, just like I said! Isn't that wonderful!"

Total silence fell over the front yard, and one bird chirped, inappropriately cheerful. Willow let out a long, exhausted sigh and slowly covered her whole face with her hand. Tara didn’t say a word. She just looked at Buffy with a very small, deeply amused smile on her lips, her eyes warm with private laughter she was too polite to let out.

Lucian froze on the top step with one hand still half raised from adjusting his jacket. A faint flush crept up his neck, not from shame but from the sheer absurdity of it, and he slowly turned his head and made direct eye contact with Xander, who had just walked up behind Anya. His jaw worked once, but he didn’t defend himself. He only waited for the chaos to pass.

Xander, still distracted by his own life crisis, nodded along automatically with Anya and moved his hands up and down in weighing gestures, as if he were explaining basic math.

Xander said, "Yes. Normal penis good. Demon penis bad."

Then he stopped and blinked as his brain finally caught up with the words that had just left his mouth. He looked at Lucian’s wet hair, and he looked at Buffy, and his eyes went wide.

Xander said, "Wait. What? Lucian? And the Buffster?"

He stared for a long, awkward second, then slowly raised both hands and pointed finger guns at them.

Xander said, "Oh. Okay... Good for you guys."

Before Buffy could even begin to recover from the embarrassment, a low, unnatural rumble started echoing down the street, like a stampede of wild animals mixed with frantic, heavy breathing. The bushes at the side of the house rustled hard, and Dawn came sprinting across the front lawn, completely out of breath and terrified.

Dawn yelled, "Lock the doors! We have to lock the doors right now!"

CHAPTER 13 The Siege and the Shield

Dawn didn’t even slow down. She blew past Buffy, Lucian, Anya, and Xander, sprinted up the porch stairs, and practically dove through the open front door.

Dawn screamed over her shoulder, "In! Everybody get inside right now!"

Buffy frowned and stepped to the edge of the porch to look down the street. A loud, collective roar of teenage angst and pounding sneakers was echoing through the quiet neighborhood. Around the corner came a huge wave of high school boys sprinting down Revello Drive with wide, manic eyes. Some of them were vaulting fences and others shoving each other out into the street, all of them drawn to the house like hounds on a scent.

Buffy’s eyes went wide, and she grabbed a fistful of Lucian’s jacket.

Buffy said, "Inside. Now."

Xander and Anya didn’t need to be told twice and scrambled up the stairs. Buffy shoved Lucian through the door. He stumbled one step and caught himself on the entryway table without knocking anything over, then turned straight back toward the window as if he were already measuring the threat. Buffy jumped inside after him, slammed the heavy wooden door, and threw the deadbolt just as the first body hit the porch outside with a heavy thud.

They were all crowded into the entryway. Outside, the house was surrounded in an instant, hands slapping frantically against the windows and muffled, obsessed voices beginning to chant Dawn’s name.

Willow pushed past Xander with her face gone pale. She raised both hands toward the front door, closed her eyes, and started chanting rapidly in Latin, her fingers trembling. The air in the living room crackled with a sharp smell of ozone. A faint, translucent golden dome swelled out from Willow’s hands, passing through the walls and windows until it had closed over the whole property, roof and porch and lawn. Outside, the boys were shoved back a few feet and pressed up against an invisible wall, their palms flat against nothing.

Willow dropped her hands as though she had just sprinted a mile, sweat beading on her brow and upper lip. She swayed and caught herself on the coat rack, her eyes wide with real panic at how much the spell had taken out of her.

Willow said, "Okay. The shield is up. But... but it's not going to hold for long. An hour, maybe two. That's... that's my limit right now."

Buffy looked at the groaning windowpanes and then back at Willow. She said, "An hour?! Willow, look at them! If that shield drops, they are going to tear this place apart! The house is going to get destroyed again!"

Willow snapped, the fear of her missing magic flaring up into sudden, defensive anger.

Willow yelled, "I am doing my best, Buffy! I don't know why, but the juice just isn't flowing! What do you want from me?!"

The room went dead silent. Willow’s outburst hung in the air, so unlike her that for a fraction of a second it drowned out the mob outside. Then Xander, Anya, Tara, Buffy, and Lucian all slowly turned their heads at the same time toward the one person cowering near the staircase, the one who had come home with half the boys in Sunnydale on her heels.

They all said in perfect unison, "What did you do?"

CHAPTER 14 The Siege of Revello Drive

The translucent golden shield around the house flickered wildly, and Willow’s knees suddenly buckled as the golden light fizzled out with a sound like a blown fuse. She went completely limp, her eyes rolling back as she fainted from magical exhaustion. Xander and Tara lunged forward together and caught her by the arms just before she hit the hardwood floor.

Tara said in a panic, "I've got her!"

Tara hooked her arms under Willow’s shoulders and, with surprising strength, dragged her backward into the cramped, dark hallway coat closet, where only thin slivers of light came through the louvered door. She held Willow up against the heavy winter coats and cradled her face in both hands, her voice desperate and stuttering.

Tara said, "C-c-c-com... come on, sweetie, we need you. Come back to me."

In the living room, without the shield, the sheer weight of the mob pressed against the house until the big front bay window shattered inward in an explosion of glass. Two huge linebackers on the porch hoisted a small, terrified-looking freshman boy up into the air and threw him like a lawn dart straight through the broken window. The kid sailed across the room and landed perfectly, face first and incredibly softly, in the middle cushions of the floral couch, bounced once, and lay there knocked out cold, snoring lightly.

Xander stared at the kid on the couch, then at the horde pouring in through the window and the splintering front door. The fear went out of his face, replaced by the battle-tested confidence of a man who had survived this exact thing before, and his voice boomed across the room.

Xander said, "Okay, people, listen up! I have survived a magically induced female stampede, and the rules are the same! Keep them moving, use their momentum against them, and protect the target! No lethal force!"

Buffy grabbed an oak dining chair and snapped the legs off it as if they were breadsticks, keeping two to use as blunt escrima sticks, and sighed.

Buffy said, "Why can't they ever just be normal monsters? Humans break too easily!"

Anya had armed herself with a heavy brass candlestick off the mantel. She looked at the snarling, hormone-crazed boys crawling in through the window and spoke in complete seriousness.

Anya said, "Well, they are teenage boys. Those are monsters, right?"

Xander paused in the middle of his fighting stance and looked at her, a small chuckle escaping him.

Xander said, "Honey, your mind just... ooo... I love you. Later! Fight now!"

The front door finally gave way and crashed off its hinges, and the mob swarmed in. The living room turned into a chaotic, non-lethal brawl, sneakers screeching on hardwood, fists thudding into drywall, furniture scraping over, glass from the bay window glittering across the rug underfoot.

Lucian used his height and leverage, grabbing two aggressive jocks by their letterman jackets and simply pivoting to toss them smoothly back out through the shattered window into the rose bushes. He planted himself between the broken window and the staircase landing, blocking the straight path to Dawn without leaving Buffy’s side open. When a third boy lunged, Lucian caught him by the belt and steered him into Xander’s wrestling hold instead of throwing him into the furniture. The seam at the shoulder of his jacket tore with the motion, and his damp hair stuck to his sweaty forehead.

Xander was basically wrestling, wrapping boys up in thick-armed holds and heaving them into the drywall with practiced construction-site leverage, grunting with every throw as the plaster cracked and picture frames fell.

Buffy was a blur of precision, with drywall dust streaking her sleeves. She wasn’t punching. She used open-palm strikes to the chest and clean sweeps to take their legs out, dropping teenagers to the floor left and right without breaking a single bone. The chair legs cracked against forearms and shins with controlled force, all economy and placement and no wasted motion.

Suddenly Josh burst through the crowd, hyper-focused, his eyes glassy and locked on Dawn, who was backed up against the bottom of the staircase, and he charged at her like a bull. Buffy stepped perfectly into his path without even shifting her stance. As he lunged, she threw a single, lightning-fast, carefully controlled punch that cracked into him just a fraction of an inch above his right eyebrow.

Josh’s eyes rolled up into his head. His momentum carried him forward while his knees gave out, and he collapsed onto the living room rug, out cold but breathing steadily. Buffy looked at the sheer number of boys still pouring in.

Buffy shouted, "Too many! Upstairs!"

She grabbed Dawn by the back of her denim jacket and hauled her up the stairs and into Dawn’s bedroom, slamming the door and throwing her whole weight against it just as three heavy bodies slammed into it from the hallway. The wood groaned and the hinges started to bend. Dawn backed up against her vanity with both hands over her mouth.

Dawn said, "Buffy, the door is going to break!"

Buffy was straining, her Slayer strength tested hard by the dead weight of a pile of obsessed teenagers pushing from the other side. As she looked back over her shoulder at her sister, her eyes locked on the side of Dawn’s head.

Tucked neatly behind Dawn’s ear, forgotten in all the chaos, was the silver clam shell hairpin, and the tiny pearl inside it was glowing faintly with a sickly green and purple light. Buffy’s eyes narrowed as it all came together, and she gritted her teeth against the door.

Buffy said, "Dawn. Do not move."

Buffy deliberately eased off the pressure for one split second, and the door buckled inward just enough. In that instant she reached out, snatched the hairpin out of Dawn’s hair, and let the door fly all the way open, so that four boys stumbled in and fell to the floor.

Buffy’s boot came down hard, heel first, with all her Slayer weight and no hesitation. The silver clam shell crunched flat against the floorboards, the glowing pearl inside shattering into glittering dust that scattered across the wood like crushed glass as the sickly light died in a spark.

A visible wave of green and purple energy burst out of the crushed pin and ripped through the bedroom, the hallway, the stairwell, and the whole house, blowing the curtains and lifting the dust. Every lovestruck mind in the building snapped clear at once. Buffy looked down at the mess under her boot, completely deadpan.

Buffy said, "See. It’s always the shiny."

Downstairs the obsession died all at once. The boys froze mid-lunge, mid-punch, and halfway through the broken window, and their glassy expressions collapsed into dawning confusion. A few of them looked around as if they had just woken from a nightmare. One linebacker blinked down at his own bloody knuckles and then at the crooked couch, quietly horrified.

The linebacker said, "…What the hell is going on?"

Across the room several boys slowly backed away from the staircase, looking embarrassed and a little sick, and a couple of them started helping their dazed friends up off the floor. Nobody wanted to meet anybody’s eyes, and the frantic energy drained out of the house in seconds. Buffy came down the stairs.

Buffy said in a low, exhausted voice, "Everybody out."

The boys didn’t argue. They shuffled awkwardly out through the broken bay window and the ruined front door, muttering apologies and avoiding the Scoobies’ stares, and disappeared into the dusk. The house fell strangely quiet except for the settling of debris, the tick of a cooling pipe, and one unconscious freshman still snoring softly on the floral couch in the middle of the wreckage.

Xander rubbed his jaw and looked around at it all. He said, "Well… that was new."

Tara’s voice came from the hallway. Tara said, "Willow? Sweetie, can you hear me?"

In the coat closet, Tara was crouched down, cradling Willow’s face gently in her hands. Willow’s eyes fluttered open, unfocused and glassy, her voice weak and disoriented.

Willow said, "…Did we win?"

Tara let out a soft breath of relief and smoothed Willow’s hair back. She said, "We did. You did. Come on, let’s get you some water."

She carefully helped Willow to her feet, and Willow swayed unsteadily as the two of them shuffled toward the kitchen together.

The damage in the living room was extensive. Broken glass lay across the floor in glittering fans from the bay window. The furniture was overturned, the floral couch knocked crooked, a lamp lying on its side, and picture frames face down. In the kitchen, the crack from the morning’s bedroom activity had spread across the ceiling, white dust still drifting down, and the whole house smelled of drywall and sweat and trampled rose bushes.

Xander stood in the middle of the destruction, turning slowly in a circle with one hand still holding his sore jaw. He was adding up the cost of every broken window and splintered chair leg in his head while his work boots crunched on the glass, his dusty shirt torn at one shoulder from wrestling teenagers.

Anya walked over to him, brushing drywall dust off her crop top, and spoke quietly.

Anya said, "What kind of spell does that? One minute they’re normal disgusting teenage boys, the next they’re a horny zombie horde."

Xander muttered, still turning, "I’m gonna need a lot of lumber…"

Buffy walked over to the couch, stepped carefully around Josh where he lay unconscious on the rug, and sank down onto the cushions, pulling her knees up tight to her chest and wrapping her arms around them. The adrenaline was gone, and the exhaustion and the old familiar weight were creeping back in.

Lucian watched her for a moment, then walked over without a word. He peeled off his rumpled jacket with its torn shoulder seam, dropped it over the back of the couch, and sat down beside her. He didn’t crack a joke, and he didn’t try to fix anything.

Buffy stayed curled up for a long moment. Then she slowly let her breath out, and the tension in her shoulders eased, and she turned her head, leaned in, and breathed him in deeply with her eyes closed.

Lucian opened his arms at once, and Buffy slid into them and pressed her face against his chest. He wrapped her up tight and held her in complete silence, one hand gently stroking her damp hair and the other resting flat between her shoulder blades. It was steady pressure, with no patting and no fixing, until her breathing slowed and her body relaxed all the way into the hold. They stayed like that while the others moved quietly around the ruined living room.

Xander turned toward Buffy, his voice tired but sincere. He said, "Hey, Buff, look, I’ll pay for all of this. The windows, the door, the drywall — everything. No charge."

Dawn stood near the bottom of the stairs looking nervous, and from the couch, still wrapped in Lucian’s arms, Buffy lifted her head slightly, waiting.

At the foot of the couch, Josh had finally started to stir. He slowly sat up among the wreckage, pressing an ice pack someone had handed him against his swelling eye, drywall dust whitening his letterman jacket. He blinked at the broken bay window, then at Dawn, somehow hopeful despite the bruise blooming above his eyebrow.

Josh said groggily, "So… uh… it’s still cool that I spend the night, right? Like Dawn said?"

The entire room went dead silent. Glass ticked as it settled, and the unconscious freshman at the other end of the couch snored once, oblivious.

Every head slowly turned toward Dawn: Buffy from inside Lucian’s arms, Xander in the middle of his cost estimate, Anya with her hand stopped mid-brush on her crop top, Willow swaying on Tara’s arm in the kitchen doorway, and Lucian with one eyebrow lifting. They all said in perfect, exasperated unison, "DAWN!"
