Taking the Plunge Part 2

Download this script (.txt)

Buffy the Vampire Slayer. Season six, episode 14. Taking the Plunge Part 2.

PREVIOUSLY ON...

Absolute silence hung over the dark, and then Anya’s upbeat, slightly blunt, unmistakable voice cut straight through it.

Anya said, "Last time on Buffy the Vampire Slayer..."

Memory hit in rapid flashes.

In the police station basement, Willow and Buffy stood in a cinderblock hallway while the translucent blue perception filter around them glitched hard and shattered into sparks. Willow clutched her temple, wincing from the phantom pain of the memory wipe.

Willow said, "Oh... wow. Vertigo."

In the Summers living room Xander looked nauseous as he pointed at the television, where police tape stretched across the beach.

Xander whispered, "Shed... human skin. At the beach."

Buffy’s eyes went dark.

Buffy said flatly, "Fish men."

Back in the police station corridor Willow’s hand popped the cork off a small glass vial and a sickly orange glow spilled from the liquid inside. She knocked the potion back, and fiery orange veins webbed up her neck and over her cheeks, burning bright beneath her skin before they faded.

In Joyce’s car the rearview mirror caught Willow’s pale face. She was breathing hard, and a thick drop of dark red blood ran from her right nostril before she wiped it away with the back of her trembling hand.

In the sea cave out on the north rock island a terrified college girl was pinned to the jagged rock by thick, glossy, hardened dark-green glue.

The girl said through her sobs, "Please... someone help me..."

The massive bone-white Alpha fish-man stepped onto the shore, his eyes glowing a piercing intelligent crimson in the dark, and he let out a guttural clicking purr.

In the Summers living room Willow paced in front of the couch and blurted out the science.

Willow said, "Their leader isn’t just killing those college kids. It’s actively converting them to multiply the brood."

She stopped mid-sentence. Her eyes fluttered shut, her knees buckled, and she collapsed sideways onto the floral sofa, out cold.

Buffy held Dawn’s translucent flip phone to her ear and stared at the heavy wooden weapons chest in the corner, a determined little smirk pulling at her mouth.

Buffy said, "Alright. Let’s go fishing."

A massive broadsword snicked free of a leather scabbard, and then it all went black.

CHAPTER 1 The Bridal Blindspot and the Broken Web

In the Summers master bedroom early that Saturday evening, the sharp, rhythmic click of a mascara wand against its plastic tube layered over a heated, one-sided argument. Outside the window the last of the daylight was draining out of the sky. Tara stood near the wooden footboard of the bed with her arms crossed tight over her chest, stressed to the bone in a long earthy skirt and a soft top. Anya leaned close to the bright bathroom mirror in a vibrant, expensive-looking evening party dress and worked dark eyeliner along her lash line with expert care.

Tara gestured with both hands, stuttering slightly as she tried to make Anya see sense.

Tara said, "Anya, I j-just don’t understand how you aren’t grasping the math here. The beach is a designated hazard zone!"

Anya did not look away from the mirror. She kept applying the makeup in sharp, efficient strokes, her jaw tight with stubborn determination.

Anya said, "The tiki bar is on the south end of the beach, Tara. Fish men historically prefer the north coves because of the tidal drag. Besides, the deposits are non-refundable, and Hallie RSVP’d the night Xander proposed. Do you know how hard it is to get a vengeance demon to commit to a casual social gathering without a blood sacrifice?"

Tara pinched the bridge of her nose, eyes squeezed shut, and the fear for everyone she loved sat heavy in her voice.

Tara said, "It doesn’t matter where the bar is! It’s a basic law of Sunnydale physics, Anya. Party, plus Slayer, equals monsters. You are throwing a bachelorette party in a literal feeding ground!"

Anya turned from the mirror and pointed the mascara wand at Tara like a weapon, her eyes flashing with fierce protectiveness over her wedding plans.

Anya said, "I am throwing a structurally sound, highly organized celebration of my impending, legally binding union to Xander. If a giant fish tries to ruin my open bar, I will personally rip its gills out and serve them as appetizers."

Willow lay under the covers in her sleep clothes, pale and emptied out from the collapse. To her the argument sounded muffled and far away, as if it had sunk underwater, and a low, vibrating hum took over. Her eyes darted hard beneath her pale lids in distressed REM sleep, and she sank down into her mindscape.

Inside that mindscape everything was pitch black, a sprawling, infinite void. Then glowing threads of energy began to light the dark in electric shades of deep purple, bright blue, and sharp cyan, hanging there like a vast nervous system or an intricate spider web. The web was badly damaged. Several of the cyan threads were snapped and sparking weakly, knitting themselves together with faint healing light. Right in the core of Willow’s magical pathways a massive, jagged hole of absolute nothing waited where the threads simply stopped, unable to cross the chasm. It was the physical scar of her memory wipe, the reason her magic kept fizzling out.

Willow’s consciousness floated near the edge of that hole as a faint, translucent projection of herself, her brow deeply furrowed in confusion and quiet terror. She reached toward the void and pulled back again, overwhelmed by an emptiness she could not explain. She stared into the dark, and she did not remember causing this or know why she was broken.

A violent flash of white light and a sharp gasp shattered the hum.

In the master bedroom Willow sat up hard, eyes flying open, and let out an exhausted groan. She pressed the heels of both pale palms against her eyes and dragged her hands slowly down over her cheeks, trying to rub the dream away. The muffling was gone, and the sharp, clear sound of the bedroom rushed back in.

Anya’s voice came from the bathroom doorway.

Anya said, "...and frankly, the floral arrangements alone cost more than Xander’s car!"

Willow turned her head slowly, pale and hollow-eyed, the black hole from the dream still sitting cold behind her eyes. She looked at Tara and Anya, and both women stopped dead and stared at her sitting up.

Tara and Anya said in perfect unison, "Willow!"

Anya and Tara blinked at each other for a split second, then looked back at Willow.

CHAPTER 2 The Steam Room Survivor and the Rupture

Downstairs in the Summers living room, night had fallen. The lamps were on, the plywood was still nailed over the bay window, and the lamplight fell across the dark, heavy weapons chest sitting open on the floor. Buffy sat on the left end of the floral sofa, geared for combat in a dark burgundy leather moto-jacket, black tactical cargo pants, and heavy black combat boots. Lucian sat close beside her in a tailored charcoal-grey overcoat over a black shirt with a dark embroidered collar, open at the throat. The small silver cross earring glinted against his long jet-black hair, and his bright aqua-blue eyes were steady on the room. Dawn sprawled on her stomach on the rug in an oversized faded t-shirt and flared jeans, a thick history textbook open in front of her. She scribbled furiously on a notepad and ignored the apocalypse. Xander paced between the coffee table and the television in an unbuttoned blue flannel over a plain white t-shirt, gesturing wildly as he told Lucian a story.

His face was flushed with horror and forced humor, eyes wide as he relived it, a nervous laugh trying to paper over the leftover trauma.

Xander said, "I am telling you, man, it was the steam. One minute I’m sitting in the locker room sauna, sweating out the absolute horror of high school PE, and the next thing I know, I am inhaling pure, unfiltered, aerosolized fish-steroids. It just bonded right to my lungs! I was practically craving kelp by third period."

Lucian sat perfectly still, aqua eyes locked on Xander with calm intensity as he processed it.

Lucian said, "Fascinating. So the mutagen is airborne as well as fluid-transmissible. And this... vapor... it didn’t leave any lasting biological markers on you?"

Xander shuddered slightly.

Xander said, "Just a lingering phobia of public pools and a very strict, zero-tolerance policy against sushi."

Dawn kept her head down but flipped the page with extra force, her face scrunched in disgust.

Dawn said, "Can we please not talk about aerosolized fish-juice while I am trying to find the geopolitical causes of the Cold War? It’s thoroughly grossing me out. Please and thank you."

The wooden stairs creaked slow and heavy. Everyone in the living room stopped and looked up.

Willow appeared on the staircase in an oversized, loose deep green peasant blouse, a dark denim midi skirt, bare legs, and soft cotton slippers. She came down one agonizing step at a time, one hand locked on the banister so hard her knuckles went white, pale and emptied of energy.

Buffy leaned forward at once, her voice thick with maternal concern.

Buffy said, "Will... hey. You look like you just went twelve rounds with a ghost. You shouldn’t be vertical right now."

Lucian did not speak. He only raised one dark eyebrow, sharp aqua eyes scanning Willow’s fragile state.

Xander dropped the comedy entirely, walked straight to the bottom of the stairs, and met her as she reached the last step. He wrapped his arms around her shoulders in a warm, fiercely protective bear hug, then pulled back just enough to press a gentle kiss to her forehead.

Xander said softly, "Glad you’re back in the land of the living, Supergirl. We were getting worried."

Willow took a deep, shaky breath, let go of the banister, and forced her spine straight. She shook off what she could of the exhaustion as she walked past him into the middle of the living room, rubbing her arms.

Willow said, "I’m okay. But... I need to tell you guys something before we go anywhere. I can’t be point on this one."

Buffy stood up from the couch.

Willow said, "I have a tear. Inside. It’s like... a localized metaphysical rupture in my magical pathways. A big one. It’s healing, but it means I need time before I can cast reliable magic again. If I try to pull too much ambient energy right now, the spell won’t just fizzle. It could completely backfire."

Buffy frowned and crossed her arms, deeply concerned.

Buffy said, "A tear? Will, you’re the most powerful witch in the western hemisphere. How does something like that even happen? Was it the hair pin that Dawn stole? The magic hair pin that I smashed, that made the wave afterwards?"

Willow looked down at the carpet, her voice dropping to a vulnerable whisper.

Willow said, "That’s the thing, Buffy... It happened before that. A week ago I just felt off and did not say anything to anyone. Then I started trying to use magic, and, well, you saw it when I tried to make the shield. Horny teenagers on a rampage? I fizzled out..."

She played slowly with her fingers in front of her.

Willow said, "That is why I took the magic pick-me-up... To force my magic to work at the police station. I can’t use that stuff again. It might kill me next time or make me unable to use magic for the rest of my life."

Buffy went to her, very slowly cupped the back of Willow’s head in both hands, and pressed her forehead to Willow’s in a warm, tender embrace.

Lucian’s expression shifted from calm observation to something deeply troubled. He knew what a volatile magic user could mean, and he did not like the blind spot.

Xander blinked rapidly, his brain stalling out on the magical jargon.

Xander said, "Wait. Rupture? Pathways? Fizzle? ...Huh?"

Before he could ask for a translation, footsteps sounded on the stairs again. Anya came down fully dressed for the bachelorette party in a shimmering emerald-green halter dress, sheer black pantyhose, and strappy black heels, her heavy wedding binder held against her chest like a shield. Tara walked close beside her in a flowing dark red bohemian skirt, a lace-trimmed cream peasant top, and a long brown suede vest. She glanced nervously between Willow and Buffy as they reached the living room.

Nobody in the room said anything for a long moment. They were bruised and worn out and running short on ideas, and somewhere out along the coast ten college kids were still missing.

Buffy turned her head slowly with an exhausted, deadpan look that took in the whole ridiculous weight of her life, and sighed.

Buffy said, "Well... if we are going to go... let’s do this."

She kicked the open lid of the weapons chest all the way back with a heavy metallic clang.

CHAPTER 3 The Firebird Arrival and the Sins of the Father

Outside the tiki bar the last of the dusk was long gone. The sky over the rolling ocean was deep black and scattered with stars, and the beachfront venue glowed with the warm yellow flicker of bamboo tiki torches and strings of cheap bulbs.

A deep, throaty muscle-car rumble rolled in, and a pristine black 1981 Pontiac Firebird Trans Am Special Edition pulled into the sandy lot, its gold trim catching the torchlight before the engine cut out. Lucian stepped from the driver’s side and walked around the front of the hood to open the passenger door. He had left the charcoal overcoat at the house, and under it he wore a sharp, tailored black suit over the black embroidered-collar shirt, open at the throat. His long jet-black hair was tied back at the nape, his bright aqua-blue eyes caught the torchlight, and the silver cross at his ear finished the look without effort.

Buffy stepped out in her old white Spring Fling dress, which she had changed into upstairs before they left, flowing and floor-length, stark against the heavy ankle-high black leather combat boots she had kept on from her patrol clothes. She wore blueish-grey eyeshadow and soft pastel pink lipstick, and a heavy dark canvas duffel of concealed weapons was slung over her shoulder. Lucian took her hand to help her out of the low car and did not let go. He lifted it and pressed a soft kiss to her knuckles while his other hand stroked the side of her face, and Buffy looked up at him with a genuine, soft smile breaking through the hardened exterior.

Anya had rolled the wedding rehearsal, her bachelorette party and Xander’s bachelor send-off into one night at the tiki bar to save the deposits, so the bar was chaos, awkwardly split between the loud sunburned Harris family in cheap floral shirts and Anya’s demon friends trying and failing to blend in. Anya, Willow, Tara, and Dawn clustered near the buffet, and as Buffy and Lucian walked toward the torchlight everyone turned. The loud chatter dropped into a dead stunned silence for three full seconds while they took in the pair of them.

Lucian felt the silence land. He did not straighten his jacket or pose. He only squeezed Buffy’s hand once with steady pressure and let her lead him forward through the staring faces. Then, like a record skipping back onto the track, the chaotic commotion resumed.

Buffy and Lucian walked into the crowd. Near the tiki bar Xander stood cornered by his father, Tony Harris, who was flushed red, clutching a cheap plastic cup of beer, and leaning aggressively into Xander’s space. His voice slurred with mean, bitter condescension.

Tony said, "You are making the biggest mistake of your entire pathetic life, Alexander. And you are too stupid to see it. You think marrying this... this weirdo is gonna fix you? You’re gonna crash and burn, and I’ll be right here to watch you do it."

Anya stood ten feet away, hands dropping to her sides, face falling into pure vulnerable hurt. Willow rolled her eyes hard and crossed her arms in disgust at Tony’s predictable cruelty. Tara stood silently, swallowing hard, her empathy drinking in the awful tension. Halfrek — Hallie — in a structured deep crimson halter dress narrowed her eyes, dropped her cigarette into the sand, and took a purposeful aggressive step toward Tony, fully prepared to rip his arms off.

Then Hallie stopped, because Xander’s posture had changed entirely; he stood tall, his face set like stone. Hallie saw the shift, slowly crossed her arms, and a wicked, deeply impressed smile spread across her face.

Xander shoved his index finger hard into his father’s chest. Tony lost his balance and stumbled back a step, beer sloshing over his hand. Xander’s eyes were burning with hard-won clarity as years of fear and anger crystallized into resolve.

Lucian kept Buffy’s hand in his but tracked Xander and Tony with his aqua eyes, measuring the father without intervening. When Xander shoved Tony back, Lucian gave one small approving nod only Buffy could catch.

Xander’s voice came out low and intensely steady, stripped of all his usual humor.

Xander said, "No. You don’t get to do this. You don’t get to project your miserable, pathetic failures onto me anymore."

Tony stared, too shocked by the physical push to speak, and Xander did not look away.

Xander said, "I am not like you. I will never be like you. And the truth is, I never was. I am not going to waste my life bouncing from one failure to the next just trying to barely survive. I am digging in. I am sticking through the hard things, and I am building something real. With her."

His voice rose with raw power, his face flushed and his body coiled, as he claimed his future over the sound of the ocean.

Xander said, "I am the man you never were, Dad. And I’m done being afraid of your shadow."

The ambient noise of the party died. The entire crowd, human and demon alike, went absolutely silent and stared at Xander and Tony, and the only sound left was the heavy crash of waves against the shore.

Tony Harris looked around at the silent crowd, then back at his son’s hardened unyielding face, and the bully in him broke. He turned without a single word and walked slowly away from the bright warm lights of the tiki bar, trudging alone up the dark sand toward the street, leaving his son behind him.

CHAPTER 4 The Anchors (The Toasts)

Inside the tiki bar the night had deepened into a velvety black over the ocean, and the space was lit only by the flickering orange of the tiki torches and the soft yellow of the string lights overhead. A small bamboo-framed stage sat at the corner of the dance floor with a single chrome microphone in the center. The air had shifted. Xander’s confrontation with his father had stripped away the awkward party tension and left a raw, honest quiet in its place. Human and demon guests sat at the round tables with ice clinking softly in their glasses.

Xander and Anya sat together at a small sweetheart table near the front, Xander holding Anya’s hand tightly on the tabletop.

Tara stood from her table and walked slowly but purposefully toward the small stage while the crowd watched in total silence. She stepped up to the microphone and gently wrapped both hands around the cold metal stand, and terror flickered across her face for a fraction of a second as she swallowed. Then she closed her eyes, and when she opened them the fear had melted into fierce, quiet certainty.

Her voice was soft but carried perfectly clear through the speakers, with no stutter at all.

Tara said, "Most of you know that I don’t... I don’t really take up a lot of space. I like to be quiet."

She looked directly at Anya.

Tara said, "But Anya doesn’t know how to do anything quietly. When I first met her, she terrified me. Because Anya doesn’t hide. If she wants something, she demands it. If she doesn’t understand the rules, she breaks them until they make sense. Anya taught me that being completely, brutally honest... is its own kind of magic."

Anya’s eyes welled up, and she squeezed Xander’s hand. Tara shifted her gaze to Xander.

Tara said, "And Xander... Xander is the bravest person I have ever met. Because he doesn’t have superpowers. He doesn’t have magic. But when the monsters show up, he is always the first one to stand in front of the people he loves. You two... you make each other incredibly brave."

The crowd stayed dead silent, captivated by the uncharacteristic power of Tara’s voice. Tara smiled softly, stepped away from the microphone, and walked back to her seat.

Buffy stood, and the white Spring Fling dress caught the firelight as she left her weapons bag under her chair and walked to the stage, her combat boots thudding softly on the wood. She slid the microphone out of the stand, her tired smile genuine and her eyes soft with old memories.

Buffy said, "When I first moved to Sunnydale, I was running. I just wanted to disappear. Xander was the very first person who made me want to stop running and actually stay. He treated me like a normal girl when the rest of the world expected me to be a soldier. And Anya... Anya literally gave up the entire universe just to be in the same room as him. You guys are the proof that the good guys actually get to win sometimes."

She raised an imaginary glass, winked at Xander, slid the microphone back into the stand, and walked off.

Willow stood slowly from her chair, still wrapped in the oversized green blouse, fragile and pale, and moved to the stage with her head up. She gripped the microphone stand the way Tara had, using it to support her weight. Her voice was barely above a whisper and raw with vulnerability.

Willow said, "Sometimes... things get broken. And you don’t know how to fix them. You feel completely empty. But looking at the two of you right now... it reminds me that you don’t have to fix everything by yourself. You just need someone who is willing to sit in the dark with you while you put the pieces back together. I love you both."

She gave a small tearful nod and carefully stepped down, returning to Tara’s side.

Halfrek — Hallie — stood from the bar and smoothed down the deep crimson halter dress, a lit cigarette between her elegant fingers, then stalked toward the stage with absolute demonic confidence. She did not touch the microphone stand. She only leaned into it and exhaled a thin plume of grey smoke, and her voice dripped with dark, aristocratic silk.

Hallie said, "For over a thousand years, Anyanka was the patron saint of scorned women. She dealt exclusively in pain, retribution, and creative dismemberment. She was a legend."

She paused, her sharp eyes scanning the human crowd and relishing the slight, terrified flinches from Xander’s relatives, then looked down at Anya and Xander. Her gaze softened just slightly, and a small, genuine smile touched her lips, rare for her.

Hallie said, "But looking at her now... sitting next to this deeply flawed, fiercely stubborn mortal boy who just stood his ground... I realize her greatest revenge was actually just finding happiness."

She lifted the cigarette in the air like a champagne flute.

Hallie said, "To Anya and Alexander. May your enemies always bleed out before dessert."

The quiet crowd finally erupted. A sudden massive wave of genuine applause, cheers, and the clinking of glasses filled the beach air.

Lucian stood near the back of the crowd with his arms loose at his sides. He did not cheer. He watched Buffy return from the stage with quiet pride, then glanced at Xander and Anya with the faintest respectful half-smile before he looked away.

Anya let out a loud watery laugh and wiped her eyes carefully to save her makeup. Xander leaned over and kissed her deeply as the applause swelled around them.

CHAPTER 5 The Blessing and the Breach

Outside the tiki bar the flickering torches lit the center stage while the edges of the beachfront venue dropped off into pitch-black darkness. The lingering applause and clinking glasses slowly faded as an older pastor stepped onto the small bamboo stage and stood between Xander and Anya. He was balding, in a slightly oversized, wrinkled beige linen suit. Xander stood tall, holding both of Anya’s hands. Anya glowed in the emerald-green dress, looking up at him with complete adoration.

The pastor awkwardly adjusted the chrome microphone stand between them, and it let out a short, high-pitched screech of feedback. He cleared his throat and spoke with practiced, monotone warmth.

The pastor said, "And so, as we conclude this evening’s rehearsal, we look forward to when you will formally bind your lives together in the presence of your family, your friends, and your community. You may practice the kiss."

Xander smiled warmly and leaned in and kissed Anya, not a quick peck but a deep anchoring kiss.

A massive guttural clicking roar shattered the night air, so loud and deep the ice rattled violently inside the glass cups on the tables. Xander and Anya broke apart instantly, his romantic demeanor vanishing into hardened tactical focus.

Buffy stood near her table, the white dress stirring in the ocean breeze, and reached down into her heavy canvas bag without a trace of panic. She pulled out a massive, gleaming silver battleaxe and spun it with practiced ease, eyes sharp and ready, a small wry smile on her mouth.

Buffy said, "Well. At least this time they had the courtesy to wait until the end of the ceremony."

Beside Lucian’s table a long, beautiful broadsword with a dark leather-wrapped hilt stood planted in the sand, where he had quietly driven it point-first into the shadows when they arrived. Lucian’s hand closed on the hilt, and as he tightened his grip his hand trembled with a subtle, grounded tremor of real fear. He took a sharp, steadying breath, forced the shaking to stop, and ripped the heavy blade out of the sand with a loud shing.

Xander stepped off the edge of the stage and cracked his neck, then turned back to Anya. She pointed a finger at his chest and let her hand linger there, her voice fierce but her eyes soft with fear and love.

Anya said, "Go play. But you come back to me!"

Xander gave her a sharp single nod and drew a heavily weighted nightstick from his belt.

Bamboo shattered and canvas ripped. Screams erupted from every direction, and the crowd went into absolute chaos.

A pack of massive fish-men exploded through the fake palm fronds and decorative bamboo walls, through the surf line behind the tiki torches, and up the sandy path from the parking lot. They were hulking nightmares, taller than any man, dripping murky seawater and thick green slime and reeking of brine and rot. Their milky dead eyes rolled independently, and their webbed claws, the size of dinner plates, flexed and dripped.

One lunged at a table of Xander’s screaming relatives and yanked a middle-aged woman in a floral print dress backward by her hair. Its claws sank into her shoulder as it dragged her toward the water, her blood mixing with the wet sand while her husband swung a folding chair uselessly at its back. Another smashed headlong into a cluster of college friends near the buffet, flipping the table in a crash of champagne flutes and shrimp platters. A young man in a rumpled suit was raked across the back, and a horned demon guest in a torn tuxedo tackled the creature off him, roaring and goring it with curved horns. A third burst from the surf and grabbed a fleeing guest by the ankle, pulling the screaming man into the shallows. Two more barrelled through the main seating area and sent human guests scrambling under tables or trampling each other. In the middle of it a scaled demon guest hauled an elderly relative up out of the wet sand and shoved him toward the parking lot. Before the water could take them, demon guests and frantic relatives pulled the woman in the floral dress and the man in the shallows back up the beach, soaked and screaming but alive.

Willow, Tara, and Dawn were shoved together in the chaotic rush. Willow stood rigid in the oversized green blouse. Tara grabbed Dawn by the shoulders and yanked her behind them to shield her from the charging monsters. Willow raised her hands defensively, eyes wide with terror, and instinctively tried to pull ambient energy. Then her face twisted with frustration and pain as she remembered the massive tear in her magical web. She balled her hands into tight fists and dropped them to her sides, forcing herself not to cast. She was terrified of the monsters, and even more terrified that the magic would kill her if she tried to save her friends with it now. She threw her arms back and joined Tara in making a human barrier around Dawn.

Xander, Lucian, and Buffy charged forward into the screaming crowd with weapons raised as the fish-men tore the reception apart.

CHAPTER 6 The Patron Saint and the Melee

Outside the tiki bar several torches had been knocked over, and their shadows jerked across the sand while the ocean behind them sat like a black void. Through the panicked crowd, humans ran in terror while demon guests snarled and fought back.

One of the massive slime-covered fish-men barrelled through the fleeing humans and locked its dead milky eyes on Dawn, Tara, and Willow, then let out a wet hiss and lunged with webbed claws extended.

Halfrek — Hallie — stepped smoothly into its path in the structured crimson halter dress, placing herself directly between the charging monster and the huddled trio. She did not take a combat stance. She just stood perfectly straight, entirely unbothered, ancient aristocratic authority radiating off her, and locked eyes with the towering creature. Her voice dropped low, vibrating with dark, heavy magic.

Hallie said, "No one shall harm a woman in my presence."

The fish-man ignored the warning and lunged. Hallie slowly tilted her head to the side, raised her hands with effortless precision, and violently smacked her palms together. A sharp concussive boom rang out, and a translucent shockwave of rippling crimson energy erupted from her hands in a perfect cone and hit the charging fish-man directly in the chest. The creature did not get thrown backward. The crimson energy disintegrated it on contact, thick green scales and muscle and slime dissolving in a fraction of a second into a cloud of fine dark ash. A perfectly clean, bleached-white skeleton collapsed into a heap on the sand at Hallie’s strappy black heels.

The other fish-men in the immediate vicinity froze, instincts overriding aggression, and actually recoiled a step from the terrifying demon in the red dress.

Through the chaos the rest of the fish-men swarmed the venue, but they were not slaughtering everyone. Several of Anya’s demon guests, some with scales and others with horns, all in torn wedding attire, were fighting back with brutal efficiency. Clem in his loud Hawaiian shirt casually body-slammed a fish-man through a buffet table, sending potato salad and a flailing monster flying. A large tusked demon guest ripped one creature’s arm half off with a wet tear and beat it to the ground with its own limb. Human guests cowered under overturned tables or ran for the parking lot. A vampire guest helped a woman in a ruined evening gown up off the sand and turned on the monster lunging after her.

The vampire hissed, "Not tonight, fish-face."

Then he drove a broken chair leg into the monster’s eye.

Buffy, Lucian, and Xander did not give the monsters a chance to recover from Hallie’s display. Buffy rushed in on one flank in the flowing white dress, gripping the heavy silver battleaxe. Lucian came in on the other in the sharp black suit, long broadsword raised, pale-faced and wide-eyed, because this was not training anymore, this was real. Xander charged straight up the middle with the weighted nightstick.

Buffy used her Slayer momentum to leap off the edge of a wooden table, spun mid-air, and brought the heavy silver battleaxe down in a brutal sweeping arc. The blade cleaved completely through the first fish-man’s collarbone, spraying dark thick fluid across the sand, and the creature dropped instantly.

The second fish-man snarled and swung a massive webbed backhand at Xander’s head. Xander ducked the blow with practiced street-fighter speed, stepped inside the creature’s guard, and drove the heavy nightstick into the monster’s knee joint with a sickening crack. The fish-man dropped to one knee roaring in pain, then twisted toward Lucian, who was moving in from the side.

Lucian’s hand was white-knuckled on the hilt, and the sword felt impossibly heavy. The creature’s milky eyes locked on him and it lunged with terrifying speed, one massive clawed hand swiping low. Lucian tried to bring the blade up in time, but the claws caught the sleeve of his tailored jacket and the shirt beneath, tearing fabric and raking a hot line of pain across his ribs and bicep. He gasped, a sharp involuntary sound of shock and fear, and stumbled backward in the loose wet sand. He slipped and went down on one knee with sand grinding into his palm.

The fish-man loomed over him, both claws raised for a killing blow, ichor dripping from its maw. Pure animal panic and adrenaline flooded Lucian’s system. There was nothing elegant here, only his breath coming in ragged gasps, and with a raw, desperate shout, half terror and half fury, he gripped the broadsword with both hands and drove it upward with every ounce of strength he had. He went not for the neck but straight into the soft under-jaw and throat as the monster descended. The steel punched through scale and flesh with a wet, grinding resistance. Dark, thick fluid gushed down the blade over his hands and the front of his suit. The creature convulsed, claws raking across Lucian’s left shoulder and chest, tearing more cloth and skin. Lucian cried out again but did not let go. He twisted the hilt savagely and used his legs to shove the dying monster sideways off him. It collapsed into the sand with a final wet gurgle, the sword still buried deep. Lucian had to plant a foot on its chest and yank the blade free with a sickening pull, nearly falling again from the effort. He stood there for a half-second, shaking violently and staring at the body. His own blood mixed with the monster’s on his ruined embroidered shirt and torn jacket, and he was breathing like a man who had just run a mile.

Xander stood up straight, breathing hard, face flushed with exertion and adrenaline, and spun the nightstick in his hand as he looked over at Lucian, then at Buffy. His eyes widened slightly at the state of Lucian’s clothes and the blood.

Buffy snapped the axe down once to clear it, eyes scanning the chaotic perimeter, her breathing steady and her body coiled for the next wave. She glanced at Lucian, saw the trembling, the blood, the wild aqua eyes, and said nothing. She gave him only a small, tight nod of respect, because he was still standing.

She flicked the last of the dark fluid off the blade.

Buffy said, "Alright. The appetizers are dealt with. Where’s the main course?"

CHAPTER 7 The Deep End and the Ghost of Gage

Out on the shoreline the firelight of the tiki bar faded toward the pitch-black ocean, and the only light on the water was the cold silver reflection of the moon. Buffy walked to the edge of the surf holding her battleaxe and stopped, eyes narrowing as she scanned the dark coastline.

Standing on a jagged black rock jutting out of the surf was the White Alpha, towering nearly seven feet tall, his bone-white scales stark against the dark water. He slowly turned his massive head, and his glowing crimson eyes locked perfectly onto Buffy’s from hundreds of yards away. He let out a low, clicking snarl, turned his back to the shore, and dived flawlessly into the black ocean.

Buffy’s eyes narrowed with cold resolve, and she settled into her stance, because she had let this one swim away once before.

Buffy said, "No. Not this time."

Lucian stepped up behind her. Buffy turned and shoved the heavy silver battleaxe into his chest.

Buffy said, "Hold this."

Without taking her eyes off the water she reached behind her neck and unzipped the white Spring Fling dress. She let the heavy fabric drop into the wet sand and stood there in a functional black athletic two-piece bikini. She kicked off the heavy leather combat boots and snatched the battleaxe back out of Lucian’s hands. Before he could say a word she broke into a dead sprint and dived headfirst into the crashing black waves.

Inside the sea cave on the north rock island it was pitch black except for patches of sickly bioluminescent green moss on the walls. Buffy broke the surface of the water with a heavy, echoing gasp and waded out of the waist-deep cavern pool gripping her axe. She looked up. The missing college students hung on the walls, trapped in the glossy, hardened dark-green glue.

Her tough exterior cracked for a second. She recoiled and brought her free hand up to cover her mouth in absolute horror and disgust.

A heavy splash and the scrape of claws on stone sounded nearby. Buffy dropped her hand instantly, shifted her grip on the axe haft, and stepped into a low, grounded Shotokan stance, kiba dachi, her weight settled through the balls of her bare feet in the shallow water. She crept toward the mouth of a narrow rock hallway where the bioluminescent moss ran in green veins along the jagged walls and threw a sick glow across the flooded floor.

The White Alpha filled that hallway like it had been built around him. Bone-white scaled bulk, hunched and huge, crimson eyes burning in the dark, claws as long as kitchen knives dripping cave water as he came. He swung a massive armored arm in a brutal horizontal swipe aimed at Buffy’s head. She dropped under it so hard her knees kissed the water, and the claws missed her wet blonde hair by a breath. The Alpha’s hand smashed into the stone wall beside her with a crack that threw white sparks off the rock and shivered down the tunnel.

Buffy did not waste the opening. She brought the axe handle up across her body like a bar, both hands tight on the wood, and drove the middle of the haft hard into the elbow of that still-extended arm. The arm was still pinned against the wall from the impact, and she shoved the joint sideways and forced the bigger body off its line. The Alpha snarled and ripped the arm free, twisted, and came back with a vicious backhanded slash across her midsection. Buffy got the axe up high on her right side and met the claws on the steel. Sparks burst green-white where metal and nail scraped. The sheer power of the blow knocked the axe sideways in her grip, the head jerking wide as her wrists screamed, and she had to hop back two quick steps through the splash to keep hold of it.

He pressed in, and the hallway was too narrow for either of them to dance free, sixteen feet long and barely wider than his shoulders, so every big motion was a gamble. The Alpha used that, crowding her with his bulk so his next downward rake would pin her to the wall. Buffy slid left, bare sole skidding on slick stone under the water, and let the claws tear sparks out of the rock where her shoulder had been. She snapped a Shotokan front kick, mae geri, straight into the soft hinge of his lead knee while his weight was still committed forward, and the joint buckled. He roared and caught himself on the wall with one clawed hand, and Buffy stepped in on that stumble, hips turning under him the way judo had taught her. Her right hand left the axe long enough to seize the thick wrist above her head while her left kept the weapon tight against her ribs. She dropped her weight, turned her hip into his center, and tried to throw him with a modified seoi nage off the compromised knee.

He was too heavy, and the throw only half landed. The Alpha went over her shoulder crooked, smashed into the opposite wall instead of the floor, and the whole tunnel shuddered. Green moss shook loose and drifted down like sick snow. Buffy staggered, breath hard, and got both hands back on the axe as he shoved off the rock and came at her low, trying to take her legs out from under her with a sweeping claw. She jumped the sweep, landed short, and brought the axe down in a tight vertical chop that would have split a human skull. The blade bit into the scaled meat of his upper arm and stuck for a heartbeat in the thick armor there before she wrenched it free, dark ichor spraying across the glowing moss.

The Alpha backhanded her with the wounded arm anyway. The blow caught her across the left shoulder and spun her into the wall. Stone kissed her cheekbone. She tasted copper, shoved off with her right foot, and used the rebound to drive the butt of the axe handle into the hinge of his jaw as he lunged in after her. His head snapped sideways, and spit and brine hit the water. Buffy followed with a short rising cut meant for the throat, but he got a forearm up and the steel sparked off bone-white scale hard enough to jar her elbows numb.

They broke apart for one thin second, both of them lit by the green glow of the moss, the shallow water around their feet shivering with the leftover force of the exchange. The Alpha’s crimson eyes tracked her with something too sharp to be animal. Buffy felt the old Slayer cold settle behind her own eyes, jaw locked, weight forward on the balls of her feet, the axe held in a tight two-handed guard across her body. He feinted along one wall, but she did not buy it. When his real attack came from the other side she was already moving, slipping inside the arc of his claws where his reach turned from advantage into clutter. Close in, she slammed her shoulder into his midsection, planted, and tried to lever him with a hip throw off her right side, koshi nage, using his own forward drive against the mass of him.

This time it worked well enough. He went up and over, not clean, not pretty, and crashed onto his back in the flooded floor with a wave that slapped the walls. Buffy went after the head with the axe. He rolled, claws scything, and one tip opened a thin hot line along her ribs as she twisted away. She hissed through her teeth, did not look at it, and kicked him hard in the side of the skull with her heel while he was still on all fours. The Alpha skidded sideways on his clawed hands and found his feet with terrifying speed for something that size. Instead of closing again, he did the intelligent thing.

He turned and fled deeper into the tunnel, bone-white bulk pounding through the green dark, water sheeting off him with every stride.

Buffy raised the axe to chase and froze mid-step. Recognition hit her like a second blow. She saw the specific curve of the spine, the hunch of the shoulders, and on the lower back a faded stretched remnant of a Sunnydale High swim-team tattoo warped almost past reading by the white scales. It was the boy from the swim team, the one she had already failed once.

Her voice broke as she called after him, old pain cracking through the Slayer flatness.

Buffy said, "Gage Petronzi."

The White Alpha stopped dead in his tracks.

Buffy swallowed hard, her eyes filling with sorrow as her face softened.

Buffy said, "I’m sorry, Gage... Gage Petronzi."

She tightened her grip on the axe.

Buffy said, "I can’t let you go... not this time."

Hearing his human name triggered something primal and agonizing in the monster’s brain. The Alpha threw his head back and let out a deafening, tortured, blood-curdling roar that slammed off the narrow walls. Then he spun and charged at Buffy with blinding speed, both sets of webbed claws fully extended for a killing blow.

Buffy did not back up. She waited until the last millisecond, reading the weight shift in his hips the way she read any larger opponent. When Gage committed, she sprinted diagonally toward the cavern wall and planted her bare right foot hard against the vertical black rock. She used her Slayer strength to run two full steps up the wall, defying gravity while his claws tore empty air where she had been. She pushed off the stone and flipped backward through the air, directly over the Alpha’s charging bulk. As she fell behind him she brought the heavy silver battleaxe around in a brutal two-handed horizontal arc, the green moss-light flashing along the edge. The blade cleaved through the thick scaled armor of his neck. A clean decapitation.

Buffy landed perfectly on her feet in the shallow water. A second later the Alpha’s massive headless body crashed to the stone floor behind her, and the head hit somewhere farther on with a wet, final sound. She stood up slowly, breathing hard, and looked down at the water, her face a mask of profound exhaustion and sorrow. Then she turned and dived back into the dark water to begin the long swim back to the main beach. Every muscle she had burned as she swam, because the cave fight had taken time, far too much time.

CHAPTER 8 The Morning Sun

Out on the shoreline, hours later, the sky was moving from pitch black into a deep, bruising navy, a faint cool early-morning light tracing the ridge of the hills behind the town, the ocean a vast dark mirror. Buffy walked out of the dark surf and onto the sand. Far out on the water the tiny pinprick lights of a few early fishing boats could just be made out, and the low sound of their engines carried faintly over the waves.

She was soaked to the bone, still in the black two-piece bikini, her hair plastered to her face in wet, tangled strands and the pastel pink and blueish-grey makeup smeared down her cheeks. She carried the silver battleaxe low at her side, her body covered in scrapes and sand, and trudged up the shoreline, sagging with exhaustion.

The group — Willow, Tara, Dawn, Xander, Anya, and Lucian — had waited out the night huddled together near the water’s edge after the melee, some of them bandaged with napkins or strips of tablecloth. They looked up as one when they saw her. Lucian stood in the ruined black suit and torn embroidered shirt, streaked with blood, with knotted strips of white tablecloth bound around his ribs and his upper arm and another folded pad of it pressed under his shirt over the claw marks on his left shoulder and chest.

Buffy walked straight up to Willow and shoved the heavy battleaxe into her chest, her voice hoarse and exhausted.

Buffy said, "Call the Coast Guard. Tell them to check the cave at the north rock island. The kids are alive."

Willow nodded rapidly, struggling a little with the weight of the axe, and stepped back. She fumbled her cell phone out of her bag with shaking hands and started dialing, pacing a few steps toward the parking lot, her voice low and urgent as she spoke to the dispatcher.

A while later, far out over the black water, the first faint white and red navigation lights of the responding Coast Guard vessels appeared, tiny against the vast ocean, and began to turn and head north.

Buffy turned to Lucian, and he stepped forward and wrapped his arms around her, pulling her wet body tightly against his tailored suit, but he was not his usual composed self. He was shaking violently, a deep, bone-rattling tremor of pure adrenaline and terror, his hands clutching at her back as if he were afraid she would vanish.

Buffy pulled back slightly, brow furrowing, and looked up searching his face. Lucian’s sharp confident aqua eyes were red-rimmed and brimming with tears, and the stoic mask had shattered.

Slowly his legs gave out, and Lucian fell to his knees in the wet sand right in front of her and reached out, gripping both of her bare forearms with trembling hands, anchoring himself to her like a drowning man. His voice cracked, and tears streamed down his face as his body shook with raw terror and love.

Lucian said, "It isn’t fair. This life... what you do... it isn’t fair to you, and it isn’t fair to me."

Buffy’s breath hitched. She stared down at him, her own eyes filling with tears as she heard the raw desperation in his voice.

Lucian said, "I pretend, Buffy. I wear the suit, I hold the sword, and I pretend that I am strong enough to stand in this world with you. But I am just a man. And standing out here... staring at that black water... not knowing if you were ever going to come back up... it broke me. It is breaking me. The magic, the monsters... it is all just waiting to take you away."

Buffy immediately dropped to her knees in the wet sand, sinking down to be level with him. She gently took his tear-streaked face in both of her hands, thumbs softly wiping away the wet under his eyes, sand clinging to both of them. Her voice was a thick whisper of sorrow and absolute understanding.

Buffy said, "I know. It is a nightmare. And if you want to wake up... if you need to leave to save yourself... I will not blame you. I promise. You can go."

Lucian stared at her. His hands slid up her wet arms, his trembling fingers gently touching the sides of her neck and tangling in her wet hair. Fierce, desperate devotion burned through his tears, his aqua eyes raw.

Lucian said, "Walk away? Buffy, I have spent my entire existence living in the cold dark, pretending the shadows were enough. Then you walked in. You are the only thing in this world that actually makes me feel alive. I am terrified, yes, but I am terrified of losing you. I love you. And I would rather burn to ashes than ever walk away from my morning sun."

Buffy let out a choked wet sob as her tough Slayer exterior melted away, and she pulled his face to hers and kissed him — long, deep, and desperate at first, tasting of salt water and tears, the terror of the night pouring out. Her hands slid into his damp hair. His arms wrapped around her waist, crushing her against him. They stayed locked like that for a long moment, kneeling in the wet sand, breathing each other in.

A few feet away, Tara saw where the kiss was going before anyone else did, and she quietly took Dawn by the shoulders and turned her around to face the parking lot, keeping one hand firm on her back while Dawn huffed and stared at the cars.

Slowly they sank and rolled together until she was fully on top of him, straddling his waist, her knees sinking into the sand on either side of his hips. The kisses turned slower, more tender and present. Her hands worked at the front of his ruined shirt, pushing it open carefully around the knotted strips of tablecloth so her palms could slide over the unhurt side of his chest. She felt his heart hammering under the large black raven tattoo that spread its wings across his chest and left shoulder, the raw claw marks scored through its feathers. His hands moved up her back, over the wet skin, holding her close as their bodies found each other.

As she moved on top of him in that slow powerful rhythm, Lucian’s hands slid higher up her bare back. His fingers found the clasp of her bikini top and he reached behind her and unclasped it with a quiet click.

At that exact moment the sun fully crested the hills behind the beach in a blazing, blinding explosion of golden-orange light. Against the glare, Buffy and Lucian became pure black silhouettes, two shadows moving together in perfect intimate rhythm. The unclasped top fell away, lost in the blaze. From where the others stood, only the graceful, powerful motion of their joined shadows could be seen, rising and falling against the dawn like two human flames intertwined.

A few feet away the group had watched the raw emotion build into this powerful, undeniable moment. They were frozen, completely transfixed by the intensity of the two shadows moving together against the blaze. Xander, Anya, Willow, and Tara, one hand still on Dawn’s back, stared in stunned silence, mouths agape, faces flushed with embarrassment, awe, and secondhand emotional overwhelm. They could not look away.

Xander blinked rapidly, cheeks flushing red, and cleared his throat with a loud, incredibly awkward cough.

Xander said, "Ahem."

Buffy and Lucian did not even pause. They were completely lost in each other.

The group snapped out of their trance and immediately spun around, turning their backs on the couple. Tara quickly reached down and gripped Dawn’s hand, scooping up Buffy’s sandy white dress and combat boots with her other arm from where they had lain all night at the edge of the surf, and pulled the teenager along toward the cars waiting in the parking lot.

Tara said quickly, "Okay, looking at the ocean now. The ocean is very pretty."

Anya straightened her dress and walked away with complete, blunt pragmatism.

Anya said, "Well, statistically speaking, surviving a massive influx of lethal adrenaline does heavily trigger the biological urge to aggressively procreate. It’s actually a very healthy trauma response."

Xander pinched the bridge of his nose and ushered everyone toward the parking lot.

Xander said, "And I thought my dad was the most inappropriate thing on this beach. Keep walking, people. Do not look back."

The awkward, retreating group faded behind them, and the ocean and the growing light filled the beach.

Buffy and Lucian remained completely lost in each other, moving in a perfect, slow-burning rhythm of profound passion and need. The bright golden sun rose fully over the hills behind them, washing the beach and the water in warm morning light.

The two Coast Guard response boats were clear now across the glowing ocean, searchlights active, converging on the dark, jagged silhouette of the north rock island in the distance. Figures moved on the decks. One spotlight swept across the cave entrance where the trapped college kids were still pinned in glue, and the rescue was finally underway.

On the beach, golden morning light spilled across everything: the sand, the water, the boats, the island, and the two people who had found each other in the aftermath.
