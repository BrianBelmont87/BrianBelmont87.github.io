Buffy the Vampire Slayer. Season seven, episode 02. First Comes Blood.

CHAPTER 1 Almost Smell You

The basement under Sunnydale High was cold and wrong. Flickering fluorescent tubes overhead threw harsh pools of light and deep shifting shadows across dirty concrete. In the center of the floor, the Seal of Danzalthar pulsed with a sickly red-orange glow that brightened and dimmed irregularly, like something breathing beneath the metal. The glow crawled up the walls in long feverish shadows. The air smelled of wet stone, old dust, and something faintly metallic.

Spike sat in the far corner, back pressed hard against the cold concrete wall. He was barefoot and shirtless, wearing only his black jeans. His pale skin was streaked with dirt and fresh sweat. Healing burns, pink and raw at the edges, marked his cheek and shoulder, and a half-closed gash ran across his chest. His torn, salt-stained duster lay crumpled in the corner beside him. He rocked slowly back and forth, elbows on his knees, both hands clamped tightly over the sides of his face and his fingers digging into his temples, his whole body trembling with small constant shakes and his eyes squeezed shut.

The red-orange glow from the seal pulsed slowly across the floor.

A low hum rose under the slow pulse of the seal, and a cold blue light gathered in the air in front of him, and out of it the First appeared as Buffy, already kneeling directly in front of him, close enough that their faces were nearly level. She looked exactly like Buffy, in the white sweater and grey slacks she had died in, with the same wind-tousled blonde hair and the same face, except that her eyes were too still and too knowing and held no warmth at all. None of the small movements of real breathing touched her, and her chest never rose or fell.

Spike slowly lowered his hands, and his eyes locked onto her. For two full seconds he just stared, breathing hard through parted lips; a muscle ticked once in his jaw, and his right hand twitched forward an inch before he stopped himself.

Spike said, his voice raw and trembling, barely above a whisper, "…I can almost smell you."

He reached out with his right hand, his fingers shaking as he moved them toward her face, and his fingertips passed straight through her cheek with no resistance. No warmth. No give. Just empty air.

The First watched him with a small cold smile that did not reach her eyes. She stood up slowly, the motion unnaturally smooth, and turned her back to him. When she spoke, her voice was sharp and deliberately cruel — the kind of brutal honesty Buffy was capable of when she wanted to cut someone down — only slightly too smooth, with a faint layered echo underneath.

The First said, "You still don’t get it, do you?"

She let the silence hang for a second. Spike’s breathing got shallower, his eyes still locked on her back.

The First said, "You think I came back the same? I didn’t. I clawed my way out of a coffin, and something came back wrong with me. I feel it every time someone touches me. Warm hands. Soft hands. His hands."

She glanced back over her shoulder, eyes flat and merciless. The smile was gone now.

The First said, "He sleeps in my bed. He makes me breakfast. He thinks he’s holding a girl, and he has no idea what he’s holding. You’d know. You’d always know. So why is he the one up there, and you’re down here in the dirt like a kicked dog?"

She turned fully away from him again, the movement too precise.

The First said, "Until he’s gone… you’ll never touch me. You’ll never taste me. You’ll never feel my legs around you. Because you’re not enough."

Then the cold blue light went out, the hum dropped to silence, and she was gone.

Spike stayed completely still for a long moment, his hand still hovering in the air where her face had been, and then his face began to change.

His eyes went glassy and unfocused, but not empty — full of pain and obsession and something darker twisting underneath. He slowly lowered his hand and stared at the empty space where she had stood as if he could still see her, a muscle ticking hard in his cheek.

He started rocking again, harder now. His hands came back up to the sides of his head, fingers digging in with visible force, and a low broken sound escaped him, somewhere between a growl and a whimper.

Spike whispered it to himself, voice shaking, repeating it like a mantra, "…fight him… have to fight him…"

Over and over the words came, his back knocking against the wall with every rock. His eyes stayed locked on the spot where the First had stood.

He suddenly slammed the back of his head against the concrete, hard, with a dull and sickening sound, and then he did it again, and again. Not full rage yet — more like he was trying to punish the thoughts out of his own skull. His eyes were wide and glassy, tears of frustration and pain starting to form but not falling.

Spike said, his voice cracking, still whispering, more desperate now, "…prove it… have to prove it to her…"

He stayed there, rocking, muttering fragments under his breath, eyes wide and glassy. The red-orange glow from the seal continued to pulse across his bare chest and face like a slow feverish heartbeat.

CHAPTER 2 Sweet Home Alabama

Inside the movie theater, it was dim. A soft glow from the screen washed over the rows, seats creaked, and popcorn rustled somewhere in the dark as an emotional scene from Sweet Home Alabama played.

The five of them sat side by side in a single row near the middle of the theater: Dawn on the far left, then Lucian, Buffy and Tara, with Willow on the far right.

Dawn wore her oversized grey hoodie over a faded t-shirt and jean shorts, her hair still a bit messy from earlier in the day. Lucian sat next to her in a black shirt with dark embroidered collar and cuffs, sleeves pushed up to his forearms, and dark jeans, looking slightly uncomfortable in the theater seat. Buffy wore a simple white tank top and light-wash jeans, a light denim jacket draped over the back of her seat, hair loose. Tara sat in a soft lavender cardigan over a cream tank top and a long flowy skirt in muted earth tones, long golden dirty-blonde hair down and slightly wavy. Willow wore a light green button-up shirt with the sleeves rolled down and faded jeans, red hair down.

On screen, Melanie’s voice carried through the dark.

Melanie said, "I left because I didn’t know how to stay. I was scared of becoming… nothing. Of turning into my mother. Of never being more than this town."

Jake said from the screen, "And you think running away made you something?"

Dawn’s eyes were glassy, and she dabbed at them quickly with the sleeve of her hoodie, trying to be subtle, breathing through her mouth so she wouldn’t sniffle too loudly.

Lucian sat next to her with his right hand under his chin, index finger extended along his cheek. He leaned forward slightly, eyebrows gently furrowed like he was deeply considering the scene. His eyes were half-lidded, and a slow subtle yawn escaped him, quiet but noticeable; he quickly covered it by rubbing his hand over his mouth, then went right back to the fake thoughtful pose. He was clearly fighting sleep.

Buffy sat next to Lucian, crying quietly, nothing dramatic, just a few slow tears rolling down her cheeks. She wiped one away with the back of her hand. She kept her eyes on the screen, occasionally glancing at Lucian out of the corner of her eye with faint amusement when he yawned.

Tara sat next to Buffy with a paper napkin in her hand, gently dabbing at the corners of her eyes. She wasn’t trying to hide the tears. Her face was open, the fine crinkles at the corners of her eyes showing as she blinked rapidly.

Willow was completely focused on the movie, her eyes wide and her mouth slightly parted, leaning forward a little in her seat. Every so often she nodded very slightly at something on screen.

Jake said from the screen again, "You didn’t have to become nothing just because you stayed here. You could’ve been something with me."

Buffy leaned slowly toward Lucian and brought her mouth close to his ear, her breath warm against his skin. Her voice was low and soft and teasing — just loud enough for him to hear over the movie.

Buffy said, "You’re being such a good sport… sitting through this whole thing for the three of us. I know you’re dying in there."

She paused for a second, then her tone turned warmer.

Buffy said, "Later tonight… I’ll make sure you get a proper reward for enduring all this."

She gently pressed a soft kiss to his cheek, lingering there for a moment. Then, without pulling back too far, she playfully caught his earlobe between her teeth — just a light teasing bite — before finally sitting back against her seat.

Lucian’s eyes, which had been half-lidded from boredom, suddenly sharpened. A small surprised breath escaped him through his nose. He turned his head just slightly toward her, the corner of his mouth twitching upward. A faint flush crept up the side of his neck.

Without saying anything, he slid his right hand over and gently squeezed her thigh just above her knee. His grip was firm but not rough — more of a quiet deliberate response. His thumb brushed once against the denim of her jeans in a slow subtle stroke. His fingers lingered for a second longer than necessary before he slowly pulled his hand back.

Buffy’s breath caught for a second. She shifted slightly in her seat, her back straightening a little as she turned her head to look at him. Her eyes were still wet from earlier, but now there was a playful, slightly mischievous glint in them. A small knowing smile curved her lips as she met his gaze. She didn’t look away. Her breathing had changed — a little quicker, a little warmer.

She held his eyes for a few seconds, the smile never fully leaving her face, before finally turning her attention back toward the screen. Even then, she kept her body angled slightly toward him.

The others stayed wrapped up in the movie. Tara was still gently dabbing at her eyes with the paper napkin, lost in the scene, Willow’s eyes were still wide and completely pulled into the film, and Dawn wiped her cheek with the back of her hand and sniffled quietly.

Melanie said from the screen, "I was so scared of staying… I didn’t realize I was even more scared of leaving you."

The row stayed like that a little longer: the quiet charged little moment between Buffy and Lucian while the rest of the group stayed wrapped up in the movie.

CHAPTER 3 Actually Happy

The women’s restroom had bright fluorescent light and large mirrors above the sinks. Muffled film sound drifted in from another theater. Water ran, and voices echoed softly on the tile.

The four women entered the restroom together after the movie. They moved with the casual comfortable energy of people who had done this a hundred times before.

Willow and Tara headed straight for two adjacent stalls near the end of the row. Buffy and Dawn walked over to the long counter with the sinks and large mirror.

Willow stepped into her stall, and Tara went into the one next to her. At the sinks, Buffy kept her denim jacket on but pushed the sleeves up. Dawn’s eyes were slightly red and puffy from crying during the movie.

Inside their stalls the two of them sat on the toilets, leaning forward with their elbows on their knees, and their voices carried easily over the low partition between them.

Willow said, her voice soft and casual, "That last part got me. When she finally said it out loud… I don’t know. It hit different tonight."

Tara said, her voice quiet and warm, "Yeah. It did."

There was a short comfortable pause between them.

Willow said, choosing her words carefully, "You think Buffy’s happy? Like… actually happy with how things are going with Lucian?"

Tara said, "I think she is. She looks at him different than she used to look at anyone else. Softer. Like she doesn’t have to hold everything up by herself when he’s around."

Willow said, "Yeah… I’ve noticed that too. She seems lighter lately. Even with everything going on."

She kept her tone light and observational, not pushing too hard. She knew she had set the whole thing in motion, so she was careful not to sound like she was fishing.

At the sinks, Buffy gently turned Dawn toward her and started fixing her makeup. She used a clean tissue from the dispenser to carefully wipe away the smudged mascara under Dawn’s eyes first. Then she took out a small tube of concealer from her bag and dabbed it under Dawn’s eyes with her ring finger, blending it softly. After that, she used a little pressed powder to set it so it didn’t look cakey. She worked quietly and gently, focused on the task.

Dawn stayed mostly quiet, just watching Buffy in the mirror with a tired but appreciative expression. She didn’t say anything.

Buffy said, voice low, while she fixed Dawn’s makeup, "He’s… good for me. I didn’t think I’d ever say that about anyone again. But he is. He doesn’t try to fix me or tell me what I should be doing. He just… stays. Even when I’m a mess."

She paused for a second, still blending the makeup.

Buffy said, "And when we’re alone… it feels easy. Like I don’t have to be the Slayer every second. I can just be his girlfriend. That part still surprises me sometimes."

Willow and Tara stayed quiet for a moment in their stalls, listening. Tara glanced toward the stall wall like she wanted to say something supportive but didn’t interrupt.

Willow said, her voice soft and careful, "Do you think it’s getting serious? Like… serious-serious?"

Buffy said, smiling a little while she finished Dawn’s makeup, "Yeah. I do. And it doesn’t scare me as much as I thought it would."

She stepped back and looked at Dawn’s face in the mirror, checking her work.

Buffy said, "There. Better."

Dawn gave her a small grateful smile but still didn’t speak.

When they were finished, Tara stood and let her skirt fall back into place, smoothing it down with both hands, while Willow pulled up her jeans, buttoned them and tucked her shirt back in.

They both flushed at almost the same moment, the sound echoing lightly in the tiled bathroom, and Willow and Tara stepped out of their stalls and moved to the sinks to wash their hands. Tara pumped some soap into her palm while Willow ran the water, both of them still casually continuing the conversation.

Buffy said, still standing at Dawn’s shoulder in front of the mirror, "He makes me feel… steady. Like I don’t have to keep proving I’m okay every five minutes. And when we’re together, it’s not this heavy thing. It’s just… good. Really good."

She capped the concealer and slipped it back into her bag, then gently brushed a stray piece of Dawn’s hair out of her face with her fingers.

Tara said, her voice soft and sincere, "I like having him around the house. It feels… fuller. Like the space actually gets used now instead of just being somewhere we sleep and eat. He’s quiet, but it’s a good kind of quiet. He fits."

Willow finished washing her hands and reached for a paper towel. She glanced over at Tara with a small, slightly guilty smile.

Willow said, "Yeah, and he can cook…"

She caught Tara’s eye and immediately put both hands up, palms out, in a quick defensive gesture.

Willow said, "But not as good as you."

Tara said, laughing softly, "Yes… a man that can cook."

Buffy let out a small laugh while she checked Dawn’s face one last time in the mirror, tilting her head slightly as she examined her work.

Buffy said, "Deffo a good thing. Speaking of which, his ears are probably buzzing right now."

All four of them laughed lightly at that. The mood in the bathroom was warm and easy. Dawn gave a small tired smile in the mirror.

They finished up and headed toward the restroom door together. Buffy held it open for the others as they filed out into the hallway.

CHAPTER 4 First Comes Blood

Outside, the wet asphalt of the theater parking lot reflected the yellow theater lights. Small patches of oil created dark slick spots on the ground. The rain had eased into a light drizzle. The night air was cool. Traffic hummed in the distance, and boots hit the wet asphalt.

The five of them were walking across the lot toward Joyce’s silver sedan after leaving the theater, their clothes slightly rumpled from the long day and the rain, and through the rear window the neon yellow Frisbee still lay on the back seat.

Four Bringers in hooded brown robes, their faces empty where their eyes should have been, suddenly emerged from between the cars. Two came from the left side, two from the front-right. They moved low and fast, blades already raised. Their movements were silent and coordinated.

Willow reacted instantly. She threw her right hand out toward the nearest Bringer on the left side. There was a sharp invisible telekinetic twist in the air. The Bringer’s right leg torqued outward at the knee, the lower leg wrenching sideways until it jutted almost straight out from where it should have pointed, in a single unnatural snap. A loud muffled crack sounded as the bone broke inside the robe. The Bringer dropped hard onto its left knee but made no sound of pain. It immediately began crawling forward on its hands and one functioning leg, dragging the broken limb behind it, still trying to reach the group. The wet asphalt made a scraping sound as the broken leg trailed behind.

Willow planted both feet and pushed both hands forward, attempting a larger binding spell. Her voice was strained and urgent.

Willow said, "Thicken—!"

The air in front of her rippled and began to visibly thicken like dark tar for half a second, slowing one of the advancing Bringers. Then the spell violently collapsed inward. Willow cried out in pain and clutched her chest with her left hand, stumbling back half a step. Her breathing turned sharp and pained. A visible flicker of failed magic sparked around her hands before dying out.

Tara stepped in front of Willow without hesitation and threw both arms outward, palms facing forward. A translucent shimmering barrier erupted from her hands and expanded in a wide arc. It slammed directly into the chest of the Bringer that had been slowed by Willow’s failed spell, driving it backward and slamming it down onto the wet asphalt with a heavy wet impact. The Bringer was pinned to the ground for several seconds, struggling against the barrier. The rain continued to fall on the glowing shield. Tara’s sleeves pulled tight across her shoulders with the effort.

Lucian grabbed one of the two Bringers coming from the front-right by the front of its robe and hauled it in with both arms locked around its torso, holding it steady. The Bringer struggled violently, but Lucian kept it in place, his face set with effort. His shirt stretched across his back.

Lucian called out, "Buffy—!"

Buffy stepped in and began punching the held Bringer in the face — short heavy downward strikes. After the third punch, the Bringer that Tara had pinned suddenly broke free of the fading shield and lunged from Lucian’s left-rear side. It slipped slightly on an oil patch on the wet ground, causing its blade trajectory to shift. Instead of a clean upward stab toward the heart, the knife drove in at a lower angle, sinking into Lucian’s lower left side just under the ribs.

Lucian let out a sharp pained scream. He staggered but didn’t release the Bringer he was holding. Blood immediately began soaking through the left side of his shirt, spreading quickly across the fabric. His grip loosened slightly from the shock.

Buffy’s expression changed instantly. Her eyes went flat and distant, and something cold and mean flicked on behind them.

She spun on her left foot and delivered a fast powerful roundhouse kick with her right leg. The kick traveled in a wide arc, sweeping up from low near the ground and connecting hard with the side of the attacking Bringer’s head. The Bringer was knocked sideways and stumbled on the wet ground.

Buffy then grabbed the Bringer that Lucian was still holding, yanked it away from him, and drove it down hard onto the wet asphalt. She immediately mounted it, straddling its torso. She began punching downward in rapid heavy strikes — her right fist rising and falling in short powerful arcs. Each impact landed directly on the Bringer’s face. Blood began to spray upward with every punch, speckling across her forehead, cheeks, and the front of her white tank top. Her breathing was short aggressive exhales through gritted teeth. Her eyes remained unfocused and cold as she continued striking. Her hair swung with the force of each punch.

After several heavy punches, she paused for half a second, chest heaving, then delivered one final heavier downward strike before stopping. She stayed mounted on the unmoving Bringer, fists still clenched, eyes distant, blood slowly running down her forehead toward her eyebrow. Rain mixed with the blood on her face and tank top.

The two Bringers still on their feet hesitated for a moment, then began retreating into the shadows between the cars without a sound. The Bringer with the broken leg stayed on the ground, the limb trailing uselessly behind it on the wet asphalt.

Lucian had dropped to one knee, one hand pressed against the stab wound in his lower left side. Blood was seeping between his fingers and soaking into his shirt. He was pale, his breathing labored but controlled.

Willow was still clutching her chest, face pale and pained. Tara’s shield had fully faded. Dawn stood a few feet away with a shallow cut on her upper arm, staring at the scene in shock, her eyes wide.

The remaining two Bringers had disappeared between the cars. The only sounds left were the rain dripping from the vehicles and the ragged panting of the group.

Willow screamed, her voice cracking, "BUFFY!"

Buffy’s head snapped up from where she was still kneeling over the dead Bringer. Her eyes focused sharply. She quickly pushed herself up, her boots splashing through shallow puddles as she ran toward the others. Blood was still speckled across her forehead, cheeks, and the front of her tank top. Her breathing was fast and ragged.

The group moved fast and messy toward Joyce’s car.

Willow yanked the driver’s side door open and threw herself inside, slamming the door behind her. Tara ran around to the front passenger side, pulled the door open, and got in quickly.

In the back, Buffy pulled open the rear door on the passenger side and held Lucian up. He was moving slower now, one hand pressed firmly against the stab wound in his lower left side while blood went on seeping between his fingers and into his shirt. Buffy guided him into the back seat first and climbed in after him, pulling the door shut, while Dawn ran around to the driver’s side, slid in behind Willow, and pulled her own door closed.

Inside Joyce’s car, it was cramped and tense. Rainwater dripped from everyone’s clothes onto the seats and floor. Rain streaked the windows, and the dashboard light was dim.

Willow was in the driver’s seat and Tara in the front passenger seat. In the back, Dawn sat behind Willow on the left, Lucian sat in the middle with his wounded left side turned toward her, and Buffy sat on his right.

Dawn quickly pulled off her oversized grey hoodie and pressed the bunched-up sleeve firmly against the wound in Lucian’s lower left side. Her hands were shaking badly, and tears were running down her face as she kept steady pressure on the wound, breathing fast through her mouth.

Lucian was leaning back against the seat, his head slightly tilted. His eyes were glassy and unfocused, and he looked pale and a little loopy from the blood loss, his breathing shallow and uneven and his shirt dark with blood where Dawn pressed.

Buffy sat close to him and grabbed both of his hands with hers, holding them tightly, her grip firm, blood still speckled across her forehead and cheeks. She looked at him and spoke in a low intense voice.

Buffy said, "Stay awake. Don’t you dare fall asleep on me."

She then turned her head toward the front of the car, eyes locked on Willow in the driver’s seat.

Buffy said, "Will. Step on it. Hospital. Now."

Willow didn’t answer with words. She just nodded once and slammed the car into drive. The tires spun for a split second on the wet asphalt before catching, and the car lurched forward as she sped toward the exit of the parking lot.

Outside, the car sped away, tires splashing through puddles as it headed toward the main road and disappeared into the night.

Behind it, the Bringer with the broken leg was still on the ground. It felt no pain. It slowly dragged itself forward using its arms and its one good leg, the broken limb trailing uselessly behind it. In its right hand, it still gripped the knife, using the blade against the wet asphalt to help pull itself along. It continued crawling in the direction the car had gone, completely uncaring of its injury. The rain continued to fall on its hooded form.

CHAPTER 5 You Didn’t Break

At Sunnydale General, the waiting room was too bright after the dark wet parking lot. Vending machines hummed low. Distant hospital sounds came and went: beeping, footsteps, muffled announcements.

The group was clustered together in the corner of the waiting room.

Dawn sat hunched forward with her face buried in her hands, crying quietly, her hair messy and damp in places and her shoulders shaking with every sob, and a square of white gauze was taped over the shallow cut on her upper arm where a triage nurse had cleaned it while Lucian was being wheeled back.

Tara sat right beside her, one arm wrapped protectively around Dawn’s back while her free hand gently stroked her hair in slow steady motions. Tara’s face was pale and tired, but her expression was calm and grounding. She kept her focus on Dawn, occasionally glancing over at Buffy and Willow with quiet concern.

Buffy and Willow sat across from them. Buffy’s tank top had dried blood speckled across the front and collar. Her hands were covered in dried blood — dark flakes around her knuckles and under her fingernails. Willow was holding both of Buffy’s hands tightly in her own.

The plastic chairs were uncomfortable. The low hum of the vending machines was the only constant background noise.

Buffy stared down at the floor between her feet, breathing slowly. There was a slight tremor in her hands that Willow could feel.

Buffy said, her voice rough and low, "I lost it back there, Will. When Lucian was stabbed, I completely lost it. For a second I didn’t feel like me anymore. I felt like Faith. Like something cold and mean just flicked on inside me and I stopped caring about anything except hurting it. I wanted to break it. I wanted to make sure it never moved again. The Slayer… it was just there. Like it had been waiting for an excuse."

She swallowed hard. Her fingers tightened around Willow’s.

Buffy said, "It’s still in me. That darkness. Even after everything I’ve tried to do to push it down… it’s still there. Waiting for the right moment to come back up. And tonight it did."

Since the grave, some quiet part of her had wondered whether all of her had come back, or whether something else had climbed up through the dirt with her. Tonight that thought felt like it had a voice.

Willow didn’t speak right away. She just held Buffy’s hands and thought, her brow slightly furrowed. Her thumbs gently stroked the backs of Buffy’s hands in small steady motions. When she finally answered, her voice was soft but carried that quiet clever weight she got when she saw the larger picture.

Willow said, "It was testing us. Not just trying to hurt us physically… but watching. Seeing how we’d react when it got personal. How fast we’d break. How we’d fight when someone we love was in danger. That attack wasn’t random, Buffy. It was a message. ‘I can reach you. Even when you’re trying to have a normal night. Even when you think you’re safe.’"

She gave Buffy’s hands a small steady squeeze. Her eyes stayed on Buffy’s face, watching her carefully.

Willow said, "And you didn’t break. You got angry. There’s a difference."

Buffy let out a shaky breath and slowly looked down at her own hands again. The dried blood was flaking in places, and she flexed her fingers once, watching it shift across her skin, her hands trembling slightly. She brought them closer to her face and tried to rub some of the dried blood off with her thumb, but it didn’t come off easily, and she kept rubbing, harder now, her breathing quiet but uneven. The blood flaked away in small pieces, but the stains remained on her skin.

She stopped and just stared at her hands for a long moment, her expression distant and heavy. Across from her, Dawn’s sobs had quieted into uneven breaths under Tara’s palm. The vending machines kept humming. Somewhere down the hall a monitor beeped, ordinary and relentless.

CHAPTER 6 House Is Clear

Outside the Summers house, it was deep night. The only light came from the porch light above the front door. The car’s headlights cut across the driveway as it pulled in. Crickets sang, and the engine ticked as it cooled.

The car pulled into the driveway and came to a stop. For a few seconds, no one moved. The house looked normal from the outside — porch light on, windows dark. But after the attack in the parking lot, the silence felt wrong.

Buffy was the first to get out, with blood still on her tank top and her face and a stake held ready in her right hand. Willow, Tara, and Dawn followed slowly, their steps dragging. Lucian was not with them — he was still at Sunnydale General, sedated and stable. Buffy had checked with the nurses before they left.

They entered together. No one spoke at first. Buffy locked the door behind them and slid the new deadbolt into place. It stuck a little in the patched, still-crooked door, and she tested it twice with her hand, making sure it was secure.

The four of them stayed close as they began moving through the house as one unit. Buffy led with the stake ready. Willow and Tara stayed near the middle. Dawn walked between them, arms wrapped around herself. Everyone’s faces were tired and on edge — and every creak made them think of Lucian still hooked up to monitors across town.

They started with the ground floor, and in the living room they turned on every light. Buffy checked behind the couch and pulled the curtains aside. Willow opened the coat closet and moved the jackets aside with her hand. Tara checked under the coffee table. Dawn stood near the center of the room, arms wrapped around herself, watching the others with wide eyes. No one found anything, but they still moved carefully.

In the kitchen, Buffy opened every cabinet. Willow checked the pantry, moving boxes aside. Tara looked behind the refrigerator. They even opened the oven and the microwave. Dawn crouched and checked behind the kitchen island. The sound of cabinet doors opening and closing echoed through the quiet house.

They cleared the dining room quickly. In the hallway, Buffy opened the small linen closet and pushed the towels and sheets aside with the stake. Willow checked the ground-floor bathroom — behind the shower curtain, under the sink, and inside the cabinet. Tara stood in the doorway watching the hallway while they worked.

They moved upstairs together. The wooden steps creaked under their weight. Buffy went first, stake ready, moving slowly. At the top of the stairs, she paused and listened before motioning for the others to follow.

Upstairs, they turned on the hallway light. Buffy checked the small closet near the top of the stairs. Willow opened the door to Dawn’s room and stepped inside.

In Dawn’s room, they checked under the bed, inside the closet, and behind the curtains. Dawn stood in the doorway, arms still wrapped around herself, watching the others with a tired anxious expression. No one found anything.

They went into Willow and Tara’s room together. Tara checked their closet while Willow looked under the bed. Buffy opened the window slightly and checked the porch roof outside before closing and locking it again. They even looked inside the bathroom connected to their room, and on the way back out Buffy pushed open the hall bathroom across from her own bedroom door and swept the shower curtain aside with the tip of the stake.

Buffy’s room was checked last on the upper floor. She opened her closet herself and moved the clothes aside. Willow checked under the bed. Tara looked behind the dresser. Dawn stayed close to the door, leaning against the frame.

Before heading back downstairs, Buffy opened the large hallway closet. She moved the vacuum, old coats, and boxes aside while Willow held a flashlight for her. Nothing was found.

They went down to the garage last. Buffy turned on the overhead light. The space felt colder than the rest of the house. They checked the empty space where Joyce’s car usually sat, along both side walls, and in the corners. Willow even climbed up on an old step stool to look at the rafters. Tara checked behind the workbench. Dawn stayed near the door leading back into the house, watching the others with heavy eyes.

Once the garage was cleared, they stood in silence for a moment. Everyone looked exhausted. Buffy finally lowered the stake slightly.

Buffy said quietly, "House is clear."

No one moved right away. The tension was still there, even though nothing had been found. They stood together in the quiet garage, the only sound being the faint ticking of the cooling car engine from outside.

CHAPTER 7 The Anchor

Upstairs in Willow and Tara’s room, it was dim and quiet. A thin strip of moonlight cut through the gap in the curtains, casting a pale line across the floor, and a single lamp on the nightstand gave off a soft warm glow.

Buffy stood in the middle of Willow and Tara’s bedroom, arms loosely at her sides. She still had dried blood on her tank top and hands. Her posture was tired but alert. Willow and Tara stood facing her by the foot of their bed, both with their arms crossed, looking at Buffy as Willow explained.

Willow said, "So… we’re putting up a barrier around the whole property. It’s not like a force field that stops people from walking in. It’s more… reactive. Anyone who steps onto the property with bad intentions — like, actively planning to hurt someone inside — they’re gonna feel it right away."

She shifted her weight, still keeping her arms crossed as she talked. Her voice had that familiar, slightly nerdy but warm tone she got when she was explaining magic.

Willow said, "It messes with their inner ear. Starts as this low vibrating tone that gets louder and more intense the closer they get to the house. Their balance goes completely sideways. They’ll stagger, drop to their knees, or just fall over. It won’t hurt them physically, but it’ll make it almost impossible to keep moving forward while they’re feeling that hostile. The second they back off or drop the intent, it stops."

Tara nodded once beside her, arms still crossed, listening.

Willow said, "It’s not perfect, and it won’t stop something that’s already inside… but it’ll give us warning. And right now, after everything that happened tonight, I want that warning."

Buffy stayed quiet for a moment, processing. She glanced between the two of them, then gave a small nod.

Buffy said, "Okay. Do it."

Willow and Tara uncrossed their arms and moved to the center of the room. They sat down facing each other on the wooden floor, knees almost touching. Between them, they joined hands — palms pressed flat together, fingers interlaced, and closed their eyes.

Willow’s breathing was slightly uneven. There was still tension in her shoulders from the failed spell earlier and the pain in her chest. Tara was calm and steady, her breathing slow and deep.

Tara said, soft and grounding, "I’ve got you. Just let me be the anchor. Don’t force it. Let it move through me."

Willow nodded once without opening her eyes, and they began.

A low soft hum started between their joined hands. It was barely audible at first — more of a vibration than a sound. A faint blue-white light began to glow between their palms, pulsing gently like a slow heartbeat. The light traveled up their arms in thin glowing lines before spreading outward across their bodies.

The light moved through the floor beneath them, spreading like water across the wooden boards. It reached the walls and began to climb. It moved through every surface — the ceiling, the windows, the doorframe. Outside the house, the same light briefly became visible as a faint shimmering dome that expanded outward before fading from normal sight. The barrier settled into place, invisible.

Inside the bedroom, Willow’s face tightened near the end of the casting. Her breathing grew sharper. The pain in her chest flared. Tara felt it through their connection and gently tightened her grip on Willow’s hands, steadying the flow of magic. The light between them pulsed stronger for a few seconds, then slowly faded as the spell completed.

Willow let out a long tired breath and opened her eyes. Tara opened hers at the same time. They stayed sitting on the floor for a moment, still holding hands.

Willow said quietly, relieved, "It’s done."

Tara said, smiling softly, "You did good. I could feel you trying not to push too hard."

Willow gave a small tired laugh and rested her forehead against Tara’s for a second.

Willow said, "Thank you for being the anchor. I don’t think I could’ve held it together without you."

They stayed like that for a little while longer, the house now wrapped in the quiet protection of the spell. Buffy watched them from a few feet away, her expression softening slightly. Dried blood still marked her tank top, and the last soft blue-white afterglow faded from the boards.

CHAPTER 8 First Time?

At Sunnydale General, Lucian’s room was quiet except for the steady rhythmic beeping of the heart monitor. The lights were dimmed, with only a small lamp above the bed and the glow from the hallway seeping in through the small window in the door. Lucian lay in the hospital bed, hooked up to an IV drip. His eyes were closed; the medication had pulled him into a heavy dreamless sleep, and his breathing was slow and even, his face pale against the white pillows. The left side of his hospital gown was slightly open at the chest, showing the edge of the bandages wrapped around his lower torso from the stab wound.

The door to the room opened slowly, and Spike stepped inside.

He was wearing light blue scrubs and a white male nurse’s jacket that sat a little too tight across his shoulders and chest. He held a clipboard in one hand. He closed the door quietly behind him and stood there for a moment, just looking at Lucian. His expression was unreadable — a mix of something cold and strangely focused.

He walked over to the side of the bed without making a sound. For a few seconds, he simply watched Lucian sleep. His eyes moved slowly over Lucian’s face, then down to the bandages. He reached out with his right hand and very gently ran two fingers across Lucian’s lips. The touch was slow and almost tender, tracing the shape of his mouth — not a strike, not a shove, just contact that didn’t try to hurt. His fingers lingered there for a moment before he pulled his hand back. The chip stayed quiet; there was no intent to harm in it for the chip to catch.

Spike picked up Lucian’s chart from the end of the bed and flipped through the pages slowly, reading with a small cold smile on his face. He let out a quiet amused breath through his nose.

Spike said softly, almost conversationally, "First time?"

He closed the chart and set it back down on the foot of the bed. He looked at Lucian again, tilting his head slightly as he studied him. His eyes were sharp, but there was something darker behind them.

Spike said, "You’re gonna have to get used to this, mate. This is what happens when you try to stand where you don’t belong."

He stepped closer to the bed again. His voice dropped lower, quieter, but there was a sharp edge underneath it now.

Spike said, "You’re not the one for her. You never were. You’re just… an imitation. Something warm she’s using to pretend she doesn’t still need the dark. To pretend she doesn’t still want someone who understands what she really is when the lights go out."

Spike leaned in a little, his eyes fixed on Lucian’s sleeping face. His jaw was tight.

Spike said, "She belongs with me. She always has. In the dark. Where we both belong. Forever."

He stayed there for a few more seconds, just watching him. His fingers twitched slightly at his side, like he was fighting the urge to touch Lucian again. Then he straightened up, gave Lucian one last long look, and quietly turned to leave. He opened the door, stepped out into the hallway, and closed it softly behind him.

The room returned to silence, broken only by the steady beep of the monitor.

CHAPTER 9 Not William

In the basement under Sunnydale High, Spike was back in the corner, back pressed against the cold concrete wall, barefoot and shirtless again in only his black jeans. His skin was pale and streaked with dirt. The healing burns on his cheek and shoulder stood out raw in the red light, and the gash across his chest had only half closed. He was rocking harder now, elbows on his knees, both hands clamped tightly over the sides of his face. His fingers dug into his own temples. His whole body trembled with small constant shakes. His eyes were wide and glassy. He was muttering under his breath — fragments of the First’s words mixed with his own fractured thoughts.

The air suddenly felt heavier.

A cold blue light gathered in the deepest shadow near the seal, a low hum threading in under the red pulse of the metal, and a figure stepped out of it.

It was Angelus.

He looked exactly as he had in his prime — black coat, silk shirt, that cold aristocratic smirk. He walked with that lazy predatory grace, circling the seal once before turning his attention to Spike. His movements were smooth and deliberate, too smooth, with no real breath behind them, hollow in the same way the Buffy-shape had been hollow: the First wearing a dead man’s face.

Spike’s head snapped up, and his whole body went rigid. For a second he just stared, eyes wide, as his hands slowly lowered from his head.

Angelus said softly, almost fondly, "There he is. My beautiful, broken boy."

Spike flinched like he’d been slapped and pressed himself harder against the wall.

Spike said, low and rough, "You’re not here."

Angelus said, smiling wider, "Neither is she. But that didn’t stop you from talking to her earlier, did it?"

He took a few slow steps closer, hands in his pockets, head tilted like he was studying a disappointing piece of art. His eyes were sharp and cold.

Angelus said, "I heard what she told you. About what came back with her. About the boy in her bed. Brutal, wasn’t it? Almost sounded like something I would say."

Spike’s fingers curled into fists against his thighs.

Spike said, "Piss off."

Angelus said, ignoring him, circling closer, "But look at you now, William. Sitting in the dirt like a kicked dog. Rocking back and forth like some trembling little poet who can’t handle the dark. What happened to the Spike who used to rip throats out for fun? The one who made entire villages scream just to hear the music of it?"

He stopped directly in front of Spike and crouched down so they were eye level. His voice dropped colder, and his smirk faded into something darker.

Angelus said, "You went to see him tonight. The boy. The one who’s been touching what’s yours. You stood over his bed… and you touched his lips. That’s what you did with all that rage?"

Spike flinched hard. His eyes flicked away for a second before he forced them back.

Spike said, quiet and defensive, "I was making a point."

Angelus said with a soft, cruel laugh, "No, William. You were hesitating. The Spike I made would’ve torn his throat out and left the body in her bed as a message. Instead you played nurse. You cared about whether he lived or died."

He leaned in even closer. Spike’s breathing had become ragged.

Angelus said, "You’re not a monster anymore. You’re not even a man. You’re just… soft. All that fire I put in you, and she’s turned you into this pathetic, lovesick thing that follows her around hoping she’ll let you lick her boots."

Spike suddenly lashed out — a quick vicious backhand through the air where Angelus’s face was. His hand passed straight through with no resistance. No warmth. No give. Angelus didn’t even blink. He just kept smiling that cold beautiful smile.

Angelus said, quietly amused, "Still trying to hit what isn’t there. That’s new. You used to be better at knowing the difference between real and pretend."

Spike’s chest was heaving now, his eyes glassy with a mix of rage and something closer to fear, his hands shaking.

Spike said, his voice cracking, "She wants me. She just… she needs to see it. Needs to see I can still be what she needs."

Angelus stood up slowly, looking down at him.

Angelus said, "Then stop being William. Stop being this sad little poet who writes her love letters in blood and then cries when she doesn’t read them. Be what I made you. She came back wrong, William. That boy can’t see it. You can."

He started walking backward toward the seal, still smiling that cold beautiful smile. His coat moved with that same lazy predatory grace — fabric that didn’t disturb the air the way real cloth should.

Angelus said, "Next time you stand over him… don’t touch his lips, William."

Angelus said, "Take something he’ll never get back."

The cold blue light around him thinned, the hum dropped to silence, and Angelus faded into the shadows near the seal until only the red-orange glow remained.

Spike stayed pressed against the wall, breathing hard, eyes locked on the empty space where Angelus had stood. His hands were shaking. After a long moment, he slowly brought one hand up and touched his own lips — the same place Angelus had mocked him for touching on Lucian. His expression twisted into something ugly and determined.

Spike whispered to himself, "…not William. Not anymore."

He started rocking again, but now there was a different rhythm to it, harder and more purposeful. His eyes stayed wide and glassy, filled with something dark and newly focused. The seal pulsed across his bare chest like a fever.

CHAPTER 10 Breakfast in Bed

In the morning at Sunnydale General, soft light filtered in through the window of Lucian’s room, mixed with the cooler glow of the hospital lights. The heart monitor beeped steadily. Paper rustled softly.

Everything felt slow and thick for Lucian.

The first thing that registered was sound — a steady rhythmic beeping somewhere to his left. It wasn’t loud, but it was constant. Then came the low mechanical hum of the lights above him. His eyelids felt thick, like they were weighted down. He tried to move his fingers and they responded, but sluggishly.

He blinked once, then twice, and the world was only blurry shapes and soft light at first. His mouth was dry. There was a dull deep ache in his lower left side that pulsed with every breath.

Slowly, the blur started to sharpen.

He was in a hospital bed. White sheets. An IV line taped to the back of his hand. The beeping was coming from the monitor beside him. He turned his head slightly and the motion made the room tilt for a second before it settled.

Three people were in the room with him.

Buffy was sitting in the chair closest to the bed, a white paper bag resting in her lap. She had changed into clean clothes, but the tiredness from the parking lot and the waiting room still sat in her eyes. Tara was on the small couch against the far wall, holding a small bouquet of flowers wrapped in brown paper. Willow stood near the window with her arms crossed, but she turned when she heard him shift against the pillows.

Lucian’s eyes opened fully. He looked groggy, his face still pale from blood loss and medication, but he was awake. His hair was slightly messy against the pillow. There was a faint line on his cheek from where he had been pressed against it.

Buffy noticed the moment his eyes focused. She stood up without a word, carefully balancing the paper bag in her arms as she moved to the side of the bed. She set the bag down on the rolling tray table and began pulling things out one by one — a wrapped Doublemeat Medley burger, a carton of fries, and a large drink with a straw already pushed through the lid.

She sat down gently on the edge of the bed next to him, being careful not to put any pressure on his left side. The mattress dipped slightly under her weight. Her expression was soft but tired, with a small knowing smile.

Buffy said softly, with that same small knowing smile, "See? Breakfast in bed… equals waking up here."

Lucian let out a short pained laugh. It clearly hurt his side — he winced and pressed his hand lightly against the bandages under his hospital gown — but the laugh still came out. His eyes crinkled at the corners despite the discomfort.

Lucian said, his voice rough but warm, "…Yeah. I walked right into that one."

He slowly pushed himself up a little higher against the pillows, moving carefully. His face tightened with discomfort, but he managed to get himself into a more upright position. He looked at Buffy, then glanced over at Tara and Willow.

Lucian said, "I don’t regret it. Any of it. But… I need more training. If I’m gonna be any kind of help to you out there."

Buffy studied him for a moment. Her expression was serious, but there was no pity in it — just quiet understanding. She reached over and gently adjusted the pillow behind his back without being asked.

Buffy said, "You’re strong. Lean. Quick, for a human. And you handle a sword better than most people who pick one up for the first time. But your hands and feet… that’s where we start. I’ll teach you."

She said it plainly, like it was already decided. Like she was already thinking about where they’d begin.

From near the window, Willow spoke up, her voice gentle but clear. She uncrossed her arms and stepped a little closer to the bed.

Willow said, "Dawn’s at school. She didn’t want to go, but we made her. She said she’d come straight here after last period."

Tara stood up from the couch and walked over to the bed. She was still holding the small bouquet of flowers. She set them carefully on the tray table next to the food, then leaned in and pressed a soft kiss to Lucian’s forehead. She sat down on the other edge of the bed, across from Buffy, so the three of them were gathered close around him.

Tara said quietly, with a small smile, "We’re all here."

Lucian looked at the three of them — Buffy on one side of the bed, Tara on the other, Willow standing nearby with her arms now resting at her sides. He let out a slow breath and nodded once, accepting it without needing to say anything else. His eyes were tired but calm.

The morning light coming through the window was soft and pale. Outside, the hospital continued its quiet routine, but inside this room, for a little while, everything felt still.

CHAPTER 11 What She Doesn’t Understand

That night at the Summers house, Willow and Tara’s room was dark and quiet. Only a thin strip of moonlight cut through the gap in the curtains, casting a pale line across the bed. The lamp on the nightstand had been switched off for the night.

Willow and Tara were asleep. Willow lay on her side, turned away from Tara. She was wearing a metallic red silk camisole and matching silk shorts. Her red hair was spread across the pillow. Her breathing was slow at first, but there was already tension in her shoulders.

Tara slept facing her, but the blanket was pulled up over her waist. Long golden dirty-blonde hair spilled across the pillow. She was wearing only a simple white bra. One of her arms rested loosely across the sheets between them.

Willow’s face began to change in her sleep.

Her brow tightened, and her mouth twitched, lips parting slightly as if she were trying to speak, but no sound came out. Her breathing grew sharper. A small pained sound escaped her throat — barely audible. Her fingers curled into the sheets, gripping them tightly.

Then the visions hit.

Heavy static. A strong red and purple tint bled across the image like bruised light. The picture warped and glitched. She saw the vague shape of a massive green hill under a dark sky. At the top sat a blurred shifting figure on what looked like a twisted throne. The figure was pointing forward — pointing directly at her. The face kept glitching between different ages and expressions, never settling on one. The image distorted violently, colors smearing like wet paint. A low warped drone pulsed underneath.

More heavy static. The same red-purple haze. The image was extremely warped and unstable. She saw the vague shapes of three bodies at the bottom of what looked like stairs. They were broken and burned beyond recognition. One of them had something metallic and cracked near their face. The image pulsed and distorted so badly that the details were impossible to make out clearly. The colors smeared and glitched. A faint distorted sound of crackling fire and something wet underneath.

The static was even worse. Red and purple light bled heavily. She saw a bathroom mirror. A distorted reflection stared back. The face was heavily cracked and veined with dark lines. The eyes were completely black. The reflection looked wrecked — mascara or something dark streaked down the cheeks. The mouth moved, but the sound was warped, muffled, and distant, like it was underwater and glitching at the same time. The image kept tearing and resetting.

Willow’s body jerked violently in the bed.

She suddenly sat upright, gasping as she woke. Her eyes were wide and unfocused, staring straight ahead at the wall across from the bed. Her chest rose and fell rapidly. Sweat clung to her skin, making the metallic red silk of her top stick slightly to her back and collarbone. Her breathing was sharp and panicked, but she didn’t make a sound, and Tara slept on beside her undisturbed, her breathing slow and even and her golden hair spread across the pillow, unaware of any of it.

Willow stayed sitting up for several seconds, just staring at the wall. Her right hand slowly rose from the sheets and pressed flat against her chest, right over her heart. Her fingers spread slightly as she felt her own heartbeat hammering beneath her palm. Her eyes were still wide, and there was a look of deep confusion and fear on her face — she didn’t understand what she had just seen. The images stayed broken and nameless. No story attached itself. Only the wrongness.

She stayed quiet while her breathing gradually began to slow, but she didn’t lie back down. She just sat there in the dark, hand on her chest, eyes fixed on nothing, the remnants of the distorted visions still flickering behind her eyes. A single tear slipped down her cheek, but she didn’t wipe it away.
