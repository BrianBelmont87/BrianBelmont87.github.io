Under the Willow Tree

Buffy the Vampire Slayer. Season six, episode 16. Under the Willow Tree.

CHAPTER 1 The Healing and the Distance

Late that night, harsh white fluorescent light washed the upstairs bathroom into something clinical and vulnerable, and hot water hissed against porcelain.

Behind the frosted glass of the shower stall, Willow stood with her back to the room, completely bare, her arms raised and her palms pressed flat against the white tiled wall, hiding her chest, her head bowed and her wet red hair clinging heavily to her neck.

Willow was breathing incredibly slowly, her shoulders rising and falling in a deliberate, meditative rhythm as she tried to calm her nervous system.

As she breathed, she sank inward, down past skin and bone into the pitch-black void of her mindscape, lit only by neon, bioluminescent energy, where a complex, three-dimensional web of glowing magical pathways hung suspended in the dark, its main colors electric cyan and deep, bruised purple, around a massive, jagged, empty void at its center.

The pathways were not rotting; they were actively healing. Tiny, sparking tendrils of cyan and purple light reached across the black chasm. They moved slowly, like vines growing in time-lapse, sparking and sometimes fizzling out, but they kept pushing forward, hungrily seeking connection to knit the massive tear back together. It looked painful, but the magic was undeniably repairing itself.

The mindscape fell away, and Willow was back in the shower.

Willow let out a long, shuddering exhale, reached out with her right hand and twisted the chrome faucet handle closed, the old pipes knocking once inside the wall, and the rushing water shut off, leaving a heavy, echoing silence in the tiled room.

Moments later the master bedroom was mostly dark, bathed in cool silver moonlight from the window, except for the warm amber glow of a single bedside lamp on Tara’s side of the bed.

Tara was lying on the left side of the large bed in a thick, soft, dark-blue terrycloth bathrobe over her sleepwear, a heavy, defensive layer, facing away from the center of the bed, staring blankly at the amber glow of the bedside lamp.

The heavy wooden door clicked open, and Willow walked in wearing a lighter grey cotton bathrobe, tied securely at the waist.

Willow climbed onto the far side of the bed, and the mattress shifted under her weight.

Tara reached out and clicked the bedside lamp off, and the room dropped into heavy, blue-tinted moonlight and shadow.

Willow slid across the sheets and pressed her body flush against Tara’s back, wrapping herself around her partner. Her left hand gently stroked the side of Tara’s face in the dark, her thumb brushing softly over Tara’s cheekbone.

Tara didn’t lean into the touch; she stayed perfectly still.

Willow’s left hand slowly trailed down from Tara’s cheek. It glided softly down Tara’s neck, over the thick shoulder of the dark-blue bathrobe, and began to slide downward toward Tara’s breast, a slow, familiar question.

Instantly, Tara’s right hand snapped up and caught Willow firmly by the wrist, stopping her before she reached her destination.

Tara shut her eyes tightly, her face contorting with a deep, complex mixture of exhaustion, love, and profound, lingering hurt from the club, and her words came out jagged and barely there.

Tara whispered, "Ple… ple… please. Not… not now. Just hold me instead."

Willow’s body went completely still, as if she’d been slapped. Her breath stopped for a second, her eyes went wide and glassy in the dark, and a visible flinch ran through her shoulders. The rejection landed deep, and it wasn’t anger, just raw, devastated understanding of how much damage she had caused only a few hours ago at the Bronze, and of the painful wedge she had driven between them.

Willow’s voice cracked, thick with regret.

Willow said, "Tara, I... I..."

Willow stopped, because she knew there were no words that could fix this, and she swallowed hard against the tightness in her throat. Then she nodded slowly in the dark, her chin brushing the soft terrycloth at Tara’s shoulder.

Willow said in a voice that was barely audible, "Okay."

Tara’s fingers slowly loosened around Willow’s wrist, lingered for a heartbeat, and then let go. Willow drew her left hand back and wrapped her arm securely but neutrally around Tara’s waist, burying her face softly into the back of Tara’s shoulder.

Tara let out a long, shaky breath, her eyes remaining squeezed shut as the heavy, heartbreaking distance settled over the bed.

Neither of them slept for a long time. They lay curled together in the blue moonlight without speaking, Willow’s breath warm against the back of Tara’s robe, listening to the old house settle around them in the dark.

CHAPTER 2 The Sins of the Mother

In the back room of Rack’s den that night, the light was a deep, suffocating blood red, and thick grey incense smoke drifted lazily through it like dirty fog.

Amy was in her new clothes from the mall, with Buffy’s old Sunnydale High hoodie pulled on over them and the hood up. Rack stood behind his wooden table, hands clasped behind his back, his expression flat and wary.

Rack looked at Amy for a long beat, then sighed through his nose like a man who already regretted this.

Rack said, "One hit. That’s it. Then you get the hell out of my shop."

Amy said, "That’s all I need."

Rack didn’t waste time; he reached out and pressed two fingers against the center of Amy’s forehead. A sharp, electric blue spark jumped between them.

Amy’s entire body jerked hard, as if she had been plugged into a live socket, and her eyes snapped wide open as, for the first time since she was de-ratted, the current of magic flooded back into her system. Her pupils blew out, and a visible shiver ran through her shoulders and down her spine.

She let out a shaky, almost sexual gasp of relief.

Amy said, "Oh… fuck yes."

Rack said, "We’re done."

Amy swayed slightly, riding the sudden rush. She looked drunk on it — euphoric and dangerous at the same time.

Rack said, "Whatever is happening with you, the red witch, the Slayer, the darkness that is coming for all of you ‘from beneath you it devours’… I want no part of it. That circle is closing in on itself and I’m not getting caught inside it."

He made a small, sharp gesture with his hand.

The entire back room rippled like heat haze. The walls, the beaded curtain, the Victorian couches — everything began to blur and fold in on itself.

Rack said, "Now get out before I move the whole place with you still inside."

Amy stumbled backward toward the heavy wooden door, still buzzing hard. She gave Rack one last feral grin.

Amy said, "You have no idea what you just let back into the world."

She pushed through the door, and the moment it slammed shut behind her, the entire den vanished with a low, wet thump of displaced air, leaving nothing but a blank, dirty alley wall where the door used to be.

Up in the Madison attic that night, the candlelight was a sickly, flickering, jaundiced yellow, and thick dust and burning sage swirled in the air.

Mr. Madison’s mummified corpse stood upright in the corner like a grotesque coat rack, bound tightly to the floorboards by thick, thorny magical vines. His leathery face was frozen in a permanent silent scream.

Amy appeared, still riding the high from Rack, her movements loose and predatory, the hood pulled down and her hair wild.

She walked slowly to the heavy wooden altar in the center of the room, where a massive open spell book and a silver athame rested.

Amy picked up the athame and pressed the flat of the blade against her own palm, staring at the mummified remains of her father.

Amy said, "Mom wasted all this juice trying to fit back into a pleated skirt. Such small-town thinking. Right, Daddy?"

She turned away from the corpse and looked down at the altar, but her gaze was clearly focused somewhere far away — across town, toward the Summers house.

Amy said, "You treat the power like it’s a disease, Will. You’re trying so hard to stitch it back together… to lock it away where it can’t hurt anyone."

Amy’s grip on the dagger tightened. A wicked, deeply starved smile spread across her face.

A fine tremor of hunger ran through her whole body, from her bare fingers on the bone handle of the athame to her knees beneath the altar.

Amy whispered, "Go ahead. Heal. Knit it all back together. Pack the pantry nice and full."

Amy said, "Because I spent three years eating seeds in a plastic box… and I am starving."

The low hum of dark magic in the room spiked into a sharp, ugly crescendo.

CHAPTER 3 The Ghost at the Table

The sunlight pouring through the Summers kitchen window that morning was bright, but it felt cold. The warm golden hues the kitchen usually had seemed washed out, leaving the room slightly sterile and chilly.

Tara stood at the stove, transferring pancakes to a serving platter in a soft, flowing sage-green long-sleeve blouse and a long denim skirt, with no makeup to speak of, and faint shadows under her eyes showed the exhaustion of the night before.

Buffy sat on the left barstool in a fitted slate-blue shirt with three buttons undone, her hair twisted up in a tortoiseshell claw clip, her makeup just mascara and a neutral lip balm.

Dawn sat on the far right barstool in a brightly colored, chunky striped sweater and flared jeans, with glossy pink lip gloss and slightly uneven, sparkly silver eyeshadow.

Willow was seated on the middle barstool, trapped between Buffy and Dawn in a drab brown oversized flannel shirt that swallowed her frame, over a plain white t-shirt, her face bare and unnaturally pale, with bruised-looking dark circles under her eyes.

The ordinary sounds of breakfast overlapped: silverware scraping against ceramic, the sizzle of the griddle, orange juice being poured.

Dawn was aggressively cutting a pancake into tiny, uneven pieces, looking over Willow’s head directly at Tara.

Dawn said, "I’m just saying, Tara, it is completely unreasonable for Mr. Miller to assign a four-page essay on the French Revolution right before Xander’s wedding. I am going to be entirely too sleep-deprived to properly analyze the socioeconomic impact of the guillotine."

Tara turned from the stove, setting the warm platter of extra pancakes in the center of the island. She gave Dawn a soft, patient, but visibly tired smile.

Tara said, "I think Mr. Miller probably isn’t factoring our family’s social calendar into his syllabus, sweetie. Just do an outline tonight, and I can help you proofread the rough draft tomorrow afternoon before the wedding."

Dawn smiled, looking relieved.

Dawn said, "You are a literal lifesaver. Thank you."

Before Willow could utter a single syllable, Buffy spoke, stepping right over her.

Buffy said, "Speaking of the wedding, did Anya confirm if the caterers are actually human? Because after the tiki bar fiasco, I am putting a hard veto on any waitstaff that has scales, tentacles, or a sudden urge to lay eggs in the potato salad."

Tara wiped her hands on a dish towel, walking over to pour herself a cup of coffee.

Tara said, "Yes. Xander double-checked the vendor list. They are strictly mortal. And he specifically requested that the venue lock the emergency exits from the inside, just to be safe."

Buffy let out a short, cynical laugh, using her fork to spear a sausage link.

Buffy said, "Good. Because I left half my arsenal in the trunk of Lucian’s car, and I really don’t want to fight off a demon invasion wearing a bridesmaid dress. The taffeta is restrictive enough as it is."

Dawn leaned forward, resting her elbows on the counter, bypassing Willow’s physical space to look at Buffy.

Dawn said, "Oh, please. Like you even care about the dress. I saw what you two were doing on the beach the other morning... after the chao..."

Buffy actually blushed, a rare, genuine flash of color hitting her cheeks. She pointed her fork threateningly at Dawn, though there was absolutely no heat behind it.

Buffy said, "Eat your pancakes, squirt, before I decide you’re too young for the champagne toast."

The lively, normal family conversation flowed rapidly back and forth right over Willow’s head, as if she were entirely invisible.

Willow sank an inch lower on the stool, and the brown flannel seemed to swallow her even more. She looked down at her plate, where a perfectly cooked hashbrown sat in a small puddle of red ketchup.

Willow didn’t eat; her pale, trembling hand gripped her fork, and she slowly, methodically pushed the hashbrown through the red ketchup, smearing it across the white ceramic plate.

Buffy said casually, mid-conversation, "I’m just hoping the DJ doesn’t play the Macarena."

Buffy put two pats of butter in the middle of her hot pancakes and reached for the maple syrup. The glass bottle was sitting on the island, directly to the right of Willow’s plate.

Instead of saying, "Willow, can you pass the syrup?" Buffy simply leaned her entire upper body to the right.

Buffy’s arm reached directly across Willow’s chest, invading Willow’s personal space. The sleeve of Buffy’s slate-blue shirt brushed roughly against Willow’s chin.

Buffy grabbed the heavy glass syrup bottle and pulled it back, pouring it over her own plate without ever making eye contact with Willow, continuing her conversation with Tara.

Buffy said, "Because if I have to watch Xander try to do coordinated dance moves, I might actually self-stake."

Willow’s chest caved inward, and the fork in her hand stopped moving. The hashbrown was mashed into the blood-red ketchup, a ruined, ugly mess on the white plate.

Willow slowly lowered the fork and let it clatter softly against the ceramic.

She sat there in the cold morning light, surrounded by the three people she loved most in the world, and utterly alone.

CHAPTER 4 The Sub-floor and the Syntax

That same morning, with the sun still low over the horizon, the brutalist concrete buildings of the Calax Research and Development campus sat in cold, slate-grey shadow.

The deep, throaty rumble of a heavy V8 engine broke the quiet morning air.

Lucian’s pristine black 1981 Pontiac Firebird Trans Am Special Edition, its gold trim catching the low light, pulled smoothly up to the reinforced steel security gate.

He rolled down the driver’s side window but did not speak to the heavily armed guard in the booth; he simply held up a thick white keycard. The guard scanned it, gave a sharp nod, and hit a button.

The heavy steel gate rolled open, and Lucian drove through, bypassed the main visitor lot entirely, and parked tight against the imposing, windowless side wall of the main building.

Lucian swung the heavy door open and unfolded himself from the low driver’s seat into the chilly morning air, and the dark, brooding, tailored-suit look Buffy’s friends knew was nowhere to be seen.

He was wearing a crisp, stark-white, knee-length lab coat over a dark turtleneck and trousers. His long black hair, usually tied back at the nape, was pulled up tightly into a neat bun at the back of his neck. He looked sharp, clinical, and embedded in the corporate machine.

He shut the Firebird’s door with a solid thunk, rolled his shoulders once against the cold morning air, and fell into a measured walk, hands loose at his sides, eyes forward.

He walked up to a heavy, reinforced steel side door. Bolted to the concrete wall beside it was a stark red and white sign:

AI RESEARCH AREA - SECURITY LEVEL FIVE REQUIRED.

Lucian swiped his badge, and a heavy electronic deadbolt clunked open, and he stepped inside.

The Level Five floor was lit by long banks of brilliant, shadowless fluorescent panels set flush into the ceiling, and the contrast with the dark morning outside was jarring.

Dozens of researchers in white coats were already seated at sleek, minimalist terminal desks, typing away in absolute, clinical silence.

Lucian walked smoothly through the aisles, not making eye contact, not offering a single "good morning" to anyone. He reached his designated terminal, sat down, and immediately began typing, seamlessly blending into the silent, working hive.

He adjusted his cuff once under the lab coat, settled into the chair with a quiet exhale, and let his fingers find their rhythm on the keyboard.

Separating the desk floor from the hardware was a massive, three-story wall of reinforced, bulletproof glass, proof of how much the facility had expanded since its early days.

Beyond the glass was the true heart of the operation, the server farm.

It was an endless canyon of towering black server racks, all blinking. Thousands of fiber-optic cables ran in perfectly organized, color-coded bundles along the high ceiling.

Far below it all, beneath the solid, reinforced concrete floor and deep in the earth, was something else.

Down there the sterile fluorescent glare was gone, and the room was dimly lit by the humming blue glow of standalone hardware and heavy tactical monitors.

It was a hidden bunker that appeared on no blueprint and in no budget, poured in concrete deep under the server farm and reached by no stairway the researchers above had ever seen.

Four people stood around a compact, advanced standalone AI terminal. The lead scientist was in his fifties, arrogant, wearing a high-end suit without a tie. Beside him were two lab assistants and a military general in his sixties with a chest full of ribbons and sharp, cynical eyes.

The lead scientist said, "You are looking at the prototype, General. Once we establish massive, global hardware nodes identical to the facility sitting directly above our heads, this artificial cognitive framework will permanently alter the entire paradigm of human warfare and intelligence."

The general said, "I’ve heard the Silicon Valley pitches before, Doctor. Faster processing, predictive analytics, unmanned drones. I don’t need a sub-basement tour to know what a supercomputer does."

The lead scientist said, "With respect, General, this isn’t Silicon Valley. And we aren’t just predicting troop movements. We are translating the ether."

The lead scientist turned to one of the lab assistants and snapped his fingers.

The lead scientist said, "Bring up the primary feed. Show him the cognitive translation."

The nearer assistant bent over the keyboard and typed rapidly, his fingers rattling over the keys while the blue light of the monitor washed across his glasses.

The screen did not show normal lines of code, binary, or algorithms.

Instead, jagged, ancient, glowing red runes scrolled rapidly across the black screen. They were literal demon scripts: ancient Sumerian, Enochian, and dark magical dialects.

Beside the scrolling runes were fluctuating, real-time energy graphs charting massive, erratic spikes of ambient dark magic.

The general said, "What the hell am I looking at? That isn’t code."

The lead scientist said, "No, sir. It is the raw, ambient energy frequency of this exact town, captured and converted into digital syntax."

The lead scientist stepped up next to the general, the blue light of the monitor reflecting in his eyes.

The lead scientist said, "For centuries, the occult—magic, demonic energy, supernatural occurrences—has been treated as a myth because it was fundamentally unpredictable. It couldn’t be measured. But energy is just data, General. And data can be processed."

He tapped the glass of the monitor right where a massive spike of magical energy registered.

The lead scientist said, "This cognitive AI isn’t just reading the demon scripts. It is learning the language of the dark. It is measuring the energy happenings in real-time. Once the system achieves full cognitive understanding... we won’t just be able to predict supernatural anomalies."

The lead scientist turned his head and looked at the general with terrifying, unchecked hubris.

The lead scientist said, "We will be able to program them."

The general stared at the scrolling, glowing red demon script. The heavy hum of the supercomputer filled the concrete bunker, hidden beneath the feet of the oblivious researchers above.

CHAPTER 5 The Silent Door and the Empty Park

That afternoon the Summers backyard was bathed in beautiful, golden California sunlight, warm and idyllic.

Buffy was kneeling in the dirt in a faded, cutoff Sunnydale High t-shirt and old denim shorts, carefully loosening the soil with a small metal hand fork.

Dawn was sitting cross-legged directly across from Buffy in a bright orange tank top and overalls, holding a delicate green tomato starter plant, waiting to place it into the newly dug hole.

Tara was wearing a wide-brimmed straw sun hat and a light, floral sundress, kneeling beside a flat of green tomato starter plants.

Willow was sitting on the bottom step of the back porch, completely removed from the dirt, still wrapped in that heavy, drab brown flannel, her arms crossed tight around her stomach.

Tara gently tapped the bottom of a small plastic pot, sliding a leafy green tomato starter out into her palm. She set it delicately into a small hole in the soil, patting the dirt around the base.

Tara said, "There. If the roots take, we should actually have fresh tomatoes by late summer. It feels good to actually grow something instead of just cutting things down."

Dawn groaned loudly, staring at her own dirt-covered hands in absolute teenage disgust.

Dawn said, "I don’t understand why we can’t just buy tomatoes at the grocery store like normal people. They come in nice, clean plastic containers without earthworms trying to crawl under my fingernails."

Buffy stopped digging. She looked over her shoulder at Dawn, a mischievous, highly dangerous spark lighting up her hazel eyes.

Buffy said, "Are you complaining about the manual labor, squirt? Because you know the penalty for complaining on a sunny Saturday."

Dawn’s eyes went wide. She immediately recognized the look and scrambled backward on the grass, holding her dirty hands up defensively.

Dawn said, "No! No, Buffy, I take it back! The earthworms are my friends! I love the dirt!"

Buffy said, "Too late. The Slayer has locked onto a target. Prepare to be tickle-slayed."

Buffy dropped the hand fork in the dirt and lunged across the garden bed with a gleeful, wicked grin, her bare knees skidding on the grass.

Dawn screamed, a loud, piercing shriek of pure, joyous laughter, and tried to roll away, but Buffy was impossibly fast. She tackled her gently into the soft green grass, pinned her, and ruthlessly dug her fingers into Dawn’s ribs.

Dawn thrashed, kicking her legs, laughing so hard no actual sound was coming out.

Dawn said, "Stop! Stop! I surrender!"

Buffy was laughing too, a bright, unburdened, genuine sound that echoed across the yard.

Tara watched them from the garden bed. She covered her mouth with her dirty hand, laughing softly at the sheer, beautiful normalcy of two sisters wrestling in the grass.

Tara’s smile slowly faded as she turned her head and looked past the wrestling girls toward the back porch.

Willow was sitting on the step, watching them, and she wasn’t smiling; her face was crumbling, because the stark contrast between their vibrant, physical joy and the cold, empty void inside her was finally too much.

Willow let out a sharp, quiet sob.

She immediately brought her left hand up to cover her face and hide the tears. She stood up abruptly from the wooden step, turned her back on the sunlight, and hurried inside the house.

Tara dropped her gardening trowel at once and stood up, the joy gone from her face, and with a heavy, heartbroken sigh she rushed toward the sliding glass door to follow her.

Willow’s footsteps thudded fast and heavy through the kitchen and the living room, almost running.

She was weeping openly as she headed for the front door, her left hand clamped over her face, blinded by her own tears. She grabbed the handle, pulled the door open, and stepped out onto the porch.

Tara burst into the entryway just a second too late, reaching her hand out toward the open door.

Tara called out after her, her voice thick with desperate empathy.

Tara said, "Willow! Wait! Will, please..."

Willow stopped on the porch. She didn’t turn around to look at Tara. Without a word, she simply raised her hand and gave a small, dismissive backward wave.

There was no violent energy, no purple sparks or crackling ozone.

The front door, still hanging a little crooked since it had been rehung, simply swung shut on its own. It closed with a firm, magically enforced click, locking Tara inside. There had been no flash and no sound but the latch, and somehow that quiet, effortless little spell hurt Tara more than any slammed door could have.

Tara dropped her hand and stared at the closed wooden door, a deep, sinking unease settling in her stomach, and then she turned around and ran back through the house.

Buffy was back in the dirt, finishing up the tomato plants.

The sliding glass door ripped open, and Tara rushed out onto the back patio, breathless and deeply anxious.

Tara said, "Buffy. Willow just left. She was crying, and she magically shut the door right in my face."

Buffy’s hand fork stopped halfway into the soil, and she sat back on her heels to look up at Tara.

Buffy brought the back of her hand up, swiping it just under her nose to wipe away a bead of sweat. She stood up slowly from the garden bed, dusting the loose dirt off her knees.

She looked toward the house, a flash of genuine concern crossing her face. But she sighed, her practical, older-sister instincts taking over.

Buffy said evenly, trying to settle them, "She probably just needs to be alone for a bit, Tara. It’s been a really rough couple of days for her."

Tara shook her head rapidly, stepping off the patio and onto the grass.

Tara’s voice was clear and firm, cutting through the quiet of the yard.

Tara said, "No, Buffy, you know how fragile her emotions can be right now. She’s completely isolated herself. Something doesn’t feel right about this at all."

Buffy paused with her dirty hands hanging at her sides and looked properly at Tara for the first time, at the tightness around her mouth and the worry swimming in her eyes.

Buffy rested the back of her wrist against her hip as she took in Tara’s words, and the defensive posture dropped. Tara was right; they had been treating Willow like a ghost all morning.

Buffy sighed, her voice dropping into a guilty murmur.

Buffy said, "Yeah... I guess we were harder on her than we should have been."

Buffy’s eyes dropped to the dirt for a second, and her jaw tightened with quiet self-reproach. She wiped her hands on her shorts a little harder than necessary, the small physical tell of someone who had just realized she’d helped make a loved one feel like a ghost in her own home.

Buffy looked down at Dawn and dropped the metal hand fork and her dirty gardening gloves right into her lap so she could finish the tomatoes.

Buffy then looked up, locking eyes directly with Tara.

Buffy said with a perfectly straight face, "You have Dawn watch."

Tara gave a firm, immediate nod, understanding the assignment.

Buffy didn’t wait for another word; she turned and walked quickly across the grass.

She moved with rapid, determined strides through the sliding glass door and straight through the kitchen and the living room. She reached the entryway, threw the front door open, and stepped out onto the porch, her eyes scanning the empty street.

Down Revello Drive, Willow was walking along the suburban sidewalk. She had lowered her left hand from her face, but now she was gripping her own sides, arms wrapped tightly around her ribs, as if she were trying to physically hold herself together.

She was weeping quietly, tears streaming steadily down her pale cheeks. She ignored the sunny, idyllic neighborhood around her and walked on with a blind, devastated purpose.

A while later, at the Sunnydale playground, the bright afternoon sun was heavily blocked by massive old oak trees that cast deep, long, melancholy shadows over the park.

The play equipment was painted a dark, faded green. A rusted merry-go-round sat perfectly still in the woodchips. To the right was an aging half-court basketball hoop, its chain-link net hanging broken on one side.

There was nobody in the park at all, not a child on the slide or a dog on the grass, and the only movement was the slow sway of the oak leaves overhead.

Willow walked slowly into the heavy shade of the trees, looking small among the dark green metal structures, and went past the swings, her arms still wrapped tightly around her aching sides, completely alone in her grief.

CHAPTER 6 The Swing and the Scissors

Later in the afternoon the sun was dipping lower, sending long, fractured beams of golden light through the heavy, dark leaves of the old oak trees, and the park felt secluded and quiet.

Willow was sitting on the dark green wooden seat of the swing, hunched forward in her oversized brown flannel shirt, her head bowed, her chin nearly resting on her chest.

The swing gave a faint metallic squeak as it shifted under her, and beneath it was the sound of her quiet, ragged sobbing.

A long, dark shadow slowly crept across the woodchips and fell over Willow.

Willow stopped crying for a fraction of a second, her breath hitching, but she didn’t look up.

Buffy walked up through the woodchips without saying a word, and she simply lowered herself onto the empty swing right next to Willow, the chains groaning softly under the Slayer’s weight.

Buffy stared straight ahead at the empty park, her posture relaxed but highly vigilant. She let the silence stretch, giving Willow all the space she needed.

Willow kept her head down, staring at the woodchips between her scuffed shoes, and when she finally spoke, her voice was barely audible.

Willow said, "In preschool... I was the weird kid. I talked too fast, and I cried when the blocks fell over, and I didn’t know how to play the games right."

Buffy didn’t interrupt; she just listened, her eyes tracking a gentle breeze blowing through the trees.

Willow said, "Everyone ignored me. Or they were just... cruel. Kids are so mean when they find the weak one. Until Xander. He was the only one who sat with me."

Willow took a shaky breath, a bittersweet, watery smile touching her lips as she got lost in the memory.

Willow said, "One day... this boy named Tommy grabbed a pair of classroom scissors. He snuck up behind me while I was coloring and cut off a massive chunk of my hair. I just sat there, holding the hair in my hands, totally frozen."

Willow finally turned her head slightly, looking over at Buffy.

Willow said, "Xander didn’t yell. He didn’t tell the teacher. He just... pounced. He hit Tommy so hard they both went crashing into the cubbies. Xander pinned him to the floor. He had both of his hands around Tommy’s throat, choking him."

Buffy slowly turned her head, her hazel eyes widening slightly in genuine surprise.

Willow said, "He was just staring down into Tommy’s face like an animal. Like he was perfectly willing to kill him over a piece of my hair. Mr. Peterson had to physically pick Xander up with one hand by the back of his shirt and rip him off the kid."

Willow looked back down at her shoes, the tears starting to fall again.

Willow said, "I know, right? Xander. No way. But it’s true. The other kids... they never touched me again after that day. He’s always been my shield, Buffy. And now he’s leaving."

Willow’s shoulders started to shake. The nostalgia evaporated, overwhelmed by the crushing guilt of her own recent actions. She turned her body fully toward Buffy, her green eyes pleading and desperate.

Willow said, "Buffy, what I did with Amy... I was just so scared. I got too drunk and listened to her. I followed her away from the Bronze, it’s all my fault. Tara... if anything happened to her... I would die inside... I am so sorry. Please... please believe me."

Buffy stared at Willow’s broken, pleading face, then stood up from her swing, and Willow flinched slightly, thinking she was going to walk away.

Instead, Buffy walked slowly around behind Willow’s swing and placed both hands firmly, securely, on the center of her back.

With a slow, gentle, deliberate motion, Buffy pushed forward.

The rusted chains squeaked as Willow floated forward into the air, her feet lifting off the woodchips, and swung back. Buffy caught her gently, absorbing the weight, and pushed her again, a little harder this time.

Buffy pushed her friend in total silence. The rhythm was steady, safe, and anchoring, nice and slow, building momentum with every pass.

Willow’s hands slowly relaxed their death grip on the chains, and the violent trembling in her shoulders stopped. She closed her eyes, letting the cool afternoon air rush over her tear-stained face, and finally found a sliver of peace in the quiet park.

CHAPTER 7 The Picnic and the Perimeter

The sun had dropped even lower, the golden-hour light fading into heavier, darker blues and greys, and the shadows of the oak trees stretched long across the rusted play equipment.

Tara and Dawn were walking quickly down the paved pathway leading into the playground.

Tara was carefully carrying Joyce’s old silver serving platter, polished bright, with a red-striped doily on it and a massive pile of thick, freshly baked Oreo-stuffed brownies stacked on top. Dawn walked right beside her, awkwardly juggling a two-liter plastic bottle of orange Fanta and a stack of red Solo cups.

Tara spotted Willow and Buffy by the swings and gave a small, relieved sigh.

Tara said, "Hey. We brought reinforcements."

Buffy stopped pushing the swing, and Willow slowly dragged her feet in the woodchips, bringing the swing to a complete halt.

Tara tipped her chin toward an old, weathered, dark green wooden picnic table that sat beneath one of the massive oak trees, its benches worn smooth and grey by years of weather.

Tara said, "Let’s sit."

The four of them gathered around the beat-up wooden table, its peeling green paint flaking off the top, and Tara set the silver platter of brownies in the center.

Willow sat on the bench across from Tara, looking down at the table with her shoulders still slumped, visibly struggling to meet Tara’s eyes after locking her out of the house.

Tara didn’t push her to talk; she just nudged the platter an inch closer to Willow.

Willow slowly reached out, her fingers trembling slightly as she picked up one of the heavy, chocolate-loaded brownies.

She didn’t take a bite right away but brought the brownie up beneath her nose, closed her tear-stained eyes, and took a long, incredibly deep, slow inhale, pulling in the warm, comforting scent of the chocolate and the familiarity of Tara’s baking.

She slowly brushed her lips across the soft, baked top of the brownie, a deeply grounding, sensory motion. Then, she took a slow, deliberate bite, chewing quietly.

Willow opened her eyes and finally lifted her chin to look directly into Tara’s, the defensive walls gone.

Willow said, "I love you, baby... so very much."

Tara’s face softened as the lingering hurt from the bedroom rejection and the slammed door washed away, and she gave Willow a small, deeply devoted smile, reaching across the flaking green table to gently lay her hand over Willow’s.

While Tara and Willow shared that moment, Buffy quietly took over the mechanics of the picnic.

Buffy took the two-liter bottle of orange Fanta from Dawn, twisted the cap off with a sharp crack of carbonation, separated two red Solo cups, and poured the bright, aggressively orange soda into them, handing the first cup down the bench to Dawn.

Dawn didn’t hesitate; she grabbed a massive brownie off the platter and took a huge, entirely un-ladylike teenage bite, chewing happily while balancing her Solo cup.

Buffy poured a cup for herself, sat down on the bench next to Dawn, and took a brownie with her free hand.

She took a bite and sipped the bright orange Fanta, looking relaxed, sitting with her family in the quiet park, but even as she laughed at something Dawn said, her hazel eyes never once stopped moving. She casually scanned the thick brush, checked the rusted merry-go-round, and glanced up into the overhanging branches of the oak trees, following the conversation with her posture coiled tight, the Slayer on high alert and waiting for the other shoe to drop.

The ordinary sounds of the park, the wind in the leaves and the distant hum of traffic, slowly dropped away, leaving an eerie, unnatural silence over the picnic table.

CHAPTER 8 The Ambush and the Spark

The late afternoon sun was fading fast, plunging the park into deep, cool-blue shadow.

Buffy did not turn her head, and she spoke softly, her voice flat and stripped of all emotion.

Buffy said, "Dawn, stay here at the table, and do not run no matter what happens."

Buffy slowly shifted her eyes to Tara.

Buffy said, "Same goes for you."

Willow looked confused as she slowly lowered her half-eaten brownie and set it back on the platter, then looked around the empty park, her breathing picking up.

Buffy slowly stood up from the wooden bench and turned around, her back to the table, and looked far to the left, staring directly into a dense, dark thicket of bushes near the tree line.

Buffy casually folded her arms across her chest and tilted her head slightly, radiating absolute, unbothered Slayer confidence.

Buffy said, "Come out of there, bitch. I know you’re in there."

For a moment nothing happened. Then dead leaves crunched heavily somewhere inside the thicket, branches parted with a dry rustle, and Amy Madison stepped out of the dark bushes and onto the grass.

Amy was wearing a dark leather jacket, and around her neck hung a thick silver chain holding a raw, jagged purple stone that gave off a faint, pulsing purple glow. In her right hand she gripped a pale, carved bone wand set with deep red rubies and garnets.

Amy stopped ten feet away and smiled, a wide, manic, completely unhinged grin.

Amy said, "Buffy... you know I don’t fight with my fists..."

She dragged the final word out into a sharp, venomous hiss.

Amy violently thrust the bone wand forward. There was no visible beam of light, just a massive, concussive wave that distorted the air.

The telekinetic blast hit Buffy square in the chest and launched her backward off her feet, and she flew ten feet through the air, clearing the picnic table, and violently slammed back-first into the thick, solid trunk of a massive oak tree.

There was a sickening, heavy thud of bone and muscle hitting solid wood.

Buffy dropped to the ground, landing hard in the dirt and woodchips. She rolled onto her side, coughing violently, genuinely struggling to push herself up onto her hands and knees.

Willow’s face twisted with raw panic and fury. She slapped her hands flat against the peeling green paint of the table, pushed herself up to her feet, and stepped away from it to place herself firmly between her family and Amy.

Willow brought her hands up, palms facing inward toward each other, squeezing her eyes shut as she desperately tried to focus.

An orange, fiery light slowly began to form between her palms, but it was entirely unstable, flickering wildly and crackling with tiny, erratic sparks of static, and it stuttered, faded to black for a split second, and then flared back up in a jagged burst.

Willow threw her right arm aggressively forward toward Amy.

A wave of wild orange magical fire erupted from Willow’s hand and tore across the grass toward Amy, but it had no precision. The flames spread wide in front of Amy and missed her body entirely.

Amy didn’t even flinch as the flames roared past her, and her manic smile only stretched wider across her face.

Amy casually swept her bone wand to the side, treating Willow’s attack like a minor annoyance, and the wand’s invisible force wrenched the fire aside so that it crashed into the bushes and tree trunks to her right and instantly set the dry brush ablaze.

The deep blue shadows of the park were suddenly illuminated by the aggressive, flickering, violent orange glow of the burning bushes.

Amy stared at Willow through the smoke, the rubies in her wand catching the firelight, her voice dripping with sadistic glee.

Amy said cheerfully, "My turn!"

CHAPTER 9 The Wall of Earth

The chaotic orange light from the burning brush flickered across the heavy blue shadows of the park.

Amy stood amidst the smoke, her eyes wide with sadistic thrill. She pointed her ruby-embedded bone wand directly at Willow.

Amy snapped her wrist forward, and another invisible kinetic wave exploded from the tip of the wand and tore across the playground, ripping up a trench of woodchips and grass in a straight line directly toward Willow’s chest.

Willow did not try to summon her broken, short-circuiting fire again. She widened her stance, planting her feet firmly into the dirt, and dropped her center of gravity to tap directly into the primal, ambient magic of the earth itself.

Willow said, "Mother of earth, hear my call, create a wall!"

Willow threw both of her hands violently upward, palms facing the sky.

Instantly, the ground in front of Willow ruptured. A thick slab of dirt, roots, and jagged rock erupted upward from the earth, forming a six-foot earthen barricade between her and Amy.

A split second later, Amy’s kinetic wave slammed directly into the dirt wall.

The impact went off with a deafening, explosive boom, like a mortar shell hitting a trench.

The kinetic force shattered the earthen wall completely. A blinding cloud of pulverized dirt and clods of soil blew backward over Willow and hid her from view.

When it cleared, Willow was standing unharmed in the center of the debris. Her oversized brown flannel was coated in grey dust, but she hadn’t moved an inch. The earth had absorbed the entire blow.

Willow risked a quick, desperate glance over her left shoulder, looking back at the peeling green picnic table.

Tara had pulled Dawn in against her side when the fire went into the bushes and was holding her tightly, but she was sitting up much straighter now. Tara’s eyes were locked onto Willow, shining with absolute faith and fierce, unyielding pride.

Tara said, "Go get her, honey!"

CHAPTER 10 The Conduit and the Crushing

The orange fire from the burning brush continued to cast wild, flickering shadows across the dark park.

Amy’s smile dropped into a vicious snarl. She thrust her bone wand forward, but this time, instead of pushing, she pulled.

An invisible, gravitational lasso yanked Willow off her feet. She flew forward through the air and slammed chest-first into the hard dirt with a sickening thud that knocked the wind out of her.

Before Willow could even push up, Amy snapped her wand downward. A massive, invisible weight dropped onto Willow’s back and pinned her flat against the earth.

Willow gasped, her fingers digging desperately into the dirt, entirely paralyzed by the sheer crushing pressure of the spell.

Amy reached behind her back and, with a smooth, metallic ring, drew a wicked, deeply curved blade with a pale, carved bone handle.

Amy took a step forward, but she was moving slowly.

Tara watched Willow struggling in the dirt and Amy slowly closing the distance with the curved blade, and she didn’t run, and she didn’t scream.

Tara stepped up onto the wooden bench and then climbed onto the top of the peeling green picnic table. She stood tall in the center of it and spread her arms wide, opening her chest to the dark sky, completely exposing herself.

She took a deep breath, acting as a living, emotional battery of light magic.

Tara’s face was open and completely vulnerable, and tears were already welling in her eyes, but she didn’t wipe them. Her voice carried everything she felt for Willow — the fear, the love, the refusal to let the darkness take her. Her hands trembled slightly at her sides as the song left her body.

Tara sang, her voice clear and beautiful, piercing the heavy silence of the park, "I lived my life in shadow

Never the sun on my face

It didn’t seem so sad, though

I figured that was my place"

Still pinned under the crushing weight, Willow slammed her palms flat into the dirt. A look of fierce, desperate focus hardened on her face. The first threads of orange energy began to flicker weakly around her fingers.

Amy flicked a glance toward the picnic table and let out a short, ugly laugh. She never stopped walking, slow and steady, the bone wand locked on Willow and the curved blade loose at her side.

Amy said, "Oh, that’s adorable. She’s singing. You really think a love song is gonna save you, Will? Here’s how tonight goes. First I take your scalp, because do you have any idea what a witch’s hair goes for down below? Then I gut you. And then I go finish off the Slayer."

Tara sang, her voice gathering power, "Now I’m bathed in light

Something just isn’t right"

Tara sang, the note swelling until it filled the park, raw and soaring, "I’m under your spell

How else could it be

Anyone would notice me

It’s magic I can tell

How you set me free

Brought me out so easily"

As the long, powerful notes rang out, Willow began to push upward against the invisible weight. Orange lightning crackled violently across her body. The physical toll was immediate and brutal. Fresh scrapes tore across her palms and knuckles from the dirt, and a thin cut opened across her forehead from the strain. A heavy stream of blood poured from her nose, mixing with the dust and woodchips. Her arms shook violently as she forced herself from her stomach onto her knees, then planted one scuffed shoe and began to rise. Jagged, brilliant orange lightning arced beneath her skin, reconnecting pathways in visible, painful surges of energy. Blood tracked down her cheeks like crimson tears.

Tara sang, "I saw a world enchanted

Spirits and charms in the air

I always took for granted

I was the only one there

But your power’s shone

Brighter than any I’ve known"

As the song built, Willow planted both feet and began to stand fully upright. The kinetic pressure around her buckled and cracked like shattering glass. She turned her head, locking her blazing orange eyes on Amy, and raised one bloodied hand as the energy surged.

Tara sang, pouring everything she had into the next lines, voice soaring with devotion and power, "I’m under your spell

Nothing I can do

You just took my soul with you

You worked your charm so well

Finally I knew

Everything I dreamed was true

You made me believe"

As Tara sang the final lines, Willow channeled the full power of the song. She slammed her palms forward, and a massive, concentrated blast of orange magical fire and redirected kinetic energy erupted from her hands and tore across the distance. It hit Amy square in the chest just as she tried to close in with the blade and hurled her backward off her feet. Her bone wand shattered in her grip from the overload, the rubies and garnets exploding into shrapnel that cut her arm and face. Amy crashed to the ground, gasping, the dark magic draining out of her in a rapid pulse as the song’s power finished reconnecting Willow and filling her with strength.

Willow stood tall in the flickering firelight, bleeding from multiple wounds, glowing with dangerous orange light, staring down at the defeated Amy.

Willow’s voice came out calm, layered with a booming magical echo.

Willow said, "I think it’s about time I ended you."

CHAPTER 11 The Feedback Loop and the Statue

The orange firelight from the burning brush still flickered across the park, and for a few seconds more the bright, erratic orange lightning kept arcing over Willow’s body, tearing through the deep, unnatural blue shadows around her before it began to fade.

The song-powered blast had already done its work. Amy was on her knees, cut and bleeding from the shrapnel of her own shattered wand, a jagged sliver of bone embedded in her shoulder just below her collarbone and her right hand shredded where the rubies had burst, the dark magic draining from her body in a fading pulse. Willow’s glowing orange eyes snapped back to their normal bright green as the pressure vanished, and she let out a long, shaky breath, blood still streaking her face. Then she turned her back on the wounded Amy, wiping her nose with the back of her hand, and looked toward the picnic table in relief.

Buffy, finally recovered from the tree, limped over and stopped right beside Willow.

Amy’s face was pale, bleeding, and twisted with absolute, unhinged hatred, and she didn’t care about dying; she only wanted to drag Willow down with her.

Amy forced her right arm upward, pointing her bleeding, shredded hand directly at Willow’s unprotected back.

Amy said, "I shall look upon my enemy! I shall look upon her and the dark place will have her soul! Corsheth, take her!"

A violent, swirling beam of pitch-black, soul-stealing energy erupted from Amy’s hand, tearing across the park toward Willow’s spine.

Tara said, "BUFFY!"

Tara grabbed the heavy silver platter, its polished underside shining like a mirror, and hurled it like a discus across the playground straight at the Slayer.

Buffy’s Slayer reflexes snapped into overdrive. She didn’t hesitate, and she didn’t ask questions.

Buffy dropped to her right knee and caught the spinning platter cleanly out of the air. In one fluid motion she flipped it, angling the mirrored underside outward like a shield, and intercepted the beam of dark magic inches before it hit Willow.

The black beam smashed into the mirrored silver and rebounded in a blinding flash of light, tearing back across the woodchips twice as fast, faster than Amy could move, and struck her square in the chest.

Amy said, "NNNOOOOOOOO!"

The transformation was rapid and brutal. A cold, grey, petrifying texture spread outward from the center of Amy’s chest. It crawled up her neck, froze her screaming jaw, washed over her terrified eyes, and locked her outstretched right arm into solid, unmoving rock.

Stone ground and hardened with a heavy, grating sound, and then there was total silence as the orange brush fires slowly began to die out.

Amy Madison was a permanent, grey stone statue, frozen forever on her knees in the dirt, her face locked in a silent scream.

Buffy slowly stood up from her knee, lowered the platter, and stared at the detailed, horrifying stone statue kneeling a few yards away.

Buffy tilted her head to the side, completely deadpan.

Buffy said, "Well. I guess we don’t have to buy a birdbath for the garden anymore."

Willow swayed on her feet as the adrenaline and the magical strain finally caught up with her, and her knees buckled.

Buffy immediately dropped the platter and caught Willow by the arm, holding her up.

Tara jumped down from the picnic table and sprinted across the torn-up woodchips.

Willow weakly stepped out of Buffy’s grip, stumbling forward to meet her.

Willow threw her arms around Tara’s neck, pulling her in, and kissed her deeply, desperately, then buried her bloody, dust-covered face against Tara’s chest.

Tara wrapped her arms tight around Willow’s waist. With a surge of pure, unadulterated relief, she lifted Willow off the ground and spun her around once in the cool dusk air, in the shadows of the old oak trees. She buried her face in Willow’s neck and kissed her right back.

CHAPTER 12 The Anchor and the Garden Ornament

Early the next morning, soft, warm sunlight bathed the Summers backyard, washing away the heavy blue shadows and the violence of the night before.

Willow was sitting sideways across Tara’s lap on the porch swing, with minor cuts and scratches on her forehead, her knuckles, and the tops of her fingers, and the corners of her eyes were still red and puffy from the fight the night before, but she was relaxed, the heavy brown flannel gone, replaced by a soft, comfortable cotton tee. Tara had her arms wrapped securely around Willow’s waist, one hand gently stroking the side of Willow’s face and tracing her jawline.

Tara leaned down, and Willow tilted her head up to catch Tara’s lips, her hand sliding to the back of Tara’s head, and her fingers threading into Tara’s dirty blonde hair as they shared a long, deep, passionate kiss, their heads moving slowly against each other in perfect rhythm.

Over the quiet romance came the loud scrape of heavy stone dragging across grass, along with exaggerated, breathless grunting.

Xander Harris had brought the solid stone statue of Amy Madison over from the park at dawn, wrapped in a moving blanket in the bed of a borrowed work truck, and now he was hauling it the last stretch across the Summers backyard by hand. Buffy had wanted it gone before the first kids showed up at the playground, and before anyone got the bright idea to take a hammer to it.

Xander had dressed for the job in heavy leather work gloves, a yellow hard hat, and a sweat-drenched white sleeveless t-shirt.

Buffy was standing a few feet away, holding a plastic cup of bright orange Fanta, unbothered.

Buffy said, "Be careful, Xander. Don’t break it."

Xander stopped dragging and dropped the heavy stone shoulders of the statue onto the grass with a heavy thud, resting his hands on his knees and panting heavily.

Xander said, "You know... you have super strength. You could... easily carry this..."

Buffy said, "The women did the hard work last night. You can do it, Xan. Almost there. Just a little to the left."

Xander groaned, grabbed the statue by the shoulders again, and went back to dragging it toward the freshly turned dirt of the tomato bed.

Buffy said, "Put your back into it, Harris. Tara made your favorite, low-fat turkey with cucumbers, and it’s strictly a no-statue, no-sandwich policy."

Willow and Tara had broken the kiss, their foreheads resting against each other. They were both smiling, the air between them soft and loving, and when Willow spoke, her voice was barely audible.

Willow said, "The song... our song... it hit me like this massive wave inside of me. I felt like I could hold up the entire world when I heard the sound of your heart coming from your voice."

Tara smiled, her eyes shining with pure, unyielding devotion. She gently brushed a stray strand of red hair behind Willow’s ear.

Tara said, "You don’t ever have to hold up the world by yourself again, Willow. My heart is yours. It always has been. I just needed you to hear it loud enough to bring you home."

Willow’s smile widened, and she leaned in to kiss Tara again.

Buffy said, "There! Right there. Stop."

Xander dropped the statue right into the tomato bed, where it knelt among the freshly planted tomato starters. He stood up straight, ripping off his yellow hard hat and wiping a massive sheet of sweat from his forehead with the back of his leather glove.

He squinted, looking down at the horrifically detailed, screaming stone face of the statue.

Xander said, "Man... this thing looks really familiar. Are you sure you want this weird, screaming gargoyle thing right here in the yard?"

Buffy didn’t smile; she lifted her plastic cup and took a slow, deliberate sip of the bright orange Fanta. She swallowed, her hazel eyes locking onto the petrified face of Amy Madison.

Buffy’s voice went flat and ice-cold.

Buffy said, "It is right where it’s supposed to be. And it’s staying there."

Behind those frozen grey eyes, Amy was still in there, alive and awake, and she wasn’t going anywhere.

Nobody was in any hurry to see Xander go, even though he was getting married in a matter of hours. He peeled off his gloves and dropped onto the porch steps with Tara’s sandwiches, and they lingered out there in the morning sun long after the job was done, just glad to have him around for a little while longer before the suits and the dresses and the rest of the day swept them all away.
