Buffy the Vampire Slayer. Season six, episode 09. Sugar Water.

CHAPTER 1 The Wake-Up Call

That morning in the Summers house, the light was rich and golden, catching on the steam from the cooking and on the dust motes that danced in the beams like slow, sparkling snow. It felt almost too bright and too alive for the way Buffy felt inside.

Tara stood at the stove in a simple cream-colored blouse and a long blue jean skirt, her golden dirty-blonde hair hanging loose. She picked up a large plate of fluffy scrambled eggs and sausage links, caught the corner of a plate of muffins with her other hand, and carried them through to the dining room to set them in the center of the table. Then she went back for a pitcher of orange juice and set it down easily beside them.

Tara put her hands on her hips and looked everything over, then sat down at the head of the table, on Willow's left.

Dawn was already at the table in her oversized sweater of clashing pinks, yellows and blues, her hair still damp from her morning shower.

Willow sat across from Buffy in a plain dark green sweater and dark red lounge pants, her hands wrapped around a black ceramic mug, her expression soft but watchful.

Buffy wore an oversized grey sweatshirt that swallowed her frame, no makeup, her hair unbrushed, and she reached for the pitcher of orange juice Tara had just set down.

Buffy sat perfectly still with the glass pitcher in her hands, her eyes glazed, staring a million miles away, and began to pour. The bright orange juice reached the rim of her cup and kept going, spilling over in a slow cascade and pooling on the dark wood grain of the table, the vivid color stark against the worn surface.

Tara and Dawn said nothing and didn't react at all, because Buffy had done this so many times before.

Willow took a sip from her mug, and her tone was casual, but gently probing.

Willow said, "So… what are we thinking about Lucian?"

The question worked like a tether. Buffy blinked and snapped out of the stare, and when she saw the juice overflowing she tipped the pitcher back and set it down with a thud, then grabbed a paper napkin and dabbed frantically at the puddle.

A flush of pink bloomed across her pale collarbone and crept up her neck into her cheeks, and she shifted in the wooden chair until it creaked, suddenly aware of her own body in a way she hadn't been since before the grave. She dropped the napkin and looked up to meet Willow's eyes, and her defensive walls slammed straight back into place.

Buffy shook her head a little and cleared her throat, and her voice came out slightly defensive.

Buffy said, "I’m not… thinking anything. He’s just around."

Dawn, sitting next to Willow, rolled her eyes and let out a dramatic, hissing sigh of teenage annoyance, slouching lower with her arms crossed so that the legs of her chair scraped the floor.

Tara smiled softly across the table at her.

Tara said, "Well, for someone who dresses like he just walked off the set of a Victorian gothic romance, he actually seems incredibly nice. Very… grounded."

Buffy's shoulders dipped a little at Tara's comment, and a little more color rose to her cheeks.

Tara raised an eyebrow at Buffy's reaction and smiled a little as she poured herself a cup of coffee.

At the sight of the food, Dawn's annoyance vanished. She loaded up her plate, scraping the serving spoon against the platter, and before she took a bite she looked across at Willow with a wide, falsely innocent smile.

Dawn said, "Speaking of nice and grounded boys… can Josh come over tomorrow night? For movie night?"

The dining room went dead quiet for one long moment, with only the ticking of the old clock to fill it. Buffy stayed completely out of the parenting, her face blank as she dabbed at the spill again, even though it was mostly gone.

Willow stopped mid-sip and slowly lowered the mug, looking at Dawn with a deadly serious, unamused expression, until she let out a defeated sigh.

Willow said, "Fine. Yes. He can come over. But you have to keep your room door open at all times when he is here."

She held Dawn's eyes for one more second and added, "And no hands in pants on the first date."

Dawn let out a high-pitched squeal of victory, grabbed her fork, and started shoveling scrambled eggs and sausage into her mouth, cheeks puffed out.

Tara didn't say a word, only sipped her coffee with her eyes dancing over the rim of the mug, and shot Dawn a subtle, knowing wink.

Just as the breakfast table settled into a comfortable, almost normal rhythm, work boots hit the front porch, and the old boards groaned under them.

CHAPTER 2 The Echoes of the Grave

Somewhere down the hall, the front door swung open, and work boots thudded up the hallway with the clink of a tool belt keeping time, the old floorboards trembling a little under every step. Xander pushed into the dining room with his flannel sleeves rolled to the elbow and a bright yellow hard hat tucked under his left arm, bringing the smell of sawdust and leather in to mix with the breakfast.

He walked straight past Dawn, reached over the table with his free hand, snatched a thick sausage link right off the serving platter, and bit it in half with a loud snap, and then he stepped in behind Willow and wrapped both thick arms around her shoulders in a big bear hug, hard hat and all, his voice filling the whole room.

Xander said, "Behold! The working class has arrived, and it requires immediate caloric bribery."

Still hanging on to her, he tipped his chin down beside her ear, chewing.

Xander said, "Morning, Wills."

He let her go and stepped over to Tara, leaning down to plant a loud, smacking kiss on her cheek, and Tara smiled her soft, lopsided smile and reached up to brush a crumb off the front of his flannel with two fingers.

Tara said, "Good morning, Xander. Try using a plate next time?"

Xander spread his hands wide with the other half of the sausage still pinched between his fingers and gave her a wounded look.

Xander said, "Plates slow me down, Tara. Time is money, and money buys more donuts."

He moved around to the other side of the table and stopped right behind Buffy's chair, and all the noise went out of him at once as he rested one big, calloused hand gently on her shoulder and bent to press a soft, lingering kiss to the top of her blonde head, and when he spoke his voice came out low, with the worry sitting just underneath the warmth.

Xander said, "How's the Slayer? Ready to face the world, or should I call in a Scooby intervention? I brought my trusty orange vest for protection."

Buffy didn't look up from the puddle of spilled juice. She kept her eyes on the soggy napkin and forced her face into a stiff mask of calm that didn't convince anybody at the table, letting one thin breath out through her nose as she fought to keep the cold of the grave from showing.

Buffy said flatly, "Just facing breakfast. And maybe a rogue pastry if it threatens world safety."

Xander's hand stayed on her shoulder a second too long, long enough to feel how rock hard the muscles underneath had gone, and he slowly drew it back with one last gentle squeeze.

Xander said, "Call me if you need anything, Buffy. Anything at all. Even if it's just to complain about the weather."

Over the top of Buffy's head he traded a long, worried look with Willow and Tara, and Tara gave him a small nod that said she saw it too. Then Xander scooped a muffin off the plate, put his hand between Dawn's shoulder blades, and steered her down the hall for the school run.

Buffy stood up so fast that her wooden chair scraped hard against the floorboards, and she didn't look at anyone.

Buffy said, "I'll be right back."

She walked out of the dining room with her head down before anyone could ask if she was okay, and went stiffly up the stairs.

Upstairs, Buffy pushed the bathroom door shut behind her, and the moment it closed, the muffled, ordinary sound of Willow and Tara talking downstairs cut off. The little room went so still it felt like being deep underwater. She turned the metal lock with a sharp click that echoed off the tile and, without looking in the mirror, dropped onto the closed lid of the toilet, the cold of the porcelain coming up through her dark jeans. She put her face straight down into her hands, hunched forward over her knees.

Then a low hum began to build in the air around her. It wasn't one sound but a dozen whispering voices laid over one another, scratching against the inside of her skull and talking too fast for her to catch a single word, a buzzing hive of static that felt wet and close and seemed to be inside her head and right behind her at the same time. Buffy squeezed her eyes shut and pressed the heels of both hands hard against her ears, but it didn't help, because the sound was coming from inside her. Her whole body curled in on itself on the lid, as though she could make herself small enough to slip out from under it.

Then, as suddenly as it had started, the buzzing cut off and left nothing but dead silence. Somewhere in the house a board settled with a soft creak. Buffy slowly opened her eyes and lowered her hands to her knees, while the bathroom sat perfectly still around her.

Then a woman's voice came, a wet, raspy whisper so close it felt like lips right over her right shoulder.

The voice said, "From beneath you, it devours."

Buffy gasped, and her head snapped up in raw panic, but there was no one there. She was completely alone in the small room and had never been more terrified, sweat beading along her forehead as her fingers clamped down on the edge of the seat so hard the porcelain creaked.

She didn’t move. She stayed hunched on the lid with her arms wrapped tight around herself, trembling from head to toe, too scared to look over her shoulder or even turn her head. The minutes crawled by in the silence while she sat there shaking, waiting for her heart to slow.

CHAPTER 3 The Cover-Up and the Garden

A little later, the breakfast dishes were done and the house had gone quiet again. It had been more than twenty minutes since Buffy left the table, long enough for Tara's brow to draw together with worry and for Willow, who had felt it too, to rise stiffly from her seat. Now the two of them came up the stairs together, their footsteps soft on the worn treads. Tara stepped up to the bathroom door first, tipped her head toward the wood, and knocked twice, softly.

Tara asked gently, "Buffy? Sweetie, are you okay in there?"

On the other side of the door, Buffy was standing now, staring into the mirror above the sink, her breath coming jagged and uneven. She looked down at her hands, which were still shaking, and gripped the thick edges of the porcelain until the tremors quieted by sheer will.

She closed her eyes and took one long, shuddering breath that filled her chest. When she opened them again she slowly made the muscles of her face pull the corners of her mouth upward into a hollow, dismissive half-smile with nothing behind it. Then she turned the cold tap on, splashed the freezing water up onto her face a few times, and shut it off again with a squeak of the old metal handle, the water dripping off her chin.

The lock clicked and the door opened. Buffy stood in the doorway with both hands shoved deep into the front pocket of her sweatshirt, so they wouldn't see the tremors, holding on to the half-smile she had just practiced in the mirror.

Buffy said, "Yeah. Fine. Just… zoned out on the throne for a minute. Lost track of time."

Willow didn't buy the excuse for a second. The dark circles under Buffy's eyes looked worse than they had at breakfast, and her skin had gone a sick kind of pale. She and Tara traded a brief, silent look.

Instead of pushing for an answer, Tara simply stepped forward and wrapped her arms around Buffy, and a second later Willow came in from the other side, so that the three of them stood locked together in a tight, protective hug in the middle of the hallway, and Tara pressed her lips gently to Buffy's cheek in a full kiss of warmth and love.

Buffy stood stiff inside the hug with her hands still buried in the sweatshirt pocket, and she didn't hug them back, but she closed her eyes tight and leaned in just enough to feel them, soaking up the warmth of their bodies to chase off the cold of the grave that still clung to her bones.

Willow rested her chin on Buffy's shoulder.

Willow said softly, "We’re going to tackle the backyard today. It’s a complete thorny jungle out there. Go get changed into something you don't mind ruining."

Buffy nodded, a mechanical little dip of her chin, and stepped back out of the hug with the half-smile still pasted on. Tara let her hand slide down Buffy's arm and gave her elbow one light squeeze as she let go.

Tara said, "We’ll meet you downstairs."

Buffy went to her room, and Tara and Willow watched her go before they walked down to the master bedroom at the end of the hall, which had been Joyce's room and was theirs now, to change into their outside clothes.

Out in the backyard, the midday sun was bright and harsh, and a small battered radio sat on the back porch playing Loreena McKennitt’s “The Mummers’ Dance,” while somewhere beyond the fence a neighbor's mower hummed along underneath it.

The garden was roughly fifteen yards long by five wide and completely overrun: dead brown winter weeds and thorny vines tangled through crabgrass gone wild. The whole yard smelled of damp earth, dead grass and old leaves.

Buffy had traded the sweatshirt for an old faded grey t-shirt and her worn dark jeans, Willow had pulled on an old t-shirt and comfortable pants, and Tara had swapped her blouse for an old long-sleeved shirt and laced a pair of work boots on under her skirt, so that all three of them were ready for the dirt to find them.

The three of them stood in the tall grass looking at the mess. Willow's fingers twitched at her side in a quick, hungry flex toward the weeds before she caught herself, though her voice still carried a hint of dark temptation.

Willow said, "You know, with one minor localized-decay spell, I could turn all of this into nutrient-rich ash in about twelve seconds."

Tara turned her head and gave her a look, gentle but firm, holding the boundary without ever raising her voice.

Tara said, "Will… no shortcuts."

Willow sighed dramatically and pouted, then broke it with a smile and waved both hands in surrender.

Willow said, "I know. I was only kidding, Tara. I didn't mean it. Hands in the dirt. It builds character. Or callouses. Probably both."

Tara's brow furrowed with worry for a second, but she nodded. The two of them fetched trowels and leather gloves from the side of the house and knelt side by side along the bed, loosening the stubborn roots one at a time, quiet and steady, and together.

Buffy was a different story, because she wasn't gardening, she was going to war. She grabbed deep-rooted clumps of crabgrass barehanded and ripped them out in single brutal yanks that showered dirt and dead roots over her boots and her old grey t-shirt. When she hefted a bag of damp yard waste onto her shoulder, it left a smear of dark mud across her back and up the side of her neck, which she ignored as she threw the bag onto the compost pile with a thud that shook the fence.

Willow sat back on her heels and wiped the sweat off her forehead with the back of her wrist, watching Buffy tear a thorny bush out of the ground by brute force, and grinned up at her, keeping her tone light but careful.

Willow said, "Hey, Buff? You know you're supposed to pull the weeds, right? Not physically intimidate them into submission. You look like you just lost a wrestling match with the backyard."

Buffy stopped and looked down at her dirt-caked clothes, her muddy arms and the pile of wreckage she had made, and for the first time that day one corner of her mouth tugged up into a small, real smirk.

Buffy said, "They started it."

Tara laughed softly and patted the loose soil flat in front of her knees with her gloved palm.

Tara said, "It’s working, though. There is something nice about just… fixing a small piece of the earth. Making room for things to breathe again."

Buffy let out a long, tired breath as the fight drained out of her, and she sat down on the back porch steps and leaned her head against the railing, eyes closed, the ache in her arms finally loud enough to drown out the buzzing in her head.

Lucian's voice came from the other side of the fence, low and warm.

Lucian said, "Need another set of hands?"

Buffy opened her eyes. Lucian was standing on the other side of the low wooden fence in his dark, structured Victorian clothes, the silver raven buckle at his belt catching the sun and his long black hair tied back. He looked completely out of place in a bright suburban backyard, but in one hand he held a pair of leather work gloves, because he had come to help.

Tara looked up from the dirt and waved him over, knocking her gloves together as she sat up on her knees, and Buffy shot a look at Willow, who smiled shyly and shrugged her shoulders once.

Willow said, "We invited him over, Buffy."

Buffy sighed and looked at them both, then turned her gaze back to Lucian across the yard and muttered, more to herself than anyone.

Buffy said, "Of course they did."

Lucian hooked one long leg over the low fence and swung himself across, then rolled his embroidered sleeves up to the elbow and tucked his shirt tails into his belt, making it clear he meant to work.

Tara smiled up at him from her knees.

Tara said, "Lucian! Perfect timing. Come help us be terrible at manual labor."

CHAPTER 4 The Break and the Catalyst

Later that afternoon, the shadows had started to stretch long across the grass, and the yard was beginning to look like a real garden again, with patches of dark, cleared soil showing between the weeds that were left.

Lucian had taken off his outer shirt during the heavy lifting and folded it over the porch railing, and he worked now in a fitted black tank top. With the worst of it done, the four of them gathered around the back porch steps, sweaty and covered in soil.

Tara came out through the back door with a tray holding a sweating pitcher of lemonade and a plate of soft sugar cookies, set it on the top step, and poured the glasses, handing one to Lucian and one to Buffy.

Willow picked up a cookie, took a bite, and slid the whole plate across in front of Buffy.

Willow said, "Eat up, Buffy."

Then she looked up at Lucian and locked eyes with him, her tone light but deliberately protective, because she was laying down the rules for taking care of the Slayer.

Willow said, "She burns through energy like a blast furnace. Slayers have a radically higher metabolic rate than a normal human. If she doesn't take in a massive amount of calories, she crashes hard."

Lucian took a slow sip of his lemonade and set the glass down with care before he answered. He understood exactly what she was doing: she was handing him the owner's manual, because he was stepping inside the circle. He gave her a slow, respectful nod.

Lucian said, "I’ll remember that."

Buffy grabbed a cookie and looked back and forth between the two of them, dry and worn out.

Buffy said, "Yes. Which is why Mom—I mean, Willow—is finally trusting me with the incredible responsibility of walking to the end of the block by myself."

Willow bumped Buffy's shoulder playfully with her own, and a real smile passed between them while the sun sat warm on the steps and nobody was in a hurry to move, until Willow turned to Lucian, still chewing.

Willow said, "Okay, where did you learn to dig like that? Because that was some serious, like, industrial—"

Tara said, "Backhoe."

Willow said, "Backhoe! Yes. You’re a human backhoe."

Lucian smiled a little and turned his glass slowly on the step.

Lucian said, "A few summers hauling stone. It stays with you."

Tara watched him with a soft, knowing look.

Tara said, "You like it, though. Working with your hands."

Lucian said, "It’s quiet. Nothing in the dirt wants anything from you."

Buffy's eyes had drifted to the old rosebushes along the fence, overgrown and half wild and tangled in with the weeds.

Buffy said, "Mom planted those roses. Our first summer here. I kind of let them go."

Lucian followed her gaze to the fence and looked at the roses for a long moment.

Lucian said, "They’re not gone. Cut them back, and they’ll come back."

Buffy looked at the roses a little longer while the quiet sat easy between them all, until Tara brushed the sugar off her fingers and clapped her hands once on her knees.

Tara said, "Alright, we still have to move the fertilizer before we can officially claim victory over the yard."

They set their glasses down, and Buffy and Lucian walked over to a stack of thick plastic bags of compost and manure. Buffy squatted at one end of the top bag, worked her fingers underneath it, and nodded across at him.

Buffy said, "Grab the other side. On three."

They both gripped the plastic, and Buffy counted it off.

Buffy said, "One… two… three!"

They heaved the bag upward, and the cheap, sun-baked plastic gave way with a loud, violent rip. The bottom blew clean out, and a thick cloud of dry, dark brown manure dust exploded over both of them at once. Buffy gasped, coughing, and dropped her side of the torn bag, her blonde hair, her pale face and her grey t-shirt all coated in an awful layer of it, while Lucian looked just as bad, his black tank top and his face smeared with it.

For a second nobody moved while the dust settled. Then Buffy let out a snort, and then a few more, until they turned into a real laugh, not her hollow, sarcastic sound but a caught-off-guard, full-chested laugh at how ridiculous it all was. Lucian coughed into his fist and wiped his face with the back of his wrist, which only smeared the dirt worse.

His voice came out low and dry.

Lucian said, "Well. That’s one way to become one with the earth."

They both picked up heavy metal hoes and started working the manure into the topsoil in a steady rhythm, Lucian matching her pace without trying to lead, and when Buffy laughed again he glanced over at her once, as if to make sure she was still right there in it with him, while over by the porch Tara and Willow stood side by side and stared, amazed at how easily the two of them worked together and at the laughter that kept coming out of Buffy.

When the work was done, Tara scooped Lucian's shirt off the porch rail on her way in and tucked it into the laundry basket without even thinking about it.

Afterward, Buffy and Lucian stood a little awkwardly in the hallway just inside the open back door, both of them streaked head to toe with manure dust and sweat, dirt clinging to their hair, their faces and their clothes, and they were still breathing hard, from the work and from the sudden quiet that had settled between them, when Lucian glanced up toward the stairs.

Lucian asked, "Mind if I use the shower upstairs?"

Buffy wiped a streak of dirt off her cheek with the back of her thumb, suddenly very aware of him standing there, and her voice came out a little lower than she meant it to.

Buffy said, "Only if you promise not to track the entire backyard into the drain."

Lucian gave her a small, appreciative smile and headed up the wooden stairs, his boots thudding softly on the treads, and on the third step he paused with one hand on the banister and looked back down at her over his shoulder, not pushing, only checking that she was still there, before he went on up.

Buffy waited at the bottom of the steps and stared up the empty staircase, and with him gone the warmth of the afternoon went too, because the laughing and the sun and the work had held the grave off for a few hours, and now it was coming back, her breath going quick and thin as the icy cold clawed its way up her throat again and panic rose with it. She needed something to hold her down to the earth, and the only warm thing she could think of was him, and with her jaw clenched she waited one more moment, and then quietly, deliberately followed him up the stairs.

CHAPTER 5 Fire Under the Water

Upstairs, the shower was already running, and the rush of the water carried through the solid wooden bathroom door, while on the hallway floor just outside it lay Lucian's manure-streaked black tank top and dark trousers in a rough heap, where he had passed them out through the door for the wash before he stepped under the water.

Buffy's pale hand eased the door open, reached inside, and flicked off the overhead light, leaving only the weak daylight through the small frosted window above the shower, and she stepped in and locked the door behind her with a soft click. Then she struck a wooden match, and the sharp smell of sulfur mixed with the rising steam as, one by one, she lit the thick white candles on the counter, along the edge of the tub and on the tiled windowsill. Last, she reached over to the small radio on the counter and switched it on, and “Sugar Water” by Cibo Matto came up low under the sound of the water, the slow, heavy bassline rolling through the steam.

Behind the blurred, steamy glass of the shower door, Lucian went perfectly still with the water running down over his broad shoulders, watching her in quiet awe as the warm flicker of the candles slowly filled the room.

Buffy turned and met his eyes through the wet glass, her face set with a terrified kind of resolve and a boldness she had made up her mind to, because she needed his warmth more than she was afraid of what came next, and without a word she began to undress, slowly and on purpose, every movement meant to shake him, watching his face the whole time and reading it, and when she saw his breath catch and his hands go still, she kept going.

She pulled the manure-streaked grey t-shirt up over her head in one deliberate motion and let it fall, then slid her worn jeans down her legs, bending forward at the waist so the curve of her lower back showed for a moment before she straightened again and stepped out of them, and standing tall, she raised her left hand with her white panties hooked on her index and middle fingers and held them there for one long, loaded moment, staring straight at him through the steam and the glass with a flat, challenging look, before she let them slip off those two fingers to the floor beside her.

The steam and the candle shadows softened the shape of her, the curve of her hip only half seen. Inside the glass, the falling spray caught the flame light like drops of amber.

She opened the heavy glass door and stepped into the shower with him, and the hot water hit her skin at once, carrying the dark streaks of backyard dirt down to the silver drain in thin brown rivulets while the steam thickened around them.

They stood close under the spray, and Buffy reached up slowly and laid her palms flat on his chest first, and then on his shoulders, the water running between her fingers and his skin, and she leaned up into him and kissed him, slow at first, familiar and testing, her lips pressing a little deeper with each kiss as her hands slid up his arms to the back of his neck, her fingertips tracing the wet skin there, until his hands found her waist and drew her in against him and there was no space left between them.

The kiss deepened by degrees, their heads tilting and their breath catching and passing between them in the small spaces between, her fingers tangling up into his wet hair while his palms slid down the long line of her back, and every move they made stirred the steam and sent the candlelight skittering across the drops on their skin.

The kiss turned urgent, and Buffy lifted her left leg and wrapped it around his waist, pressing her whole body against his, and her left shoulder dipped down between them as her hand slid lower, and then she gasped softly, her breath shaking, as their bodies came together.

She began to move against him, slow, rising and sinking, her breath loud and rhythmic in the small tiled room, her eyes locked on his and her lips parted, a shudder running through every breath that left her, and his face answered hers, his eyes going half-lidded and his mouth opening on the same breath as the awe in him gave way to something deeper while the water poured down over them both.

Lucian brought both hands up to her face and cupped her cheeks, gentle but sure, and drew her into a deep, slow kiss, their mouths moving together while she kept the same slow rhythm and his thumbs stroked along her wet cheekbones as he held her there.

Then he let his back slide down the wet tile wall and brought her down with him, so that she ended up straddling him on the shower floor with her knees out wide and her feet planted one on each side of his hips for balance, her hands going to the back of his head and her fingers gripping into his hair as she pulled him in close against her chest with every motion.

The rhythm built until she pulled his head back by the hair with both hands so she could see his face, her breath completely out of control now, loud, shuddering gasps breaking into soft moans she couldn't hold in, and her eyes stayed locked on his even as they went hazy at the edges with what was building in her, while the same climbing intensity showed in his face and his eyes never once left hers.

Then it broke over her, and her whole body went tight and shuddered hard against him as her breath tore loose into one long, uncontrolled moan, and she gripped his shoulders with both hands as the wave crested and rolled through her.

Under her fingers, the blood rushed up and pooled beneath his skin, swelling for a moment before it settled into dark, heavy purple bruises in the exact shape of her fingertips, and Lucian's eyes flew wide in shock as a sharp, ragged gasp of real pain tore out of him, his jaw clenching tight and the muscles of his neck standing out hard.

The sound snapped Buffy straight out of the haze, and when she looked down at her hands and saw the fresh bruises she had just pressed into him, panic flooded her eyes and her whole body went rigid, her shoulders locking and a sharp breath catching in her throat as she snatched her hands back as though she had touched a hot stove and scrambled up off him, pressing herself flat against the opposite wall of the shower to put as much space between them as she could, her hands shaking in front of her. The Slayer was gone in an instant, and all that was left was a frightened girl, and her voice trembled and cracked as the tears came pouring out with it.

Buffy said, "I’m sorry... I’m sorry, I didn’t mean to. I didn’t mean to hurt you."

She wrapped her arms tight around herself with the water running down over her shaking shoulders, looking like a trapped animal about to bolt out through the glass door.

Lucian didn't touch his shoulders, and he didn't even look at them. He got to his feet and stepped slowly toward her through the heavy spray, reached out, and laid his warm, wet hand along the side of her face, his thumb stroking slowly across her cheek to wipe away the mix of hot shower water and cold tears, his face calm and steady on hers.

Buffy closed her eyes tight and let the whole weight of her head sink into his palm, a shattered, exhausted breath leaving her as the wall she had been holding up finally came all the way down while the water poured over them both, and Lucian held her by the arms and they slowly sank together to the floor of the shower, where he held her without saying anything at all, and Buffy leaned into him, trembling softly in his arms, and closed her eyes.

CHAPTER 6 The Watcher’s Exit

Across town, the late afternoon sun had begun to dip low over Sunnydale, and the street outside the Magic Box was quiet.

Inside, the cheerful ding of the cash register broke the quiet of a mostly empty shop, where the late sun caught the dust in long golden beams. Anya stood behind the glass counter in her form-fitting blouse, happily stacking a thick pile of crisp twenty-dollar bills.

Over by the arcane texts, Giles stood in his tweed jacket, placing a few personal things into a small cardboard box: an old brass magnifying glass and a worn leather-bound journal. He looked older today, as though the weight of the last few years had come down on him all at once.

He carried the box over to the counter and set it down, then took off his glasses and began cleaning them with his handkerchief, slowly, almost nervously.

Giles said, "Anya. I need to speak with you regarding the administrative future of the shop."

Anya didn't look up from her money, her fingers flying over the green bills as she answered.

Anya said, "If it’s about the markup on the white sage, I refuse to apologize. The margins are fantastic, and frankly, local warlocks will pay anything for convenience."

Giles sighed, the lines on his face sitting deeper than usual, as though the words cost him real effort to lift.

Giles said heavily, "It’s not about the white sage. I am leaving, Anya."

Anya's fingers froze mid-count with one bill hovering in the air, and confusion and something deeper crossed her face.

Anya said, "Leaving for lunch? Or leaving to go do something British and unnecessarily complicated?"

Giles's voice stayed steady but heavy as he held her eyes and gave her the truth.

Giles said, "I am moving back to England. Permanently."

Anya slowly lowered the money to the glass, and for once the blunt ex-demon had nothing at all to say. Giles looked down at the counter, folded the handkerchief over once, and went on quietly.

Giles said, "I’ve taught Buffy everything she needs to know. She is… she is surviving, in her own way. But my presence here has become more of a crutch than a guide. It is time for me to retire."

He put his glasses back on and looked around the dusty little shop he had built, with a great deal of fondness and sorrow in the look.

Giles said, "I have spoken with my solicitors. I am transferring the deed and the operational rights of the Magic Box to you and Xander."

Anya's eyes went wide as the part of her that loved money ran straight into the part of her that had just been handed real grief. She leaned against the counter with both hands flat on the bills, suddenly looking very small, and her voice came out quiet and stripped of its usual bravado.

Anya said, "You’re… you’re giving me the store? All of the inventory? The retail space?"

Giles gave a small nod.

Giles said, "Yes. Though, as the silent partner, I will naturally be taking a fifteen percent gross profit cut each month to fund my retirement in Bath."

Anya's face twitched, because the fifteen percent clearly hurt her, but the sadness was winning out over the money.

Anya asked, "What are Buffy and the others going to say?"

Giles looked down at his box and traced its edge with one tired finger while the dust hung in the window light.

Giles said, "They usually stop by the shop on Mondays for research. I suppose… I suppose I will find out on Monday."

CHAPTER 7 The Heart Boxers and the Resurgence

Back at the Summers house, later, the upstairs bathroom door unlocked and swung open, and a cloud of warm steam rolled out into the cool, shadowed hallway as Lucian stepped out first with a white towel wrapped securely around his waist and his wet black hair slicked back off his face.

Buffy stood just behind him, wrapped tightly in her own towel, her blonde hair dripping and clinging to her neck and shoulders, and her hazel eyes were clearer than they had been in months, the dead, far-off glaze from breakfast gone, so that she looked present, and fiercely grounded in her own skin.

Willow was leaning against the doorframe of the master bedroom with her arms crossed over her chest and a huge, unapologetic, knowing smirk on her face, and she held out a neat stack of freshly laundered clothes, where sitting right on top of Lucian's folded outer shirt, his dark pants and his black tank top was a pair of bright red, heart-covered boxer shorts, and her grin spread even wider.

Willow said, "Tara did a rush load of laundry for you two. Figured you wouldn't want to put the manure-pants back on. She said to tell you the boxers are a loaner from Xander’s emergency stash, but honestly, I think they really pull your whole gothic-brooding aesthetic together."

Lucian looked down at the bright red hearts, entirely unfazed, and calmly took the stack of clothes from her, his mouth quirking into a small, real smile with the warmth showing plainly in his eyes, and his voice came out deadpan.

Lucian said, "Tell Tara I appreciate the structural support."

Willow bounced a little on her toes, already turning toward the stairs.

Willow said, "Will do. We're about to start figuring out dinner downstairs if you guys want to—"

Lucian cut in gently before she could finish.

Lucian said, "Let me cook. You and Tara spent all afternoon in the dirt, and you've been managing the house. Take a break. I’ll make dinner."

Willow's smirk softened at once into a look of real, surprised appreciation, and she nodded.

Willow said, "Yeah. Okay. That sounds really nice, actually."

Lucian headed downstairs to get dressed in the downstairs bathroom, his bare feet padding softly on the wood, and as soon as he was out of earshot Willow turned to Buffy with one eyebrow raised, playful and expectant, leaning in close with her hands starting to flutter in the air between them.

Willow said, "Well? How was it, you know, I mean the thing?"

Buffy let out a short, sarcastic huff of breath and hitched her towel up tighter under her arms, trying to play the whole thing off.

Buffy said, "Well what? It was… a thing. A very naked, very impulsive thing. Don’t go reading too much into it. It’s not like we’re picking out china patterns or anything."

Willow studied her best friend for a long moment as the smirk melted into something warm and knowing, her eyes crinkling at the corners with quiet understanding and a little relief, and she didn't push, only gave a slow nod with a small smile that said she saw right through the sarcasm.

Willow said softly, "Mm-hmm."

Buffy slipped past her into her own bedroom and pulled the door softly shut behind her.

CHAPTER 8 The Spaghetti Dinner and the Anchor

That night in the Summers dining room, the warm yellow overhead light made the room feel like a safe, lived-in family home, the table fully set with proper plates and silverware and the smell of rich tomato sauce, roasted garlic bread and melting cheese filling the room, as Lucian set a big ceramic bowl of steaming spaghetti in thick sauce down in the center of the table and wiped his hands once on a kitchen towel, then caught the sound of footsteps on the stairs and straightened, without making a show of it, as Willow, Tara and Dawn sat down.

Willow's eyes flicked to the dark purple fingertip bruises on both of his shoulders, plain to see above the black tank top, and then she let it go, because he was standing there whole and breathing and setting down a bowl of spaghetti, and that was what counted.

Buffy came down the wooden stairs and walked into the dining room, and the whole table went quiet for a second, because she had changed into a fitted dark red top and clean dark jeans, her hair brushed and styled and a little makeup on, and for the first time since she was pulled out of the grave she actually looked alive. Tara and Willow shared a long, silent look of victory, Willow letting out a long, slow breath as her shoulders finally came down and Tara's eyes softening with quiet joy, while Dawn just stared, pleasantly surprised.

Buffy took her seat next to Lucian, pulled her chair in smoothly, and glanced at the steaming bowl.

Buffy said, "Smells good."

They started eating, silverware clinking against plates and drinks being poured, and for a few minutes the warm, easy conversation made it feel like a completely normal, happy family dinner, with Lucian eating alongside the rest of them, not playing host, just there at the table, passing the garlic bread toward Dawn without being asked and reaching over quietly to refill Tara's water glass, and once he caught Buffy's eye beside him with a small nod that said he was still here, and no speech was needed.

Then Dawn took a bite of the spaghetti and stopped chewing, and her fork came slowly back down to her plate with a faint clink as her eyes went wide and filled up fast with heavy tears, a sharp hitch catching in her breath as the taste hit her like a blow to the chest, and her voice cracked down to barely a whisper.

Dawn said, "It tastes… it tastes just like Mom’s."

Every sound at the table stopped, the chewing and the clinking and the talk, and the warmth drained out of the room as the old, cold weight of grief came down over it. Buffy's relaxed shoulders stiffened at once with her walls threatening to fly right back up, Willow and Tara traded a quick, pained glance as their hands found each other under the table, and Lucian sat perfectly still while the whole table held its breath.

Lucian set his fork down gently, the clink deliberate rather than startled, and turned his full attention to Dawn, his elbows coming off the table and his shoulders opening toward her as he looked at her with quiet, grounded understanding.

Lucian said softly, "I lost my mother when I was seventeen."

The room went dead silent, and Buffy turned to look at him with her eyes wide, caught completely off guard, while Lucian kept his eyes on Dawn and went on in the same low, even voice.

Lucian said, "It was an infection. Started on her skin and spread to her organs before the doctors could figure out how to stop it. I know exactly how heavy that empty chair feels."

He looked around the quiet table, at Tara holding Willow's hand tightly and at Dawn trying so hard to be brave, and last of all at Buffy, and his gaze softened on her, one hand resting flat on the table between them, steady and still, not reaching for anyone.

Lucian said, "I am incredibly sorry for your loss. But looking at this house, and looking at how fiercely you all hold on to each other… Joyce must have been a truly remarkable mother to build a family unit this strong."

Dawn let out a shaky, trembling breath, and a single heavy tear slipped down her cheek before she wiped it away with the cuff of her sleeve and nodded, comforted, and the tension broke, and they spent the rest of dinner quietly sharing soft, fond memories of Joyce while the air in the house grew lighter than it had been in months.

Later, with the plates mostly empty and the weight of the day finally settled, Tara looked across the table at Buffy with a gentle, warm expression and reached out to touch the back of Buffy's hand lightly with her fingertips.

Tara said softly, "It’s really nice seeing you like this tonight, Buffy."

Buffy's mouth tipped into a crooked half-smile as she tried to play it off, sarcastic, but not cruel.

Buffy said, "Like what? Stomach bursting with delicious spaghetti?"

Willow opened her mouth to jump in with something playful, a grin already forming, but before she could get a word out Tara raised her other hand palm-up toward her in a gentle 'wait,' her eyes staying on Buffy with quiet seriousness, and Tara's shoulders rose and fell with one slow breath before she spoke from the heart, still soft, but deeply earnest.

Tara said, "Like someone who’s remembering she’s allowed to be here. To sit in the light for a little while instead of staying in the dark all by herself. You don’t have to carry everything alone anymore. We’ve got you."

Buffy didn't have a sarcastic comeback this time, and she only looked at Tara for a long moment while something vulnerable moved across her face.

Before the moment could get too heavy, Lucian stood up and stepped around behind Buffy's chair, and without a word he slid one strong arm under her thighs and the other behind her back and lifted her up against his chest as if she weighed nothing, and everyone's eyes went wide, Willow freezing with her wine glass halfway to her lips before she broke into a big, warm smile, Tara's face softening into pure affection, and Dawn looking stunned for a moment as a complicated mix of feelings crossed her face.

Buffy wrapped her arms around his neck to steady herself, and he held her securely as he carried her out of the room, up the stairs and down the dark hall, walked into her bedroom, and gently kicked the heavy wooden door shut behind them.

A second later, the bright line of yellow light under the door clicked off into total darkness.

CHAPTER 9 The Confusion Ray

In the early hours of the next morning, across town in the basement of the house the Trio rented, fluorescent tubes buzzed over a cramped room with exposed heating pipes along its damp concrete walls. It was cluttered with glowing monitors, power tools, tangled wires and expensive fantasy memorabilia, and wooden stairs led up to a bolted metal door. The air smelled of ozone, hot metal and wet concrete.

A crackling buzz of raw electricity came off the device on the workbench, a rifle slapped together from parts that looked as though they had come off a Radio Shack shelf, all duct tape, loose wires and mismatched pieces, with a cut crystal fixed to the end of the barrel. Warren was hunched over it with his sleeves shoved up to the elbows, arrogant and manic, while Jonathan stood right beside him, sweating and eager to impress, and Andrew stood with them, cluelessly enthusiastic about all of it.

Andrew's eyes kept drifting toward the bottom of the stairs, and his face went pale with guilt. He stared down at the concrete and whispered to himself, barely audible.

Andrew said, "I can still smell the perfume."

Warren didn't look up from the wiring, but he knew exactly where Andrew was staring, and he let a slow smile curl at the corner of his mouth.

Warren said, "Hard work, carrying her all the way to the river. So cold and limp."

Andrew squeezed his eyes shut, and the memory of that night hit him hard and fast: Katrina lying dead and perfectly still on the cold concrete floor, and Warren standing over her with his face twisted, holding a heavy glass champagne bottle tight by the neck.

Then the present slammed back into place, and Andrew shook his head hard as though he could knock the picture loose. He turned back to the device with a bright, overeager smile pasted on that never reached his haunted eyes, and one more quick breath gave away how hard he was working to bury it.

Warren straightened up, grinning, his eyes wide and manic, and spread his hands over the device.

Warren said, "Phase One: Operational. The Confusion Ray."

Jonathan wiped the sweat off his brow with a trembling hand, shifting his weight from foot to foot, and his voice shook.

Jonathan said, "Are you sure this is a good idea, Warren? Causing mass confusion downtown? The Slayer…"

Warren didn't even look up from the wiring, only flicked a hand at him.

Warren said, "The Slayer is neutralized, Jonny. She's got her own little existential crisis brewing. And she can't stop all of us. This is just a test run. A distraction."

Andrew pushed his glasses up his nose, missing the gravity of it completely, and bounced on his heels.

Andrew said, "It's like The Joker setting off an ice-bomb at the Gotham City Art Gala! Subtly chaotic!"

Warren rounded on him with a sneer.

Warren snapped, "It's not subtle, you idiot, it's effective. This device fires an intense, low-level blue beam from the crystal, designed to amplify existing emotional distress and generate paranoia across the city's power grid. We'll have every person in Sunnydale fighting over parking spots by morning."

Jonathan swallowed hard.

Jonathan asked, "And Phase Two?"

Warren flipped a red switch on the side of the rifle, and it let out a low, menacing hum that vibrated up through the floor as his grin spread wider.

Warren said, "Phase Two is my time to shine. While the Slayer is busy playing referee, I get my hands on the Orbs of Nezzla'khan… And then, who's the man?"

Andrew nodded eagerly.

Andrew said, "You are, Warren. Like Doctor Doom, but… slightly less green."

Warren glared at him with cold, flat malice before he turned back to his machine.

Warren said, "Just keep the calibration steady. We launch at oh-seven-hundred hours."

The launch was set for Monday morning, at seven o'clock, while the town was on its way to work and school.

The vibrating hum of the device swelled louder and louder, until it drowned out every other sound in the room.
