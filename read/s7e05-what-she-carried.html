What She Carried

Download this script (.txt)

Buffy the Vampire Slayer. Season seven, episode 05. What She Carried.

CHAPTER 1 After the Porch

Outside the Summers house, cool moonlight laid blue-silver across the porch boards and the grass. The porch light cut hard shadows. Night insects ticked in the yard. The property barrier still hummed low under everything — the ward that turned any hostile step into a rising tone in the ear and a tilting, dizzy ground.

Buffy sat on the porch stairs in the dirt-streaked white engagement dress, one thin spaghetti strap slipped off her left shoulder. Her blonde hair was loose and wind-tousled, her mascara smudged under glassy eyes, her bare feet dirty from the grass. Her breathing stayed shallow and controlled — the kind you do when you’re refusing to fall apart.

Lucian was half-conscious on the porch boards at her back. He still wore the black long-sleeve sweater and black slacks he had changed into for dinner, now rumpled and dusty at the elbows, with his combat boots. The large raven tattoo on his left shoulder and pec showed where the sweater’s neckline had been dragged askew. His jaw was swelling purple, and his lower left lip was split. His long black hair was messy from the uppercut. The almost-healed wound at his lower left side was tender from the fall — not screaming, not forgotten.

Spike was already gone, his black duster swallowed by the trees at the edge of the front yard, and he did not come back.

Spike’s words from the porch still sat in Buffy’s chest like something with edges: Warren, Jonathan, and Andrew, and Willow with blood on her hands. He had said she put the Trio in the ground, and he had said it before the fists, before Lucian went down, before Willow’s eyes had lit. It had not been a rumor; it had been a parting knife he left in her on purpose. He had not said how, or where, or what she did afterward. It was only three names and the word dead, and that was enough to lodge under her ribs and stay.

She stared at the empty tree line for one hard second — then her eyes dropped to Lucian on the porch boards, and the decision locked in.

She pushed herself up slowly. Bruised ribs protested; fabric pulled tight across the bodice. Both hands smoothed the dress down once. Tara was already crossing the porch toward them in a soft brown long-sleeve knit over a sage-green blouse, a flowing bohemian skirt brushing her ankles, and worn brown leather ankle boots. Her long golden dirty-blonde hair was loose and frizzy, and her silver crescent necklace caught the porch light. Buffy slipped an arm under Lucian’s shoulders, Tara took his other side without asking, and together they got him upright, his combat boots dragging on the boards. They walked him toward the front door like his weight was something they had done before and would do again.

At the front door Buffy stopped. Her voice was low and steady, with steel underneath.

Buffy said, "Everyone inside. Now. Barrier’s still up."

She muttered under her breath as they guided him through — quiet, bitter, almost to herself.

Buffy said, "Getting married… getting attacked… getting married… having a life… getting attacked again. It’s a real gift."

CHAPTER 2 What Was That

In the living room, warm lamp light pooled across Joyce’s floral couch, while the rest of the room sat in cool shadow. The house smelled like the last of pizza night and fear.

Buffy and Tara got Lucian onto the couch. Buffy sat with him, helped lift his boots onto the cushions, settled his feet. Her hand stayed on his shin a moment, then let go.

Willow stood near the coffee table, anxious and fidgeting. She wore an oversized cream cotton peasant top, soft grey lounge pants, and fuzzy orange Lorax socks, her shoulder-length red hair in that messy low ponytail with strands already escaping to frame her cheeks just above her eyes. Dawn had gone quiet in the armchair, knees drawn up, watching the group and saying nothing.

Willow’s voice came thin and too fast. Her fingers found her bottom lip and stayed there.

Willow said, "Buffy — what was that? What just happened out there?"

Buffy kept her eyes on Lucian’s boots a second too long. Then she swallowed hard and side-glanced Willow while she sat on the couch with him, still helping settle his feet.

Buffy said, "Spike… he’s lost it, Will." She swallowed again. "He came here to tell me I came back wrong…"

Willow’s mouth opened. She pressed her fingers harder to her lip, eyes huge.

Willow said, "Wrong? What do you mean, wrong… oh my god, Buffy…"

Tara looked up from Lucian’s side and said nothing at first. Then she turned for the kitchen without hurry — soft boots on hardwood, skirt whispering once against her ankles. She crossed the living room into the warmer spill of kitchen light, opened the freezer with a quiet suck of cold air, and worked an ice tray free with both hands.

Cubes rattled into a dish towel she folded twice, then once more, building a pack that would hold without dripping down his neck. She walked it back the same way she had gone — measured, useful — knelt beside the couch, and pressed the cold gently to Lucian’s jaw. He flinched, then accepted it. Tara’s free hand steadied the towel when it wanted to slip. She glanced at the swelling again, decided it wasn’t enough, and made a second short trip for another pack and a dry dish towel, laying the spare within reach on the coffee table.

When she came back the second time she glanced at Willow.

Tara said, "That doesn’t matter right now, sweetie."

She looked at Buffy.

Tara said, "He hit you? How — the chip?"

Buffy held her hands on her upper arms, crossing them over her chest. She looked small and vulnerable in the ruined white dress.

Buffy said, "Spike said I came back wrong… so he could… he could… he can hit me…" She looked at Lucian, then back at Tara. "But he can’t hit anyone else… just me…"

Willow and Tara shared a long, quiet, concerned look between each other, neither of them blinking.

Willow sat down next to Buffy on the couch and put her arms around her.

Buffy flinched — slight, automatic — going rigid where Willow pressed against her. Her eyes went somewhere past Willow’s ear.

Willow pulled back a little and tilted her head, worried, her fingers still near her own mouth.

Willow said, "Buffy? What’s wrong?"

Buffy lied, her voice quiet and flat enough to almost sell it.

Buffy said, "It’s nothing, Will… Just — slayer fight or flight. Still on high alert. That’s all."

Willow didn’t look convinced, but she didn’t push, and Dawn stayed quiet in the armchair, watchful in the way younger sisters get when the adults are performing okay. Under the ice Lucian’s breathing slowly evened out, and outside, the barrier held.

Buffy sat with Spike’s other words still locked in her chest and did not open them, not here and not yet. The cover story had landed, and the truth stayed where she had put it.

CHAPTER 3 I Need Wheels

Well over an hour had passed in the living room, and the warm lamp still pooled on Joyce’s floral couch. Upstairs the house had gone quiet — Dawn in bed, door shut; Tara and Willow upstairs in their room.

Lucian stirred slowly on the couch, his jaw still swollen under the ice pack that had long since gone lukewarm. He blinked, frowned at the ceiling, then at Buffy.

She was sitting in the armchair Dawn had left empty, still in the dirt-streaked white engagement dress, her feet bare and dirty, her mascara worse than before. She was watching him wake, arms wrapped around herself. She wasn’t performing anymore. She was just waiting.

Lucian said, rough, "What happened?"

Buffy tilted her head, her mouth small and wry.

Buffy said, "You stepped out in front of the ice cream truck."

Lucian stared at her a moment, then almost smiled, and regretted it when his split lip pulled.

Lucian said, "What time is it?"

Buffy looked past him at the antique clock on the living-room wall.

Buffy said, "It’s eleven thirty-four."

Lucian sat up slowly, careful of the jaw and careful of the tender place at his side. His eyes flicked upstairs once — toward Tara and Willow’s room, toward Dawn asleep — the look of a man who knew something had gone wrong and did not yet know how deep. Buffy stood, crossed to him, and took his hand.

Buffy said, "I need wheels. I know you’re hurt. I know. But I need to go somewhere across town, and I need to go now." She looked down at both of her hands for a moment, then back up at Lucian, and said in a quiet voice, "I can’t drive. I mean, back in high school I tried to learn, but… there was never any time for it."

His eyes went upstairs again, and his mouth opened on a question that would have woken the house.

Buffy covered his mouth with her fingers, gentle and firm, skin to skin, holding the sentence in.

Buffy said, "Just me and you… not the others… okay?"

Lucian looked past her hand, along her forearm to her face. He inhaled the sweet smell of tangerine body spray, felt the heat from her skin drifting up into him, and nodded once slowly.

Buffy hooked her right arm under Lucian’s left and helped him slowly up from the couch. Lucian winced, inhaling quietly with the pain, trying not to make any sound that would carry upstairs.

Buffy helped Lucian over to the front door. She grabbed her black double-rider leather jacket off the hook by the door, and Lucian picked up his car keys with a faint jingle.

Buffy opened the front door and they both stepped outside. She held the handle down with her right thumb while she closed it so it did not click — then headed for the car.

CHAPTER 4 When You’re Ready

Outside the Summers house, the night air was cool. Orange streetlight began at the curb, and deep blue night lay over the front of the house.

Buffy pulled the black leather jacket on over the dirt-streaked white dress without a word. Lucian still held the lukewarm ice pack in one hand and the keys in the other. He moved to the driver side of the black Trans Am, and they got in, settling onto the tan seats. Buffy shut the passenger door a little harder than necessary — the metal thunk echoed.

The second the door closed she pressed her palm flat against her eyes, fingers digging slightly into her hairline like she was physically holding the pieces together. Her breathing was shaky — one long inhale through the nose, slow controlled exhale, repeated.

Lucian watched her from the driver seat but didn’t speak right away. He reached across, rested his hand on her knee — warm through the dress — then pulled back and started the engine.

Lucian said, quiet and even, "When you’re ready… I’m right here."

After a few seconds she lowered her hand, jaw tight, eyes still glassy but focused. She side-eyed him.

Buffy said, her voice controlled, "Start driving, east side central."

Lucian nodded once and pulled away from the curb. The car moved through quiet residential streets under the glow of streetlights. They didn’t talk much. The only sounds were the low hum of the engine, the occasional tick of the turn signal, and Buffy’s breathing — still not quite steady.

She stared straight ahead, arms crossed tight over her chest, shoulders slightly hunched. Every few seconds her eyes flicked to the side mirror. Lucian kept glancing at her, but he respected the silence — one hand loose on the wheel.

Three blocks passed, then four, then five. The houses blurred into sameness — porch lights, parked cars, ordinary lives behind curtains. Buffy’s foot tapped the floor mat. Her arms stayed crossed, fingers digging into her own sleeves.

Lucian took a slow turn onto a longer stretch of road. There were fewer lights here, more dark between the pools of orange. He didn’t rush the silence. His jaw worked once around the swelling.

At the next red light, the car idled, the engine humming and the indicator ticking. Buffy stared at the red glow reflected in the windshield like a warning she couldn’t unsee.

She turned her head toward him — not fully, just enough that he caught her profile. Her mouth opened, closed, and opened again. Something waited between them in the idle — not pressure, just the fact of him being there.

Buffy said carefully, the words out before she could catch them, "Lucian, I need to tell you where we’re—"

She stopped hard and swallowed. Her throat worked visibly. The window closed, and she felt it close.

Buffy said, too fast, correcting herself, "—I mean. I need you to know this might get ugly. Whatever we find tonight. However ugly it already is."

Lucian didn’t fill the gap. He let the light turn green, eased the car forward, eyes on the road — but his attention was fully on her in the periphery. After a moment his right hand left the wheel. He found her left wrist where it rested against her crossed arms — not grabbing, just holding — then let go and returned to the wheel.

Lucian said, low and even, "I’m not going anywhere. Whatever it is — you don’t have to carry it alone."

Buffy’s breath caught. She looked down at her wrist where his fingers were, then out the window again. Something in her shoulders dropped a fraction — not relief, not peace, just the smallest loosening of a knot she’d been holding since the porch. She almost told him. She almost named the basement, almost named Willow, almost made him the vault.

She did not finish the sentence she had started. She did not say the address or Willow’s name. Her jaw tightened; she pressed her forehead briefly against the cool passenger window glass, eyes closing for one second, then opening.

Buffy said, barely above a whisper, "I know."

Lucian glanced at her once more and said nothing. His hand found the gear shift — a flex, a release — and he drove on. The streetlights kept strobing. Buffy’s breathing slowly evened, not calm, but controlled. She uncrossed her arms, rested her palms flat on her thighs, fingers pressing into the fabric of the white dress through the jacket gap.

Another block passed, and another. The quiet stayed loaded between them.

After a few blocks, Buffy suddenly sat up straighter.

Buffy said, "Stop."

Lucian didn’t hesitate. He slowed and pulled over under a streetlight. The engine idled. He turned toward her, watching her face.

Up ahead, a figure stepped out of the shadows between two houses and walked toward them with that familiar, unhurried gait.

It was Whistler.

He wore the same rumpled dark suit, long coat, and scuffed shoes, and he was the kind of man who looked like he had been delivering bad news since before any of them were born.

He approached the driver side window, glanced at Lucian in the driver seat, then looked at Buffy through the glass. He gave a small, almost tired nod.

Whistler said, "It’s been a while, kid."

He looked between the two of them and gestured toward the driver seat with his chin.

Whistler said, "We gotta talk. Mind if I drive?"

Buffy stared at him a long second, eyes narrowing slightly — deciding whether she was relieved or pissed. Then she gave him a single short nod. If she was going to put the truth somewhere tonight, it would not be in a living room full of people she loved. It would be in a car with a messenger who already knew the address.

Lucian tensed visibly, side-glancing over at Buffy.

Lucian said, "You know him?"

Buffy gave the smallest confirming nod. Lucian exhaled through his nose, opened his door, and stepped out. Without another word he tipped the driver seat forward and folded himself into the back, keeping his eyes locked on Whistler the entire time — assessing, distrustful. Whistler slid in behind the wheel, pulled the door shut, and adjusted the seat with two quick practiced movements. Lucian settled in back, arms crossed, posture straight, watching everything.

Whistler put the car in drive and pulled back onto the road, turning left onto the main street.

The three of them drove in silence as streetlights strobed across the windshield and over their faces. Buffy sat with arms still crossed, staring out the side window now. Her reflection in the glass looked exhausted. Whistler kept both hands on the wheel, eyes on the road, his face giving away nothing.

Lucian watched Buffy from the back seat — quiet, steady — but his hand rested on the seat-back behind her shoulder. He stayed alert, still not knowing what they were driving toward, and still choosing her.

CHAPTER 5 This Is Where It Happened

Outside an old run-down house, a single flickering streetlamp cast sickly yellow pools on peeling paint and overgrown weeds. The abandoned two-story house had boarded windows and a sagging fence. Night wind moved through the weeds, and the distant neighborhood sounded muffled.

The car pulled up. Inside, Buffy turned to Lucian.

Buffy said, "Stay in the—"

Before she finished, Lucian reached over and took her left wrist gently but firmly. Lucian’s eyes were steady and resolute.

Lucian said, soft and resolute, "No. I’m going with you this time."

Buffy stared at him. Something in her softened, and she gave a small nod. He was refusing the safer seat, and they both knew it.

Buffy said quietly, "Okay."

They climbed out into the cold, and Whistler motioned toward the dark house.

Whistler said, "This is where it happened."

Buffy zipped her black leather jacket up to her chin, and the zipper made that familiar sound in the quiet as the leather settled around her like armor. She walked around behind the car, popped the Trans Am’s glass rear hatch, and pulled out the double-bladed battle axe. Whistler shook his head and waved a finger.

Whistler said, "You won’t need that. It’s dead in there."

Buffy let out a short bitter breath, tossed the axe back in, and slammed the hatch down. Lucian watched silently, confused but trusting — the trust of someone who had not yet seen what dead meant in this basement.

The three walked around the side to the basement door — ripped off its hinges, lying on the ground. They descended the concrete stairs. Buffy wrapped her arms around herself, nose wrinkling at the foul smell. Cold climbed her bare ankles and seeped in through the thick rider leather jacket, starting to ache in her bones.

Buffy said quietly, "It’s so cold… in here."

CHAPTER 6 What Really Happened

Inside the basement, dim moonlight through a dirty high window gave the Trio’s lair a blue-grey tone. Dust motes drifted in it.

They reached the bottom. Whistler stepped forward, looked around once, then raised his right hand. His voice was low and tired.

Whistler said, "Words won’t do this justice. You need to see what really happened that night."

He flicked his wrist. The air in the center of the room rippled like heat haze. A translucent phantom vision materialized — ghostly but crystal-clear, playing out in the space in front of them like a three-dimensional memory made solid.

Willow phased straight through the house’s solid front door, walked down the hallway, and opened the basement door at the top of the stairs, her eyes already black and dark veins cracking out across her pale skin. She wore a black Victorian corset, tight black leather pants, and heavy boots. She came down invisible stairs, the air around her boots shimmering like desert heat.

Warren, Jonathan, and Andrew were arguing near the server rack — van impounded, screaming at each other — until Andrew looked at the bottom of the stairs and asked where the stairs had gone.

Willow’s black eyes locked on Warren and did not blink.

Willow said in a cold hollow voice, "No… No one is going to arrest you... not anymore."

She flicked her right wrist. Every thick cable and cord in the room twisted like living vipers, wrapped Andrew and Jonathan from head to toe, and cinched. They toppled. A sickening heavy pop of crushing bone cut their screams short.

Warren backed into a server rack, pulled a runed stone, and started to chant. Willow sealed his mouth with a sheet of smooth flesh before a syllable got out. The stone clattered. Cyan light died. Warren clawed at the place his lips had been.

Willow’s eyes flicked to a ghost after-image on the floor — Katrina, the champagne bottle. Her head turned back to Warren.

Willow said, low and cruel, "So, Warren, you like to kill girls. You think it’s funny? Well, now I am going to show you what I think is funny."

She grabbed him by the hair, drove her right hand into his chest on a surge of dark purple crackle — no blood spray, just power — and drew it back holding his small pulsing heart. Warren dropped. Willow stared at the heart with empty curiosity.

Willow said, bored, "So you actually had one of these after all, huh?"

Then the dark dropped out of her. She staggered into the cramped basement bathroom, bumping her shoulder on the doorframe, bent over the dirty sink, and vomited into it twice, crying silent ordinary tears out of those black eyes, and then she splashed her face and hands at the tap, leaving the sink as it was, without once looking in the mirror. She came back and raised trembling hands. Silent purple flame washed the three bodies to a fine layer of white calcium powder — no scorches on the floor.

Then the vision showed home: Willow standing over Tara asleep in the master bed, still in the leather, tears on her dark-veined cheeks. Lethe’s bramble wrapped her right wrist and sank into her skin. The leather vanished into a cute oversized shirt, and an innocent smile settled on her face. She climbed into bed beside Tara and breathed in her hair like nothing in the world had happened. Her right eye fluttered shut.

The vision ended. The air rippled and faded.

Buffy’s reaction was not neat.

Her arms locked around her own stomach like she’d been kicked. A sound tried to come out and died behind her teeth. She took one stumbling step back, heel skidding on chalk dust. Her eyes stayed on the empty air where Willow had held the heart — then flicked, helpless, to the place where Willow had crawled into bed next to Tara afterward. Here, in chalk dust and moonlight, Buffy learned about the bramble and the wiped memory, and her body did not know how to hold it.

Buffy said in a broken whisper, "No—"

Her legs went. She hit the cold concrete hard on her ass, white dress pooling around bare dirty feet. Both hands clamped over her mouth. A single violent shudder went through her — not crying yet, something worse, like her body was trying to reject what it had just been shown. She rocked forward once and stopped, frozen mid-collapse, staring at the powder on the floor like it might still be warm.

She could not have explained any of it to anyone; she sat there mute and broken.

CHAPTER 7 Carry It

In the basement, moonlight lay on the white dress and the chalk on her knees. The concrete was cold, with no warmth anywhere.

Lucian had been standing a half-step behind Buffy when the vision played. He had seen Willow float. He had seen cables crush bone. He had seen a heart come out of a man’s chest in her bare hand. He had seen her climb into bed beside Tara afterward like it was Tuesday.

For a second his body forgot he was supposed to be brave.

He stepped back without meaning to — boot scraping concrete — eyes wide, breath gone shallow. He had taken meditation classes and tarot readings at the Magic Box with Willow and Tara. He had watched her drop demons without blinking. He had a scale in his head for what Willow was: monsters, self-defense, power with a leash. Never humans. Never this.

The leash was gone in that vision. There was no leash.

Lucian said under his breath, "Jesus Christ." It was not prayer; it was fear, raw and human and ashamed of itself already.

Then he saw Buffy on the floor and the fear had somewhere to go.

He dropped into a crouch in front of her — one knee cracking on concrete — and put his hand on the center of her upper back through the leather jacket. He gripped too hard, then forced it gentler. He was shaking, and he tried to hide it, and he failed; he was scared all the way down to the bone, and he was staying anyway, for her.

Buffy did not lift her head. Her breathing came in ragged pulls. A tremor ran through her, then another, and then she went still in a worse way.

Slowly her right hand uncurled from her mouth. She pressed it flat on the concrete beside her hip. Chalk stuck to her palm, white powder that had been Andrew, Jonathan, and Warren. She saw Willow’s hands. She saw Willow’s smile in bed.

A sound tried to form in her throat and died there.

Lucian stared at the empty air where the vision had been, then at Buffy, then at the stairs again like the house itself might answer.

Lucian said, voice low and unsteady, "That was — Buffy. That was Willow. I’ve seen her — Magic Box, meditation, the way she looks at Tara — I’ve seen her put down things that wanted us dead. I never — I never saw her do that to a person. She’s — God, she’s not even in the same category as the rest of us."

He swallowed hard. His swollen jaw flexed. His eyes were wet at the corners, and he was angry about it.

Lucian said, quieter, "Why… why would she do that?"

Buffy’s eyes closed, then opened. A single tear cut through the mascara on her left cheek. She did not wipe it.

A long stretch of quiet followed.

Buffy said in a broken whisper, "Jesus Christ, Will."

Whistler stood at the edge of the vision-space, hands loose, giving them the room. He did not interrupt.

Lucian’s hand stayed on her back. He breathed slow on purpose — not calm, trying to become calm — matching her by degrees.

Lucian said softly, like saying the name might summon her, "Willow."

Buffy stared at the spot where the vision had played. Her face was pale under the moonlight. When she finally looked at Lucian her eyes were glassy and exhausted and quietly shattered.

Whistler said, pointing a finger at them and wiggling it in the air, "You see, that is the thing, kids. The why, not the how, that matters the most, doesn’t it? She saw Tara die. Warren pulled the trigger, two mornings from when this gore scene happened."

Buffy understood it a beat later. He didn’t mean a death that had happened; he meant one Willow had seen coming in a vision, Tara falling with a bullet in her chest two mornings ahead, and the Trio had died before that morning could arrive. Whistler knelt down, looking Buffy right in the face with his head tilted left, his eyes searching her before he spoke again.

Whistler said, continuing, "What did you think she would do, kid? If your girl back home knew you were going to die, or be hurt by someone that would take pleasure in it afterwards? If she knew for sure that this was going to happen? She couldn’t come to you with it — the librarian, her bestie, anyone."

Buffy held his gaze, swallowed hard, and nodded softly — not agreeing, but understanding.

Lucian understood it, and that was the worst part. Willow would kill to protect Tara and this house. She already had. He put that somewhere quiet behind his eyes, where it would stay.

Buffy glanced at the open door of the little bathroom where Willow had been sick, then at the chalky powder of leftover Warren against the far wall, and then back at Lucian. Her right hand found his and gripped hard enough to hurt.

Buffy said, "And now I have to decide if I tell them. Or carry it. Again."

Whistler watched them a long moment, then spoke quietly, tone heavy but final.

Whistler said, "There’s something bigger moving. The First Evil has a champion now — someone already close to you. I can’t name them yet. Balance won’t allow it. But you have to make a choice. Keep this to yourselves, or tell everyone clean. You’ve got a choice, kid. Tell them. Or carry it. Either road, you don’t walk it back."

He glanced up at the ceiling and winced slightly before looking back down at Buffy and Lucian.

Whistler said, "I am not supposed to tell you this. But I am going to anyhow because I like you guys. If you tell Willow she might lose her mind… and you know what that means. Tara would leave for sure and not come back. Your house would fall apart from under you overnight."

Buffy looked up at him, eyes narrowed.

Buffy said, "This is a test, isn’t it?"

Whistler let out a breath and put his hands inside his jacket pockets. He looked down, then back up at her, steady.

Whistler said, "Buffy, every single moment that has any impact in your life is always a test. Good or evil, or balanced like a razor between the two. Negative and positive."

He gave a faint tired smile.

Whistler said, "You two make a cute couple. Wish I’d brought better news."

Then he was simply gone, and the basement felt emptier and somehow smaller, as if the walls had moved in around what they knew.

For a few seconds neither of them moved. Then Lucian slid one arm under her knees and the other behind her back and lifted her off the concrete. Chalk dust fell from the white dress. Buffy did not fight him; her head dropped against his shoulder, and her bare dirty feet hung over his arm.

He carried her up the concrete steps and out into the night, across the weeds to the Trans Am. He opened the passenger door and set her down in the seat. He pulled the seatbelt across her and clicked it in. Then he leaned down and kissed her forehead.

He shut her door, walked around the front of the car, and got in behind the wheel.

He did not start the engine. For a while he sat there quiet, hands on the steering wheel, flexing, gripping, letting go, gripping again. There was fear in it, and awe, and every muscle in his arms was wound tight. His swollen jaw worked once and stopped.

Then he turned and looked at Buffy.

Lucian said, "I think Willow—"

Buffy said, yelling, "Don’t you dare say you understand why she did that! Because I cannot hear that from you right now!"

Her voice cracked on the last word. She brought both hands up and covered her face, and the crying finally came, hard and ugly, shaking her whole body under the leather jacket.

Buffy said between sobs, "Just take me home… please… take me home."

Lucian turned the key. The engine caught. He let out a deep breath, and pain moved across his face. Then he put the car in gear and drove off into the dark.

CHAPTER 8 My Boy

In the principal’s office at Sunnydale High, a single desk lamp created a warm island of light; the rest of the room sat in deep cool shadow. Outside the window, the school grounds were empty and silent. Papers lay on the desk. A framed photo of young Robin and Nikki sat on the shelf. The quiet was subtle and eerie, as if something were watching.

Robin Wood sat behind his desk in a dark button-up shirt, sleeves rolled to the forearms. He was writing in steady strokes. He stopped, set the pen down, leaned back, and pinched the bridge of his nose with thumb and forefinger, eyes closed, rubbing slowly with the exhaustion only principals carry when no one’s watching.

A voice spoke soft and warm from the edge of the room — achingly familiar.

Nikki said, "My boy… I have missed you."

Wood’s hand froze. He slowly lowered it and turned his head.

Standing at the edge of the lamplight, in a faint cold blue light that had not been there a moment before, with a low hum under the quiet of the room, was Nikki Wood, exactly as she had looked alive, with a posture and presence that were painfully real. Where she stood, the carpet did not give under her weight. The air did not move around her the way it should.

Wood said, rough and disbelieving, "…Mom?"

Nikki gave a small, sad smile — the kind that said she knew how impossible this should be. She took one small step closer, staying at the light’s edge. Her voice was gentle, conversational.

Nikki said, "I know. It doesn’t make sense. Not yet. But it can. If you go down to the basement… if you do what needs to be done… we can be together again. Really together. No more missing each other."

Wood’s eyes locked on her, mouth slightly open, breathing shallow.

Wood said, barely above a whisper, "You’re not real."

Nikki said, "I’m as real as you need me to be right now. The seal can bring me back, Robin. It needs you."

She tilted her head with maternal patience.

Nikki said, "You’ve always been so strong. So careful. But the right thing sometimes means letting go of what you think you know… and trusting what your heart is telling you."

Wood’s jaw tightened, eyes shining but holding tears. He tested her.

Wood said, "If you’re really her… tell me something only she would know."

Nikki’s smile softened with old pain.

Nikki said, "You used to hide under my bed when the nightmares got bad. You said the monsters couldn’t get you if I was standing between them and the bed. You were four."

Wood let out a shaky breath. His shoulders dropped as the fight drained out of him.

Nikki said softly, "I missed you every single day, baby. I’m so tired of being apart. Go to the basement. Make the offering. Let me come home to you."

The glassy, distant look took over Wood’s face. Tension eased into eerie peace. Without looking away he reached across the desk, picked up a letter opener, its metal catching the lamplight. He stood slowly, held it loosely at his side, turned, and walked out like a man in a trance — steady, unhurried. The door closed quietly behind him.

The empty office held for a moment — desk lamp still glowing, half-finished paper sitting there. Nikki was already gone; the blue light had guttered out and the hum had dropped to silence, and she had left no footprint and no scuff behind, already finished with the room.

CHAPTER 9 The Seal

In the halls of Sunnydale High, dim red emergency exit lights ran along the floor. The concrete stairs were cold, and the air was damp. In the boiler room below, a fluorescent light flickered, and moonlight came through a dirty high window.

Wood walked down the main staircase to the basement — movements slow, graceful, trance-like, eyes glassy, letter opener in his right hand. His shoes were almost silent on the concrete.

A faint whisper drifted from the shadows.

Nikki said in a gentle whisper, "That’s it, baby… keep coming. I’m right here."

He tilted his head, listening, then continued to the old boiler room door and pushed it open.

Inside he walked straight to the center where the Seal of Danzalthar, a big round metal plate, was set into the floor. He stopped at the edge, knelt on one knee, laid his left hand flat, palm-down, on the pentagram center, and raised the letter opener.

A tiny flicker of resistance crossed his face — then it was gone. He drew the blade deliberately across his palm. Dark blood welled and dripped onto cold iron.

The seal pulsed faint sickly orange once, twice… then faded completely back to dull metal.

Wood blinked hard. His eyes cleared instantly — raw confusion, then sharp rising panic.

Wood said in a horrified whisper, "…What the hell…"

He dropped the letter opener, and the metal clattered loud. He scrambled to his feet, backed away, turned, and ran for the stairs two at a time, his breathing ragged and panicked. At the top he slammed the heavy door, fumbled with bloody shaking hands at the old padlock on the outside, finally locked it, and yanked hard to test it. He stood with his chest heaving, staring at the locked door like something might burst through. Then he turned and ran down the hallway, disappearing into the dark.

Several long seconds of nothing passed in the boiler room.

Then the corners of the pentagram peeled upward — slow, deliberate, deeply wrong. A low grinding creak echoed. The sickly glow returned, struggling.

A pale clawed hand suddenly shot up through the widening gap. Long bony fingers gripped the edge. With a wet tearing sound, the first Turok-Han — Uber-vampire — forced its shoulders through: deathly white skin stretched tight over powerful unnatural muscle, black empty eyes, jagged too-long teeth bared in a silent snarl. Thick dark fluid dripped from its body.

With one final violent heave it dragged itself fully out and crouched on the seal, dripping, chest heaving. It slowly lifted its head and looked toward the stairs with predatory intelligence.

The seal beneath it slammed shut with a heavy metallic clang.

The first of the Uber-vampires was loose beneath Sunnydale High.

CHAPTER 10 It’s Started

At the same time, across town, cold moonlight fell through bare branches outside Spike’s crypt in Sunnydale Cemetery. The crypt entrance stood half-open, its stone steps damp. Somewhere across town, a seal had just opened.

Spike sat on the bottom crypt step, hunched forward, elbows on his knees, head in his hands. His black duster was filthy and torn at one shoulder, his shirt open at the throat, blood dried on one knuckle from a wall he’d put his fist through earlier. His bleached hair was matted with sweat at the temples. His eyes were red-rimmed and hollow — not vampire-yellow, just human-broken blue. He shook intermittently — not cold, not withdrawal, something worse: a soul pressing against the inside of his skull.

He muttered in fragments, not sentences, his voice scraped raw, his British accent fraying at the edges.

Spike said in a broken whisper to no one, "—didn’t know… didn’t care… and now I do… Christ, I do…"

His head jerked sideways as if he were answering himself.

Spike said, "Shut it. Shut it shut it—"

His fingers dug into his scalp. He gasped once — half sob, half choke — and pressed his forehead to his knees. His whole frame shuddered. The porch was still in him: the fists, Lucian going down, and Buffy’s face when he put the Trio in her chest like a knife. He had told her what he knew, Willow and the Trio and the kill, and he had not known the rest; he did not even know there was a rest, and he knew nothing about Lethe’s bramble. He knew only the dead, and that was what he had left her with.

Then, all at once, he went still.

Spike said, lifting his head slowly, disoriented, "…What—"

His eyes narrowed. It was not sight but sense. Something pulled at him from across town: a pulse, a wrongness, a door opening that should never open. His head turned toward the east — toward the school — jaw clenching.

Spike said, faster, the words overlapping, "—something moved—something big—seal—no, no, not my problem, I’m not—"

Self-loathing cut through.

Spike said, "Coward. Bloody coward. You wanted a soul and now you can’t even stand up—"

He pushed himself to his feet, stumbled once on the crypt step, and caught himself on the stone jamb while the heavy duster swung around his legs. He took two steps toward the cemetery path — toward town, toward the school — then stopped hard. His hand flew to his chest as if the soul was a physical weight crushing his ribs.

Spike said in a fractured whisper, "Can’t. Can’t go in there like this. Not like this. They’ll smell it on me—"

His voice dropped, stripped of anything soft.

Spike said, "I’m not ready. I’m not ready I’m not—"

He sank back down onto the step and gripped his head in both hands, his breathing ragged and uneven. In the distance — too faint for anyone else to mark — the metallic clang of the seal slamming shut carried on the night air. Spike’s head snapped up one last time. He listened, and he knew.

Spike said, barely audible, to the dark, "It’s started."

He did not move again. The crypt held him — wrecked, haunted, awake in a way he had not been in a century. He was a hunched silhouette against the moonlit stone. There was no longing, just a soul and a seal and a man who had already done enough damage for one night.

CHAPTER 11 The Lie

Back at the Summers house, everything was quiet. A single warm lamp glowed in the living room, and the rest lay in soft shadow.

Buffy opened the door the same quiet way she had left it. She and Lucian came inside. Buffy took off her jacket and hung it back on the rack by the door; her hands slid down the leather after it settled on the hook. Her face was a mix of everything she was carrying.

She took a deep breath, let it out slowly, and headed up the stairs with Lucian behind her. They turned right at the staircase and looked at Willow and Tara’s door.

There was no light under it, only silence through the whole house. Buffy opened her bedroom door across the hall, stepped inside, and shut it quietly.

She reached back with her right hand, drew the zipper down, and let the dirt-streaked white dress slide off her shoulders and pool at her bare feet. Underneath she wore a matching white bra and panties. She slid into bed under the covers next to the window. Lucian toed off his combat boots beside the bed, then followed her and slid under the covers, still in his sweater and slacks, facing her. He took her hands in his and searched her face in the quiet of the house.

CHAPTER 12 What She Carried

In Buffy’s bedroom, faint blue moonlight through the curtains cast a soft cool glow. The room was dark except for that light.

Buffy and Lucian lay in bed facing each other, close — faces only a few inches apart, sharing the same pillow. Blankets pulled up around them. Their voices were low and careful.

Lucian watched her. His expression was calm but his eyes searching — the tender side of him that had stayed beside her in that basement even while everything he thought he knew about Willow broke apart. He reached up, brushed a strand of hair away from Buffy’s face — the one hanging over her right eye — then let his hand rest against the side of her neck.

Lucian said in a whisper, "You’ve been so quiet since we got back."

Buffy didn’t answer right away. She looked at him, her eyes moving over his face like she was memorizing it. She gave a small shake of her head and swallowed, then side-glanced away and back.

Buffy whispered, "I’m okay. It’s going to take some time for me… internally… and all that… you know…"

She let out a deep breath.

Buffy whispered, so quietly between breaths that Lucian could barely hear her, "I know you might judge me, Lucian… but part of me understands why she did it… why Willow thought and felt she had no one to go to… I can’t believe I am saying this… but… I understand it. I’m just thinking about how Tara has been in our lives — if she were gone… if she were dead…"

She let that hang between them in the silence for a few seconds.

Buffy whispered, her voice catching, "Tara is…"

Lucian said quietly, "…the home mother."

Buffy nodded, trying not to let herself cry. She swallowed hard; her shoulders moved with it.

Lucian didn’t push. He kept looking at her, his thumb slowly stroking the side of her neck.

Lucian kept his voice to a whisper, his thumb never stopping. "You don’t always have to be perfect. You don’t always have to be this powerful symbol of justice and truth. You can let yourself fall apart a little, as long as it’s for the right reasons."

Buffy’s eyes flicked down for a second, and she swallowed. Her voice went even softer.

Buffy whispered, "It’s going to take time... but I cannot and will not let her find out about this…"

Lucian was quiet for a few seconds. His hand stayed where it was.

Lucian’s whisper came low and careful. "Which one, Tara or Willow?"

Buffy let out a slow breath through her nose. She shifted slightly closer, foreheads almost touching now.

Buffy whispered, "Both."

She closed her eyes for a moment, then opened them again. Her voice dropped even lower.

Buffy whispered, "I just want you to hold me tightly, really tightly, and not let go."

Lucian nodded slowly. He understood. His arms reached around her and held her tightly against him. Buffy’s face rested against his, staring at the wall in the quiet of her bedroom.

Lucian whispered, "Then we won’t talk about it ever again."

He meant it. He would carry it for her and never say it to anyone, and he would sit across from Willow at breakfast with a steadier face than Buffy could ever manage.

He turned his head in, pressed a slow, careful kiss to her cheek, then rested his cheek against the side of her neck. They stayed like that a long moment, breathing the same air.

Buffy’s eyes stayed closed. Her hand came up and rested lightly on his chest. She slid her hand under his sweater to his bare skin, feeling his heartbeat.

Buffy whispered, barely audible, "I don’t know how to do this anymore, Lucian."

Lucian whispered back, "Then we figure it out. One night at a time."

He pulled her in a little closer until their bodies were pressed together under the blankets, legs intertwined. Buffy tucked her face into the crook of his neck.

Neither of them spoke again, and they lay as one dark shape holding each other in the quiet room, the moonlight catching on hair and blanket edges while their breathing slowly fell into the same rhythm.

Lucian’s breathing gradually evened out against Buffy’s hair. His arm stayed around her back — heavy, warm, asleep. His chest rose and fell in slow rhythm. She knew he had fallen asleep despite everything.

Buffy did not sleep. She kept thinking about the First, what it was planning. How to keep this from the others, and never let it slip. Buffy did not want to allow Willow to get away with murder. Of course not, but what choice did she have? Her mind kept racing all night long, judging herself, judging Willow. She was unable to stop the circles in her own mind.

Her eyes stayed open in the dark, and she was wide awake with her jaw tight, while the moonlight caught the track of a dried tear on her cheek from earlier that she never bothered to wipe away. She stayed awake like that through the small hours, blinking once, slow and exhausted, and then keeping her eyes open again.

The night did not end for her. Only the room went quiet around what she carried.
