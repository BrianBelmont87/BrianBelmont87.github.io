Buffy the Vampire Slayer. Season six, episode 12. Trivium.

CHAPTER 1 The Magic Box Talk

Inside the Magic Box that Wednesday morning an old acoustic alternative song was playing low from the vintage radio behind the counter, and sunlight came in through the front windows and caught the dust drifting slowly over the research table. The place smelled of old books and a faint trace of coffee from the back room, and the long wooden table was scarred with years of knife marks from past research nights. It was one of those rare peaceful mornings that everybody in the room seemed to know wouldn’t last. Giles’s packed boxes were still stacked behind the counter, and nobody could quite stop looking at them.

Xander was slouched in a heavy wooden chair at the research table, staring hard at a glossy corporate brochure for "Techmo Industries." He wore a faded plaid flannel, with sawdust still on one shoulder from boarding up the smashed bay window at the Summers house at first light. His knee bounced nervously under the table, the way it always did when he couldn’t make up his mind about something that mattered.

Anya paced behind him in sharp business-casual, hands flying, cheeks flushed. Willow and Tara sat side by side at the table. Willow was in a soft green top and lounge pants, leaning forward. Tara was in a cardigan and a long skirt, hands folded, her face soft with concern.

Buffy and Lucian had the end of the table. Lucian wore a black shirt and dark trousers, his long black hair tied back. Buffy was in a grey t-shirt and dark jeans, and all of her attention was on the food in front of her, which for Buffy these days was its own kind of healing.

Anya reached the end of the aisle, spun on her heel, and came back the other way with both hands chopping at the air.

Anya said, "It is completely absurd! Texas is a barren wasteland entirely populated by cattle and unregulated firearms. We have a thriving retail enterprise right here! But... the compensation package they offered is astronomically lucrative. And they have excellent dental. It’s a logistical nightmare wrapped in a capitalist dream!"

Xander sighed and dropped the brochure onto the table, his eyes tired and his face drawn with self-doubt, before he said the thing he had obviously been chewing on all morning.

Xander said, "Anya, look at me. I’m just a guy with a hammer. When the fighting starts, I’m a clumsy fighter. I’m the guy who gets knocked out, mind-controlled, or thrown through a wall. I don’t have superpowers. The group doesn’t actually need me here for the apocalypse."

Anya stopped pacing so abruptly her heels squeaked on the floorboards. She looked profoundly offended, planted both hands firmly on her hips, her face reddening in defense of every non-superpowered person in the room.

Anya said, "Excuse me! We provide vital tactical support! My extensive, centuries-long knowledge of demonology and my sharp, distraction-based human banter are formidable weapons against the forces of darkness!"

Xander said, rubbing his temples with two fingers of each hand, "Banter doesn’t dust vampires, Ahn."

Tara leaned forward across the table, stepping gently into the supportive role, and her voice was soft but firm.

Tara said, "Xander, you shouldn’t say things like that. You are a valuable member of this family."

Willow was nodding enthusiastically before Tara had even finished, her fingers drumming on the table.

Willow said, "Exactly! You’re the heart of the group, Xander. You’re our foundation. You’re being a completely silly, stubborn male-minded person right now if you think we don’t need you."

Lucian sat quietly through all of it, the black of his clothes stark against the bright morning in the shop, sipping from a mug of black coffee. He wasn’t really watching the argument. He was watching Buffy with a small, fond smile, his aqua-blue eyes following each donut she demolished with a kind of quiet pride, not commenting on it, just there beside her.

Buffy was ignoring the argument completely. She had a huge glazed donut in one hand, and she took a big, aggressive bite out of it and chewed happily. Without bothering to swallow, she brought a heavy ceramic mug of hot coffee to her lips and took a long drink to wash it down. While Xander and the girls went on arguing around her, she finished the first donut, reached straight into the white cardboard box on the table, and grabbed a second. She devoured that one with exactly the same ravenous, healthy energy before she went back in for a third and bit a good third of it away in one go. For everybody at that table who had spent weeks watching her push food around a plate, seeing the Slayer eat with that kind of unrestrained hunger was a quiet victory nobody dared say out loud.

Buffy cleared her throat, a loud, deliberate sound that cut straight through the overlapping voices, and the whole room went silent. She swallowed the last bite of the third donut, wiped her mouth with a paper napkin, and leaned back in the wooden chair with her arms crossed over her chest. Her face was calm, with none of the old teenage insecurity left in it. She locked eyes with Xander, her voice steady and grown-up.

Buffy said, "Xander. What do you really want to do?"

Xander sat perfectly still, frozen in his chair like a man who had just been handed the deed to his whole grown-up life and didn’t know which end to hold it by. A single drop of sweat trickled slowly down his temple. He swallowed hard and answered in a quiet voice with not one joke left in it.

Xander said, "I want to go, Buffy. For the first time in my life... I feel like this is exactly what I’m supposed to do. I want to build something real. I feel like it’s right."

The silence shattered. Anya’s voice broke on the first word, completely panicked.

Anya said, "Well, it’s wrong! Texas is a barren, culturally void dust bowl, and leaving a fully stocked, magically shielded retail haven is just bad business!"

She didn’t wait for a response. She turned on her heel and practically ran for the front door, shoved it open so hard the cheerful shop bell jangled over her head, and stormed out onto the street, letting the heavy door slam shut behind her.

Xander shot up out of his chair.

Xander called after her, "Anya, wait—"

He took two quick steps toward the door, his instinct to fix it overriding everything else, until Tara was on her feet too, sharp and urgent.

Tara said, "Xander! Stop."

Xander froze and looked back at her. Tara was stuttering a little, but her eyes were fiercely protective and she didn’t look away from him.

Tara said, "She’s a girl, Xander. Sh-sh-she need... needs to be alone right now. You can’t fix this with words right this second. She has to process it."

Xander looked at the closed front door for a long moment, and the fight drained completely out of him. He turned and walked slowly over to a plush armchair in the corner of the research area and collapsed into it, dropped his head back against the upholstery with a long, exhausted sigh, and scrubbed at his face with both hands.

Xander muttered through his fingers, "I finally make a mature life decision, and I instantly break my own girlfriend with a geographical location. The Harris curse strikes again."

Lucian had watched the entire exchange without a word. He leaned forward slightly and rested his forearms on his knees, and his voice came out low and warm and steady, the way it did when he meant something all the way down.

Lucian said, "It isn’t a curse, Xander. Growth usually feels like breaking at first. You are choosing to step out of a very large shadow you’ve been standing in for years. She just needs a minute to adjust her eyes to the new light."

Xander lowered his hands and looked over at him, surprised by the depth of it and by the complete lack of judgment in the new guy’s voice. It wasn’t cold advice handed down from the end of a table. It was plain, man-to-man respect, and Xander wasn’t used to getting that from anybody.

Buffy, still sitting beside Lucian, turned and looked at him, and her face softened into something that was very close to pride. She reached over and slid her hand over his, wrapping her fingers around it and giving it a warm, firm squeeze that anchored him right into the middle of the group. Then she looked from Lucian back to Xander with a soft smile on her face.

Buffy said, "He’s right. It takes a lot of guts to step out of the shadow, Xan. And for the record? I think Techmo is about to get a major upgrade."

Willow had gotten up from the table at some point and was standing near the counter with her hands clasped tightly in front of her. The defensive, enthusiastic energy she’d had earlier was gone, and for a second she looked very small. She walked slowly across the wooden floor to the cushioned chair where Xander was slumped, and she didn’t say anything at first. She just perched carefully on the wide armrest beside him, staring down at the floorboards, and when she finally spoke her voice was thick.

Willow said, "We were five. Kindergarten. You broke my yellow crayon, and I think we’ve basically been standing in the exact same room ever since."

Xander looked up at her, and the grown-up exhaustion on his face cracked just enough to show the goofy kid underneath it, a weak, nostalgic smile tugging at one corner of his mouth.

Xander said, "I said I was sorry about the crayon, Wills."

Willow said with a watery chuckle, "I know."

She reached out and laid her hand gently on top of his dusty work shoulder, and finally she looked him in the eyes. Hers were shining with unshed tears, but her face was fiercely supportive.

Willow said, "It’s just... Sunnydale without you isn’t really Sunnydale for me. It’s terrifying. But... if this is what you need to do... if you need to go build something real... then you have to go."

She leaned her head down and rested it against his shoulder for one brief, tender second.

Willow whispered into the flannel, "We’ll figure out how to hold the fort. I promise."

Xander reached up and covered her hand on his shoulder with his own, gripping it tight, and neither of them said anything else. It was a silent thank you between two people who had survived the end of the world together more than once.

CHAPTER 2 The Vengeance Sounding Board

Out on the Sunnydale street Anya was fuming, her hands balled into tight fists at her sides as she marched along the sidewalk. She was muttering to herself in a voice that was half furious ranting and half genuine, panicked tears, and she swiped a tear off her cheek with the back of her wrist without breaking stride.

Anya said, "Selfish. Utterly selfish. Texas! It’s entirely composed of dirt and cowboy hats! I have a cash register! I have inventory! I can’t just pack up my entire mortal existence into a cardboard box for a... a carpenter!"

She turned a corner sharply and stepped into a narrow, shaded alleyway between a generic clothing shop called "Trendy Threads" and a brightly lit "Sunnydale Stop-N-Go" convenience store. The alley was cooler than the street, long shadows falling from the buildings on either side and a single shaft of sunlight cutting through at the far end. Anya stopped there and leaned her back against the cool brick wall of the convenience store. The fight went right out of her. She covered her face with her hands and let out a raw, frustrated sob, and then she was crying quietly into her palms.

Anya said, "I don’t know how to do this. I don’t know how to be this... human."

There was a sharp, hissing sound, like ozone burning, and a thick swirl of dark purple-grey smoke erupted from the pavement grating a few feet away from her. Anya lowered her hands, sniffing, but she didn’t look alarmed in the slightest. She recognized the smell of sulfur.

The smoke cleared all at once, and standing there was Halfrek, Hallie to her friends, dressed impeccably in a sharp, high-end designer skirt suit that looked hilariously out of place in a dirty alley beside a dumpster. She sighed, her voice dripping with refined, aristocratic sympathy.

Halfrek said, "Oh, Anyanka. Look at you. Crying in an alleyway over a mortal boy. It’s absolutely tragic what this dimension has done to your constitution."

Anya wiped furiously at her eyes, trying to pull herself together, and pushed off the wall to stand up straight.

Anya said, "Hallie. What are you doing here? I didn’t summon you. I wasn’t making a wish."

Halfrek took a delicate step forward, stepping around a puddle without so much as glancing down at it.

Halfrek said, "You didn’t have to. The Bureau tracks massive spikes of emotional distress in our former top-tier girls. I felt you unraveling from two dimensions over."

She reached out and gently, almost maternally, wiped a stray tear from Anya’s cheek with her thumb.

Halfrek said, "I am not here for a contract, sweetie. I am just here as your friend. Tell me what the carpenter did. Did he cheat? Did he strike you? I can have his entrails turned into live centipedes before he finishes his coffee. Just say the word."

Anya said, shaking her head rapidly, "No! No entrails! He didn’t do anything wrong, Hallie. That’s the problem. He got a promotion. A massive one. In Texas."

Halfrek physically recoiled, her face twisting in pure, unfiltered disgust.

Halfrek said, "Texas? Oh, Anyanka, no. The humidity alone is a curse."

Anya started pacing a tight little circle in the middle of the alley, her heels clicking on the wet pavement and her hands working at the air the way they had in the shop.

Anya said, "He wants to go. He wants to build a future. And... and Buffy told him he could. But I don’t know what to do! When I was a vengeance demon, my path was clear. Someone caused pain, I inflicted punishment. It was beautifully simple math!"

She stopped and looked at Halfrek with genuine, desperate vulnerability.

Anya said, "But human relationships don’t have math! If I stay here, I lose him, and my heart physically hurts. If I go with him, I lose the shop, I lose my independence, and I might end up resenting him. How do humans make these choices without a ledger?!"

Halfrek watched her for a long moment, and the elite demon softened, looking at her old friend with something that was very close to pity. Her voice dropped low and serious.

Halfrek said, "They guess, Anyanka. They guess, and they hope they don’t destroy themselves in the process. That is the flaw in their design."

She stepped closer and placed both hands firmly on Anya’s shoulders, forcing her to look her in the eyes.

Halfrek said, "But you need to remember who you are. You were the Patron Saint of Scorned Women. You spent a thousand years bending reality to your will. You do not follow a boy to a dust bowl just because you are afraid of the quiet. You tell him what you require. And if he cannot provide it... you let him walk."

Anya stared at Hallie, and the panicked, crying human girl slowly faded away. Something older came up behind her eyes, the ancient confidence of Anyanka the vengeance demon flashing there just for a moment, her spine straightening and her chin coming up, and her voice steadied.

Anya said, "I tell him what I require."

Halfrek smiled, a dark, proud little smirk.

Halfrek said, "Exactly. Don’t let the mortal flesh make you forget your teeth, darling."

CHAPTER 3 The Carpool

Inside Joyce’s compact sedan that afternoon, with Dawn freshly picked up from Sunnydale High, the radio was playing something upbeat and slightly chaotic. The back seat looked absurdly packed, three people crammed into a car built for two and a half. Willow was driving, and Tara sat in the passenger seat staring out the window, her hands folded tightly in her lap, still turning Xander’s leaving over and over in her head.

Dawn, in her school clothes with her backpack jammed down between her knees, was wedged dead center between Buffy and Lucian, both of them still in what they had worn to the Magic Box. Lucian had one hand braced on the door handle to steady himself when Dawn drove her elbow into Buffy’s side, and he sat through it with a small, amused stillness, not stepping into the sibling war at all.

Dawn squirmed aggressively and elbowed Buffy in the ribs again.

Dawn said, "Move over! I am being completely smushed here! You have, like, half the seat!"

Buffy didn’t even flinch at the elbow. She slowly turned her head toward Dawn, narrowed her eyes mischievously, stuck her tongue out, and leaned in fast, fully threatening to drag a wet tongue right up the side of Dawn’s cheek.

Dawn shrank back against Lucian, absolutely horrified.

Dawn said, "Don’t you dare! Oh my GOD! NOOO!"

Lucian, pressed flat against the opposite door, let out a deep, rumbling chuckle at the sheer sibling chaos. Willow just sighed with her eyes fixed on the road, reached out, and cranked the volume dial on the radio until a heavy guitar riff drowned out Dawn’s shrieking in the back.

Outside in the Summers driveway the car doors all opened at once, cutting off the muffled radio, and the group piled out onto the concrete. The big front bay window was still covered with a sheet of raw plywood from last night’s siege, and the front door sat a little crooked on the new hinges Xander had hung that morning. Willow and Tara walked quietly up the front steps toward the porch, trading a supportive look on the way.

In the driveway Lucian stretched, reaching his arms overhead and rolling his broad shoulders, his dark shirt pulling tight across his chest and back. Buffy leaned against the trunk of the car and watched him do it, clearly enjoying the view. Then her eyes went from him to Dawn, who was fussing with her backpack, and an idea clicked behind them.

Buffy said, "Hey, Dawnie... want to play some ball?"

Dawn brightened instantly and dropped her bag on the grass.

Dawn said, "Heck yeah."

Lucian lowered his arms and walked over to join them, smiling down at Buffy.

Lucian said, "Mind if I join the roster?"

Buffy smirked up at him and crossed her arms.

Buffy said, "Depends. Can your ego handle losing a two-on-one against a girl whose primary superpower is whining, and a Slayer who doesn’t know the meaning of a foul?"

Lucian just grinned, accepting the challenge. Buffy walked up the driveway and hit the button for the automatic garage door, and it rumbled upward. She reached inside, grabbed a worn orange basketball off a cluttered shelf, and tossed it bouncing onto the pavement.

Out on Revello Drive a few minutes later, with the sun sinking toward the rooftops, Dawn was walking a few paces ahead of them. She was enthusiastically dribbling the basketball between her legs and every so often messing it up and having to chase it down the sidewalk. Buffy and Lucian walked shoulder to shoulder behind her, and the chaos of the car ride had settled into something comfortable and quiet between them.

Lucian watched Dawn scramble after the ball again.

Lucian said, "You have a good family, Buffy. They’re loud, and they’re messy... but they’re solid."

Buffy watched her sister too, a wry half-smile on her face.

Buffy said, "Yeah. They’re currently having a massive geographical crisis, but... they’re mine."

She turned her head and looked up at him.

Buffy said, "And you haven’t run screaming into the night yet. Which, historically speaking, breaks all kinds of Summers family records."

Lucian shrugged one shoulder, his eyes still on the road ahead of them.

Lucian said, "I don’t scare easily. Besides, I like exactly where I’m standing."

Buffy kicked a stray pebble along the pavement and looked down at her sneakers for a second, the old defensive deflection she always ran through before she let herself be honest.

Buffy said, "It’s just... usually the guys who dig the normal Buffy get totally freaked by the pointy-stake, monster-dusting Buffy. And the ones who actually like the Slayer... well, they tend to be a little heavy on the brooding ‘creature of the night’ vibe. It’s exhausting playing two different girls."

Lucian reached out and casually bumped his hand against hers as they walked.

Lucian said, "And where do I fall on this highly scientific spectrum?"

Buffy looked up at him, and her tone was all quip, but her eyes were sincere.

Buffy said, "You’re basically the glitch in the matrix. You don’t try to bubble-wrap me, but you don’t look at me like I’m just a weapon, either. I don’t have to clock out of Slayer-mode to hang out with you. It’s... nice. Weird, but a very good weird."

Lucian smiled in the last of the fading light and laced his fingers through hers as naturally as breathing.

Lucian said, "Weird works for me."

Buffy squeezed his hand, a look of deep peace settling over her face, just as the chain-link fence of the basketball court came into view down the street, the first streetlights flickering on above it.

CHAPTER 4 The Court

On the basketball court at dusk, with the sun already down behind the houses and the streetlights along the fence buzzing to life, Lucian stood at the top of the key with the ball. Since he had just scored, he kept possession. Dawn stepped up to guard him, bouncing lightly on the balls of her feet with her arms spread wide, and Lucian held the ball against his hip and casually sized up her defense for a second.

Then, without any warning at all, he threw a hard, flat chest pass straight into Dawn’s chest. Instinct took over. Dawn caught it, but the force of it made her stumble backward a couple of steps, and she stood there for a split second holding the ball, completely confused, because she was supposed to be the one on defense.

While Dawn’s brain was still buffering the trick, Lucian circled smoothly around her opposite shoulder. He used that moment of confusion deliberately, putting Dawn right between himself and Buffy. Then he swept in, smacked the ball right back out of Dawn’s hands, drove hard to the hoop, and laid it up off the glass for an easy, uncontested score.

Buffy narrowed her eyes, half amused and half dangerous.

Buffy said, "Okay, buddy... now you’re asking for it."

Lucian jogged backward grinning and retrieved the ball. He clearly wasn’t trying to overpower either of them. He was playing smart, relying entirely on angles, positioning, and misdirection, and he started at the top of the key again, dribbling slowly, his eyes on Buffy.

Buffy wasn’t letting him play games this time. She came at him full speed, playing tight, insanely aggressive Slayer defense, low and fast on the balls of her feet, ready to strip the ball the second he moved. Lucian held his ground. He waited until the absolute last fraction of a second before she would have collided with him, and then he bounced the ball hard, right between Buffy’s legs in a flawless nutmeg. He exploded past her while her brain was still reacting to the sheer audacity of the move, caught the ball on the other side, and laid it up easily before she could spin around to recover.

He landed lightly on the asphalt and turned back to look at her, grinning and breathing a little heavy.

Lucian said, "Too slow, Slayer."

For a split second Buffy looked honestly shocked that anybody had just pulled that on her. Then her face changed. The amusement burned away into pure, unadulterated competitive fire, and her voice came out quiet, almost a growl, though she was very clearly enjoying the hell out of this.

Buffy said, "Oh... you are so dead."

CHAPTER 5 The Shadows Bite Back

The easy, warm feel of the game evaporated in an instant. The last of the dusk light had drained out of the sky behind the row of tall trees along the far edge of the court, and the streetlights threw long shadows across the asphalt. Out of the deep shade under those trees Spike came walking slowly toward the center of the court, hands buried in the pockets of his duster, his bleached hair bright against the dark trees behind him. His face was a mask of cruel amusement, but his eyes were burning with barely contained jealousy, and his voice dripped with sarcasm.

Spike said, "Charming. Truly. It’s like a bloody Norman Rockwell painting. Just needs a golden retriever and a white picket fence to hide the corpses under."

Dawn stepped behind Buffy at once, all of her playful energy gone. Buffy’s whole body shifted from relaxed athlete to coiled Slayer in a fraction of a second, her weight dropping onto the balls of her feet and her jaw setting hard. Dawn’s voice shook a little, but it was defiant.

Dawn said, "Leave us alone, Spike. We’re just playing."

Spike stopped a few feet away and tilted his head, looking at Dawn with mock hurt.

Spike said, "Watch your tone, Nibblet. Short memory on this one, isn’t it? Wasn’t it just yesterday I was pulling your ungrateful neck out of the fire at the high school? Seems I’m only family when the house is burning down."

Lucian stepped smoothly to one side and placed himself squarely between Spike and the girls. He didn’t puff out his chest or square up like a man looking for a fight. He just stood there like a solid wall, and his voice was level, without a trace of fear in it.

Lucian said, "I think she told you to leave."

Spike looked Lucian up and down, and a vicious, mocking smirk spread slowly across his face.

Spike said, "Right. The new blood. Look at you. All dark clothes and quiet brooding. You look like a wannabe rocker, mate. What, did you buy the angst kit retail, or did she just dress you up like this so she wouldn’t miss the real thing when the lights go out?"

Willow and Tara came around the chain-link fence just then, both of them smiling, coming to collect everybody.

Willow called out cheerfully as they walked, "Hey guys! Dinner is—"

They both stopped dead when they saw Spike. In perfect, unconscious unison Willow and Tara crossed their arms firmly over their chests, a literal wall of disapproval, and Tara’s voice was hard.

Tara said, "Spike. You shouldn’t be here."

Spike ignored them completely. He took a step closer to Lucian and dropped his voice into a low, dangerous, intimate register, looking right at him while he pointed one finger past his shoulder at Buffy.

Spike said, "She playing house with you, mate? Playing the sweet, normal girl? Because it’s a lie. Ask her about the singing. Ask her what she did the second the curtain dropped and the music stopped."

Buffy flinched, and her breath hitched in her chest.

Spike smiled, showing a flash of fang.

Spike said, "She came right to me. Out there in the dark. Lips on mine, begging for it. Real fire, too. She didn’t cash it in, but she showed her hand. And you want to know why? Because she knows exactly what she is."

He stepped around Lucian and locked eyes directly with Buffy. All the confidence from the game was gone from her. Spike’s words were hitting the exact trauma of her resurrection, and her eyes went glassy, her face hollowed out. She stared right through him, fully believing she was corrupted, while he kept going.

Spike said, "She’s not a human being. She’s a killer. She’s a monster. Just like me. And you can’t tame a monster with a basketball and a bloody walk in the park. She belongs in the dark."

Lucian’s face went hard for one visible moment of controlled fury, and then he moved. He lunged, grabbed the leather lapels of Spike’s duster with both hands, and used his entire body weight to yank the vampire backward, away from Buffy. Spike didn’t even stumble. He just laughed, a cold, dead sound right in Lucian’s face, and with a casual display of supernatural strength he shrugged and tore Lucian’s grip clean off his duster. In the same motion he stepped down on Lucian’s left boot and trapped it, so that Lucian had nowhere to put his weight and went over backward.

The chip fired the instant Lucian started to fall. A brief, white-hot flash of pain lanced through Spike’s skull, and he flinched and hissed through his teeth, but it was gone as fast as it had come. He never threw a punch.

Lucian hit the asphalt hard and pushed himself up on his elbows, looking at Spike.

Willow’s voice cracked like a whip.

Willow said, "Tara. Take Dawn home. Right now."

Tara didn’t hesitate. She grabbed Dawn’s hand and practically dragged the teenager away from the court, moving fast. Willow didn’t check on Lucian, and she didn’t go to comfort Buffy. She walked in a straight line directly up to Spike. She looked tiny next to him, but her posture was rigid, and when she spoke her voice dropped an octave, dead and echoing.

Willow said, "Leave. Or I will unmake you right here on the pavement."

Spike glared down at her, ready to spit another insult. Then a low, barely audible hum rose from somewhere beneath Willow, and the air around her seemed to darken, as if the last of the dusk light were being drawn straight out of it. Spike paused. He breathed in through his nose, and his eyes widened.

His vampire senses had hit a wall of suffocating dark magic simmering just beneath Willow’s skin. It didn’t just smell like ozone and blood. It felt like standing on the lip of a black hole that wanted to pull all the life out of the air and swallow it whole, and it was deeply, fundamentally wrong. Spike actually recoiled, one step backward, then two, his bravado stripped clean away. He didn’t know what kind of magic she had been into recently, but he knew she was lethal. He swallowed and tried to cover the sudden dread with a sneer.

Spike said, "Right. The witch is feeling twitchy. Wouldn’t want to get in the way of whatever... that is."

He turned up the collar of his duster and took another few steps back toward the shadows under the trees. He looked back at the group, at Buffy still frozen and glassy-eyed, at Lucian getting up off the ground, and at Willow radiating death, and his voice came out bitter, masking his own broken heart.

Spike said, "I’m going. But you just wait. All of you. When I come back here... I’m going to be a new man."

Spike turned and disappeared quickly into the lengthening shadows of the trees, and for a long moment after he had gone nobody on the court said a word.

CHAPTER 6 The Long Walk Home

On the court the suffocating weight of Willow’s dark magic slowly faded, and it left the whole place feeling unnaturally cold. Buffy shook herself out of her frozen, glassy-eyed stupor and blinked rapidly, the ghost of Spike’s words, she’s a monster... belongs in the dark, still echoing visibly behind her eyes.

She turned to Lucian, stepped forward, and offered him her hand. Lucian looked up at her from the asphalt without hesitation or anger, no bruised ego and no shame in his face, only a quiet understanding, and he gripped her hand and let Buffy haul him effortlessly back onto his feet. He dusted off his dark trousers and looked closely at her face, reading what was still lingering there, but he didn’t push her to speak.

Willow turned back to face them. Her posture was normal again, but her eyes were unreadable, and her voice came out flat and drained.

Willow said, "Let’s go home."

The walk back along Revello Drive under the streetlights was silent. Willow stayed a few paces ahead with her arms wrapped around herself. Buffy and Lucian walked side by side behind her, and the easy, playful closeness from earlier was gone. Lucian stayed quiet, but he kept glancing over at Buffy, watching the way she held herself, shoulders tight, gaze fixed on the pavement, withdrawn into herself completely.

He was turning over everything Spike had said, piecing together how deep her resurrection trauma really went. But he chose to be a silent, solid presence beside her rather than force her to talk about it in the middle of the street. His hands stayed at his sides, and he didn’t reach for hers yet. He matched her pace exactly, his shoulder a careful inch from hers, there for her if she wanted him and asking nothing.

CHAPTER 7 The Dust Settles

Outside the Trio’s house that night, two nights after the killings, heavy, angry boot steps crunched up the concrete driveway. Spike marched straight to the closed garage door of the mundane suburban house, furious and vibrating with the humiliation of being pushed around by a witch and rejected by the Slayer. He needed leverage, and he meant to bully the Trio into giving him something, a weapon, a spell, anything at all to level the playing field.

He hammered his fist violently against the aluminum garage door.

Spike yelled at it, "Open up, you Boba Fett wannabes! I know you’re in there playing with your circuit boards! Open the bloody door!"

Silence answered him, and Spike snarled. He stalked around the side of the house through the shadows to the back door, and he didn’t bother knocking. He grabbed the doorknob, planted his boot against the frame, and ripped the door clean off its hinges with a splintering crack, tossed it onto the grass, and stepped over the threshold without the slightest resistance, because there was nobody left alive in that house to keep a vampire out. He went down the dark hallway to the heavily bolted metal door at the top of the basement stairs and wrenched that one open too.

Down in the Trio’s basement lair the only light came from the harsh blue-white glow of the old CRT monitors, and the room felt colder and more oppressive than he remembered. Spike came confidently down the stairs, already talking.

Spike said, "Right, playtime is over, you little—"

He froze completely. He stopped dead on the bottom step, his eyes wide, looking exactly like a deer caught in headlights. There was no sound in the room at all, only a dead, ringing silence, and the air was impossibly heavy.

His vampire senses were entirely overwhelmed. The air down here was still thick with magic, heavy and recent, the charge of something powerful that had been worked in this room and had not yet faded. It carried the exact same suffocating scent that had rolled off Willow back on the basketball court, and whatever he had felt coming off the witch earlier, this was where she had used it.

Spike stepped slowly off the stairs with every instinct he had screaming at him that he was standing in a graveyard, and he looked around the room, where nothing made a sound. Thick electric cables, internet wires, and extension cords lay violently twisted and knotted together on the floor. He took another cautious step forward, his eyes scanning the concrete, and knelt down slowly.

Spread across the floor in three distinct, human-sized patches was a fine layer of white powder. When he looked closer there were no scorch marks on the concrete underneath it at all, the floor clean. Spike reached out with a trembling finger and touched the white dust, then rubbed it slowly between his bare thumb and forefinger. It wasn’t ash. It was bone, burned down to nothing but calcium.

His eyes widened as the sheer, terrifying reality of what he was kneeling in clicked into place. There hadn’t been a battle down here. There had been an execution. Under everything else he could still smell it, faint and sweet and scorched, the witch-fire that had washed over those bodies in a silent purple flame and left nothing behind but this.

Spike stood up slowly and backed away from the white powder, and that was when he noticed the door to the Trio’s cramped little basement bathroom standing ajar. A thin, sour smell was drifting out of it. He crossed to it and pushed it the rest of the way open with two fingers.

Bottles of lotion and hair spray lay scattered across the grimy floor where somebody had knocked them off the counter. Under the smell of old water there was a faint trace of vomit, rinsed away but not gone, and on the white porcelain of the sink clung a few strands of bright red hair.

Spike stared down at them. He didn’t feel disgust. Three dead nerds didn’t trouble his conscience one way or the other. What hit him was pure shock, cold and total, because he knew exactly one person in Sunnydale with hair that shade of red.

He backed out of the doorway, away from the sink and the white powder. The rage that had brought him here was entirely gone, and a cold, primal terror had taken its place as the pieces snapped together in his mind.

Spike whispered to himself, horrified, "Bloody hell, Red... what did you do?"

A low hum began in the corners of the basement, deep and steady, like one long note held somewhere under the concrete. Spike spun around, his vampire face snapping forward and his fangs descending, and dropped into a defensive crouch, scanning the empty shadows. The hum swelled until it was in his teeth and in the walls and rattling the metal tools on the workbench, so deep it hurt, and Spike clapped his hands over his ears, grimacing, and then all at once there was nothing.

The hum dropped away into a dead, absolute silence, and the air in the basement fell twenty degrees in a heartbeat.

Standing right in front of him, hovering an inch off the ground in a cold blue light that came from nowhere, was Drusilla. She looked impossibly real, every curl and every fold of her old lace dress exactly the way he remembered, and as he watched, her porcelain face slid smoothly into her vampire one and back again. Spike froze where he crouched as she spoke in her exact lilting, singsong voice, only colder, as if it came from very far away.

Drusilla said, "Beneath you."

Before Spike could even form a word the figure changed, as smooth as water, and it was Glory standing there in the same cold blue light, in the ripped red dress she had worn the night of the tower, her blonde hair wild, the ghost of a dead god looking down at him. She spoke in her exact arrogant, purring voice.

Glory said, "It devours."

Then the cold blue light went out like a breath, and Glory was simply gone, and the basement was empty again.

Spike was left standing alone in the freezing, silent basement, shaking, his eyes wide as the weight of something ancient crashed down over him. Slowly his vampire face retracted, and what was left was just the pale, terrified face of a man who had suddenly realized he’d walked into something infinitely bigger, and infinitely darker, than a witch’s revenge.

CHAPTER 8 The Warlock’s Den

The alleyway was pitch black that night and reeked of wet garbage and old rain, and Spike was pacing aggressively along a dead-end brick wall. He wasn’t looking with his eyes. He had one hand stretched out, palm trailing an inch from the cold bricks, feeling for the unnatural, static-electric hum of a cloaking spell. He stopped, his hand hovering over an entirely blank section of the wall.

Spike screamed at the brick, furious and desperate, "RACK!"

Nothing happened, and the wall stayed solid brick.

Spike snarled. His vampire hearing caught a soft, nervous scuffling behind him, and he spun around. A customer was trying to sneak quietly past him toward the wall, a young guy in an oversized hoodie. His features were a messy human-demon hybrid, pale, sickly skin and wide, translucent, fish-like gills where his ears should have been. Spike crossed the distance in a fraction of a second, grabbed the kid by the front of his hoodie, and hoisted him right off the ground with one hand. The kid thrashed weakly, completely unable to fight a master vampire’s strength, and Spike shook him like a rag.

Spike shouted at the blank wall, "Open the bloody door, Rack! Or I snap this little guppy’s neck, and I stand out here all night. I won’t let a single paying customer into your den!"

The air in front of the bricks shimmered violently. The illusion dissolved and revealed a heavy, rotting wooden door painted in chipped black paint. Spike scoffed, shoved the fish-eared kid carelessly aside so that he dropped into the alley trash, and kicked the heavy door open.

Inside Rack’s den the air was suffocating. Clouds of sweet-smelling purple-grey incense smoke hung under dim blood-red velvet lampshades, and a low hum pulsed up through the floorboards, the sound of raw dark magic. The place was a trap house for the supernatural. Scattered across a dozen filthy velvet Victorian couches were the magic junkies. A human woman in a business suit was slumped in a corner, giggling mindlessly as she watched her own fingers stretch and warp. A huge, scaled Brachen demon lay flat on his back on a rug with his eyes rolled back, shivering as he shed patches of scales in magical withdrawal. Two teenagers huddled together, catatonic, staring at a blank wall while faint blue energy danced lazily between their teeth.

Spike ignored them all and marched straight into the center of the main room.

Spike shouted, "Rack! Get out here!"

The long, bony fingers of a hand emerged from the darkness and slowly, deliberately parted the heavy red curtain at the back of the room. Rack stepped into the dim light, tall and gaunt in a perfectly tailored but worn dark suit, moving with a predatory, floating grace. He walked into the center of the room and raised both palms toward the ceiling, his eyes closed and a look of terrifying ecstasy on his face as he bathed in the dark energy of his own den. Slowly he opened his eyes and looked at Spike, not the least bit fazed, and his voice was impossibly smooth, with a faint echo under it.

Rack said, "What’s the fire, vampire? You’re ruining the high."

Spike marched up to him and stopped only inches away. He didn’t show any fear, but the desperation in his voice was impossible to hide.

Spike said, "I need answers, mate. Tonight. I just walked into a basement across town. Three bodies, completely gone. No scorch marks, no blood. Just a pile of white calcium dust and the smell of a purple flame that burned hotter than anything I’ve ever felt."

Rack’s smooth, arrogant posture twitched, and he slowly lowered his hands.

Spike said, pressing on, "But that wasn’t the worst part. The air went dead. Something else was there. It smelled like the absolute bottom of the world, Rack. It used the faces of dead women just to play games with my head. What kind of spell does that? What the hell could do something like that?"

Rack stared at him, and the casual drug-dealer act evaporated completely. He looked past Spike into the thick smoke of the room as if he were suddenly terrified of the shadows in his own sanctuary, and his voice dropped to a harsh, strained whisper.

Rack said, "That wasn’t a spell, William. A witch may have lit the fire... but the heat woke something else up."

Rack said, taking a slow step backward, "Something is coming. Something that has never come before."

Then Rack looked directly back into Spike’s face, and the whites of his eyes flooded with pure, suffocating pitch black. When his voice came again it was no longer his own. It was a dozen overlapping, whispering voices, scratching directly against the inside of Spike’s skull.

The voices said, "From beneath you, it devours."

Spike physically recoiled, stumbling backward a step. The heavy, sweet smoke of the den suddenly felt like ice in his lungs, his breath caught, his eyes went wide with dread, and his shoulders hunched as the thing’s presence washed over him.

Rack gasped violently, his whole body convulsing as if he had just been plunged into freezing water. The pitch black drained out of his eyes at once and left them wide and bloodshot. He clutched his own throat with both hands and stumbled backward until he hit a small wooden table and knocked a brass incense burner clattering to the floor. The warlock looked utterly horrified that his own body had just been hijacked without the slightest effort.

Spike got his footing back and stepped forward aggressively.

Spike said, "What was that? What the hell did you just say to me, Rack?! Name it!"

Rack held a hand out between them, his fingers trembling, nothing like his usual arrogant grace, and his voice cracked, breathless.

Rack said, "Get out."

Spike said, grabbing him by the lapels of his tailored suit, "Not until you tell me what’s living under this bloody town! You know what it is!"

The hum in the room suddenly climbed into a high, piercing whine, and the blood-red lampshades began to flicker violently. The magic junkies scattered around the room reacted to the sudden spike of volatile energy. The giggling woman in the corner burst into terrified, sobbing tears and curled up into a ball, and the Brachen demon on the floor covered its head with its arms and whined like a kicked dog.

Rack’s fear boiled over into furious, survivalist rage. He shoved Spike’s hands violently off his suit and drew himself up to his full height, raw, crackling red energy sparking suddenly around his long fingers, and his voice boomed with magical amplification.

Rack screamed, "It doesn’t have a name! It is the thing that made the dark! And you brought its shadow right to my door!"

Spike stood his ground and bared his fangs, ready to fight the warlock right there in his own den.

Spike said, "I didn’t bring anything—"

Rack cut him off, his eyes burning with red light.

Rack said, "You reek of it! It’s watching you, vampire! This is a house of pleasure and pain, not a waiting room for ancient gods! Get the hell out of my sanctuary!"

He thrust both palms forward, and a concussive wave of invisible kinetic magic erupted from his hands. The force hit Spike like a freight train. It lifted him bodily off his feet and threw him backward across the den, and he didn’t even touch the ground before the momentum carried him straight through the heavy wooden entrance door, splintering the frame into a hundred pieces.

Out in the alley Spike came flying out of the doorway and hit the wet asphalt with a bone-crunching thud, sliding through the wet garbage and dirty puddles until he came to a stop near a rusted dumpster. He groaned and rolled over onto his hands and knees, shaking the disorientation out of his head, and looked back toward the warlock’s den. The shattered wooden door and the red light inside shimmered violently, and with a sharp, electric snap the illusion re-engaged. The door vanished completely, leaving nothing behind but a solid, blank brick wall.

Spike was left completely alone in the dark, filthy alleyway. A slow, ominous roll of thunder sounded overhead, and a light, freezing rain began to fall, pattering against the asphalt and his leather duster.

He slowly pushed himself up onto his knees. He didn’t bother wiping the grime off his face. He just stared at the blank brick wall, and the swaggering vampire was entirely gone. He knew it then, kneeling in the garbage with the rain running down his face, that he was weaker than all of them now, the witch and the warlock and even the Slayer’s new boy. He looked down at his own pale, trembling hands.

Spike whispered to himself, the rain running down his face, "Can’t fight a god when you’re just a monster. Can’t fight the dark... not when you belong to it."

Slowly he clenched those trembling hands into fists, and a new, terrifying resolve hardened in his eyes. He wasn’t looking for a weapon anymore. He was looking for a transformation, and his voice dropped into a dead, absolute vow.

Spike said, "Time to be a man. Need my soul."

CHAPTER 9 The Harris Curse and the Vengeance Blessing

Inside the Magic Box that night only the small desk lamp on the main counter was on, throwing long, harsh shadows down the aisles of dusty artifacts. The front door was locked and the "Closed" sign was flipped. Anya stood behind the counter shoving Giles’s remaining reference books into a cardboard box with jerky, furious energy, her face stained with tears she refused to acknowledge.

The back door creaked open, and heavy work boots stepped onto the hardwood floor. Xander walked out of the back hallway, still in his dusty work clothes, looking exhausted, the emotional toll of Giles leaving sitting heavily on him. His voice was soft.

Xander said, "Ahn? You shouldn’t be packing those. Giles said we could keep the index volumes."

Anya didn’t stop. She shoved a heavy leather tome into the box so violently a page ripped, and her voice was sharp and trembling.

Anya said, "He left. Just like that. People in this town just leave! They get tired, or they get scared, and they pack a box and they go! So I am packing the boxes! It’s efficient!"

Xander stepped closer to the counter with his hands held up defensively.

Xander said, "Hey, hey. Come on. He just went back to England, he didn’t die. You’re overreacting—"

Anya stopped dead. She picked up a solid brass paperweight and hurled it directly at Xander’s head. He ducked wildly, and the paperweight smashed into a wooden display rack behind him and sent crystal vials scattering everywhere. Anya was screaming now, completely breaking down.

Anya screamed, "Do not tell me I am overreacting! I gave up immortality! I gave up the cosmos! I am stuck in this fragile, squishy human body that is going to get wrinkly and die, and I did it to be here! But you... you are always looking for the door, Xander! You hide behind your jokes, and your hammer, and your excuses!"

Xander straightened up slowly and stared at the shattered glass on the floor. His usual defensive sarcasm flared up, and his mouth opened to fire back some witty, deflecting remark. But he stopped. He looked at her face, and he saw the absolute, raw terror of abandonment in her eyes, and the defensive walls inside Xander Harris finally, completely collapsed.

The anger drained out of his face and left an exhaustion so deep it looked anchored in his bones, and his knees gave out. He dropped to his knees right there on the hardwood floor in the middle of the broken glass. He didn’t look at the mess. He looked up at Anya with tears welling in his eyes, his face open and raw with fear, and when his voice came it cracked as he laid bare the thing he was most afraid of.

Xander said, "I’m not looking for the door, Anya. I’m terrified of what’s inside the house."

Anya froze, her hands gripping the edge of the wooden counter, stunned by the sudden, total surrender in the way he knelt there.

Xander’s tears were running down his cheeks now, and his voice was a desperate whisper.

Xander said, "You don’t understand what it’s like in my head. I grew up in a house where love was a screaming match. My dad is a drunk who breaks things, and my mom pretends the house isn’t on fire. That is the Harris legacy. That is my DNA. I look in the mirror every morning and I am so deeply, paralyzingly terrified that I am going to turn into him. That I am going to hurt you."

Anya stepped slowly around the counter, looking down at the broken man on her floor.

Xander said, his voice shaking, "I’m just a guy with a hammer, Ahn. Buffy is a superhero. Willow is a god. And you... you were a demon of vengeance. You have seen empires fall. You are thousands of years older than me, and you’ve seen the absolute worst of what men can be."

He reached out with a shaking hand and gently took hold of her wrist.

Xander said, "But despite all of that... despite knowing exactly how small and flawed I am... you stayed. I don’t know how to be a great man, Anya. But I know that you are my heart. You are my soul. You are the only breath of fresh air I have ever had in my entire life."

He reached into the dirty front pocket of his work shirt and pulled out a small, slightly smudged velvet box, and he flipped it open to show a simple, beautiful diamond ring. He looked up at her, completely stripped bare.

Xander said, "I don’t want to run anymore. Please. Marry me."

Anya stared at the ring. Her breath caught in her throat, and her eyes went wide and filled with fresh tears, completely overwhelmed by the sheer, unfiltered humanity of the moment.

Before she could speak, the shadows in the corner of the shop seemed to peel themselves away from the wall, and there was a soft, shimmering chime of bells. Halfrek stepped out of the darkness, changed now into an immaculate dark Victorian gown that stood out starkly against the dusty magic shop. She stayed a little behind the two of them, looking down at Xander with a mix of aristocratic amusement and a warmth that was honestly surprising, and her voice purred.

Halfrek said, "Well. Look at that. What a brave little human you are."

Xander flinched, his eyes going wide, entirely caught off guard by the demon’s sudden appearance. Halfrek glided silently across the floor until she was standing right beside him, and she reached down and traced the line of his forehead with her long, pale fingers, smoothing his hair back in a strangely maternal, oddly affectionate gesture. She left her hand resting on top of his head and looked directly at Anya, smiling, and her voice was sincere.

Halfrek said, "He isn’t lying to you, honey. Not a single word. His heart is beating like a trapped bird, but his soul is entirely tethered to yours."

Anya let out a choked, wet sob, and her face crumpled and then broke open into a huge, radiant smile, tears streaming down her cheeks, a trembling breath of pure, overwhelmed joy shaking out of her.

Halfrek lifted her hand from Xander’s head and took a step back, melting seamlessly into the shadows of the shop, and her voice faded away into a mischievous, echoing laugh.

Halfrek called, "You had better reserve a very large block of hotel rooms, Anyanka! I’m telling the entire family!"

She vanished completely, leaving only the faint smell of ozone and old perfume behind her. Xander was still on his knees, staring up at Anya and holding the ring out with a shaking hand. Anya dropped to her knees right in front of him, heedless of the broken glass, grabbed the front of his dusty work shirt in both fists, and kissed him with everything she had.

CHAPTER 10 The Cleansing and the Confession

In the Summers upstairs bathroom later that same night, Buffy and Lucian had peeled off their dusty court clothes before the tub and slid into the bath together nude beneath the suds. There was no sound but the soft, rhythmic slosh of water against the porcelain, and the room was lit entirely by dozens of flickering amber candles set along the counter, the windowsill, and the floor.

Lucian sat against the sloped back of the tub, his long black hair wet and pushed back off his face, the large black raven tattooed across his chest and left shoulder, its wings spread wide. Buffy lay back against his chest with his chin resting gently near the top of her wet blonde hair. Their feet rested at the far end of the tub, playfully and comfortably tangled together. Beneath the thick blanket of white suds Buffy had her fingers laced tightly through his, holding their joined hands directly over her heart, the suds riding high over her chest.

For a long time neither of them said anything. Buffy just breathed, the slow rise and fall of her chest moving their joined hands up and down. When she finally spoke her voice was barely above a whisper, echoing a little off the tiles.

Buffy said, "Xander was the first one to actually see me."

Lucian didn’t interrupt. He just shifted slightly underneath her, adjusting his grip on her hand to let her know he was listening.

Buffy said, very quietly, "When I first came to Sunnydale, I was trying so hard to just be the new girl. The invisible girl. And he... he just saw right through it. Not the Slayer part at first. Just me. He brought me a Twinkie when I was miserable. He... he anchored me to the earth."

She squeezed his fingers tightly, and her voice wavered, thick with the terrifying reality of that morning at the Magic Box.

Buffy said, "If he goes to Texas... if he actually leaves... I feel like one of the main pillars holding this house up is just going to crumble. Giles is gone. Xander is packing. It feels like everyone who remembers the girl I used to be is disappearing."

Lucian’s voice was low, and she could feel it vibrate against her back.

Lucian said, "It’s terrifying when the people who built your foundation decide to move out of the house."

Buffy nodded slowly, her head brushing against his collarbone. She took a deep, shuddering breath. The warm water wasn’t enough to chase away the chill of what she had to say next, and her voice tightened and dropped.

Buffy said, "Spike’s words... on the court today. They weren’t just him being cruel, Lucian. He was using ammunition I gave him."

Lucian’s hands stayed perfectly steady, and he didn’t tense at all.

Buffy made herself keep talking.

Buffy said, "When that demon was here... the one that made everyone sing until they burned. After it was over, I was standing out in the alley behind the Magic Box. I was so empty. I was just this walking corpse pretending to breathe. And Spike was there."

She swallowed hard, the shame visibly tightening her jaw, her breathing going shallow and ragged, her eyes down, her whole body tense with the weight of what she was about to say. Lucian’s thumb went perfectly still against her interlaced fingers the moment she spoke, but he didn’t pull away, and Buffy forced the rest out of herself.

Buffy said, "I kissed him. I didn’t just kiss him, I... I let him put his hands all over me... I dragged him into the shadows. I wanted to feel something. Anything. Even if it was disgust. Even if it was dirty. I wanted the monster to make me feel like I belonged in the dark, because the light up here just hurts my eyes too much now."

Buffy slowly pulled her hands free of his and turned over in the confined space of the tub. As she rolled to face him the thick white suds clung to her wet skin, tracing the curves of her shoulders and arms and leaving the whole bare vulnerability of her body there in front of him. She pressed both hands flat against his bare chest, right over the raven’s spread wings, and pushed herself up just enough to look down into his face. The candlelight caught the unshed tears swimming in her hazel eyes.

She was searching his face. She was looking for the flinch, for the judgment or the disgust, for the moment he realized she was too damaged to hold onto. She was laying her darkest, most shameful secret completely bare in front of him, her voice breaking, every bit of armor stripped away.

Buffy said, "He told me I’m a monster like him. And when I’m standing in the dark with him... I believe it."

Lucian looked up at her. Water dripped slowly from her hair onto his chest.

He didn’t look away. His bright aqua-blue eyes were entirely clear, without a trace of the judgment she was so terrified of finding there. He slowly lifted one wet hand and gently cupped the side of her face, his thumb brushing a tear from her cheek, and his voice was quiet and low and certain.

Lucian said, "You’re not a monster, Buffy."

He let the words settle before he went on.

Lucian said, "You died. You were ripped out of a place that felt like peace and thrown back into a world that keeps trying to break you. You carry your sister, your friends, this entire town on your back every single night… and you still get up the next morning."

Lucian said, stroking his thumb across her cheekbone, "Yeah, you reached for the dark when the light was too bright. That doesn’t make you a monster. It makes you human. It makes you someone who’s been through hell and is still trying to find her way back."

He held her gaze, his voice steady.

Lucian said, "I’ve watched you. I’ve seen how hard you fight to protect the people you love, even when you’re barely holding yourself together. That’s not darkness, Buffy. That’s strength most people will never understand."

Buffy’s breath caught, and the fear in her eyes cracked wide open while the water went still around them.

Lucian kept his hand on her face.

Lucian said, "Spike wants you in the dirt with him so he doesn’t have to be alone there. But I’m not afraid of any part of you. Not the Slayer. Not the girl who’s exhausted. Not the one who sometimes needs the dark to breathe. I see all of it… and I’m still right here."

Buffy let out a shattered, relieved sob and collapsed forward, burying her face in the curve of his neck. Her arms wrapped tightly around him, and her wet body pressed along the whole length of his as the warm, soapy water moved around them. Lucian held her close, one hand stroking slowly up and down her wet back, anchoring her, while the candlelight went on flickering softly across their skin and the glistening suds.

CHAPTER 11 The Waiting Game

In the Summers master bedroom that night a soft reading lamp on the nightstand spread a cozy glow across the blankets. Willow and Tara were propped up against the pillows, comfortably tangled together with a heavy leather-bound book resting across both their laps, reading quietly. The heavy bedroom door stood wide open to the upstairs hallway.

Dawn stomped past the open doorway in oversized pajamas, clutching a toothbrush and a towel, and shot a dark, jealous glare down the hall toward the main bathroom, which was currently occupied by Buffy and Lucian. She let out a loud, theatrical sigh of pure teenage suffering and stomped back the other way.

Tara looked up from the book, her face softening, and called out gently.

Tara said, "Dawnie? Your sister’s still in the main bathroom with him, honey. You can use ours."

Dawn stopped in the doorway. She hesitated, then shuffled into the bedroom with her shoulders slumped in teenage suffering and her face flushed with embarrassment, and she let out a heavy sigh.

Dawn grumbled, "It’s so gross. They’ve been in there forever. I’m gonna have to go to school tomorrow smelling like sewer water because they’re… doing whatever they’re doing."

Tara set the book aside and reached out, taking Dawn’s free hand and tugging her closer to the side of the bed, soft and warm and a little hesitant.

Tara said, "C’mere, sweetie."

Dawn reluctantly let herself be pulled in. Tara looked up at her with that gentle, understanding gaze of hers, and she spoke quietly, the small stutter coming into her voice.

Tara said, "I know it’s hard watching them. The way they look at each other… the hand-holding, the kissing… all of it right in front of you. Makes you feel kinda left behind, huh?"

Dawn’s eyes dropped to the floor. She didn’t deny it.

Tara said, just as quietly, "It’ll happen for you too, Dawnie. I promise. You just… you just have to wait like everyone else has, that’s all. And that waiting part? It really, really sucks. I know."

She squeezed Dawn’s hand.

Tara said, "But when it does come… it’ll be yours. And it’ll be worth it."

Willow watched the two of them fondly from the other side of the bed and then chimed in gently.

Willow said, "And hey — when it does happen, you’ll have way better taste than your sister. We can all agree on that."

Dawn let out a small, surprised laugh despite herself, and some of the tension finally slipped out of her shoulders. She leaned in and wrapped her arms around Tara in a tight hug, and Tara hugged her back warmly and pressed a soft kiss to the top of her head.

Tara whispered into her hair, "Go brush your teeth, honey."

Dawn pulled back with a small, genuine smile, still a little embarrassed, and went into the en suite bathroom and closed the door behind her. The bedroom was quiet for a beat.

Willow yelled toward the closed bathroom door, "Don’t forget to turn on the blower this time!"

The loud mechanical vvrrrrrmmm of the exhaust fan kicked on instantly behind the door. Willow grinned and settled back against Tara’s shoulder, returning to the book as the house settled into a rare moment of peace.

CHAPTER 12 A New Set of Rules

Inside Spike’s crypt that night the only light came from a few scattered candles, their flames throwing long, erratic shadows across the stone walls and burial markers. A single shaft of moonlight cut down through a crack in the ceiling. Spike was sitting alone in the dark, his duster draped over a broken chair, shirtless, his pale skin marked with old scars and fresh bruises from the day, his head in his hands. He looked ancient, exhausted, and utterly lost.

He slowly lifted his head. His eyes were red-rimmed, and he stared at his own hands as if they belonged to someone else.

Spike whispered to himself, "Can’t fight a god when you’re just a monster..."

He stood up slowly and walked over to a small, cracked mirror on the wall and stared into it. It gave him back nothing but the empty crypt behind him. In the glass where his face should have been there was only candlelight, but he felt the demon face flicker up over his human one for a second, and then recede. His hands closed into fists at his sides, and when he spoke again it was quieter than it had been in the rain, flat and final, a promise made to nobody but himself.

Spike said, "Time to be a man. Need my soul."

He turned, grabbed his duster, and walked toward the heavy iron doors of the crypt, and he didn’t look back. He pushed the doors open, and a cold wind and light rain blew in around him. Spike stepped out into the night without any hesitation at all, the doors swinging shut behind him with a final, echoing clang, while the rain came down harder and thunder rumbled somewhere in the distance.
