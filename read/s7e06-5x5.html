Five by Five

Buffy the Vampire Slayer. Season seven, episode 06. Five by Five.

CHAPTER 1 The Highway and the Hunted

On a rural highway at night, absolute darkness wrapped the road. Headlights caught the wet asphalt and the low fog in long stretches, and the night pressed in on both sides of the lane.

The pristine jet-black 1967 Plymouth GTX powered through the fog. Its deep throaty V8 ran lumpy, cammed, and predatory over the hiss of tires on wet pavement and the low road rumble. There was no music, just the car and the night.

Inside the cabin it was almost black, lit only by the faint green instrument glow. Passing reflectors flashed across their faces.

Angel sat rigid in the driver’s seat in a heavy dark wool overcoat with the collar turned up and a dark shirt beneath, his hair tousled from speed and tension. His eyes stayed fixed forward, both hands tight on the wheel until the leather creaked under his grip.

Faith rode passenger in a black leather jacket open over a maroon tank, dark jeans, and heavy black combat boots kicked up on the dashboard. A silver Zippo sat in her right hand. Her hair was pulled back tight, loose strands wind-whipped against her cheek.

Her thumb flicked the Zippo open and shut in perfect time to the engine, click-clack. Each open threw an orange flame reflection across the windshield.

Angel’s eyes flicked once toward the lighter, brow tightening.

Angel said, "You gonna do that the whole way into town?"

Faith smirked, eyes still on the flame, not looking at him.

Faith said, "Depends. You gonna keep brooding like someone shot your favorite puppy, or we actually gonna talk about why we’re doing ninety in a fifty-five into the Hellmouth at two in the morning?"

She snapped the Zippo shut with finality, dropped her boots off the dash with a heavy thud, and turned her entire torso toward him. The smirk died. What remained was a flat battle-hardened stare.

Angel kept his eyes on the fog.

Angel said, "I told you. Cordelia had a vision."

Faith’s mouth hardened. The cutting edge came up under her voice.

Faith said, "Yeah, she has those. Usually doesn’t end with you busting me out of max security on a forged Wolfram and Hart transfer order because a bunch of blind guys in robes tried to shank me in the yard."

Angel’s eyes flicked to her for one raw moment, then back to the road, and the car drifted toward the shoulder until he caught it with both hands on the wheel.

Angel said, "They weren’t just assassins, Faith. Bringers. The First Evil’s priests. This vision put Cordelia in a coma. End of the world starting in Sunnydale. The First is waking up. It’s trying to wipe out the Slayer line. Both of you."

Faith took it in without a word, the cocky armor bleeding out of her posture. She leaned her head back against the passenger window, the glass fogging with her breath. Her hand rose unconsciously toward the old knife scar low on her stomach, under the maroon tank, then dropped into her lap.

Faith said, her head still against the glass, her voice softening in a way that seemed to surprise even her, "Right. Group up with the heavy artillery so I keep breathing. I get it. But we both know that’s not the only reason we’re doing ninety in a fifty-five."

Angel didn’t answer. His throat worked once, and for a long moment the only sound was the engine while the Zippo lay silent in Faith’s lap.

Then a massive grotesque wasp demon dropped vertically out of the fog and slammed onto the hood with its full body weight in a heavy wet thud. The hood buckled inward in a deep V with a metallic crunch, the paint splitting and buckling, as the car nose-dived and the suspension bottomed out. The windshield caved inward in a sagging spiderweb of laminated glass. The demon’s multi-eyed glistening face punched through it, stinger tail lashing.

Angel’s boot slammed the brake. Tires screamed. The GTX fishtailed. Steam and smoke rose from the radiator.

Faith did not even flinch; she drew both knees up and drove a double front kick into what was left of the windshield, blasting the remaining glass outward. She launched horizontally over the dash and through the shattered windshield frame — arms reaching, legs wrapping.

Outside, the demon’s wings snapped open with a wet crack, beat once, and the downdraft blasted the fog. It abandoned the vehicle and rocketed upward at a steep angle, thirty feet up, with Faith locked on its thorax, legs scissored, one arm around the neck joint, the other hand already reaching for her knife.

Wind whipped her hair into wild strands across her face. She threw her head back with a wild adrenaline grin.

Faith yelled, "Wheee! Now this is what I call a homecoming!"

The GTX skidded sideways to a smoking stop in the middle of the road. One headlight was dead; the other flickered crooked into the trees.

Thirty feet up, the demon rolled in the air, trying to shake her. Faith grabbed the thick vein-mapped wing bases, planted her right combat boot in the center of the segmented spine, and yanked backward with brutal Slayer strength.

The left wing joint snapped with a wet fibrous crack, the wing folded uselessly, and the demon plummeted. They hit asphalt in a rolling skidding impact — chunks of blacktop flying.

Faith rolled over the demon’s head and came up in a low balanced crouch ten feet away. Her right hand went behind her back and reappeared with an eight-inch curved hunting knife. The demon screeched a high chittering wail, reared, barbed stinger raised and dripping. A drop of green venom hit the asphalt, sizzled, and ate a white smoking hole.

Angel kicked the driver door open so hard the hinge protested. He stepped out already moving, a heavy black police Maglite leveled like a weapon. He thumbed it on, and a blinding white beam punched into the demon’s cluster eyes.

The demon shrieked, recoiled, mandibles clacking, momentarily blinded. It turned its head toward the light, and that was the opening Faith needed.

She exploded forward in a low sprint, ducked the sweeping stinger — the barb whistled over her head — and drove the knife up under the mandibles, deep into the soft tissue under the head.

The demon convulsed. Its shell split, and dark grey-black ichor bubbled from the wound and its mouth. It collapsed into a rapidly dissolving mass of bubbling steaming sludge that ate a shallow crater into the asphalt before evaporating into foul black vapor.

Faith pulled the knife free with a wet sucking sound. She wiped the blade clean on her dirty jeans in one long stroke, gave it a fast expert forward spin, and caught it by the handle without looking. She wore a cocky, satisfied smirk. One loose strand of hair stuck to the sweat on her cheek.

Faith said, already walking back toward the ruined car, voice low and satisfied, "Yup. Sunnydale, alright."

The GTX coughed, caught, and limped on toward town on one headlight.

CHAPTER 2 The Bathroom and the News

In the upstairs bathroom of the Summers house that same night, a few nights after Spike had come to the porch, a dozen thick pillar candles of varying heights burned on the windowsill, the tub edge, and the sink shelf, their gentle golden flames flickering. Steam rose in slow columns and beaded on the mirror and the window, and the scent of vanilla and lavender oil hung in the air. The deep cramped clawfoot tub forced closeness.

The water was milky with oil, white suds floating in irregular islands. Buffy’s knees broke the surface.

Buffy lay back against Lucian’s chest in the deep tub, spine curved into him, shoulders dropped, head resting under his chin. Damp blonde strands stuck to his jaw and throat. Lucian’s right arm wrapped around her from behind, hand protectively across her upper chest just below the collarbone, fingers relaxed under the bubbles. His left hand rested on the tub edge, trailing fingers through the water. Their legs were loosely tangled.

His jaw was mostly healed from the porch but still tender — a faint yellowing bruise along the left side and a thin healing line on his lower lip, a quiet reminder of Spike’s uppercut. The almost-healed wound at his lower left side from the Bringer did not scream tonight, but it had not gone anywhere either, and he kept that side angled carefully away from the tub edge without comment.

For several seconds there was only breath and the gentle lapping of water. Buffy’s bare toes surfaced at the far end, flexed once, curled. She playfully found Lucian’s toes underwater, gently stroking the top of his foot in lazy circles. Soft ripples crossed the suds. Her breathing deepened — stomach and ribcage releasing tension she had been holding since the porch, since the vision, since the cover story she had given them in the living room. She carried all of it with her, and she did not speak it here, not in the steam, not yet.

Lucian’s thumb stroked once across skin just above the suds on her upper chest, a small absent-minded pass that still landed as deeply tender.

Buffy let out a long, low, bone-deep sigh — the sound of someone finally stopping bracing. Her head turned a fraction more into his throat, eyes closed, and for a moment she looked younger, almost weightless against him.

After a long moment she slowly rolled in the water and lifted her upper body slightly. Water and suds cascaded off her shoulders in heavy sheets, and a few soft islands of foam slid and clung there.

She settled facing him more directly, legs adjusting, one knee rising slightly. Her wet hair was plastered to her neck and face. She looked at him a long second — searching for discomfort, judgment, hesitation — and found none. Lucian’s expression stayed calm and open, quietly awed, simply there with her.

She reached up and rested a wet hand lightly against the side of his neck, thumb brushing his jaw. Water ran down her forearm in rivulets.

Buffy’s voice came soft, almost wondering, low and a little rough from steam.

Buffy said, "I can feel my own heartbeat again."

Lucian’s left hand came up out of the water, gently covered the back of her hand where it rested on his neck, and his thumb stroked once across her knuckles, slow and deliberate.

Buffy still watched his face, expression open and raw. She didn’t look away. For the first time in weeks she was not performing strength or numbness — she was simply here.

They leaned in the last few inches, foreheads resting together. Their noses nearly touched, and their eyes closed. It was two people giving each other permission to stop bracing for five minutes.

They stayed like that while the steam drifted and neither of them reached for the next thing they were supposed to be bracing for.

Then urgent rapid pounding hit the bathroom door. Three hard knocks rattled the old wood, shockingly loud and intrusive. Water sloshed as both bodies tensed.

Willow’s panicked voice came muffled through the door.

Willow said, calling through the door, "BUFFY! BUFFY!"

Buffy’s eyes snapped open. Her body went from liquid relaxation to full tension in one heartbeat. She sat up slightly; water cascaded off her shoulders and chest. Lucian’s arm tightened protectively for half a second.

Buffy rose in the tub, foam clinging and sliding. She stepped over the high tub edge, water pouring off her in streams. Her wet footprints showed dark on the light floor.

She grabbed a large white bath towel from the rack and wrapped it around herself in one smooth motion, covering herself from under her arms to mid-thigh. The towel soaked through in patches, clinging. She tucked the corner securely.

She crossed the small space to the door in three quick barefoot steps. Her free hand reached behind the door and came back holding the heavy double-bladed silver battle axe she’d brought in from the back of the Trans Am. The axe head caught candle reflection as it rose.

Buffy pulled the door open, sharp and annoyed at the interruption of the first real peace in weeks.

Buffy said, "What now?"

Hallway light spilled in — cool flat ugly blue-white that killed the golden candle warmth on her face and shoulders. Willow stood there in her oversized cream cable-knit sweater and soft grey lounge pants, red hair escaping a messy low ponytail, eyes wide, breathing fast, one hand still raised.

Willow shook her head rapidly, words tumbling.

Willow said, "No, not this time. Fred called. Angel’s on his way. Right now."

Buffy’s eyes blew wide, pupils dilating. Her mouth fell open in pure deer-in-headlights shock. Color drained from her face. Her left hand clenched the towel tighter at her chest, knuckles white. She went completely still before anything else could move.

Willow winced, delivering the second blow.

Willow said, "And… he brought Faith."

Buffy’s fingers went completely slack around the axe handle. The heavy silver head tipped, fell the last six inches, and crashed onto the tile with a deafening ringing clang that echoed off the small walls. The clang hung in the small room until the echo finally thinned out and left them staring at each other.

Buffy stood frozen, staring through Willow into the middle distance. Water dripped from her hair and the towel onto the floor — steady quiet plip… plip… plip. Her breathing was shallow and fast.

Lucian stayed perfectly calm in the tub, his face deadpan, trying to cut the tension with a little gallows humor. "Should I stay in the bubbles?"

CHAPTER 3 The Fallout and the Vanity

Later, in her bedroom, Buffy perched on the small vanity stool, leaning close to the mirror. She wore a perfectly fitted dark crimson top with a subtle V-neckline highlighting her collarbones and the tension in her neck, and dark tight jeans. Her hair was brushed out but still damp at the roots, starting to wave. Her engagement ring was on her finger. She frantically applied black mascara with short aggressive strokes.

Lucian sat on the edge of the mattress behind her. He wore a tailored black button-down shirt with silver embroidery at the collar and cuffs, and dark trousers. His right hand rested on his left knee, and he unconsciously rubbed its knuckles with his left thumb in a self-soothing loop. It was the hand Angel had squeezed far too hard in that handshake at Xander and Anya’s wedding, long since fine and working perfectly well, and the rubbing was nothing but a nervous habit. His posture was protective but slightly closed off, insecure, his shoulders rounded. The calm from the tub was gone. His eyes flicked to Buffy’s reflection, then down. He sat a little carefully, weight favoring the good side, the way he had been sitting since the Bringer cut him.

Lucian’s voice came tight, self-deprecating, with clear insecurity under it.

Lucian said, "So… Angel. Legendary vampire with a soul… and a steel grip that could crush rebar. Guess my whole ‘normal guy who got lucky’ routine isn’t winning any points downstairs."

Buffy stopped mid-stroke, the wand hovering an inch from her lashes, then lowered it slowly, and her eyes found Lucian’s in the mirror. She turned on the stool to face him directly, giving him her full attention.

A soft genuine smile reached her eyes despite the exhaustion.

Buffy said, "Hey. I spent years on the brooding creature-of-the-night thing. Turns out my type is the incredibly brave, highly intelligent man on my bed. Let me do the heavy lifting downstairs, okay?"

Lucian rubbed his knuckles more noticeably and nodded, but he was not fully convinced. He took a deeper breath and straightened his spine a fraction.

Buffy turned back to the mirror and applied her lipstick in slow deliberate strokes, then blotted with a tissue.

Lucian said, quieter, more vulnerable, "Still… steel grip. I remember how that felt last time."

Buffy capped the lipstick and set it down. She turned on the stool again, leaned forward, and cupped his face gently with both hands for a moment, thumbs brushing his cheeks.

Buffy said, "You’re not competing with a memory. You’re here. That’s what matters."

They held the look a long moment. Buffy turned back to the mirror, picked up powder and brush, dusting her face while stealing glances at him in the reflection. Lucian watched, insecurity easing slightly — but the knuckles still got rubbed once more.

Outside, a car door opened and slammed with a hollow metallic sound.

CHAPTER 4 The Door and the Intimidation

In the front entryway, the crooked front door sat a little off true in its frame. The doorbell glowed beside the frame. Cold night air waited outside.

Buffy pulled the door open; it dragged and scraped on its crooked hang. Lucian stood a half-step behind and to her right, one hand resting lightly on her lower back for grounding.

On the front porch, a single harsh yellow bug-light bulb above cast strong downward shadows. Beyond the porch, the yard and street were swallowed in darkness.

Angel stood at the center of the porch, statue-still, in his heavy dark overcoat, buttoned, collar up, hands at his sides. His stillness was predatory and patient.

Faith stood to Angel’s right with her weight on one hip, in the same scuffed leather jacket and maroon tank, combat boots planted wide, arms crossed.

Buffy stood in the doorway in her crimson top and dark jeans, shoulders squared, chin lifted, her engagement ring catching a glint of the bug-light.

His lean mortal frame made Lucian look almost fragile next to the three. His right arm crossed over his stomach, his hand covering the old injury at his side.

Willow had come up at Buffy’s left shoulder.

Angel took a deliberate step forward, intending to cross the threshold — and stopped dead against nothing, the old invisible wall every uninvited vampire meets, his coat settling around him as he recoiled half an inch.

Willow’s voice came calm, controlled, steel underneath.

Willow said, "Angel. You can come in."

She pointed one finger at Faith, voice dropping.

Willow said, "I didn’t say you."

Faith froze. A faint hum had started deep in her inner ear the second her eyes went hard on Willow. She took one slow deliberate step backward on the porch, both palms up in a no-threat gesture.

Faith’s voice came low, almost respectful.

Faith said, "Whoa, down girl. I’m on a leash. Wolfram and Hart rules. I literally cannot leave his sight. Not here to start a body count, alright?"

Angel said, "It’s true. On paper, they released her into my custody. Probationary thing."

Willow’s answer was flat, ice cold.

Willow said, "Good."

Willow dropped her hand. Faith’s shoulders loosened, and the hum in her ear faded. Willow stepped aside and gestured them in with two fingers.

In the living room, warm light came from a floor lamp and the ceiling fixture.

Faith kicked her combat boots up onto the wooden coffee table without asking. Dried mud cracked and dropped onto the surface. She slouched until her spine was almost horizontal, her left arm behind her head.

Tara stood at the bottom of the stairs, looking up toward the landing with the quiet warmth she carried when she was watching over someone. Her long golden dirty-blonde hair hung loose in soft waves over a soft brown long-sleeve knit and a flowing skirt, her worn brown leather ankle boots quiet on the floorboards.

Tara’s stutter fluttered light under a gentle voice.

Tara said, "D Dawnie… come on down from there, sweetie. Grown-ups just need to talk. I’ll sit with you till you fall asleep, okay?"

Dawn nodded from the landing, in casual teen jeans and a simple top, her hair down. Tara climbed the stairs to her and took her hand, and they went up together, Tara’s free hand trailing the banister.

Some minutes later, soft footsteps came back along the upstairs hall, and Tara came down the stairs alone. She slipped into the kitchen and returned through the archway carrying a wooden tray with both hands. On it sat a plate of homemade chocolate chip cookies she had pulled from the oven while they waited for Angel, still warm, steam visible. Beside it stood a pitcher of iced tea with lemon, a few tall glasses, and a neat stack of cloth napkins. Her hostess calm was on the surface only; her eyes were tight at the corners.

She set the tray down on the coffee table right beside Faith’s boots. She straightened one napkin that had shifted, stepped back, and folded her hands in front of her.

Faith did not wait. She lunged forward, snatched three cookies at once in one hand, shoved one whole into her mouth, grabbed a glass with the other hand, and guzzled half of it in three long swallows. Crumbs tumbled down her chin onto the tank top and the couch. She chewed with her mouth open, completely feral, zero manners, a cookie in one hand and the glass in the other, eyes half-lidded in pure animal pleasure.

Tara folded her arms slowly across her chest and stood directly over Faith, looking straight down at her face — not the tray, not the mess, not the room. Only Faith.

The stutter surfaced again, her voice careful but with a steel cable underneath.

Tara said, "S sure. But still… Faith. I know all about you. You will respect my home, and my family."

Faith kept chewing. She swallowed a huge lump with visible effort and, without looking up, reached for a fourth cookie in complete, deliberate disregard.

Something in Tara’s face set hard. The stutter vanished.

Tara said, loud, clear, cutting through the entire room like a bell, "I want to hear you say it!"

Chewing stopped mid-bite. Faith slowly lifted her head and looked up at Tara, tilting her head like a curious animal. She studied Tara for one long moment the way a predator assesses a threat, then something in her shifted, the cockiness flickering off her face.

Faith said, deadpan, almost amused, but with real respect underneath, "Yes, ma’am."

Faith took a smaller, controlled bite of the cookie still in her hand. Her eyes stayed on Tara one second longer than necessary, a silent acknowledgment, then dropped back to the tray. She chewed more quietly.

Tara held the stare two full seconds and nodded once, sharp and final. She uncrossed her arms, turned, and walked back toward the kitchen archway without another word. In this house, the rules were hers.

The tension in the living room held a moment longer while Buffy took the armchair across from the couch and Angel lowered himself onto the far end of it, leaving Faith sprawled at the other.

Angel leaned back, palms pressed hard to his eyes.

Angel said, "It’s gonna be a hell of a night."

He lowered his hands and looked at Buffy with a completely deadpan expression.

Angel said, "So… you had another birthday party?"

Buffy’s eyes widened. She pressed her lips together tight, trying hard not to laugh, her cheeks puffing slightly. A few loud involuntary snorts escaped through her nose. She bent forward at the waist, one hand gripping her stomach, and tried to hold it in — but couldn’t. She cracked, letting out a sudden loud genuine laugh that sounded like it had been trapped in her chest for weeks, and rocked forward in the chair, laughing hard.

Faith paused mid-bite, watching Buffy with a small surprised smirk she quickly hid. Lucian leaned back slightly, quietly relieved to see her laugh.

The laughter slowly died down, and as it faded the tension crept back into the room.

Angel cleared his throat.

Angel said, "By the way… the wedding was beautiful."

Faith’s eyes lit up with cruel amusement. She leaned forward, elbows on knees.

Faith widened her eyes in mock surprise, her voice dripping false innocence. "Wedding? Congrats, B. So like you. Always the domestic type."

Buffy’s face hardened instantly, all humor gone.

Buffy said, "Wasn’t my wedding. Xander and Anya’s."

Faith’s smirk twisted into something vicious. She leaned in further.

Faith said, voice low and cutting, "Huh. I took his virginity. Guess the other girl just got my seconds."

Buffy’s voice dropped low in a cracking register, the fake biting humor doing nothing to mask the fury underneath.

Buffy said, "Oh, Faith… being a whore… that is so you."

The temperature in the room dropped. Buffy’s eyes flashed with rage. She stood up fast from the armchair, fists clenched at her sides, body tense and ready.

Faith immediately rose from the couch, squaring up across from her in the same aggressive stance.

Faith said, smiling now, eyes locked on Buffy, "Yeah… right there. Just like that, B."

Before either could move, Angel grabbed the back of Faith’s jacket and yanked her backward onto the couch with controlled force.

Angel said in a low warning, "Don’t make me chain you up and put you in the trunk again."

Willow said from off toward the kitchen archway, "Can I watch?"

Angel turned his head, looked at Willow with a tired exasperated expression. He let out a long sigh.

Angel said, "This isn’t a game. We have to work together."

CHAPTER 5 Caleb Enters

It was close to three in the morning when the doorbell rang.

Buffy was still facing Faith, the anger not finished. She looked once at the others in the room, then started for the door without letting Faith out of her attention.

Buffy said, "I’ll get it."

She reached the door handle and opened it without looking out, still oriented toward Faith and Angel on the couch.

Caleb stood there in a charcoal suit jacket and trousers, his white shirt open at the collar, hands behind his back, smiling, head tilted to the side. He was fully corporeal, real weight on the porch boards, real breath in the cold air, and a wrongness that sat under the smile like rot. If a thin whine had started in his ear, he smiled straight through it.

Caleb said, voice calm, "Do you have the time to hear about my lord and savior?"

Caleb’s fist took her in the forehead before anyone saw it move, knocking her off her feet and into the wall opposite the front door. Buffy went out cold and limp as her back hit the wall — she bounced and smashed down through the small entry table against that wall, the keys-and-mail console collapsing under her. Joyce’s framed photo had been sitting on it; when the table hit the floor the glass cracked straight across Joyce’s face.

Everyone moved at once.

Lucian and Willow lunged forward, each grabbing one of Buffy’s wrists. They dragged her backward toward the kitchen in a panicked stumbling retreat — Buffy’s feet skidding on hardwood, body still half-limp from the impact.

Caleb stepped fully inside, shoving the front door so hard it slammed back into the wall and tore loose from its top hinge, then spread his arms wide, palms open like a preacher welcoming sinners. He filled the doorway. His eyes were black. He looked straight at Faith.

Caleb’s voice came low and calm, and deeply wrong. "Another dirty girl… I am going to purify you first."

Faith’s expression hardened into something feral. There was no hesitation.

Faith said, dark, almost amused, already moving, "Come try it, preacher."

She exploded forward. Her right backhand cracked into Caleb’s forehead with a sickening meaty impact, and his head snapped back. She drove her boot into his ribs before he could recover — a deep wet thud.

Caleb’s reply was instant and brutal. He backhanded her across the face with terrifying strength — the crack of knuckles on cheekbone loud and sharp. Faith launched sideways, crashing through the coffee table. Cookies, glass, iced tea exploded in every direction. She hit the floor hard, rolling, struggling to get her hands under her, blood already at the corner of her mouth.

Angel’s face turned to cold, focused, ancient rage.

Angel said, his voice low and dangerous, "Wrong house, preacher."

Caleb surged at him — right, then left, brutal one-two. Angel ducked the first, weaved the second, then kicked straight into Caleb’s right knee. The joint buckled with a horrible wet pop. Caleb went down hard, crashing into the wreckage of the smashed entry table near the front door. Broken wood and glass skittered across the hardwood under him.

Caleb pushed himself up fast, breathing steady, almost relaxed. He brushed glass off his jacket like it was nothing.

Caleb said, voice calm, almost pleasant, "You people really do love your little houses. So nice and thoughtful you have all gathered here together for me."

Then his eyes flickered — not black anymore, something wrong and fractured behind them. He stumbled backward half a step, hands coming up to his ears as if a high-frequency whine only he could hear were splitting his skull. His balance went, and he swayed.

Faith saw the opening and took it. She was already moving, knife unsheathed in one fluid motion. She slashed across his chest in a vicious diagonal, then immediately cut a second time across his left bicep. Fabric tore. Blood welled dark and fast.

Caleb’s hand shot out and clamped around her wrist like a vice. He spun her violently, using her momentum to turn her to face Angel, then hurled her into him. Faith collided with Angel hard, and both of them staggered.

Caleb was losing it now — stumbling, hands still clamped over his ears, eyes unfocused, breathing ragged. Whatever was happening was getting worse by the second.

Then a low hum rose in the broken doorway, and a cold blue light gathered there out of nothing.

The First stood inside it in Buffy’s form, in the white sweater and grey slacks Buffy had died in, calm and cold and perfectly wrong. She put no weight on the boards, stirred no air, and left no footprint; she was nothing but light and a hollow stillness.

Willow’s breath stopped. Buffy was on the floor behind her.

The First looked at Caleb with something almost like maternal disappointment.

The First spoke in Buffy’s voice, calm and soft and utterly terrifying. "Caleb… my only son. Leave now. You can kill them later."

Caleb stared at her for one fractured second, and then he ran, straight through the image of Buffy as if she were nothing but light, and vanished into the night. Behind him the cold blue glow went out and the hum dropped to silence.

The front door, hanging from its bottom hinge, swung crookedly shut behind him in the night draft.

Silence crashed back into the room, broken only by heavy breathing, the slow drip of blood onto hardwood, the faint crackle of broken glass under shifting feet.

CHAPTER 6 Aftermath

In the living room, the warm domestic lights now felt fragile. The broken Joyce photo caught a sliver of light on the floor.

Faith was already moving on pure instinct, knife still in hand, heading for the door after Caleb.

Angel’s voice came sharp, commanding but not cruel.

Angel said, "No. Use your head like I have been teaching you."

Faith stopped cold, turned, and looked at him, breathing hard.

Faith said, quiet, almost childlike for half a second, "But boss…"

Angel just looked at her and said one word.

Angel said, "Faith."

She exhaled through her nose, and the fight left her. She turned and walked over to where Buffy was, the knife disappearing back into its sheath, and dropped into a crouch a few feet away, watching but not crowding.

Angel moved to where Caleb had fallen and knelt slowly. He dipped two fingers into the blood on the floor, rubbed them together, lifted them to his nose, and inhaled once — deliberate, focused. His expression shifted as he pulled his hand away, staring at the blood on his fingers.

Angel’s voice was low and grim. "He is human… but not. There is something inside of him."

He stood, wiped his fingers on his coat, and started toward Buffy — then stopped. He saw Lucian already there and deliberately stayed back, giving them space. His eyes flicked once to Lucian’s face, then away.

Lucian had Buffy in his lap on the floor. She was completely out, her head lolled, her breathing shallow but steady. A large dark bruise was already blooming above her right eye, the skin split in a small ugly crack bleeding slowly down toward her temple. Lucian’s hands trembled as he held her face — thumbs brushing her cheekbones with desperate gentleness, like he was afraid she’d disappear if he let go. His breathing was ragged. He kept swallowing hard, eyes locked on her face. His healing jaw clenched. When he leaned, the cut under his shirt pulled once and he ignored it. None of that mattered next to her being out.

Tara came back from the kitchen with a clean washcloth dampened with warm water. She knelt beside them without a word and gently dabbed the blood on Buffy’s temple, touch careful and practiced.

After a minute Faith got up, went into the kitchen, and sat on one of the stools at the island, silent, elbows on the countertop, staring at nothing, her fingers white where she gripped the edge.

Willow was still standing near the archway, breathing hard, eyes darting between everyone.

Willow said, voice shaky but clear, "The barrier worked. We put it up because the Bringers have no eyes… no tongues. But they have to see somehow. It affects the inner ear."

Angel looked over at her, genuinely impressed despite everything.

Angel said, "That’s smart."

Willow nodded once, turned, and headed upstairs fast — footsteps on the steps, then a drawer opening and closing. She came back down with a small brown bottle of smelling salts from the Magic Box stock, knelt beside Lucian and Buffy, uncapped it, and held it under Buffy’s nose.

Buffy woke up instantly — eyes flying open, body jerking upright in Lucian’s arms. She sucked in a sharp breath, disoriented for half a second, then her gaze snapped around the room, taking in everyone. The bruise above her eye was already darkening, blood still trickling from the split.

CHAPTER 7 Faith & Tenderness

The kitchen sat half-lit under the lamp, with cooler light leaking in from the damaged front doorway.

Everyone looked exhausted, still wound tight from the fight.

Faith pushed away from the counter, restless.

Faith said, "I need a cigarette."

Buffy’s head snapped toward her, eyes flashing. The stress of the night was still right under her skin.

Faith clocked it instantly. She rolled her eyes and lifted both palms in a sarcastic whoa gesture, her voice dripping mock innocence.

Faith said, "Chill, B. I know… not in the house. I remember."

She slipped around the edge of the group, boots quiet on the kitchen floor, pushed through the back door into the yard. The door creaked once, clicked shut behind her.

Out back, almost no light reached the yard. Weak spill from the kitchen window and a distant streetlight through the trees left the small enclosed space deep in shadow, the house warmth cut off the moment the door closed.

Faith stood with her back against the siding, a few feet from the door. She tilted her head left, then right, in slow deliberate neck cracks, sharp and physical in the quiet.

She reached into her jacket side pocket and pulled out a battered cigarette pack. Her right thumb flipped the top open with a practiced flick. She brought the pack to her mouth, teeth gently catching the filter, and drew a cigarette out slowly, the paper rasping against her teeth. She closed the pack, tucked it away, and produced the Zippo.

The lighter flared, a bright orange flame in the dark reflecting in her eyes for half a second. She lit the cigarette and took a long deep drag. The cherry glowed hot. She exhaled a slow stream of smoke into the night air, watching it dissipate.

Her breathing was steady but shallow; she was still coming down from the fight.

She didn’t turn her head, but her eyes flicked sideways toward her left shoulder in a tiny movement. The mask on her face slipped for a split second, and a decision landed.

Faith said, quiet, almost to herself, "Fuck it."

She flicked the cigarette down, stepped on it once with her boot, and pushed off the wall, already turning like she was about to head somewhere — anywhere but back inside.

As she turned, she found Tara standing a few feet away, arms crossed, half in shadow. Tara had slipped out after her without a sound and had been watching, waiting. Her face was calm, but her eyes were sharp and soft at the same time, her long golden dirty-blonde hair catching the faint streetlight.

Faith froze. Her hands slid into her jacket pockets, her posture defensive, not aggressive.

Faith said, "I thought you didn’t want me here? I thought you knew about me?"

Tara didn’t answer right away. She stepped forward slowly, closing the distance. Her voice was quiet, with no hesitation — the clear, grounded tone she used when she was seeing straight through someone.

Tara said, "I see people. I see how much you’re hurting. More than anyone I’ve ever met. It’s… it’s a lot to carry alone."

Faith opened her mouth to deflect — classic smirk starting — but Tara didn’t let her. She moved in the last step and wrapped both arms around Faith, pulling her into a full steady hug. She rested her cheek against Faith’s shoulder, eyes closing, her breathing calm and even.

Faith’s whole body went rigid. Her hands twitched at her sides, fingers flexing as if fighting the instinct to push away or run. Small expressions flickered across her face: shock, anger, something dangerously close to grief. Her eyes squeezed shut for one second.

After a long moment, Faith’s arms finally moved, very slowly, as if the decision had to be dragged out of her one muscle at a time. Her hands came up and settled on Tara’s back — not tight at first, then gradually pulling in until the hug was mutual and she meant it. Faith’s face, half-hidden against Tara’s hair, cracked completely. No sound came out of her — only the raw silent release of someone who hadn’t let anyone hold her in years.

The night sounds dropped lower. Tara’s breathing stayed steady; Faith’s grew increasingly ragged. There was a single quiet hitch in Faith’s breath, and a faint rustle of fabric as her fingers finally gripped the back of Tara’s soft knit.

They stayed like that longer than Faith would ever have allowed from anyone else. Neither of them counted the seconds. Tara did not rush her, and Faith did not pull away.

Tara didn’t speak. She only held Faith steady against her, arms firm without trapping her.

CHAPTER 8 Holding the House Together

By the time the cleanup was in full swing, the long night had worn down to its last hour. In the living room, lamps threw light across the wrecked entrance. Plaster dust hung in the air over broken glass on the floor, blood spots and cookie crumbs tracked through the mess, overturned furniture shoved aside, and the front door hanging badly on its frame.

Lucian and Buffy were at the front door, working fast. Together they had lifted the ruined door off its last hinge and leaned it against the porch rail, and now Lucian held a big sheet of plywood up into the empty doorway while Buffy hammered in wooden cleats along the frame to brace it, so the sheet could be eased aside and set back like a door, all of it small practiced teamwork. His healing jaw was tight with effort, but he kept going. When he shifted his weight, the cut at his lower left side pulled once; he adjusted without making it a speech.

In the kitchen, Willow was on the old yellow corded wall phone, pacing as far as the curly cord would let her, her voice fast and tight.

Willow said, "Xander, listen… some guy just came in here. He looked like a backwoods preacher, but he was scary strong. Scary strong. He knocked Buffy cold in a single punch. Yes, she’s fine, you know Buffy, she can take way more than that. Oh, he also threw Faith through the coffee table like she was nothing. And he just walked right in. No invitation. He’s human… or at least he looks like one. But the hex I put on the house! It worked! It messes up the inner ears of anyone that wants to hurt someone inside the house, like anyone evil."

She listened for a second, nodding.

Willow said, "We need a real door this time. Like the ones your crew used to fix before you moved. Something big. Something strong."

Willow said, "Buffy… Xander wants to talk to you."

Buffy set the hammer down, wiped her hands on her pants legs, crossed into the kitchen, and took the receiver from her.

Buffy let her voice go raw, letting Xander hear the truth. "Hey… Yeah. It was bad, Xand. I’m… I’m scared. I’m really scared this time. This guy hit me harder than Glory did. One hit and I was out cold. I woke up on the floor with Lucian holding me and I couldn’t even pretend I was okay."

Buffy heard Xander swallow on the other end.

Buffy said, "And… Faith is here too. Angel brought her."

Xander went completely quiet on the line for a long moment.

Xander said finally, "…So everything bad hitting all at once again? Got it."

Buffy said, "Faith is here to help this time… I think."

Xander let out a short, very Xander laugh — half disbelief, half dark humor.

Xander said, laughing a little on the line, "Faith helping? Wow. The universe really decided to throw the whole special edition apocalypse at us, huh? Alright, tell her I said not to burn the place down while she’s ‘helping.’ I’ll call my old crew, get them over ASAP. Hang in there, Buff. We’ve survived worse… mostly."

Buffy smiled faintly despite everything.

Tara had taken charge of the living room. She started picking up glass and debris near the coffee table. Faith grabbed black trash bags and a broom, ripped a bag open with her teeth, and swept and bagged aggressively around the couch and broken table right beside her. The two of them worked side by side in focused silence.

Dawn came downstairs and walked straight to the shattered picture frame of Joyce on the floor. She knelt, carefully removed the photo of her mother from behind the broken glass, brushed shards off with her thumb, hugged it briefly to her chest, and carried it protectively into the kitchen.

The phone eventually moved from Buffy to Dawn. Dawn took it, her voice a mix of trying to be brave and still shaken.

Dawn said, "Hey Xander… It’s Dawn. Yeah… it was really scary. I was upstairs and heard everything. But we’re all here now. Even Faith and Angel. Tara’s making everyone feel a little safer. When are you coming?"

She listened, nodded, voice softening a little more by the end of the call, then handed the phone back to Willow.

Angel stood near the damaged front door. He suddenly winced as a thin beam of first dawn light crept dangerously close across the floor through the gap the plywood didn’t cover yet. He stepped back sharply, crossed through the kitchen, and pulled open the door to the garage at the top of the steps.

He glanced at Buffy right before he went.

Angel said, low and genuine, "Sorry…"

He went down the steps into the garage, and the door closed behind him with heavy finality.

CHAPTER 9 The Choice

Back in the living room, Buffy tied off a large black trash bag and handed it to Tara. Faith tapped her arm twice and motioned upstairs with a small head tilt. Buffy nodded and followed her up.

At the top of the stairs, Faith pushed into the upstairs hallway bathroom directly across from Buffy’s bedroom door.

Faith flicked on the light. The overhead light flickered once, then steadied, harsh and white over the mirror and the clawfoot tub. She crossed to the toilet in the corner, unbuckled her belt, pushed her jeans down and sat, tired-eyed, a small private smirk on her face.

Buffy followed her in, shut the door with a soft click.

Buffy said, arms crossed, half-exasperated, "What?"

Faith’s voice came low, genuine concern under the edge.

Faith said, "Are you sure about being with a normal person like that, B? I saw him kneeling over you… he almost lost it when you were knocked out."

Buffy leaned against the sink, expression softening into something raw and deep.

Buffy said quietly, eyes steady on Faith, "He saw me when I was dead inside, Faith. Not the Slayer. Just the girl who wanted the quiet of heaven back. He didn’t try to save the hero. He stayed for the broken one… and made me want to choose life anyway. That’s not normal. That’s everything I didn’t know I needed."

Faith tore off some toilet paper from the dispenser, wiped, and flushed, then stood, pulled her jeans up, and zipped and buckled them.

She stepped to the sink, turned on the faucet, and washed her hands while the water ran, looking at Buffy in the mirror, more open than usual.

Faith said quietly, drying her hands on a towel, "I missed this house, you know? Before everything…"

After a moment, she spoke softer.

Faith said, "I am sorry about your mom."

Buffy said, soft and genuine, "Yeah… me too."

Faith reached for the door handle. Buffy stepped forward, gently but firmly grabbed her wrist, stopping her. Faith paused, tilted her head with a small curious smile.

Faith said with a soft smirk, eyes warm, "What, B?"

Buffy let go, voice still earnest.

Buffy said, "You don’t understand what he did for me."

Faith laughed low, glanced meaningfully down at Buffy’s stomach area for a split second, then back up with a classic wicked-but-not-cruel grin.

Faith said, "Oh… I understand exactly what he does for you."

Buffy rolled her eyes hard, exasperation back but with reluctant fondness.

Buffy said, "I forgot who I was talking to."

Faith grinned one last time, opened the door, slipped out into the hallway. Buffy lingered alone for a single moment, small smile tugging at her mouth despite everything, fingers lightly brushing her engagement ring. The door clicked shut behind Faith.

CHAPTER 10 The Life Left Behind

In the garage at early dawn, a single hanging work light threw a pool of yellow across the concrete while pale dawn leaked under the garage door and through a small high window. Angel stayed deep in the shadows near the back wall. The garage held quiet, with faint birds beginning outside.

Angel, in his heavy dark coat, tired but regal, stood with his arms crossed.

Lucian entered from the house door carrying two mugs, in his black button-down with the silver embroidery, sleeves rolled now. Careful and respectful, he stopped a polite distance away.

Lucian said quietly, offering a mug, "Figured you might still be in here. Sun’s coming up."

Angel took the mug with a small nod and wrapped both hands around it, and for a little while neither of them said anything.

Angel said softly, almost nostalgic, "She was fifteen when I first saw her. All fire and attitude… but there was this light in her eyes. Like the world hadn’t broken her yet. She made me believe in something better."

Lucian listened, expression open and humble — no jealousy, quiet understanding.

Lucian said with a thoughtful, small smile, "I can see it. She still has that fire. But now… she lets herself be tired sometimes. Lets herself be human. I think that’s what she needed most."

Angel studied him a long moment. The morning light crept closer, and Angel shifted deeper into shadow but didn’t look away.

Angel’s voice came low and genuine, with a quiet grace in it. "This is the life… well, you are the life… that I left her to have."

He raised his mug slightly in a small toast. Lucian nodded once, respectful and grateful. They stood in silence a few seconds.

Angel looked toward the house door with a small, almost proud smile.

Angel said softly, "Take care of her."

Lucian said, a quiet promise, "Every day."

They held the look while the faint morning light grew outside.

CHAPTER 11 The Altar and the Son

Across town, as the sun came up over Sunnydale, the underground wine cellar of the abandoned winery flickered with warm candlelight. Dozens of thick black and red candles on the floor and ledges threw deep shadows across cold stone walls and dusty old wine racks, blood-red accents catching in the wax. A crude altar waited on an overturned barrel draped in black cloth, the First’s symbol carved and burned into it. The cold cellar air held still.

Caleb knelt shirtless in the center, facing the altar. His chest and left arm showed the fresh deep knife slashes Faith had given him in the living room fight.

The wounds visibly healed shut — dark blood stopped flowing, edges pulled together with a wet fleshy sound, skin knitting closed unnaturally fast, leaving angry red scars.

Caleb remained perfectly still, head bowed, breathing slow and reverent.

He stayed kneeling there a while longer, the new red scars shining wet in the candlelight while the wax ran down onto the stone.
