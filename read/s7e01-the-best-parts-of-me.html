The Best Parts of Me

Download this script (.txt)

Buffy the Vampire Slayer. Season seven, episode 01. The Best Parts of Me.

CHAPTER 1 The Weight of the Past

A few weeks after the thing wearing Glory’s face had laughed at them in the Summers living room, early in the morning at the Magic Box, soft light came through the half-open blinds on the front windows and caught the dust in long pale beams across the worn wooden plank floor. Crystal trays waited on the glass-topped wooden counter, and apothecary jars lined the tall shelves against the warm beige walls. The round dark-stained research table in the middle of the shop was scattered with leather-bound books and loose papers, and Buffy’s black three-quarter-length leather jacket hung over the back of a mismatched antique chair beside it, creased at the elbows.

Tara stood behind the register, quietly organizing a small tray of crystals on top of the glass display case, her movements slow and careful, almost mechanical, as she set each stone back in its place. Dark circles sat under her eyes, and her long golden dirty-blonde hair was pulled back loosely in a low ponytail that had already begun to slip. She kept her eyes away from Buffy and Willow at the research table, and her fingertips lingered a little too long on every stone she touched, as though she were grounding herself through the cool weight of it.

Buffy and Willow stood near the research table, speaking in low voices. Buffy was in jeans and a fitted black tank top, a thin silver chain with a small cross pendant resting against her collarbones, her blonde hair in a low messy ponytail. She held a white paper coffee cup with a plastic lid in her right hand. Willow, in a burgundy top and white canvas sneakers, had her arms crossed tight over her chest, shoulders hunched slightly forward.

Buffy said quietly, sounding tired, "Lucian’s been looking at some contract work. Tech consulting, systems work… the kind of stuff he used to do before everything at Calax went to shit. He’s been quiet about it, but I can tell it’s still eating at him. I.R.I.S. lying to him like that… what she actually did down there. He doesn’t talk about it much, but I know it’s sitting with him."

She took a slow sip of coffee through the little opening in the plastic lid, and her fingers tightened around the paper cup until it gave a soft crackle.

Buffy said, "I keep telling him he doesn’t have to rush into anything. But he says he needs to stay busy. Keep his hands in something real."

The something real, for now, was a contract job on the tech-support floor at Daniels Technologies across town, the first offer that had come through and far beneath the work he used to do. There was a long pause after Buffy said it, and Willow glanced over at Tara at the register and then back down at the table, her voice dropping even lower.

Willow said, "She still can’t sleep unless I’m holding her. Not just… holding. I have to squeeze her. Hard. Until her breathing changes and she finally goes under. Because of whatever that thing was... wearing Glory’s face."

Willow’s throat worked, and her eyes stayed fixed on the scarred wood of the table between them.

Willow said, "And even then… sometimes she wakes up shaking. She won’t talk about it in the morning. But I know what it is."

She took a slow breath, and her voice dropped until it was barely above a whisper.

Willow said, "She remembers. Everything. From when she was like that. When I had to… change her. Clean her. Feed her. She remembers being gone. And she remembers me doing all of it."

Willow’s eyes were glassy, but she didn’t let the tears fall; she just kept staring at the wood grain of the table like she was trying to disappear into it.

Buffy didn’t say anything right away; she just watched her friend for a long moment, and then she set her coffee cup down on the table, stepped in close, and wrapped both arms around Willow’s shoulders. Willow didn’t hug back at first — her arms stayed crossed — but she leaned her forehead against Buffy’s shoulder.

Buffy said, quiet, close to her ear, "I know."

She didn’t offer any big promises; she just held her, her palms resting flat and steady on Willow’s back between the shoulder blades.

Buffy said, "We’ll figure it out. Whatever this thing is… we’ll deal with it. Same way we always have."

Willow didn’t answer; she just stayed there a few seconds longer, eyes closed, breathing slow and uneven against Buffy’s shoulder.

Tara glanced over from the register and saw the two of them holding each other beside the research table, and without saying anything she turned back to the crystals and went on organizing them with slow, tired hands.

A customer approached the counter from the front of the shop, the brass bell above the door chiming softly. Tara straightened slightly, trying to reset her face into something professional while Buffy and Willow remained at the research table.

CHAPTER 2 The Cracks in the Normal

Tara stood upright behind the counter with her hands resting lightly on the glass display case, trying to look steady and professional, but her polite smile never quite reached the tightness around her eyes. She looked like she could cry at any second if she let her guard down. Her right hand trembled once as she reached for a stone. A flash of memory crossed her face — the weight of a body that wouldn’t move on its own, the smell of soap and fear.

The customer said, "I was reading that bloodstone is good for emotional healing and grounding… but I’ve also heard tiger’s eye is better for protection and confidence. I’m not sure which one I need right now."

Tara paused before answering. Her voice was soft and knowledgeable, but there was a slight tremor underneath it that she was trying hard to control.

Tara kept her voice soft, trying hard to sound confident, and said, "Bloodstone… it’s very grounding. It helps with emotional balance and can support you when you’re feeling… scattered or drained. A lot of people use it when they’re working through heavy feelings or recovering from something difficult. It’s also tied to circulation and vitality."

She gently picked up a small polished bloodstone from the tray and turned it slowly in her fingers so the customer could see the deep green with red flecks. Her eyes lingered on the red flecks a moment too long, and her throat tightened.

Tara said, "Tiger’s eye is more about protection and inner strength. It helps with clarity and shielding you from outside negativity or fear. It’s good if you’re feeling anxious about the world around you… or if you need to feel more… solid in yourself."

She carefully placed the bloodstone back down, her fingertips pressing into the cool surface as if anchoring herself. The memory hit — Willow’s hands on her skin, gentle but shaking, washing away the nothing that had been left behind.

Tara said quietly, "It… depends on what you’re carrying right now. If it’s more about healing what’s already inside… bloodstone might help more. If it’s about feeling safe from what’s outside… tiger’s eye might be better."

The customer nodded and started asking another question, but Tara’s attention was already slipping. Her eyes were glassy. She drew a shaky breath through her nose and forced a small, polite smile as she finished the interaction and rang up the purchase. Her hand shook as she took the money and counted out the change. As soon as the customer turned to leave, Tara’s composure cracked; she stepped out from behind the counter fast and walked toward the back hallway without looking at Buffy or Willow, slipped into the small bathroom, and shut the door behind her a little too hard, so that the slam echoed through the quiet shop.

Willow and Buffy both looked up at the same time and stared at each other. Buffy started to move toward the bathroom, but Willow gently caught her by the forearm and stopped her. Willow shook her head once, not unkindly, but firmly, then let go of Buffy’s arm and walked toward the bathroom door herself, her steps careful, the way someone would approach a wounded animal.

Willow stopped in front of the door. She could hear Tara on the other side — quiet at first, then the sound of sobbing, raw, muffled crying that she was clearly trying to keep quiet. The sound hit Willow hard — the same broken sounds from when Tara was gone, when Willow had to change her, feed her, bathe her, hold her while she stared at nothing.

Willow raised her right hand slowly toward the door handle, fingers starting to glow with a faint soft light — preparing to magically unlock it. The glow flickered once, thin and unstable, and a tiny spark of pain crossed her face as her damaged magic protested even that much.

Tara said, her voice breaking from inside the bathroom, "Don’t…"

Willow froze. Her hand stayed raised for a second, then slowly lowered, and she rested her palm flat against the door instead.

Willow said, her voice strained with worry but kept quiet because of the customers, "Tara, please let me in, even if it’s just to sit in the corner. Baby, I can’t be away from you right now."

Six long seconds passed before the lock clicked on the inside and the door opened slowly. Tara stood there with her eyes red and wet and her cheeks streaked with tears, and without saying a word she stepped back so Willow could come in.

Willow slipped inside quickly and closed the door behind her. The bathroom was small — closed toilet, sink, single bulb, plain wood door with a frosted glass panel. She immediately pulled Tara into a tight hug. Tara collapsed into it, gripping the back of Willow’s top like she was drowning. Willow stroked her hair slowly, one hand cradling the back of Tara’s head, thumb moving in small arcs against her scalp. Tara sank down onto the closed toilet lid, still holding onto Willow.

She buried her face against Willow’s stomach and sobbed, shaking, broken sounds that she was no longer trying to hold back, her whole body trembling, real tears tracking down her cheeks and a stress flush rising on her cheeks and upper chest.

Willow stayed standing, gently rocking her, one hand continuously stroking Tara’s hair while the other rubbed slow circles on her back. She didn’t speak; she just held her, with her own eyes closed tight and her breath coming shaky. Tara’s tears soaked into Willow’s shirt at her stomach.

Out front, Buffy had taken over the register, and two customers were now waiting in line. She glanced toward the back hallway with a worried expression, then turned back to the customers and forced a small, tired smile as she started helping them. Her hands moved on autopilot, but her eyes kept flicking back toward the hallway.

Now she was helping a middle-aged man with a scruffy salt-and-pepper beard and a worn black leather jacket. She was holding up a large glass jar filled with dried, gnarled pieces of what looked like mushrooms.

Buffy said, trying to sound professional, "So this is toadstool. It’s… good for certain protection spells and banishing work. You just have to be careful with the dosage because it can be pretty potent."

She unscrewed the lid to show him the contents. As soon as the seal broke, a thick, pungent, earthy-rot smell hit her full in the face. Buffy’s entire body recoiled, and she almost dropped the jar, catching it at the last second with Slayer reflexes, then gagged hard, turning her head away and coughing.

Buffy said, her voice strained, "Jesus— okay, yeah. That’s… that’s strong."

The customer laughed — a warm, genuine laugh at her reaction.

The customer said, "Yeah, it’ll do that. My old supplier used to keep it in a sealed box for a reason."

Buffy quickly screwed the lid back on, still making a face and waving her hand in front of her nose. She set the jar down on the counter like it personally offended her.

At that moment, the bathroom door opened.

Willow stepped out first, followed by Tara. Tara’s eyes were still a little red, but she looked noticeably more grounded than before, her posture straighter; she’d clearly made an effort to pull herself together. She glanced at Buffy behind the counter, met her eyes, and gave her a small, quiet nod — a silent I’m okay now — before walking over and gently taking over the register.

Willow stayed near Buffy for a second, speaking low so the customer didn’t hear.

Willow said, "I’m gonna run across the street and grab some stuff from Espresso Pump. Coffee and muffins for everyone. You want anything specific?"

Buffy said, without hesitation, "Chocolate chocolate. The big one. And hazelnut cream with butterscotch, large."

Willow gave a small smile.

Willow said, "Got it."

Willow headed toward the front door. The bell above it rang softly as she stepped outside.

Tara finished ringing up the customer’s purchase and handed him his bag with a polite, if slightly forced, smile. Once he was gone, she turned to Buffy. She took a quiet breath, then spoke.

Tara said softly, trying to sound normal, "You need any help?"

Buffy looked at her for a second — really looked at her — then gave a small, tired smile back.

Buffy said, "Yeah. Please."

Tara nodded once and moved closer to help Buffy with the next item on the counter. Neither of them said anything else for a while; they just worked side by side in silence.

A little later, Willow returned carrying a cardboard tray with three paper cups under purple plastic lids and a brown paper bag. She set everything down on the counter.

Willow said, "Hazelnut cream with butterscotch for you."

She placed one cup in front of Buffy and handed her the big chocolate chocolate muffin.

Willow said, "Blueberry and crumb for Tara. Poppyseed and banana for me. All black otherwise."

Buffy immediately grabbed her muffin and started eating. Tara just stared at hers, unmoving.

Willow watched Tara for a moment, then gently picked up the blueberry muffin. She broke off a small piece and held it to Tara’s lips. After a second, Tara leaned forward and took it. Willow stroked the side of her face as Tara chewed. Tara leaned in and gave her a soft, tired kiss. Willow kissed her back.

Willow said quietly, "I will always love you. You’re my heart. The best parts of me… they’re all you."

Tara rested her forehead against Willow’s for a second, eyes closed.

Then a faint hum rose at the back of the shop, just outside the sunlight, a cold blue light gathered in the shadow there, and out of it a voice spoke, low and familiar and wrong.

It was Spike’s voice, and it was the First, wearing Spike’s face.

The First said softly, almost gently, "She still flinches when you touch her, Red."

The First said, "Even when she’s trying so hard not to."

The figure stood in the shadow beyond the window beams. It looked exactly like Spike as he used to be, unburned and whole, in dark layered clothes and a black coat, but hollow. Its eyes were empty. There was no breathing and there were no small movements; its hair was perfectly still.

Buffy didn’t hesitate; steady, she grabbed the small crossbow from under the counter and fired straight at its heart. The bolt passed clean through the figure’s chest, right over the heart, with no dust and no wound, and buried itself deep in the wall behind it.

Buffy’s face went perfectly deadpan, and her voice came out flat. "I knew it."

She had known it the moment she saw it. It was like Glory in the living room: a vision, not real.

The apparition looked down at the place where the bolt had passed through its chest. Then it looked back up at Buffy, surprised but not scared, one eyebrow lifting with that old cocky tilt of Spike’s head, and slowly parted its hands.

The First’s voice came soft and cold. "You can’t kill what’s already dead, Slayer."

The First said, "But you’re running out of time to save what’s still breathing."

It smirked at her, cocky, Spike’s smile with nothing behind it, and then the cold blue light went out, the hum dropped away to silence, and it was simply gone, with no dust and nothing left behind except the bolt still buried in the wall.

Buffy kept the crossbow raised for another second in the quiet it left. Tara had gone completely still, and Willow’s hand still rested on Tara’s face, but her eyes were locked on the empty shadow where the thing had stood. Slowly Buffy lowered the crossbow onto the counter and looked at Willow.

Buffy said calmly, "Will, research a way for that thing to stop being able to haunt us in our private spaces. We need to find a way to keep it out."

Willow’s brow furrowed in deep thought and she nodded to the suggestion.

CHAPTER 3 The Smaller Life

At Daniels Technologies that afternoon, the office was a standard open-plan tech support floor, rows of grey fabric cubicles under harsh fluorescent lighting. The company logo — gold lettering on a green oval background — was visible on the wall near the break area. The atmosphere was quiet except for the low hum of computers and occasional keyboard clacking.

Lucian sat at his desk near the back, focused but clearly tired. His long black hair was pulled back into a clean, tight ponytail. He wore a white lab coat over a black button-up shirt, the coat the only remnant of his old life at Calax. His posture was straight, but there was tension in his shoulders. His right foot tapped steadily against the floor under the desk, restless, rhythmic, unconscious.

On his monitor, lines of code scrolled slowly as he worked. The script was clean but basic — far below the level of work he used to do at Calax.

Without taking his eyes off the screen, Lucian reached over with his right hand, took a slow sip from a paper coffee cup, set it back down beside the keyboard, and went on typing.

From the cubicle directly behind him, a head suddenly popped up over the wall. Jenette leaned forward, resting her chin on her crossed wrists. She had multiple facial piercings — nose, eyebrow, and lip — dark eye shadow, and dark lipstick. Her black hair was tied back messily with strands falling around her face. She wore a pink and purple striped top. Tattoos were visible on her chest and forearms. Several silver rings sat on her fingers.

Jenette said casually, chewing her gum, "Hey… you got a sec? I’m stuck on one-forty-three C-S-E-S again. Keeps timing out on the overtime loop. I’ve been staring at it for twenty minutes and it’s starting to feel personal."

Lucian didn’t look annoyed. He stopped typing and turned his head slightly toward her, his eyes tired but kind. Under the desk, the foot tapping slowed but didn’t stop.

Lucian said quietly, measured as always, "Check the sleep timer on line forty-seven. You’re probably hitting the API rate limit before the loop finishes. Try adding a two-second delay after the query call and see if it stabilizes."

Jenette’s eyes lit up a little. She nodded quickly.

Jenette said, "Ohhh. Yeah. That might actually work. Thanks, man."

She dropped back down into her chair and immediately started typing again — fast, almost aggressive.

Lucian watched her head disappear behind the cubicle wall, then turned back to his own screen and took another slow sip of coffee that had gone lukewarm and bitter. Under the desk his right foot started tapping again, faster now, and he stared at the basic log parser script on his monitor for a long moment with an expression no one could have read.

CHAPTER 4 The Bathroom Break

Later, Lucian sat at his desk, looking more tired than before. He rubbed his eyes with his thumb and forefinger, still trying to focus on the screen, his foot drumming faster under the desk. He reached for his coffee cup, took a slow sip, and set it back down.

He kept typing, the keys clicking steadily under his fingers, and then, all at once, everything stopped.

The constant low hum of the office vanished. Every keyboard around him went silent mid-click. The water cooler stopped bubbling. The computer fans and hard drives went quiet. Even the distant sound of the air conditioning cut out completely.

Total silence held the whole floor for five full seconds.

Lucian slowly lifted his head. He looked around the office, turning slightly in his chair. No one else seemed to notice. Everyone was still at their desks — fingers frozen above keys, mouths half-open mid-sentence — but nothing was moving. There was no sound at all; even his own breathing felt too loud inside his skull, and the silence felt thick and wrong.

Then, all at once, the sound returned. Keyboards resumed clicking. The water cooler gurgled. Computer fans spun back up. The normal office noise flooded back in as if nothing had happened. Lucian stared for another second, then slowly rubbed both hands down his face. He picked up his coffee cup, drained what was left, and set it down. He went back to typing, though his movements were slower now.

Above him, the fluorescent lights flickered once — a quick, unnatural stutter — then returned to normal.

Lucian leaned back in his chair. He raised both arms above his head and stretched, letting out a long, tired exhale. His eyes stayed half-lidded for a moment. He reached to the right side of his monitor and picked up a small white plastic sign. In bold black lettering it read: BATHROOM BREAK. The sign had a small built-in number display on the bottom — like old suitcase combination wheels. Lucian carefully adjusted the tiny dials with his thumb and forefinger until the numbers read the current time.

He stood up, picked up his empty paper coffee cup, and walked away from his cubicle. No one looked up or acknowledged him, and the office simply carried on as if he weren’t there. At the corner where the hallway turned left he dropped the cup into the trash can without breaking stride and kept walking.

He stopped at the water fountain, leaned down, and drank deeply for several seconds, and water ran down the side of his mouth; he wiped it with the back of his hand and continued down the short hallway.

He pushed open the bathroom door and stepped inside.

CHAPTER 5 You’re Already Dead

In the bathroom, Lucian flushed the toilet and stood up. He adjusted his pants and fastened his belt, then pulled the stall door open and stepped out into the main bathroom area. The tiles were off-white and slightly scuffed, with four sinks along the left wall and a long row of stalls on the right, and it was quiet.

Lucian walked over to the middle sink. He turned on the cold water, cupped his hands under the stream, and splashed his face, twice. Water ran down his chin and dripped onto the collar of his black shirt beneath the lab coat.

He gripped the edges of the sink and stared at his own reflection in the mirror. His eyes were tired, and the sadness from earlier was still there, sitting just behind his expression. He didn’t move for a few seconds; he just watched the water drip from his jawline.

He turned off the tap and wiped his face once with his hands.

A faint hum had crept into the room under the buzz of the lights, and a cold blue cast lay over the far end of the long mirror, where Ben stood at one of the sinks pretending to finish washing his hands, with no water running and his hands perfectly dry. He was wearing a white lab coat identical to Lucian’s, with a name badge on his chest that clearly read CALAX RESEARCH, and his skin looked slightly hollow, his movements too smooth.

Lucian moved down to the sink beside that one, turned the water back on and began washing his hands, and as he did, he glanced sideways into the long mirror, and his eyes caught the name badge in the reflection. It wasn’t from Daniels Technologies.

Lucian slowly turned his head and looked directly at the man standing beside him, his body very still.

Ben turned his head slightly and offered a small, tired smile, his voice gentle, almost kind.

Ben said, "Hey. Rough day?"

Lucian studied him for a second. His voice stayed calm, but there was a hint of recognition in his eyes — and fear. His hands were still under the running water, but he had stopped moving them.

Lucian said, "You… worked at Calax."

Ben said softly, "Yeah. I did."

Ben looked around the bathroom slowly, taking in the cheap tiles, the flickering fluorescent lights, the scuffed floors. His movements were delayed by a fraction — like the image was catching up to the sound.

Ben said, "Look at you now. From working on something that actually mattered… to this. Sitting in a grey box, fixing other people’s broken reports for eight hours a day. You used to build things that could think. Feel. And now you’re just… existing. Clocking in. Clocking out. Becoming smaller every single day."

Lucian’s hands curled into fists under the stream, knuckles white. Water kept running over them.

Lucian said, "I.R.I.S. wasn’t nothing. She destroyed herself to stop something that would have killed a lot of people. That wasn’t nothing."

Ben’s expression didn’t change. He just kept looking at Lucian with that same quiet, almost sympathetic gaze. He took one small step closer, and his voice dropped lower, and the air around him felt slightly colder.

Ben said, "She’s gone. And you’re still here. Doing this."

Ben said, "How long until she looks at you differently? When the money runs out and everything you built starts falling apart… do you really think she’ll still see you the same way?"

Lucian’s hands started to shake. His breathing changed — sharp, shallow. Anger flashed across his face. He suddenly lunged forward and grabbed Ben by the lapels of his lab coat — and his hands went straight through him. No resistance. No warmth. Just empty air and the faint wrongness of the mimic.

Lucian stumbled forward slightly, catching himself on the sink. His eyes widened. Water from the still-running tap splashed onto his coat.

Ben said quietly, almost gently, "You’re already dead, Lucian. You just haven’t stopped breathing yet."

Ben smiled faintly, a cold and empty version of a smile, and then the blue cast drained out of the mirror, the hum dropped to silence, and he was gone, while the fluorescent lights overhead flickered once more.

Lucian was left standing there, breathing hard, hands still gripping the edge of the sink. His reflection in the mirror looked shaken, water droplets clinging to his face and collar, and the water kept running, and he didn’t turn it off.

CHAPTER 6 Dawn’s Normal Day

At Sunnydale High, Dawn’s classroom was half-full. Students were quietly working on an assignment under flat institutional fluorescent light. Dawn sat near the back, doodling absently in her notebook, her pencil moving without much purpose. She wore jeans and an oversized grey hoodie, her overstuffed backpack on the floor beside her, and there were tired shadows under her eyes. She was trying to blend in, but everything she carried showed in her hunched posture and the way she kept her head down.

Over the PA system, a calm female voice spoke.

The PA announcer said, "Dawn Summers, please report to the principal’s office. Dawn Summers to the principal’s office."

Dawn looked up at the speaker on the wall. She let out a long, tired sigh and hung her head for a second, then pushed her chair back, the legs scraping loudly, and grabbed her backpack off the floor with a rustle.

The teacher said, "You can go, Dawn."

The rest of the class turned to look at her. No one said anything. They just stared — the kind of quiet, knowing look that said they assumed she was in trouble again. Dawn avoided their eyes, slung her bag over one shoulder, and walked toward the door with slightly clumsy, awkward steps. The weight of the backpack made her list a little to one side.

Dawn walked down the main hallway alone, the echo of her sneakers far too loud in the empty corridor. She turned right at the end of the corridor and stopped in front of a door with a small plaque that read ROBIN WOOD, PRINCIPAL. She hesitated for a moment, adjusting the strap of her backpack, then opened the door.

Inside, Robin Wood sat behind his desk, calm and composed. He wore a crisp white button-down with the sleeves rolled once and a dark tie loosened slightly, and his sharp eyes were not unkind. He gestured with one hand toward the chair in front of him. The office was neat, almost aggressively normal — no mystical clutter, just files and a coffee mug.

Robin Wood said, "Please. Sit down."

Dawn dropped her backpack onto the floor with a thud and slumped into the chair. She folded her arms and rolled her eyes slightly. Her body language screamed I don’t want to be here. A clock ticked on the wall behind Wood’s head.

Wood picked up his mug of coffee, took a slow sip, and set it back down. He flipped through a few pages of Dawn’s file before finally looking up at her, folding his hands together.

Robin Wood said, "I’ve been looking over your records. I know about your family’s history with this school… and the incident at the junior high involving unnatural forces."

Dawn opened her mouth, but Wood calmly raised one hand to stop her. She closed it again, lips pressed tight.

Robin Wood said, "I’m an intelligent man, Dawn. I don’t pretend to ignore what happens in this town. I see the patterns clearly. Your test scores are erratic, but in certain areas they show real brilliance. You’re smarter than you seem to believe. The issue isn’t intelligence — it’s emotional stability and control. Which is normal for someone your age."

He reached into a drawer and slid a plain white envelope across the desk toward her.

Robin Wood said, "This is a letter for your sister, Buffy. I’d like you to give it to her."

Dawn took the envelope and stuffed it into the front of her backpack with more force than necessary.

Robin Wood said, "I’m aware that problems happen in this town. However, I want to make one thing perfectly clear — this school is not going to have the same issues the old one did. That letter contains my direct contact information if Buffy has any questions. But the message is simple: keep the supernatural away from my students. That includes you while you’re at my school."

His tone stayed calm but became more serious. Dawn’s shoulders curled inward a fraction.

Robin Wood said, "If you have any problems while you’re at school, I want you to come to me directly — not any other teachers. My door is always open. You can sit right there and tell me what’s going on, face to face."

He glanced up at the clock on the wall. The second hand ticked loudly in the quiet office.

Robin Wood said, "I also want to meet your sister in person during parent-teacher night. Not one of her friends standing in like they used to do in junior high. I expect Buffy to show up herself because she is your legal registered guardian and caregiver."

Robin Wood said, "You may go back to class now. The bell’s about to ring for lunch."

Dawn stood up, grabbed her backpack, and headed for the door without another word, her sneakers dragging a little on the way out.

Back in class, the bell rang loudly. Students immediately started packing up. Dawn quickly stuffed her notebook into her bag and walked up to the teacher’s desk to grab the homework sheet she had missed while she was in the principal’s office.

The teacher said, "Here you go. Try not to miss the next one."

Dawn nodded and headed out into the hallway.

In the cafeteria, the lunch line moved slowly. Dawn grabbed a tray and slid it along the metal rails. She got a square slice of pizza, some limp green beans, and a carton of chocolate milk. She paid and turned around with her tray.

Josh appeared beside her with a grin. He was a little shorter than Dawn, stocky and strong for seventeen, with clean pale skin covered in light freckles across his nose and cheeks. He had bright blue eyes and a short blonde buzz cut. A small silver stud earring sat in his left ear. He was wearing a loud, colorful nineties-style button-up shirt tucked into baggy jeans.

Behind him were his two friends, both built like football players. Jayden was tall and broad-shouldered, dark skin, short cropped hair, and an easy, loud energy. He was carrying two trays like it was nothing. Arturo was slightly shorter than Jayden but just as solid, with warm brown skin, short dark hair styled neatly, and a calmer vibe. He had a small tattoo on his forearm that was half-hidden by his sleeve.

Josh said, "Hey."

He leaned in and gave Dawn a quick, gentle kiss on the lips. She kissed him back for a second before pulling away with a small smile.

Dawn said, "Hey."

They started walking toward an empty table near the middle of the cafeteria. A few tables away, a big circular table full of cheerleaders all turned to look at Dawn at the same time. Their stares were cold and obvious. One of them leaned over and whispered something to the others, and they all laughed quietly.

Dawn noticed but pretended she didn’t. She sat down and put her tray on the table, her eyes fixed on the pizza. Josh sat across from her while Jayden and Arturo took the spots on either side.

Jayden dropped into his seat loudly and said, "Bro, that last drill almost killed me. Coach is actually trying to murder us before the game."

Arturo said, calmer, "You’re dramatic. It wasn’t that bad."

Josh hesitated a moment, then turned to Dawn.

Josh said to Dawn, smiling, "So… you still down for that haunted movie night thing this weekend? I found this old VHS tape at my uncle’s house that looks cursed as hell."

Dawn immediately looked down at her pizza square and picked at the edge of it with her fork.

Dawn said, "This pizza actually looks cursed. Look at this cheese. It’s like… plastic."

She took a big bite and chewed slowly, clearly trying to change the subject. Josh watched her for a second, then decided not to push it.

Josh said, playing along, "Yeah, that looks radioactive. You sure you don’t want my fries instead?"

Dawn said, with her mouth half full, "No, I’m good. This is… fine."

She took a long sip from her chocolate milk carton, using it as an excuse not to keep talking.

Jayden said, oblivious, already halfway through his food, "Yo, did you guys see what happened in gym today? That one dude tried to do a backflip off the bleachers and almost died. It was awesome."

Arturo said, "He didn’t almost die. He just looked stupid."

Dawn kept eating, clearly grateful that the conversation moved on. She glanced once more toward the cheerleader table. One of the girls caught her eye and didn’t look away. Dawn quickly looked back down at her tray.

Josh said softly, only to her, "You okay?"

Dawn said quietly, "Yeah. Just… long day."

She took another bite of pizza and washed it down with more chocolate milk, clearly done with the topic.

CHAPTER 7 From Beneath

Back at the Magic Box, the afternoon light had shifted through the windows, warmer and flatter. The crossbow bolt remained buried in the wall at the back of the shop where Buffy had put it through something that wasn’t Spike.

Willow stood at the fax machine near the counter, holding the corded phone to her ear.

Tara was nearby, quietly organizing a display of crystals, but she kept glancing over with a tense expression. She did not look at the shadowed corner where the voice had come from that morning.

Buffy was a few feet away, leaning against the counter while holding a cordless phone to her ear. Her eyes flicked once toward the bolt in the wall and away again.

Willow said into the phone, "…Yeah, it’s still a little unstable sometimes. I’ll be fine for a while and then it’ll just… glitch for a second. But it’s getting better. Slowly."

Giles said, his voice filtered through the phone, "That’s good to hear, Willow. I was concerned after everything that happened. Just… continue being careful. Don’t push yourself too hard."

Willow nodded even though he couldn’t see her.

Willow said, "I won’t. I promise."

There was a brief pause on the line. Then Giles’s tone shifted — more serious.

Giles said, "Willow… there’s something you all need to be aware of. The First Evil has become active again. I’m faxing over some information now. Most of my older books are with me here in Bath — they’re irreplaceable, and I need them for study — so I had to pull what I could from memory and a few texts I still had access to."

As he spoke, the fax machine began printing. Several pages came through with ancient Sumerian text and drawings. The first pages showed hooded figures in brown robes, their faces empty where their eyes should have been: the Bringers. Another showed the First’s symbol. The final page showed what appeared to be a large round metal plate or seal covered in intricate designs, though no location was marked on it.

Willow watched the pages print with a concerned expression.

Willow said, "Okay… it’s coming through now. There’s drawings of the Bringers, the symbol… and something that looks like a big metal seal or cover."

Nobody in the shop needed the drawings explained. Buffy's eyes went from the curling pages to Willow and then to Tara, and all three of them were back in the red-strobing lobby at Calax, the silent eyeless monks gliding at them across the tile and the cool voice of I.R.I.S. naming the Seal of Danzalthar as a symbol of the First Evil. Buffy's grip tightened on the cordless.

Giles said, "Yes. The First Evil can appear to people as anyone who has died. It can mimic them perfectly — voice, mannerisms, even memories. However, it cannot touch or interact with anything in our physical world. It has no corporeal form."

Buffy, listening on the cordless phone, suddenly spoke up with a small, dry smirk.

Buffy said into the phone, "So… we have the advantage because we can drink coffee and it can’t? That’s something, right?"

There was a short pause on the line.

Giles said dryly, "Technically accurate, though I doubt that will be the deciding factor."

Willow gave Buffy a small, tired smile. Tara didn’t smile at all — she just looked more uneasy, her hands resting on the counter as she listened.

The bell above the front door rang as Dawn walked in carrying her backpack. She walked over to the counter, dropped her bag down, and reached inside. She pulled out the white envelope from Robin Wood and handed it to Buffy.

Buffy took the envelope and looked at it, then at Dawn.

Buffy said, "I really hope this is about parent-teacher night…"

Dawn didn’t answer. She just sat down on the stool behind the counter and exhaled, clearly not wanting to talk about it yet.

Willow was still looking at the fax pages with a worried expression as Giles continued on the phone.

Giles said, "Whatever it’s planning, it’s only just begun. Stay alert. All of you."

CHAPTER 8 The Best Parts of Me

That evening everyone at 1630 Revello Drive sat around the dining-room table for dinner, and the sounds of forks and knives and quiet chewing filled the room. Steam rose from the large roasted honey ham in the center of the table, and the half-empty bottle of rosé beside it caught the warm light of the dining-room lamp.

Five people sat around the table: Tara, Buffy, Willow, Lucian and Dawn. Lucian’s white lab coat was draped over the back of his chair; he was in his black button-up, his long black hair in the tight clean ponytail, pale from the office, with light stubble. Dawn was still in the oversized grey hoodie, her backpack on the floor. Buffy’s purse sat on the floor beside her chair.

Willow finished chewing a bite of ham and mashed potatoes, swallowed, took a small sip of her iced tea, and spoke. Her voice was thoughtful but there was a faint tremor underneath — the same strain from when her hand glowed at the Magic Box bathroom door.

Willow tilted her head, thoughtful, and said, "I’ve been reading the texts Giles faxed us. We need to find the seal. It’s probably somewhere inside the town. The seal is what’s holding the entire First Evil back. If we can locate it—"

Tara looked at her gently but firmly.

Tara said softly, "Will… not at the table."

Her right hand reached for her wine glass. It trembled, and not from the wine.

She lifted it and drank the entire glass in four steady gulps, then set it down, picked up the bottle with both hands, and poured herself another full glass, her breathing a little faster, and she kept her eyes on her plate for a moment.

Buffy continued eating — ham and broccoli — watching Tara quietly but not saying anything. She took a sip of her own wine, then suddenly paused mid-chew and glanced down at the floor beside her chair.

Buffy said, remembering, "Oh— right."

She leaned over, picked up her purse from the floor, and set it on her lap. She opened it, reached inside, and pulled out the white envelope from Principal Robin Wood. She opened the envelope, unfolded the letter, and read it while still holding her fork in her other hand. Her expression shifted from neutral to tired amusement as she read. She finished, lowered the letter slightly, and let out a small, dry laugh.

Buffy said, her voice quippy and dry, "Well… at least he’s not pretending he doesn’t know what planet he’s living on. That’s new."

She folded the letter, tossed it onto the middle of the table near the ham platter, and rolled her eyes — not dramatically, just a tired little roll.

Buffy said, "He wants me at parent-teacher night. In person. No proxies this time. And he’d like me to keep the supernatural away from his students. You know… the usual."

Everyone glanced at it for a second, and the table went quiet again.

Lucian had been eating steadily but now set his fork down and looked around at everyone with a serious, steady expression. When he spoke, his voice was low and deliberate.

Lucian said, "I have something I need to tell all of you."

Everyone turned their attention to him. Even Dawn stopped moving her fork. Lucian rested his hands on either side of his plate.

Lucian said, "Today at work… in the bathroom… I saw Ben. The guy from Calax. The one who died down there. He was standing at the sink like it was nothing. Same coat. Same name badge. He talked to me. Told me I was already dead. That I was just… clocking in and clocking out. Getting smaller every day. He said you’d eventually look at me differently once the money runs out and everything falls apart."

The dining room fell into complete silence. No one moved, every fork had gone still, and the only sounds left were the faint tick of the antique clock from the living room and the low hum of the refrigerator drifting through the kitchen doorway. Tara’s hand stayed wrapped around her refilled wine glass, knuckles pale. Willow’s eyes were wide and worried, locked on Lucian. Buffy’s jaw was tight, her eyes on him. Lucian sat perfectly still, serious and exposed.

Tara’s hand, still slightly unsteady, slowly slid across the table and came to rest on top of Lucian’s. She didn’t squeeze. She just left it there — warm, present, and steady. Her breathing was quiet but a little shallow. She kept her eyes on their hands rather than looking up at anyone.

Willow watched Tara’s hand move. After a moment, she shifted closer and gently rubbed slow circles on Tara’s back with her palm, the same way she did in the Magic Box bathroom earlier. Her touch was light but constant. She didn’t speak.

Buffy stared at Lucian for several long seconds, her expression unreadable. Then she reached for her wine glass, lifted it slowly, took a small, deliberate sip and set it back down without a sound, her eyes still on him.

Dawn looked between everyone, clearly wanting to say something but sensing the weight of the moment. Instead, she quietly picked up the bowl of mashed potatoes and slid it a few inches closer to Lucian’s plate, even though he still had plenty left, her movements small and careful.

Lucian didn’t speak; he just sat there taking it in, and his shoulders dropped a fraction of an inch. He looked at Tara’s hand on his, then at Willow’s fingers moving on Tara’s back, then at Buffy’s eyes, and finally at Dawn sliding the potatoes toward him.

His breathing changed — a slower inhale through his nose, then a controlled exhale. His eyes stayed glassy, but he didn’t look away from the table.

Nobody broke the quiet. Willow’s hand kept moving in slow circles on Tara’s back, Tara’s fingers stayed warm on top of Lucian’s, and out in the living room the antique clock went on ticking.
