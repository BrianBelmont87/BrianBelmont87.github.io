Something to Chew On

Buffy the Vampire Slayer. Season seven, episode 03. Something to Chew On.

CHAPTER 1 Happy Eating

Several days after Lucian came home from Sunnydale General, bright midday sun threw hard shadows across the cracked asphalt of the Double Meat Palace parking lot. The Double Meat Palace sign hung ahead with its cartoon cow logo faded and crooked, cheap stucco and peeling paint below, and a greasy haze in the air around the building.

Joyce’s silver sedan pulled into the lot and settled into a space near the entrance, and when Willow killed the engine the road noise dropped away.

Willow sat behind the wheel in a bright red Strawberry Shortcake graphic t-shirt under an oversized light-wash denim jacket with thick white shearling at the collar and cuffs. She wore medium-wash jeans and white chunky sneakers, her red hair down and a little windswept. Tara sat in the passenger seat in a soft oversized sage green knit sweater slipping off one shoulder, dark brown high-waisted corduroy pants, and brown leather ankle boots. A long thin dark green scarf was loosely wrapped around her neck, and her golden dirty-blonde hair was half-up with a few strands framing her face. In the back, Buffy and Lucian sat close with their hands linked — his right in her left. Buffy wore a black long-sleeve ribbed top under a faded brown leather jacket, dark blue jeans, and black combat boots, hair down and messy from the day. Lucian wore a black shirt with dark embroidered collar and cuffs, sleeves pushed up to his forearms, black jeans, and black boots. He held a subtle stiffness through his torso from the healing wound in his side.

Willow glanced at the rearview and caught them in the back, expression neutral but watching.

Car doors opened with a clear metallic latch. Lucian got out first from the left rear door and moved carefully — not obvious about the injury, but there was a protective tension in his torso as he straightened. He walked around the back of the car to the passenger side and opened Tara’s door first. She stepped out, adjusting her sweater on her shoulder with one hand. Then he moved to the rear right door and held it for Buffy. She got out still holding his hand until the last moment, squinting half a second into the midday sun before her face settled into something more determined.

Willow was already heading for the entrance. Lucian moved ahead of her with quiet purpose, reached the glass door first, and pulled it open for her. Willow gave him a small, slightly surprised nod as she stepped inside.

Inside, harsh fluorescent tubes buzzed overhead. The walls were faded red and yellow, a peeling cartoon cow behind the counter, old posters hanging crooked, and the floor a scuffed off-white linoleum with a dull sticky sheen. The air was thick with old grease, fried onions, and cheap lemon cleaner that was losing the fight. From the kitchen came a low grill sizzle and the occasional clank of tools.

Two employees in red-and-yellow uniforms moved behind the counter with the slow energy of people who had been on shift too long. One wiped the same section of counter in slow circles. The other stared blankly at the order screen. A handful of customers were scattered — teenagers in one booth, and one tired man in a work uniform eating alone near the window.

The group filed in, and the glass door swung shut behind them with a dull thud and a soft pneumatic hiss.

Buffy let go of Lucian’s hand and walked straight toward the main counter with purpose. The others hung back a few steps near the entrance. Lucian’s eyes did a quick scan of the room — old habit — before settling on Buffy as she approached the counter.

Behind the counter stood Debbie, mid-thirties, red-and-yellow uniform, name tag clearly visible. A forced, overly bright customer-service smile activated the second she saw Buffy coming. The smile did not reach her eyes.

Buffy stopped at the counter and said, "Hey. I heard there’s some kind of eating competition happening here. Any idea when that’s going down?"

Debbie’s smile widened with the same trained empty enthusiasm.

Debbie said, faking cheerfulness, "Yes! You mean Yǒng’è! She’ll be here tomorrow afternoon! It’s so exciting to have a famous competitive eater from the eating circuit coming all the way to Sunnydale! We’ve been getting calls about it all week!"

Buffy didn’t let her finish the full spiel.

Buffy said, interrupting, "Cool. I want in."

Debbie blinked once. The smile never faltered. She reached under the counter, pulled out a clipboard with a form and legal disclaimer attached, and slid it across toward Buffy with mechanical efficiency.

Debbie said, "Wonderful! Just fill this out and we’ll get you registered. The entry fee is thirty-four dollars and fifty-seven cents to cover the cost of the food you’ll be eating."

Buffy looked down at the form, one eyebrow slightly raised at the price. Before she could speak, Tara stepped up beside her and placed two twenty-dollar bills on the counter without hesitation.

Tara said quietly, "Here."

Debbie took the money, opened the register, and counted out the change with quick practiced movements. She handed the five dollars and forty-three cents back to Tara, then looked at Buffy again with the same wide empty smile.

Debbie said, overly cheerful, "First prize cash payout is twelve hundred and fifty dollars! HAPPY EATING!"

She slid the clipboard a little closer to Buffy.

Behind them near the entrance, Willow and Lucian exchanged a quick glance. Lucian’s mouth twitched — the smallest barely-there reaction to how aggressively fake Debbie’s enthusiasm was. Willow caught it and almost smiled before looking back toward the counter.

CHAPTER 2 Creepy Ancient-Sounding

Late that afternoon, the car moved at a steady suburban speed. Afternoon sun cut through the windows at a low angle as they passed trees and buildings. The engine purred low, the tires hummed softly on the road, and the turn signal clicked faintly when Willow changed lanes.

Willow reached up and adjusted the rearview so she could see Buffy clearly in the back seat. Her eyes flicked between the road and the mirror.

Willow said, "Buffy… that name. Yǒng’è. It sounds really familiar. Like something out of mythology or old folklore or something."

In the back seat Buffy was casually eating fries from a small paper bag in her lap. She didn’t look fazed at all. She popped another fry into her mouth, chewed once, then answered with dry sarcasm.

Buffy said, "Yeah, well… if some girl wants to give herself a creepy ancient-sounding name to try and intimidate everyone before she stuffs her face on camera, that’s her business. Still gonna wipe the floor with her tomorrow."

Willow kept glancing at Buffy in the mirror for a couple of seconds, turning the name over in her head, brow still slightly furrowed. After a moment she shrugged, accepting it for now, and looked back at the road.

Willow said, "Yeah… okay, I guess."

She eased the car forward a little faster. The engine note rose slightly.

In the back seat Buffy picked up another fry between two fingers and turned toward Lucian with a little smirk, clearly about to try feeding it to him. Lucian caught her wrist gently but firmly with his free hand before the fry could reach his mouth. His grip was careful, not rough. He tapped his own stomach once with his other hand.

Lucian said, "Hey… I will kiss you after you eat that, sure. But I’m still human, remember?"

Buffy smirked at him, eyes playful, but lowered the fry without pushing it. She kept looking at him a second longer than necessary, still amused.

The car kept on through the late-afternoon streets, the low road hum and the drone of the engine filling the space between them.

CHAPTER 3 Still Empty

That same afternoon, in a cheap dimly lit motel room on the edge of town, the curtains were drawn tight. The only light came from a single lamp on the nightstand and the flickering television in the corner, which was playing a cooking show on mute. The screen cast shifting blue-white light across the room.

Yǒng’è stood in front of the full-length mirror on the closet door. She looked small, delicate, and deceptively innocent — long dark hair brushed straight, wearing a simple white sundress that made her look almost doll-like. Her posture was perfectly still.

She stared at her own reflection with completely blank eyes, no expression, just emptiness.

Slowly she opened her mouth… and it kept opening. The jaw unhinged with a wet sickening crack. The sound was sharp and wrong. Her lips pulled back as the jaw dropped far wider than any human mouth should. Rows of small needle-like teeth were visible in the mirror reflection. For a moment her reflection showed something ancient and starving looking back at her — eyes completely black, skin stretched too tight over the bones.

She closed her mouth with a soft wet snap. The jaw reset. She smiled sweetly at herself in the mirror — practicing the cute harmless expression she’d wear at the competition. The smile was perfect. Too perfect.

On the small table beside her were the remains of last night’s meal: a pile of men’s clothing, stained dark with old blood. Next to it sat a half-eaten raw chicken carcass, bones cracked open, marrow sucked out. The meat was pale and glistening.

Yǒng’è picked up a piece of the raw chicken with delicate fingers. She brought it to her mouth and ate it in one unnaturally smooth motion. Her throat barely moved as she swallowed. The wet sound of tearing meat and swallowing was quiet but distinct.

She tilted her head slightly, still staring at her reflection. Her voice came out soft, slightly broken, with something ancient and hollow underneath.

Yǒng’è said, "Tomorrow… so many little mouths. Watching. Cheering. How many can I put inside me… before they start to scream? Still empty. So empty inside."

She ran both hands slowly down her flat stomach, pressing in gently with her palms like she was trying to feel something that wasn’t there. Her expression shifted — almost sad for a moment, then blank again.

She turned away from the mirror, picked up a small tube of lip gloss from the table, and carefully applied it while humming a soft eerie melody. The innocent girl look was fully back in place. She tilted her head one more time in the mirror, checking the smile from a different angle.

CHAPTER 4 Use the Ground

Back at the Summers house, the garage had been cleared enough for movement. A heavy punching bag hung from the ceiling in the center. A weight bench and free weights stood against one wall. Stacks of Magic Box inventory boxes were pushed to the back. Afternoon light came in through a small side window and the open side door, mixing with a single bare bulb overhead.

Buffy stood in the middle of the open space barefoot in a black sports bra and grey sweatpants. Lucian stood opposite her, shirtless, black jeans still on, the raven tattoo spread across his chest and left shoulder. Fresh bandages showed on his lower left side. He moved with noticeable stiffness and care, protecting the wound.

Buffy said, "Feel that back heel? Right when your fist is about to land — drive it down. Hard. That’s where the power actually comes from. Not your arm. Use the ground."

She threw a slow controlled right punch toward the bag. At the exact moment of impact her heel drove hard into the concrete floor, toes flexing. The fabric of her sweatpants shifted slightly at the hip from the grounded drive. Her fist connected. The bag swung once with a heavy dull thud.

Buffy said, "Now make a fist. Roll it in from the pinky side and squeeze it tight. Like you’re trying to crush all the air out of it. Turns your hand into a hammer instead of a bag of bones."

She showed him the fist — thumb wrapped over the fingers, pinky tucked, entire hand visibly tensed — and held it up so he could see.

Buffy said, "Only hit with these two knuckles. Top two. You start throwing with the whole hand and you’re gonna break something. These two — you hit harder and you don’t wreck your wrist."

She threw another slow punch, this time rotating the fist so the top two knuckles led. Her hand and wrist stayed aligned through the full motion.

Lucian watched closely, then set his stance, careful with his left side. He threw a right punch — decent form, but still stiff. His back heel drove down late. The bag moved, but not with much force.

Buffy stepped in immediately. She placed one hand on his right shoulder and the other on his hip, gently adjusting his weight distribution.

Buffy said, "Again. But this time, drive that back heel the second your fist is about to land. Not after."

Lucian reset his feet and threw another punch, and this time his back heel slammed down at exactly the right moment. The bag swung harder. A small grunt escaped him from the effort and the pull on his injured side.

Buffy nodded once, watching his body closely.

Buffy said, "Okay, good. Now blocking. We’re using a strong front stance — Shotokan style. Like this."

She stepped into Zenkutsu Dachi. Left leg forward and bent, knee directly over the ankle. Right leg straight back, back heel planted hard into the floor. Hips square, back straight, weight about seventy percent forward.

Buffy said, "Front leg bent, back leg straight. Back heel drives into the floor — same grounding as the punch. This keeps you from getting knocked on your ass when something hits you."

She demonstrated a slow outside block — Soto Ude Uke. Her right forearm swung from inside to outside, using the meaty lower outer forearm near the elbow to make contact. Her back heel drove down on the block.

Buffy said, "When you block, don’t use your wrist or hand. Use the thick part of your forearm, right below the elbow. That’s what actually takes the hit."

She demonstrated the inside block — Uchi Ude Uke — bringing the forearm across from outside to inside, again using the stronger meat near the elbow, and her back heel drove down again.

Buffy said, "Same thing every time. Drive that back heel down hard. It roots you."

Lucian stepped into the stance, slower and more careful because of his side. He tried the outside block first. His back heel didn’t quite drive hard enough. Buffy stepped in, put her hand on his back calf, and gently pressed down so he felt the heel plant.

Buffy said, "There. Feel that? That’s what stops you from getting pushed around."

He tried again, and it was better this time, and then he moved on to the inside block. Buffy watched his elbow position and gently tapped the inside of his elbow once to correct the angle. He adjusted.

Buffy walked to the wall where weapons were leaning. She picked up the Oakeshott Type XIII sword — broad blade, dark well-used finish, simple straight crossguard — turned, and handed it to Lucian hilt-first.

Buffy said, "Here. Take this."

Lucian took it with both hands at first, testing the weight. He settled into a two-handed grip, mindful of his injured side. The blade caught a sliver of window light.

Buffy said, "Hold it out at an angle. Flat of the blade becomes your shield. Tip toward my chin."

He adjusted the angle while Buffy watched his grip closely.

Buffy said, "When you swing or thrust, use that same pinky flex. Right before the edge makes contact, give it that little squeeze with your pinky. That drives all the energy straight into the target."

She demonstrated the motion in the air with her own hands — subtle pinky engagement at the moment of imagined impact, aligning wrist, elbow, and blade.

Buffy said, "It keeps the edge straight. No wobble. All the power goes exactly where you’re aiming it."

Lucian tried a slow controlled downward cut, still stiff on the left side. He remembered the pinky squeeze at the end of the motion. The blade cut cleanly through the air with a soft whoosh. His bandaged side pulled slightly and he exhaled through his nose.

Buffy stepped closer, watching his alignment. She placed two fingers lightly on the flat of the blade near the tip and moved it a fraction of an inch to correct his angle.

Buffy said, "There. That’s it."

Lucian held the position for a second, breathing steady, focusing.

Up at the top of the steps, the door from the kitchen opened, and Willow leaned in with one hand on the frame.

Willow smiled and said, "Dinner’s ready, you two. Tara made actual food, not Double Meat Palace mystery meat."

Buffy lowered her hands and glanced at Lucian with a small smirk. Lucian carefully lowered the sword, breathing a little heavier from the work.

CHAPTER 5 A Magical Journey of the Mouth

That evening, the dining room was warmly lit. Steam rose gently from the plates. The table was set with real plates and silverware — no paper or plastic.

On the table: golden-brown fried pork chops, creamy mashed potatoes with melting pats of butter, sweet corn on the cob, and a generous bowl of warm apple sauce mixed with soft cinnamon-cooked apple slices.

Tara sat at the head, with Willow beside her, Buffy and Lucian side by side, and Dawn across from them.

Everyone was actively eating. Forks and knives clinked softly on plates. There was occasional chewing and low murmuring, and the faint scrape of a chair leg when someone shifted.

Lucian cut into his pork chop with his knife. The crispy exterior gave a clean crack under the blade. He speared a piece, took a bite, and closed his eyes for a second as he chewed slowly. His expression softened with genuine pleasure.

Willow chewed a bite of corn and looked at Buffy.

Willow said, "So… how excited are you about tomorrow’s eating competition? Be honest."

Buffy speared a piece of pork chop, dipped it in apple sauce, and grinned while her mouth was still half full.

Buffy said, mouth half full and quippy, "Excited? I’m gonna make that girl wish she’d stayed home and stuck to kale smoothies."

Lucian swallowed his bite and looked at Buffy with quiet warmth. A small genuine smile sat on his face.

Lucian said, "Thank you for teaching me that stuff back in the garage. I keep going over it in my head… the back heel, the pinky flex, the forearm blocks. It makes sense now."

Buffy took a sip of Orange Fanta from a glass. She smiled at him, relaxed.

Buffy said, "You’re picking it up fast. Just wait till we add footwork."

Tara watched Buffy take another big bite of pork chop. She raised one eyebrow, half amused, half gently scolding.

Tara said, soft, pointed but affectionate, "This is real food, you know."

Buffy gave her a playful guilty look while still chewing, cheeks slightly puffed. She didn’t stop eating.

Lucian took another bite of mashed potatoes mixed with apple sauce. His eyes widened slightly in pleasant surprise at the combination. He chewed slower, clearly enjoying the flavors.

Lucian said, "Tara… you’re an amazing cook. When you take that first bite? It’s like a transformation happening. One flavor just melts right into the next."

Willow grinned and took a sip of Seven-Up.

Willow said, grinning, "It’s a magical journey of the mouth."

Tara smiled softly, a little shy but clearly pleased. She ducked her head slightly and focused on her own plate for a second.

Tara said, "You’re welcome, Lucian."

Dawn had been mostly quiet, pushing corn around her plate. She speared a piece, chewed, then swallowed and spoke up. Her voice had a hopeful but slightly nervous energy.

Dawn said, after chewing and swallowing, "School was actually okay today. Josh asked if I wanted to hang out after class tomorrow. Like… just us. It’s starting to feel like something good, you know? Not weird or forced."

She took a quick drink of Orange Fanta, eyes flicking between the others like she was both excited and bracing for reactions.

The table stayed warm and comfortable. Soft clinking and low chewing continued underneath the conversation. Steam kept rising gently from the food.

CHAPTER 6 Little Piggies in a Blanket

Outside, the backyard was dark and quiet. Moonlight filtered through the trees across the grass and fence. The only strong light came from the dining-room window, warm and inviting. Inside, the family dinner continued, blurred by the glass. There was movement, and muffled laughter and conversation drifted out, distant from out here.

Spike stood motionless in the shadows just outside the back fence, half-hidden behind a large tree in the neighbour’s tree line. Only the lower half of his face and one eye were lit by the warm light spilling from the window, catching the raw edge of the healing burn on his cheek. The rest of him was swallowed in darkness. His torn black duster blended into the shadows, and his whole body stood rigid.

Through the window the dinner table was clear enough to make out faces. Tara was serving more food. Willow was gesturing animatedly while talking. Dawn was smiling. Buffy leaned in close to Lucian and said something that made him grin. The warm light made the scene look safe and connected.

Spike watched with dead hollow eyes. A muscle twitched once in his cheek, and his breathing was slow but shallow.

Spike said, quiet and venomous, almost whispering to himself, "Look at you… all cozy in there. Playing house. Laughing. Eating real food like a proper little family."

Spike said, bitter, "You all turned your backs on me the second you didn’t need me anymore. The second you had your shiny new toy to keep you warm at night. I bled for you. I burned for you. And now I’m just… garbage you threw out with yesterday’s trash."

He took one slow deliberate step closer to the window, still staying in shadow. His eyes stayed locked on Buffy laughing at something Lucian said.

A low vibrating tone started deep in his inner ear. The ground tilted under his boots. He steadied himself with one pale hand on the top rail of the fence and went no further.

A low hum rose in the dark directly behind Spike, underneath the ringing the ward had set off in his ear, and a cold blue light gathered there before a figure stepped out of it.

The First appeared as Drusilla. She wore a flowing tattered black Victorian dress with deep red accents. Her long dark hair was loose and wild. Her movements were slow, graceful, and deeply wrong — like a broken music box ballerina. She tilted her head sharply to one side, eyes wide and dreamy. There was no real breath in her. No weight on the grass. Hollow stillness under the singsong.

The First said, as Drusilla, singsong, "Ohhh… look at them, my sweet Spike. All tucked in together like little piggies in a blanket."

She did a slow eerie twirl. Her arms floated up as if she were dancing with an invisible partner. The fabric of her dress moved too slowly, almost floating. Her bare feet made no sound on the grass.

Drusilla said, "We used to eat families, didn’t we, darling? Rip them open like presents on Christmas morning… slurp up all their warm, sticky secrets while they screamed for mummy."

She suddenly stopped twirling and glided directly behind Spike. She leaned in close, resting her chin on his shoulder without actually touching him — no contact, no warmth, only the shape of closeness. Her lips hovered right next to his ear. Her eyes stayed locked on the happy dinner scene inside with hungry intensity.

Drusilla said, voice dropping into a childish whisper, almost pouting, "But now they’ve got a new monster to play with. A soft, warm one with pretty eyes and a heartbeat. They don’t need their wicked old Spike anymore…"

She giggled. The sound was jagged, broken, and completely wrong for the moment.

Drusilla said, "Poor Spikey… left out in the cold like a bad puppy. Shall we take the warm one away from them, my darling? Then she’ll have to come back out into the cold, where you are."

Spike’s hands slowly curled into tight fists at his sides. His whole body went rigid, and he didn’t look away from Buffy.

Spike said, voice low and broken, "He’ll be gone. And she’ll remember who stayed."

Behind him, Drusilla smiled wide and unhinged. She slowly dragged one long pale fingernail down the side of his face — close enough that it should touch, but it never made contact. Her nail stopped just short of his skin. Empty air. Nothing there to feel.

The warm light from the dining-room window kept glowing softly in front of them, completely at odds with the cold empty look in Spike’s eyes. He was souled, and the chip was still in his head, and he stood watching a house he could not walk into, listening to a ghost that wasn’t really her, until the blue light behind him thinned, the hum dropped to silence, and he was alone again at the fence.

CHAPTER 7 No Fire Engine Sounds

Later that night, the upstairs hallway was dimly lit by a single warm lamp on a small table. Most of the house was quiet and dark. The only strong light came from the open bathroom doorway.

Dawn stood in the hallway bathroom doorway in light pink pajama shorts and a matching tank top, hair pulled up in a messy bun. She was vigorously brushing her teeth, the toothbrush moving in aggressive circles. White foam built at the corners of her mouth.

From inside Buffy’s bedroom — door fully closed, no light visible underneath — a loud deep breathy moan echoed out. Completely unfiltered and unmistakable.

Dawn froze mid-brush and rolled her eyes hard, all the way up toward the ceiling.

Dawn yelled toward the closed door, annoyed, "Buffy! I have school tomorrow! No fire engine sounds, please!"

From further down the hall, coming from Willow and Tara’s open bedroom door, soft laughter — Willow’s bright high giggle mixed with Tara’s warmer quieter laugh.

Dawn turned her head toward their room, toothbrush still hanging out of her mouth with foam on her lips. She looked deadpan.

Dawn peeked inside and said, "It’s not funny."

In their room, Willow and Tara were sitting up in bed together. Willow wore a metallic red silk camisole. Tara wore a soft white tank top, long golden dirty-blonde hair loose on her shoulders. Both were trying — and failing — to stop smiling. Willow had one hand over her mouth. Tara’s shoulders were shaking slightly with held-back laughter.

Tara said, soft, gentle and sincere, with a warm smile, "No, you’re right, Dawnie…"

Tara said, eyes full of quiet affection, "It’s beautiful. It’s not funny at all."

Dawn gave them the most exaggerated eye roll possible, then turned back toward the sink without another word.

Dawn muttered, "Whatever…"

She went back to brushing her back teeth aggressively, jaw working hard. The sound of the toothbrush scrubbing was loud and rhythmic. After a few seconds she spat into the sink with a wet splat, rinsed her mouth thoroughly with water, and wiped her lips with the back of her hand.

She picked up a round makeup removal pad, poured a little micellar water onto it, and started gently wiping off her makeup in the mirror — first under her eyes, then across her cheeks. Her expression was a mix of annoyed and tiredly amused. She kept glancing toward Buffy’s closed door with a small shake of her head.

CHAPTER 8 Car Alarms Across the Street

The next morning, soft light filtered through the curtains of Buffy’s bedroom. The room had that quiet slightly messy post-sleep feeling. The bed was unmade, sheets tangled and half-pulled off. A faint warm smell of sleep and skin still lingered in the air.

Lucian stood beside the bed, shirtless. Fresh white bandages were taped over the stitched wound on his lower left side. He was pulling up his black jeans, the fabric sliding up his legs with a soft rustle. He winced slightly as he shifted his weight to zip them — not dramatic, just a small careful adjustment to protect his side. He gathered his long black hair with both hands, pulled it back into a neat ponytail, and secured it with a hair tie. The movement made the muscles in his back and shoulders shift under his skin.

Buffy was still lying in bed, propped up on one elbow, watching him. The sheet was pulled up just high enough to cover her chest. Her hair was messy from sleep. A prominent hickey showed under her left ear — red with deep purple and brownish bruising from the night before. She had a lazy satisfied smirk on her face as she watched him get dressed.

Buffy grinned, voice still a little husky, and said, "You know… if you keep doing that thing with your tongue from last night, I might have to start calling in sick to saving the world."

Lucian turned to look at her. A playful smirk tugged at his mouth. He threw her own cocky style right back at her without hesitating.

Lucian raised an eyebrow and said, "Yeah? Well if you keep making those sounds you made around two a.m., we might set off car alarms across the street."

Buffy let out a genuine laugh — bright, warm, and unselfconscious. She started sliding out of bed, the sheet slipping down her body as she moved.

The bedroom door opened. Willow stepped inside and stopped a couple steps into the room.

Buffy, her back to the door, pulled a top over her head without hurrying; she and Willow had shared a house too long to be shy about it.

Willow said, "Morning, you two. Tara and I were thinking since it’s such a beautiful day, we’d go to the park for a bit, maybe do a little picnic. Would you mind driving Buffy to the eating contest later this afternoon?"

Lucian said, "Of course. No problem at all."

From off to the side Buffy snorted as she buttoned her jeans, her back still to them as she faced the closet.

Buffy said, amused, "Translation: they don’t want to sit in a building that smells like twenty-year-old grease while I try to inhale my body weight in burgers."

Willow grinned and shrugged, not even trying to deny it. She leaned against the doorframe, watching them with a small knowing smile.

Buffy finished pulling her shirt down and ran a hand through her messy hair. Lucian eased a dark turtleneck down over the bandages, moving carefully, covering the wound for the day ahead.

CHAPTER 9 Blaming You

Later that morning, the bathroom was bright with natural daylight coming through the small window above the sink. The counter was cluttered with makeup, hair products, and a hairbrush.

Buffy stood in front of the mirror in a dark red bra and tight black yoga pants, her top and jeans folded on the counter so she wouldn’t get makeup on them. Her hair was still slightly messy from sleep, and the hickey from the night before still showed dark under her left ear.

Lucian sat on the closed toilet lid, leaning back against the tank. He was already dressed, black jeans on, the bandages covered for the day. He watched her with a relaxed slightly amused expression, one arm resting on his thigh.

Buffy reached for her foundation bottle first. The pump made a wet squelch as she dispensed some onto her fingers, and she started blending it into her skin in small circular strokes while she talked.

Buffy said, still blending, "Dawn was in a weirdly good mood this morning. She was humming in the kitchen. Humming. When was the last time she did that?"

She put the foundation bottle down with a solid thud.

Lucian smiled and said, "Maybe she finally got a good night’s sleep. Or maybe Josh said something nice to her."

Buffy picked up a small eyeshadow palette. The plastic case made a loud sharp click as she opened it, and she dipped the brush in and swept color across her lids.

Buffy said, the brush moving in small strokes, "Tara’s been stress-cooking again. Did you see how much food she made last night? I think she’s trying to feed the entire neighborhood through us."

She set the palette down with another sharp clack of plastic on the counter.

Lucian said, "She likes taking care of people. It’s her thing."

Buffy picked up her lipstick. The tube made a soft twist-click as she opened it, and she applied it in slow, careful strokes while she kept talking.

Buffy said, between strokes, "Willow’s been weirdly quiet lately. I don’t know if that’s a good thing or if she’s just waiting for the next magical disaster to happen."

She pressed her lips together, then set the lipstick down with a sharp tap.

Lucian said, "She’s probably just enjoying the quiet while it lasts."

Buffy grabbed her eyeliner pencil. It made a small scraping sound as she twisted it up. She leaned in closer to the mirror to do her eyeliner.

Buffy said, her eyes fixed on the mirror as she drew the line, "And then there’s this stupid eating contest. I still can’t believe I signed up for it. What the hell was I thinking?"

She finished the wing on her left eye and set the pencil down with a final clack.

Lucian said, amused, "You were thinking you could beat some random girl at eating burgers. And you probably still can."

Buffy glanced at him in the mirror, smirking as she adjusted her bra strap. The hickey on her neck was clearly visible in the reflection.

Buffy said, "Yeah, well… if I throw up on live camera, I’m blaming you."

Lucian chuckled quietly from his spot on the toilet. The sound was low and warm in the small bathroom.

Buffy turned slightly, checking her reflection from another angle. She looked at herself for a second longer than necessary, then exhaled through her nose — half amused, half resigned about the day ahead.

CHAPTER 10 Slayer Tingles

The afternoon sun was bright but starting to lean toward golden hour. The black 1981 Pontiac Firebird Trans Am Special Edition sat in the driveway — gold trim along the sides and the Turbo 4.9 hood, T-tops catching the afternoon light — looking slightly out of place on the quiet suburban street. Lucian kept kissing her, one hand moving to the back of her head while the other stayed on her face. He didn’t rush it. He just held her and let her fall apart against him.

Outside the car, the neon motel sign continued to flicker. The pool at the side of the motel had gone completely quiet.
