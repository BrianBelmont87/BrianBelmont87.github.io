My Only Son

Download this script (.txt)

Buffy the Vampire Slayer. Season seven, episode 07. My Only Son.

CHAPTER 1 The Garage Breakfast

That morning the big door of the Summers garage stayed shut tight. Bright sun leaked in thin sharp slivers around the door edges and through the small high window, cutting golden blades through the dim air. The rest of the space sat cool under the bulbs, and Angel kept himself well clear of every one of those slivers, because outside that sun was still deadly.

They had turned the garage into an emergency dining room. The weight bench stood in as a long table with plates of scrambled eggs, sausage, orange juice and toast crowded down the middle of it, with dumbbells and a yoga mat working as risers. Folding chairs and kitchen stools had been dragged in from the house, and the whole setup was chaotic and warm and trying hard to feel like enough. Xander’s old crew had scattered since he left for Texas, and nobody could come out to Revello, so the plywood stayed.

Everyone squeezed in. Tara sat at the head of the weight-bench setup in a soft pale-blue blouse and beige cardigan, her long golden dirty-blonde hair still messy from the morning. Her right hand reached out to put more food on people’s plates while her left hand steadied the juice pitcher. Faith sat on one side of Lucian in a black tank and dark jeans with her combat boots planted under the bench. Buffy sat on Lucian’s other side in a fitted tank and jeans, hair still a little damp at the roots, the bruise above her right eye from Caleb’s punch already fading to yellow-green under her Slayer healing and the split in it sealed to a thin pink line. Angel held the deepest shadows near the back wall in his heavy dark wool overcoat, silent and watchful. Willow took a stool in her lavender peasant blouse. Dawn sat on a folding chair, already talking with her mouth full.

Tara put her hands on her hips for a second and looked around the table, making sure everyone had enough, her brow drawing together a little.

Tara said in a soft but firm voice, "Eat. All of you. You’re not going anywhere until you eat something."

Everyone had plates. Light chatter rose while forks clicked on china and people sipped orange juice over eggs and sausage, trying to feel normal after last night. Tara kept putting more food down.

Faith was already halfway through her plate of fluffy scrambled eggs. She leaned toward Lucian and said something low that made him chuckle. Buffy glanced over with a small smile, then went back to her food. Lucian sat between them in a black shirt with a dark embroidered collar and cuffs, worn open at the throat. His swollen jaw was mostly healed but still stiff from the porch, so he chewed carefully.

Dawn buzzed on, talking with her mouth full. Willow ate quietly while watching Tara. Angel stayed silent in the shadows.

Faith finished a bite of greasy sausage, wiped her mouth with the back of her right hand, and grinned at the table.

Faith said with a real laugh, "Okay but real talk — this is the first time I’ve had breakfast that wasn’t gas station food or prison slop in years… and we’re eating it in a freaking garage. On a weight bench."

She laughed — loud, rough, genuine. Lucian laughed with her.

Still laughing, Faith casually reached over with her right hand and grabbed Lucian’s upper thigh, giving it a squeeze like they were just buddies sharing the joke.

The table went dead silent.

Even Angel lifted his head slightly in the shadows.

Buffy’s eyes narrowed. Without saying a word she reached over with her right hand, grabbed Faith’s wrist, and firmly lifted her hand off Lucian’s leg. She held it there a second, giving Faith a clear warning look.

Buffy said, almost a hiss, "He’s mine, Faith."

Then she let her wrist go.

Faith froze… then burst out laughing again, pulling her hand back and holding both palms up.

Faith said through the laughter with zero shame, "Whoa — okay! Hands to myself. Got it, B."

Faith went right back to her eggs, still chuckling. The tension broke. Small nervous laughs rippled then turned genuine. Even Angel’s mouth twitched in the shadows.

Light conversation picked back up.

Then the phone rang, loud, cutting through the garage.

Buffy sighed and stood up, setting her plate down on the main makeshift table and putting her fork down with her right hand.

Buffy turned her back to everyone, went up the steps into the house, and came back down a moment later with the cordless phone, already answering as she walked back in.

Buffy said into the phone, "Hello?"

She listened, and her face changed.

Buffy said, "Oh. Principal Wood. Hi."

She listened again, eyes flicking toward Dawn.

Buffy said, "Yeah… she’s not feeling great this morning. I was gonna call."

On the other end Principal Wood’s voice was audible. Dawn, still talking excitedly at the table, did not realize how loud she was.

Dawn said, bright and animated, "— and then I sang and the whole movie theatre turned into this huge thing and Josh got stabbed but then he came back and all the demons just melted into smoke and—"

Buffy closed her eyes for a second and turned slightly away from the table.

Buffy said into the phone in a flat voice, "…She’s sick."

Wood’s voice came back skeptical — he could clearly hear Dawn in the background.

Buffy rolled her eyes hard.

Buffy said, "Yeah. Really sick. I’ll have her bring a note tomorrow."

She thumbed the button to hang up and stood there a moment with the phone in her left hand.

The table had gone quiet. Everyone was looking at her.

Buffy finally turned back and dropped the phone with a soft thunk onto a bundled pink blanket resting on a cardboard box. She went back to sitting with the others and picked her fork back up like nothing had happened.

Buffy said, muttering to Dawn, "He heard you."

Dawn winced.

Dawn said, "…Oops."

Tara calmly put another piece of toast on Dawn’s plate with her right hand like this was completely normal.

CHAPTER 2 The Kitchen Sink

Tara stood from the weight bench and calmly gathered plates and cups into a stack, her beige cardigan sleeves already halfway rolled. Faith watched her a second, then got up without being asked and followed her into the house. The kitchen felt warmer, more intimate.

Tara moved to the sink with the stack of plates. She set them down with both hands, turned on the warm water with her right hand, and added soap. She was in full quiet caretaker mode, sleeves rolled up the rest of the way, focused, her expression soft and her movements steady, with an occasional caring glance toward Faith.

Faith lingered by the counter, picked up the heavy cast iron skillet from the stove with both hands, and brought it over. She started rinsing it under the running water without speaking. The two of them worked side by side in comfortable silence, Tara washing plates and Faith scrubbing the skillet.

After a little while Faith spoke. Her voice was low and rough, not her usual cocky tone, but honest and small. Her eyes stayed on the skillet she was rinsing.

Faith said, "Last night… when you hugged me in the backyard…"

A few seconds passed with no motion and no words.

Faith said, "Nobody’s ever done that for me before. Not like that."

She kept scrubbing, not looking at Tara, and a small crack showed in the armor, real but guarded, as she leaned forward a little over the sink and her throat worked once.

Tara stopped. She turned toward Faith slowly, expression soft and open. Her right hand was still wet from the dishwater. She gently lifted it and placed it against the side of Faith’s face, thumb brushing lightly near her cheekbone. The touch was careful, kind, maternal, and a droplet of dishwater ran down Faith’s jaw.

Faith flinched, a tiny instinctive twitch, her left eye fluttering at the touch the way her body always expected pain. She did not pull away. Her shoulders hitched, then slowly dropped.

Tara did not move her hand. She held it there, framing the side of Faith’s face, fingers resting lightly against skin. For a long, quiet while the only sound was the running faucet between them, and then Faith’s eyes started to shine and she swallowed hard, throat working, shoulders dropping a fraction more. She closed her eyes and leaned into Tara’s hand — not dramatically, fully. The tough exterior cracked a little. A single tear slipped from under a closed eyelid and ran down her cheek.

Tara did not say anything. She stayed there, holding Faith’s face with the same gentle, steady hand. There was no judgment and no pity, just presence.

Faith breathed slow and uneven, eyes closed, leaning into kindness like she was not sure she deserved it but could not make herself pull away.

CHAPTER 3 The Drive

Back in the garage it was quieter now, the breakfast mess clearing. Angel lay on the floor in the deepest shadow along the far left wall, well away from the slivers of sun around the closed garage door. He was turned away with his coat on, trying to rest, a subtle rustle of wool the only movement from him.

Tara sat on a folding chair with Faith nearby. Willow got up without a word, walked over, and gently sat sideways across Tara’s lap, careful with her weight, arms wrapping around her. Her right hand slowly stroked Tara’s golden dirty-blonde hair, then she leaned in and softly kissed Tara’s cheek. Tara closed her eyes and leaned into the touch, clearly needing it, her left arm coming up protectively around Willow. The group stayed quiet, Dawn shifting on her chair, Angel still, Lucian attentive from his.

Buffy looked at them, her brow set, her voice low but matter-of-fact.

Buffy said, "We need to get to the Magic Box. Check supplies, see if there’s anything we can use against whatever this new thing is. But one of us Slayers has to stay here with Dawn."

Faith did not hesitate. She nodded toward Tara and Willow.

Faith said, "I’ll go with them."

Tara opened her eyes, looked at Faith a second, and gave a small grateful nod. Willow kept her hand gently moving through Tara’s hair. They stood together, Tara, Willow and Faith, with no big discussion, just movement.

Outside, bright morning sunlight lay on the suburban street as the three of them walked out of the house to Joyce’s silver sedan. Willow headed for the driver side. Tara took the passenger seat. Faith climbed into the middle of the back seat, legs spread wide, arms crossed, body language screaming no shits given. The car started, backed slowly out of the driveway, turned left onto the street, and drove away while the Summers house shrank behind them.

CHAPTER 4 The Magic Box Ambush

Bright late-morning sun filled the alley behind the Magic Box, and its brick corners made blind spots. Tara pushed open the heavy rear door with her right shoulder and stepped out into the light carrying a heavy canvas supply bag over that same shoulder, cardigan hem fluttering in the breeze, pale-blue blouse tucked loose at the waist. She adjusted the strap with her left hand and turned sharply left around the brick corner.

Without warning, Caleb’s massive silhouette stepped from the blind left corner, his feet making no sound. His smiling face was the first thing Tara saw. His right hand rocketed forward, fingers splaying wide then clamping in a vise-grip around Tara’s jaw and lower face, thumb under her chin, fingers sealing her mouth, his grip dimpling her cheeks. He lifted her four to five inches instantly. Her leather boots scraped the concrete once, then dangled.

Tara’s eyes went wide with pure terror, her eyebrows shooting up in instant pleading, as she dropped the bag. The canvas thumped and books spilled. Both of her hands clawed at his forearm, nails digging, knuckles whitening, cardigan sleeves riding up and exposing pale skin. Her legs kicked in a frantic bicycle motion, jeans stretching at the knees, boots scuffing air. Muffled panicked screams vibrated against his palm.

Caleb’s face stayed ice-calm, his head tilting down slow like he was examining sculpture. His gaze locked on the exposed midriff where the struggle had yanked her pale-blue blouse completely free and upward, cardigan open, soft skin and belly button fully visible in the harsh sun.

Caleb said in a cold, almost reverent whisper, voice low, breath steady, "Just look at that filthy bird’s nest… so full of sin…"

His free left hand rose slow, index finger extending like a blade. He pressed the tip firmly into the soft flesh low on her left side, just below and to the left of her belly button. The skin indented half an inch before it broke. Tara’s entire body convulsed, spine arching, legs locking then jerking, eyes bulging, a raw, guttural scream muffled in her throat. A tear formed in her left eye.

Caleb let his finger linger in the wound, half an inch under her skin, while he stared into her eyes and her face of total terror, taking it all in. Then he slowly withdrew the finger. A thin smear of blood coated the tip. He lifted it between them, stared into her horrified eyes, and placed the bloody finger in his own mouth, sucking it clean with deliberate calm, eyes never leaving hers.

A hot dry wind rose unnaturally. Willow stood just behind Caleb, lavender peasant blouse sleeves and hem flapping hard, her hair instantly jet-black and whipping in a violent magical vortex. Her eyes were pure black voids. Dark energy crackled from her skin and eye sockets, sparks singeing the blouse fibers at the cuffs. Thick ugly black veins spiderwebbed her forehead, cheeks, neck, and arms in pulsing bursts.

Her body trembled with visible effort, knees locked and vibrating, chest heaving as she forced damaged magic past its breaking point. The cords in her neck stood out, and sweat beaded on her skin, her face twisting into a terrifying cocktail of love and fury.

Caleb’s smugness shattered. His eyes widened and his mouth opened as he swayed hard left, dizzy, and dropped Tara like dead weight. She crashed sideways onto the concrete, shoulder first, then hip, cardigan bunching, both hands clutching the puncture while a dark spot of blood marked the blouse waistband.

Tara curled fetal instantly, both palms pressed hard over the puncture. She gasped, then unleashed a raw, agonized scream, loud and guttural, her voice breaking on it.

Caleb staggered backward two steps, grimacing, both hands clutching his ears. Thick dark blood poured from both ear canals down his jaw and neck, dripping onto his collar. He stared at Willow for a moment.

Caleb said, "You filthy witch!"

He roared and swung a wild right hook toward Willow. It missed by inches, his fist slamming the brick wall to the left of Willow’s head. Bricks exploded in a red dust cloud, his wrist bones audibly stressed. The recoil spun him off balance and he crashed face-first onto the concrete. His left cheek scraped the ground, and dust puffed.

He tried to push up, arms shaking, palms slipping in blood, and collapsed twice more, growling, legs twitching as his rapid healing fought the disorientation.

Willow stood frozen, chest heaving, murderous protective intensity written across her face, her blouse scorched at the hems. Tara kept screaming beside her, her hands curled tight around the wound.

The heavy rear door of the Magic Box banged back against the alley wall. Faith exploded out of the doorway at full sprint, and a full-speed running kick slammed into Caleb’s ribs. Her right leg and combat boot had hip rotation behind it, and his ribs dented under the suit jacket as his body lifted two feet into the air before it drove into the alley wall. Caleb bounced off the alley wall face first and landed on his back and elbows.

Faith leapt, straddled his chest in full mount, and rained hammer-fist punches into his face, knuckles splitting, blood spattering her tank and face.

Faith screamed in fury, her voice raw, teeth bared, "You like hurting girls, huh?!"

Caleb smiled through the punches, bloody teeth showing, eyes sick with enjoyment. Faith’s brow twitched once in confusion, then she threw the next punch harder. Mid-swing he caught her right wrist in both of his hands and pinned it flat to his own chest so her forearm was trapped against him. He drove his hips up hard under her seat, rolling her weight forward over his head, and threw his left leg up and over the back of her neck while his right thigh clamped across her ribs. With her right arm locked between his hands and his body, he fell onto his left side and scissored his knees shut around that arm, her shoulder jammed to the concrete. He stretched his hips toward the sky and her elbow straightened past where it wanted to go. Faith screamed, thrashing, boots scraping for purchase, her tank riding up as she tried to yank free.

Willow stepped forward, her right hand extended, eyes pitch black, veins pulsing.

Willow said in Latin, voice echoing, "Oculi tui nihil videant nisi tenebras cordis tui…"

The words asked that his eyes see nothing but the darkness inside his own heart.

Caleb’s eyes flashed completely white. He released Faith instantly and rolled onto his stomach, groaning. Faith scrambled up and spun to the others.

Faith said, yelling urgently, "Get the books!"

Willow made a weak motion with her hand, and the scattered books and supplies levitated and flew down the alley into the open trunk of Joyce’s silver sedan where it sat parked a few yards down the alley, pages fluttering, the canvas bag zipping itself shut. Then a thin line of blood ran from her left nostril, the black drained out of her hair back to red everywhere but the roots, and she collapsed at once, knees buckling, rag-doll limp onto the pavement.

Faith shook her head once, still panting.

Faith said, exasperated, "Great… that’s just great…"

Caleb used his left hand against the brick alley wall to steady himself and slowly rose back to his feet like the killing machine that he was. A terrifying calm smile sat on his face, blood dripping from his mouth and ears.

Caleb’s voice dropped low as he spoke in the car’s direction, deeply disturbing. "I’m going to purify you slowly, little girls, slowly, each one of you… piece by piece… until you shine."

Faith moved lightning-fast. She scooped unconscious Willow in a fireman carry and threw her into the back seat, then grabbed Tara carefully but urgently, one arm under her knees and one behind her back. Tara winced hard, the dark spot on her blouse catching against Faith’s tank, as Faith pushed through the driver door and set her across in the passenger seat. Faith dove in, slammed it into gear, and floored it.

Caleb’s face suddenly appeared at the driver’s side window, next to Faith’s face, his eyes staring at her through the thin glass, his bloody smiling face pressing against it. His exhale fogged the glass. Faith gunned the engine, and the tires screeched.

Caleb reached down, grabbed the door handle, and ripped the entire driver-side door off its hinges with one hand. Metal groaned. The door flew into the alley wall.

Caleb screamed after the car, "I am the only son!"

The car sped away doorless, Faith white-knuckled on the wheel, Tara hunched and clutching the dark-spotted midriff of her blouse, Willow unconscious in back. It fishtailed into the distance.

CHAPTER 5 Doorless Return

Still late morning, Joyce’s doorless car swung into the Summers driveway. The tires screeched, and it slammed to a stop inches from the porch steps.

Faith white-knuckled the wheel, her face streaked with alley dirt and blood, her black tank splattered. Tara hunched in the passenger seat, a dark spot low on the left side of her pale-blue blouse, just below and to the left of the navel, her beige cardigan open, her right hand pressed hard on the wound. Willow lay limp in back, her lavender peasant blouse scorched at the cuffs.

Faith leaned out the missing door, voice cracking.

Faith screamed at the top of her lungs, "BUFFY!"

The big plywood sheet Buffy and Lucian had braced into the front doorway last night scraped hard aside, and Buffy sprinted out first in her fitted tank and jeans, her Slayer-alarm eyes wide. Dawn came behind her, eyes huge, and Lucian followed fast in the embroidered black shirt with the sleeves rolled and dark slacks.

Faith scooped Tara from the passenger seat, one arm under Tara’s knees and the other behind her back, and carried her cradled against her chest, Tara’s right hand still pressed hard over the wound. She carried her up the steps and through the open gap in the doorway into the house, and Dawn, Buffy and Lucian saw the blood. Faith lowered her carefully onto the couch, brushed damp hair off her forehead with her right hand for half a second, then spun back.

Buffy and Lucian reached the car together. Buffy slid her right arm under Willow’s shoulders, and Lucian took the other side, their hands locking under Willow’s knees. They half-carried, half-guided her up the porch steps. Lucian’s healing jaw clenched tight with the effort, but he held without complaint.

Dawn froze three steps inside, her face crumpling, sickly pale, tears spilling, her left hand to her mouth. A soft choked sound escaped her. She wanted to help, but the dark spot on Tara’s blouse and the way Tara clutched it were too much.

Buffy rested Willow on the couch edge beside Tara and turned. Lucian took the stairs two at a time and was back in seconds with a big white first-aid kit, his face grim.

Faith snapped her fingers.

Faith said, "Give that to me."

Lucian handed it without a word. Faith dropped to her knees before Tara, blood drying on her own knuckles, and looked Tara dead in the eyes.

Faith yanked the kit open with both hands, clicked a heavy-duty flashlight on, and angled the bright beam across the wound. It was a shallow break in the skin, the muscle underneath already darkening with bruise. She moved the beam slowly across the entire area and then held it steady.

Faith said, voice quiet and focused, "Good. Nothing is punctured."

She tossed the flashlight aside with a clatter, grabbed an alcohol wipe, and ripped it open with her teeth.

Faith said, "This is going to burn. Just breathe and look at me, Tara. Don’t hold your breath, you might pass out."

Tara placed both hands firmly on Faith’s shoulders, fingers digging into the bloody tank. She took a deep breath in… held it… and let it out slow. She nodded once, eyes steady and trusting.

Faith pressed the alcohol wipe into the open puncture. Tara’s body jerked once, a tiny sharp whimper escaping, then she kept breathing, in… out… eyes locked forward. Her grip tightened, knuckles whitening.

Faith threaded the needle with quick practiced movements and began stitching the wound shut with steady, even pulls. Tara whimpered softly on the first two stitches, then her eyes hardened and her breathing steadied as she pushed through.

The group watched in tense silence, Buffy with fists clenched at her sides, Lucian hovering, Dawn tear-streaked and frozen.

CHAPTER 6 The Aftermath

Minutes later, Buffy stood near the couch like she had nowhere else to put her body, arms crossed tight, weight on the balls of her feet. Faith was on her knees finishing the stitches. Tara lay pale on the cushions. Willow lay limp at the far end, still out.

Buffy said low, "What happened?"

Faith did not look up. The needle and thread kept up steady pulls across the puncture. Tara breathed slow through her nose, one hand still on Faith’s shoulder.

Faith said, voice rough, "It was the preacher again. He surprised us, that’s all. Willow zapped him so hard she tapped out. I punched him, B. Got on top of him and let him have everything I had. Right into his face."

She paused mid-stitch, the needle hovering. Then she lifted her gaze to Buffy. A bead of sweat rolled down her temple and mixed with the blood smear on her cheek.

Faith said, voice dropping, "B. He was smiling at me when I was hitting him. He loved it."

The room went dead quiet.

A muscle jumped in Buffy’s jaw. She uncrossed her arms, and her fists found her sides and stayed there.

Buffy said, "Then we’re gonna need something bigger to hit him with."

She turned sharp on her heel and went out through the front doorway, the plywood sheet catching once on the frame and rattling as she shouldered it aside. She came back moments later with an armful of books from the car trunk. There were thirteen of them, worn bindings mixed with newer ones. Lucian was right behind her. He took the heavier stack off her without being asked. His right hand brushed her elbow for half a second... then he looked away and kept moving.

Buffy set her half down on the living-room floor where the coffee table used to stand, with a solid thump. Faith tied off the last stitch, snipped the thread, and gave a brief pat. Tara’s breath hitched once, then leveled. Her hand settled over the fresh stitches.

Buffy looked straight at her.

Buffy said, "Tara. You need to get eyeballs deep in these as soon as Faith is finished with you."

She tipped her chin toward Willow, still out, still too still.

Buffy said, "You’re the only one here right now that can read any of that stuff. Besides Angel."

She turned to Lucian. He was already gathering the other half.

Buffy said, "Take half of these to Angel in the garage. Tell him what happened."

Lucian nodded once, with no hesitation. He headed through the kitchen for the garage door with the books balanced, boots soft on the floor.

Faith wiped her bloody knuckles on her pants and sat back on her heels. Tara reached out and lightly squeezed Dawn’s arm. Dawn pressed in close, tear tracks still drying, hands hovering like she didn’t know where to put them.

Nobody said anything for a long moment.

Buffy stood with her fists still clenched and watched Tara breathe, steady enough, while Faith watched Buffy watching, Lucian’s footsteps faded down the garage steps, and Willow did not stir.

Buffy exhaled once through her nose... and left the silence sitting there with them.

CHAPTER 7 The Pattern

That evening, books lay scattered across the floor where the coffee table used to stand. Dawn curled against Tara on the couch while Tara turned a page under the reading lamp, every small shift pulling at the stitches, a breath and a wince, before she kept reading. Faith sat cleaning a knife in the armchair, and Buffy stood by the window with her notes.

In the kitchen, the door at the top of the garage steps opened, and Angel came through and into the living room with a thick dusty book open in his hands.

Everyone turned.

Angel said low, "I got something."

He stopped in the center of the room.

Angel said, "I’ve been reading down there. Across stories of past Slayers — the ancient ones — something keeps showing up. You miss it if you’re not looking. More accessory than weapon, but the shape matches across hundreds of years... then it vanished."

He looked at Buffy.

Angel said, "I think we should call Giles about this."

Buffy stepped closer and tilted the book with her right hand, fingers on the page.

Buffy said, "An accessory that keeps showing up across all those Slayers? That’s not random. This could be bigger than another spell or blade."

Faith leaned forward.

Faith said, "Vanished, huh? Bet the Council buried it so the girls wouldn’t get any ideas. Sounds like what we need after that smiling freak."

Lucian said, "I’ll grab the cordless."

On the couch, Willow stirred. Her hands went to her face, then her temples. Her eyes opened in panic.

Willow said, weak then sharp, "TARA!"

Tara set the book aside with a sharp inhale and a wince, and pulled Willow in. Dawn shifted but stayed close.

Tara said, "I’m right here, sweetie… I’m okay. You saved us. You pushed so hard and you saved Faith and me. Breathe with me, baby. I’ve got you."

Willow clutched Tara’s arms, her face in Tara’s neck, her breathing ragged. A long quiet moment passed.

She pulled back and searched Tara’s face. Then her eyes dropped to the fresh stitches. The softness drained, and something colder settled in.

Buffy put a hand on Angel’s shoulder.

Buffy said, "We found something bigger to hit him with then. Good work, Angel. Now we just have to find it."

Willow’s eyes stayed on the stitches.

Willow said quietly, "No… it’s not okay."

Her fingers hovered above the wound without touching.

Willow said, "But when I see him again… I will make it okay."

The room went dead quiet. Tara cupped Willow’s cheek, thumb stroking, her other hand near the stitches.

Outside, the last light went.

CHAPTER 8 Buffy Takes Command

Silence held the living room after Willow’s vow, the books still scattered.

Buffy stood in the center, shoulders square. She turned slow across the room and stopped on Willow.

Buffy said, "Will, I know you’re hurt and tired… but I need you right now on those magic books. I need you researching how to fix yourself. You are right now my biggest weapon."

She looked at Tara.

Buffy said, "Tara, I need you researching anything that has to do with the Slayer lines."

She stepped closer to Willow and put a hand on her shoulder.

Buffy said, "Willow, do not absorb the dark magic books. I don’t need you getting possessed or something right now. Read them like you did when we were in school. The slow way."

Willow nodded. Her fingers twitched once, a faint residual crackle under the skin.

Faith shook her head a little. Her hand went behind her head, fingers in her hair. She looked at Willow.

Faith said, "Yeah, let’s not do that, Red. You turn into a big bad, I’d have to put you down… I’m a Slayer first, your buddy second."

Buffy turned sharp toward Faith, mouth opening, then caught herself with a long, exhausted sigh. She rubbed the back of her neck with her right hand, pinching the muscles between her thumb and fingers while walking past everyone into the kitchen.

She leaned over the sink, turned the cold water on hard, and splashed her face and the back of her neck. She gripped the sink edge with both hands, breathing hard through her nose.

Lucian walked up behind her, put both hands on her shoulders, and squeezed.

Buffy shrugged his hands off and spun around to face him, and Lucian stepped back with his hands up.

Buffy said low, "I am in Slayer mode right now. I have to shower upstairs. Alone."

Lucian nodded and lowered his hands.

Buffy looked at him, her voice quieter.

Buffy said, "I still love you, Lucian… But now is not the time for any softness. I... I just can’t right now..."

She walked past him and up the stairs. The bathroom door slammed, and the shower turned on hard.

Downstairs nobody moved. Dawn pressed against Tara. Faith’s hand came down from her hair. Angel and Lucian looked at each other once.

CHAPTER 9 Dinner

While Buffy was upstairs, Tara put dinner on, fried pork chops, mashed potatoes and roasted asparagus, moving carefully around the stitches. Lucian helped plate, Dawn set out the glasses, and Faith cracked open a can of Coke.

By the time Buffy came down damp from the shower, the dining table was already loaded and everyone was eating, forks scraping on plates.

She sat at the head, speared some asparagus, and looked at Willow.

Buffy said while chewing, "So tell me exactly what you used on the preacher when you guys got ambushed. Walk me through it."

Willow took a forkful of potatoes and swallowed.

Willow said, "It was basically the same burst I used on the house. Straight for both his inner ears — balance and hearing. But I had to push hard. I could feel him healing while I was doing it… closing almost as fast as I made it. Might not work the same next time."

Buffy said, "At least he isn’t immune to magic…"

She drank some water and wiped her mouth with the back of her hand.

Angel stood with an empty plate.

Angel said, "Willow, keys to Joyce’s car? I need to get some blood now that it’s fully dark."

Buffy said, "I need you at a hundred percent when we see him again, Angel."

Angel nodded.

Angel said, "Yeah… and that will probably be sooner than later."

Willow tossed the keys. Angel caught them, glanced once toward the driveway where the silver sedan sat with its driver side gaping open to the night air, parked beside his own GTX with its buckled hood, its empty windshield frame, and its one dead headlight, and headed for the door anyway.

Faith started to stand, her mouth full.

Faith said, "I’m riding with you, boss."

Angel turned back.

Angel said, "Stay here, Faith. Protect the house."

Faith dropped into the chair, picked up a pork chop bare-handed, took a massive bite, and grinned at Lucian.

Faith said, mouth full, "Fine. More for me."

Faith turned to Lucian.

Faith said, "You know, back in Louisiana I once wrestled a damn alligator naked after a really bad night. True story. Slippery bastard almost took my arm off. Lucian, you ever do anything that stupid-crazy?"

Lucian chuckled around his mashed potatoes.

Lucian said after swallowing, "Not quite that level, but I did kill a fish man monster once at a wedding rehearsal. Pass the salt?"

Faith nodded between chews.

Faith said, "Fish man monster? Still good for you."

Tara sipped her iced tea.

Tara said, "You two. Eat slower or you’ll choke. Dawn, more asparagus? And Willow — if you need me to ground you while you research the fix, I’m here. No pushing alone."

Dawn said, mouth full, "This is so much better than the garage breakfast. Tara, can I help research the Slayer stuff tomorrow? I’m good at spotting weird patterns now too!"

Willow said, "Buffy — I promise. But I’m going to find a way to make myself whole again for this. For all of us."

Buffy nodded.

Buffy said, "Good. Lucian, after this help Tara with the research stacks. Faith, keep that energy — we’re going to need it."

She reached for more pork chop and kept chewing. She did not say the other thing, the hope that when Willow healed, the memory of what she had done would not come back with it. Buffy glanced sideways at Willow and kept her face passive, even though inside she was churning with fear, doubt and guilt.

Faith grinned around the table.

Faith said, "It’s just old times again. I missed this, you know? The house, the town, everything."

She looked at Buffy.

Faith said, "I am glad I didn’t get left in LA with Cordy and Lorne. I really needed this."

Buffy swallowed and forced a smile. Inside she wanted to scream, cry at the same time. Nobody saw it... this time.

Later the plywood sheet scraped aside, and Angel came back in with a cooler under one arm. He nodded once at Buffy, hung the car keys back up on their wooden peg by the front doorway, carried the cooler through the kitchen and set it down by the garage door, and then went back down the steps into the dark garage with a book held in his left hand.

Conversation kept moving between bites, Dawn babbling about Josh, plates passing, the normalcy hard-won... and real enough for now.

CHAPTER 10 The Porch

Out on the back porch that night, crickets sang, a distant car passed, and wind moved in the trees.

Among the tomato plants knelt the stone statue of Amy Madison, frozen in a scream, the bone wand piece in her neck.

Buffy stepped onto the porch, arms crossed. She found the statue and shook a finger at it once.

Buffy said dryly, "And you, don’t you even think about it…"

The statue did not move. The corner of her mouth twitched anyway.

A porch board creaked behind her, and Buffy turned fast, then let the tension drop when she saw it was Lucian.

Buffy said, "Make more noise when you walk…"

He pulled her in. She folded into him, forehead against his chest, arms around his waist. His right hand spread warm and steady at the small of her back.

Buffy said, muffled, "Still want to get married to this?"

His left hand took her chin and tilted her face up. He kissed her slow, carefully, mindful of his healing lip. Her hands went into his hair. His other hand drifted across her front and rested over her left breast, present, not pushing.

Her breathing changed. She kissed him harder, then pulled back, still close, trembling.

Buffy said, voice low, "You have no idea… how bad I want to right now…"

He did not push. His hand slid back to her side, and both arms wrapped around her. She pressed her face into his chest. They rocked once with the night.

CHAPTER 11 Bait and Salt

Later that night, Willow walked the living room with a thick old book open, Tara beside her with a hand lightly on her back. Dawn trailed after them, yawning. Upstairs a toilet flushed, and two doors clicked shut.

Downstairs Buffy sat across from Faith. Lucian leaned down and kissed Buffy in front of Faith, slow, then pulled back.

Lucian said, "Goodnight."

Buffy said, "I love you."

He headed up the stairs, and his footsteps faded.

Faith and Buffy stared at each other for a long moment, and neither moved.

Faith said, "So that is serious then huh, B?"

She nodded toward the stairs.

Buffy said, "I am going to marry him."

Faith leaned forward, elbows on her knees.

Faith said, "Are you serious B? I mean I know he is handsome and gets down on you but—"

Buffy said, cutting her off, "It’s not just about that F... It’s not just about sex and animalistic feelings."

A quiet moment passed.

Buffy said, "I hope one day Faith. You learn what it’s about... that when you look at someone you feel falling into joy, everything opposite of what we are..."

Faith exhaled through her nose.

Faith said, "Maybe one day... but I doubt it... It’s not my style B... you know that..."

Buffy leaned back.

Buffy said, "I noticed something about the preacher... He keeps targeting Tara..."

Faith’s mouth curved.

Faith said, "Yeah in the alley way? He went for her first... like he was waiting for her..."

Buffy said, "We need to use that..."

Faith raised an eyebrow.

Faith said, "Going dark slayer now? Using your best friend’s mate as the bait?"

Buffy exhaled, eyes on the ceiling, then looked back at Faith.

Buffy said, "No... but we need to use her as bait... we need to finish this... he will never stop coming, you know how this works, they never stop coming until we finish it... and it will show that whatever this First Evil is... it’s not perfect. We can beat it... If we kill the preacher."

Faith said, "You sure about this B? The homemaker witch could die this time? You know how strong he is, he’s like us..."

Buffy said, "I’m sure we don’t get another clean shot if we wait. We plan it. We don’t throw her at him alone."

Faith held her eyes a second. Then she nodded once, not happy, but agreeing anyway.

Buffy stood up while Faith stayed seated, and neither of them said goodnight.

In Willow and Tara’s room, a salt circle lay poured on the floorboards. They sat cross-legged facing each other, hands clasped, eyes closed, their soft chant low and grounding.

Inside Willow was a black void where her magic had been torn. Thin threads of light sparked across it, connecting and knitting, slow and fragile.

They breathed together, in through the nose and out through the mouth. Their hands stayed clasped, and the salt circle stayed undisturbed.

CHAPTER 12 Three Fifteen

Hours after the salt circle and the talk, somewhere past three in the morning, the Summers house was deep in night.

In the bedroom Buffy sat awake at her white wooden vanity, elbows on the wood beneath the oval mirror, the lamp on. She held Mr. Gordo in her lap, absently picking at his left ear between her right thumb and forefinger. Behind her Lucian slept on his back, one arm across the empty space where she was supposed to be. She rubbed her temple and glanced once at him... then back into the dark.

She still carried what the rest of the group did not know about Willow and the Trio, and she did not speak a word of it.

Down in the garage Angel turned another page, eyes still sharp as he read the ancient Latin scripts. He traced his right index finger slowly down the page as he read in the quiet of the house.
