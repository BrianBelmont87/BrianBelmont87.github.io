Buffy the Vampire Slayer. Season six, episode 15. The Rat.

CHAPTER 1 The Iron and the Inventory

In the Summers garage that morning, twin fluorescent tubes flickered overhead, and a dusty shaft of sunlight cut through the frosted garage window.

Joyce’s old car was parked out in the driveway, so most of the floor was a wide expanse of oil-stained concrete.

The walls were lined with ordinary suburban clutter: old rakes, a pegboard covered in rusted tools, and a tangled garden hose. Taking up the entire left side of the garage, though, was a massive, towering wall of heavy cardboard boxes, labeled in thick black Sharpie: MAGIC BOX INVENTORY - FRAGILE, EYE OF NEWT (BULK), and CAUTION: HEXED.

In the center of the concrete floor sat a heavy-duty, black leather weight bench and a rack of free weights.

Somewhere under the hum of the fluorescent tubes, cast-iron weight plates clanked against one another in a slow, heavy rhythm, the sound bouncing off the concrete and the cardboard wall and back again.

Buffy was sitting on a small wooden crate near the weight rack in a grey sports bra and loose black sweatpants, her hair pulled up into a messy, utilitarian ponytail.

Lucian was lying flat on his back on the weight bench. He was shirtless, his pale skin glistening with a heavy layer of sweat, his jet-black hair damp against his temples and his bright aqua-blue eyes narrowed with effort. The black raven tattoo across his chest and left shoulder shifted with every press, and so did the raw pink claw marks from the beach, still healing across his ribs, his bicep, and the raven’s spread wings. He was pushing a heavily loaded straight barbell, easily two hundred and twenty-five pounds, in a flat chest press.

Buffy was holding a massive sixty-five-pound cast-iron dumbbell in her right hand, her elbow locked against the inside of her right thigh in flawless form for a seated concentration curl.

Buffy curled the sixty-five-pound weight upward smoothly, without a single flicker of strain on her face, and her breathing never changed.

Buffy said casually, "The trick is keeping the elbow completely anchored. If you let it drift, you’re just engaging the shoulder to cheat the weight up."

Lucian was at the bottom of his rep, the heavy steel bar hovering an inch above his chest, and then he pushed upward, the sheer physical exertion plain to see; his jaw was locked and the tendons in his neck stood out. His triceps and chest muscles twitched under the strain, and a slight, visible tremor shook his arms and legs as he fought gravity.

Lucian drove the bar up, grunting the words out.

Lucian said, "Noted. I’ll be sure to perfect my isolation technique... right after I finish... making sure my ribcage doesn’t collapse."

Lucian locked his elbows and held the heavy bar steady at the top of the press, his chest rising and falling hard beneath it, the steel trembling only faintly in his grip.

Buffy casually lowered the sixty-five-pound dumbbell, seamlessly passing it to her left hand and locking her left elbow against her knee to start her next set.

Buffy smirked, not missing a beat.

Buffy said, "You’re doing great. Just remember to breathe. And try not to drop it. If you crack the concrete, Tara is going to bill you for property damage."

Lucian drew a deep breath and braced.

Lucian said, "Tara? I thought this was your house."

Buffy laughed softly.

Buffy said, "Nope. When I was... away... Giles helped Tara and Willow legally transfer the deed so the state wouldn’t take the property and Dawn."

Lucian slowly lowered the bar back down to his chest for another rep, breathless and fighting the weight.

Lucian said, "Noted. Speaking of... how much longer are we functioning as an off-site storage facility for the occult?"

Buffy glanced over at the towering wall of cardboard boxes.

Buffy said, "Until Xander and Anya figure out what they are doing with the shop before they move to Texas. We’re just sitting on it all for now."

Lucian pushed the bar up on a sharp exhale.

Lucian said, "Comforting. I’m pretty sure the box marked ‘Miscellaneous Talismans’ has been whispering since we started the warm-up."

On his next down-stroke Lucian’s gaze flicked to the Miscellaneous Talismans box on the inventory wall. He paused for one controlled breath through the strain, then drove the bar up without breaking form or saying a word. Under the fluorescent hum, the box whispered faintly.

Buffy said, "Just ignore it. If it starts levitating, you can hit it with the barbell."

Lucian hit his failure point. His arms shook violently as he pushed the bar up for the final rep, and he guided it backward until it hit the metal catch of the rack with a loud, heavy clang.

Lucian sat up on the bench, his chest heaving, sweat dripping down his torso. He leaned forward, resting his elbows on his knees, completely exhausted but thoroughly grounded.

Buffy finished her final curl and set the dumbbell down on the concrete floor. It landed with a heavy, solid thud.

She stood up and walked over to a small cooler by the wall, pulled out two plastic bottles of chilled orange Fanta, and tossed one to Lucian.

Lucian caught it flawlessly despite his shaking arms. He unscrewed the cap and took a long, desperate drink. Buffy cracked hers open, taking a slow sip, looking down at him with a comfortable, appreciative smile.

Buffy walked over to Lucian, who was still catching his breath on the bench. She reached out with her left hand and slowly dragged her index finger along the side of his neck, collecting a thin line of sweat. Lucian’s skin pebbled instantly under the cool, deliberate touch. A visible shiver rolled down his spine and across his shoulders, and his breath hitched with a soft, involuntary sound. Buffy watched his reaction with a slow, knowing half-smile, her eyes warm and teasing. She brought her finger to her lips, tasted the faint salt of his sweat, and let the smile bloom into something playful and intimate.

Buffy said, "Just as I thought. Sugar water."

CHAPTER 2 The Expiration Date

In the Summers master bedroom that morning, bright, warm, cheerful sunlight filled the room, and it felt safe and domestic, the light catching the dust motes in the air and the soft cotton of the sheets.

The heavy winter quilts had been pushed aside for the season, leaving just a thin, light cotton sheet pooled at the foot of the bed.

Willow was sitting up against the headboard, propped on pillows, in warm-weather sleepwear, a thin white cotton camisole and light, patterned sleep shorts, her feet bare and one leg slightly bent.

Tara was sitting close beside her, not cross-legged but with her legs tucked to one side, her body angled toward Willow. She was wearing a soft, loose-fitting pastel yellow tank top and light cotton sleep pants, and she was barefoot too. Tara’s left knee rested gently against Willow’s right thigh. Willow had her right arm loosely draped around Tara’s shoulders, her fingers idly tracing small, slow circles on Tara’s upper arm, a quiet, grounding touch.

The small brown rat, Amy, was held carefully in Tara’s palms in the space between their laps. Both women had a hand on her, Tara stroking her back with her thumb in slow, rhythmic motions and Willow’s fingers now and then brushing her fur as well. Their heads were close, almost touching, and Tara rested her head lightly against Willow’s shoulder for a moment before she lifted it again.

Tara said carefully, "After... after the beach and what happened at the rehearsal dinner... I just wanted one morning that felt like it used to. Quiet. Safe. No monsters crashing through the walls. Just us. And this little guy who doesn’t know any of it."

Willow said, "I know. One normal morning. The kind where the biggest problem is whether the rat is going to chew through the sheets again. But you know this town, Tara. Normal has a really short expiration date. Especially when my magic’s been feeling... patchy lately."

Tara laughed softly, a small, genuine sound, and turned her head to press a quick, gentle kiss to Willow’s shoulder before looking back down at the rat in their hands. Her free hand slid down to rest lightly on Willow’s knee.

Tara said, "Maybe not this time. Maybe it’ll just be... normal. Just for one night. For the wedding. For us."

There was a sharp, explosive pop and the instant hiss of burning ozone. A thick cloud of bright purple smoke erupted directly from Tara’s hands and engulfed the center of the bed.

Tara flinched violently and scrambled backward off the mattress. Her bare feet hit the hardwood floor, and she stood with her back pressed against the bedroom wall, her mouth hanging open, staring at the bed, frozen by the sudden loss of the small weight in her hands and the burst of uncontrolled magic.

The purple smoke thinned and drifted apart in less than two seconds, curling away toward the ceiling and the sunlit window and leaving behind a sharp, scorched smell over the bed.

Where the small brown rat had just been, Amy Madison was now sitting up in the center of the mattress, completely naked, with her knees drawn tightly up in front of her, her feet pressed together on the mattress and her legs pressed tight together. Her head was bowed forward, her chin tucked down against her knees and her upper body curled slightly forward, and her arms were crossed over her chest and the tops of her thighs, fully covering her breasts, while the bright morning sunlight fell full on her bare skin.

Her shoulders jerked, and she dragged in one frantic, gasping breath of human air, her whole small body shuddering with it as if her lungs had forgotten how big they were supposed to be.

Amy screamed at the top of her lungs, a raw, endless shriek that filled the whole bright room.

Tara jumped, her eyes going even wider, frozen against the wall.

Willow didn’t scream, and she didn’t jump out of bed. She just blinked and looked calmly down at a screaming, naked Amy Madison curled up in the center of her sheets, and then she slowly turned her head and looked across the room at the small plastic hamster cage sitting on her dresser.

Willow said in a deadpan voice, "Well... at least it didn’t happen inside the cage."

CHAPTER 3 The False Alarm and the Bare Truth

Frantic, heavy footsteps pounded up the wooden stairs, taking them two at a time.

Buffy and Lucian rounded the corner into the upstairs hallway, Buffy moving with terrifying Slayer speed and Lucian right on her heels, still shirtless and glistening with sweat from the garage, his muscles tense and ready for a brutal fight.

Buffy didn’t bother with the doorknob. She hit the master bedroom door with the flat of her palm, shoving it violently open.

Buffy said, "Get away from them!"

Buffy’s eyes swept every corner of the room in a single, fast arc, the bed, the window, the closet, the space behind the door, and found no demon anywhere.

Tara was still plastered against the far wall, clutching her chest, her breathing jagged. Willow was standing near the foot of the bed with a folded blanket in her hands, unbothered, though she hadn’t actually handed it over yet.

Sitting in the absolute center of the mattress was Amy Madison.

Amy sat with her bare back and the curve of her hip toward the door, still curled in the same protective pose, her hair a messy bird’s nest and her eyes darting around the room in absolute panic. She had started to lift her head in shock, but her knees and arms stayed in place, covering her.

Amy said, "I have knees. Oh my god, I have knees again. And... and I’m so tall! The ceiling is so far away!"

Buffy froze in her combat stance. She blinked, her brain stalling out as it tried to process a girl who had been a rodent suddenly sitting naked on the mattress.

Buffy said, "Amy?"

Lucian stepped into the doorway right behind Buffy, his broad shoulders filling the frame. He was looking around the room, confused by the lack of monsters.

Lucian said, "What’s going on? Who screamed?"

Amy looked up. Her panicked eyes locked directly onto the very tall, very shirtless, heavily tattooed man standing in the doorway staring back at her. She blinked, momentarily forgetting her existential rat crisis.

Amy said, "Oh. Hello. I definitely didn’t have one of those in my cage."

Buffy’s Slayer reflexes kicked back in, but for an entirely different reason. Without even turning around, her left hand shot backward with blinding speed and planted itself firmly over Lucian’s eyes.

Buffy said, "Whoa. No. Eyes closed, mister."

Lucian said, "Okay, I’m blind. I’m guessing we’re not under attack anymore?"

Buffy said, "We are currently being ambushed by a naked high school classmate. Wait out in the hall."

Lucian chuckled softly. He reached up, gently holding her wrist, and let her guide him backward out of the doorway.

Lucian said, "Understood. Guarding the hallway."

Lucian planted his back against the hallway wall beside the bedroom door, arms crossed, eyes fixed on the stairs, sweat cooling on his bare shoulders, unruffled by the chaos.

Buffy stepped back out with him, reached in, grabbed the edge of the heavy wooden door, and pulled it shut with a solid click, sealing the three witches inside.

In the bedroom it was quiet for a second after the door shut.

Willow finally stepped forward and tossed the folded blanket onto Amy’s lap. Amy quickly wrapped it around her shoulders, shivering.

Tara took a deep, shaky breath, peeling herself away from the wall. She walked slowly over to the bed, looking at Amy as if she was an unexploded bomb.

Tara said carefully, "H-h-how... how did you do that? The spell... it was permanent."

Willow crossed her arms, looking at Amy with a mix of scientific curiosity and mild annoyance.

Willow said, "It wasn’t a counter-curse. There was no ambient energy pull. It just... snapped."

Amy pulled the blanket tighter around her shoulders. She looked at Willow, the manic energy starting to settle into profound exhaustion and confusion.

Amy said, "It wore off, Willow. I could feel it... like a rubber band stretching out until it finally gave way. My magic... it just ran out of juice."

Amy looked around the spacious, sunlit bedroom, her eyes welling up with sudden, overwhelming tears, and then she searched Willow’s face, completely broken.

Amy whispered, "How long was I in there? It felt like... forever. Just a massive blur of running, and sleeping, and eating sunflower seeds out of a plastic bowl. Do you have any idea how much I crave a cheeseburger right now?"

Willow and Tara exchanged a long, heavy look. The comic shock of the naked arrival had faded, leaving behind the dark reality of a girl who had just lost a massive, unmeasurable chunk of her humanity.

Willow said, "Okay. I’ll go see if I can borrow some of Buffy’s old sweatpants for you."

Tara said, "And I’ll go downstairs and get some burgers started."

CHAPTER 4 The Blur and the Borrowed Clothes

In the Summers kitchen late that morning, burgers sizzled loudly on the stovetop and cabinet doors opened and gently shut. Upstairs, muffled by the floor, Amy was talking a mile a minute.

The kitchen island was cluttered with open hamburger buns, paper towels, ketchup bottles, and a grocery bag half-full of produce.

Tara stood at the stove in fuzzy blue socks, carefully flipping burgers in a cast-iron skillet, still visibly rattled from the shock upstairs.

Willow entered quietly from the hallway, carrying a pile of neatly folded clothes against her chest: old grey sweatpants, a faded Sunnydale High hoodie, and thick socks. She dropped them gently onto the counter.

Willow said, "Found the emergency ‘Buffy stopped growing in sophomore year’ stash. They are going to stop halfway up Amy’s calves, but it covers the important parts."

Tara smiled automatically, but her eyes were distracted.

Tara said, "Do you think she’s okay?"

Willow said, "Amy?"

Willow paused.

Willow said, "No. Not even a little."

Willow turned and opened the refrigerator, then stared blankly into the cold air for a second longer than necessary.

Inside were rows of foil-covered leftovers, a carton of orange juice, a jar of pickles and a half-gallon of milk, all the ordinary things of a normal household, and Willow stood with the cold air spilling over her bare arms, looking at them as if she couldn’t quite remember what any of them were for. Her expression tightened by a small degree at the corners of her mouth, and the careful calm she had been wearing all morning began, very slightly, to slip.

Tara said, "Will..."

Willow snapped out of it immediately, blinking rapidly.

Willow said, "Right. Sorry. Brain drift."

Tara lowered the heat slightly under the skillet.

Tara said, "You got that look again."

Willow said, "What look?"

Tara said, "The one where you leave the room without physically leaving the room."

Behind Willow the refrigerator hummed low and steady, the only sound in the kitchen besides the soft sizzle of the burgers in the skillet.

Willow closed the fridge gently with a soft click. She leaned back against the cool metal door, staring at the floor.

Willow said, "I just keep thinking about it."

Tara said, "About Amy?"

Willow said, "About time."

She looked up at Tara.

Willow said, "For her, it was all blur and instinct and cages and running in circles. And suddenly she’s back... and years are just gone."

Tara watched her carefully now, the spatula going still in her hand.

Willow said, "That’s horrifying."

Tara said, "Yeah."

Willow curled in on herself slightly, her eyes fixed on a crack in the linoleum as the weight of the question settled on her chest. Her fingers tightened around the edge of the counter behind her, knuckles whitening for a second before she forced them to relax.

Willow whispered, "What if magic does that to people even when they’re still human?"

Tara went still at the stove, the spatula held motionless above the skillet, and she understood at once the real question underneath the one Willow had asked. Willow wasn’t talking about Amy anymore; she was terrified of her own mind.

Upstairs, Amy suddenly burst out laughing at something Buffy said.

Tara eased a little at the sound, and Willow let out a long, slow breath.

Buffy’s muffled voice came through the floorboards.

Buffy yelled, "Absolutely not! You cannot smoke in my bedroom!"

Amy said, "I was a rat for years! I deserve one cigarette, minimum!"

Lucian’s deep laughter rumbled faintly through the ceiling.

Tara smiled despite herself, the tension bleeding out of her.

Tara said, "She sounds okay."

Willow said, "That’s the scary part."

Willow pushed off the fridge, grabbing a clean ceramic plate from the cabinet to prep for the burgers.

Willow said, "Amy always sounds okay right before something catches on fire."

Then a loud metallic crash came from upstairs, followed by a frantic scrambling.

Upstairs, Lucian’s laugh cut off at the crash. His footsteps shifted toward the noise, then paused; he let Buffy handle it.

Buffy said, "Amy!"

Amy said, "I forgot humans have elbows now!"

Lucian laughed harder, a genuine, booming sound.

Tara finally broke into real, unburdened laughter, covering her face for a moment with the hand holding the spatula.

Willow watched Tara laughing, and for a moment the artificial calm on her face cracked.

CHAPTER 5 The Feral Appetite and the Exit

Feet thudded fast and heavy down the wooden stairs to the sound of loud bickering.

Buffy marched into the kitchen with her right hand clamped firmly on the back of a faded Sunnydale High hoodie, leading Amy Madison by the scruff of the neck like a misbehaving kitten.

Amy wore the borrowed grey sweatpants, which stopped awkwardly halfway up her shins over thick white socks, and the oversized hoodie drowned her upper body. Her hair was still a wild mess.

Buffy let go of the hoodie, exasperation plain on her face.

Buffy said, "Here is your ra... Amy back..."

Amy rubbed the back of her neck, defensive.

Amy said, "My depth perception is completely shot, Buffy! For three years, my eye-level was the baseboards. You try suddenly being five-foot-seven and navigating a floor plan without whiskers!"

Tara turned from the stove, using a spatula to slide two massive, steaming cheeseburgers onto a ceramic plate.

Tara said carefully, "F-food is ready. Careful, they’re straight off the iron."

Amy didn’t say thank you. She dropped into a barstool with terrifying speed.

Amy attacked the plate, grabbing the scorching hot cheeseburger with both hands and took a massive, almost unhinged bite. She wasn’t eating politely; she was eating with the frantic, survivalist urgency of a stray animal. Willow winced slightly, watching her chew.

Buffy leaned back against the counter beside the stove, her arms folded loosely over her chest, watching Amy eat with an expression caught somewhere between pity and alarm.

Buffy said, "We’re going to have to get you to the mall. Those high-waters are a crime against fashion. And we probably need to figure out how to un-declare you legally missing."

Before Amy could swallow her massive bite to respond, heavy footsteps echoed from the hallway.

Lucian walked into the kitchen, changed out of his workout gear into a black shirt with a dark embroidered collar and cuffs, worn open at the throat so the edge of the raven tattoo showed against his pale skin, and his silver cross earring caught the light. He looked entirely out of place in the chaotic, grease-scented kitchen. He stepped in beside Buffy, his hand finding the small of her back, and wrapped one strong arm around her waist to pull her in.

Lucian said, "Duty calls. The mainframe waits for no man."

He leaned down and kissed Buffy deeply, ignoring the fact that a formerly rodent woman was inhaling ground beef three feet away.

Buffy melted into the kiss for a second, her exasperation instantly vanishing. She pulled back, smiling up at him.

Buffy said, "Kill some spreadsheets for me."

Lucian grinned and looked over Buffy’s shoulder, giving Tara and Willow a sharp, respectful nod of farewell.

His eyes finally landed on Amy, who had frozen mid-chew, staring at him.

Lucian gave her a polite, entirely unfazed tilt of his head.

Lucian said, "Welcome back to the top of the food chain, Amy."

Lucian turned and walked smoothly out the front door, and the heavy door scraped in its crooked frame behind him until he pulled it the last inch shut.

Amy said, "Okay. I missed a lot. Who is the GQ vampire slayer, and does he have a brother?"

Buffy crossed her arms, instantly protective.

Buffy said, "He’s mine. And no, he doesn’t. Focus on your burger, Madison."

Amy rolled her eyes, taking another massive, feral bite of the burger. She talked with her mouth full, pointing a greasy finger at Willow.

Amy said around a mouthful, "You know what the worst part was, Will? Besides the sunflower seeds?"

Willow grimaced.

Willow said, "The wheel? The lack of indoor plumbing?"

Amy said, "The silence."

The kitchen went quiet. Tara stopped cleaning the skillet, looking over her shoulder.

Amy said, "When you’re a rat... the current is just gone. The hum of the universe. I couldn’t feel the magic. I was just trapped in this tiny, dumb, furry little brain, completely cut off from the power. It was like being suffocated for three years."

Amy slowly looked up. Her eyes locked directly onto Willow. The wild, frantic energy was gone, replaced by a dark, knowing intensity.

Amy said, "But you..."

She leaned forward, her voice dropping to a mesmerized whisper.

Amy said, "I can feel you from across the room, Will. You’re practically vibrating. It’s like standing next to a live wire."

Tara stiffened visibly at the stove. Buffy’s eyes narrowed, her protective instincts flaring up at the sudden shift in Amy’s tone.

Willow shifted, not quite meeting Tara’s eyes.

Willow said, "I’ve... studied a lot since high school. Things escalated."

Amy smiled, sly and pleased with herself.

Amy said, "Escalated. Right. I can smell the ozone on you, Rosenberg. You haven’t just been reading books. You’ve been playing in the deep end."

Amy picked up the second burger and took a big bite off it, her eyes never leaving Willow’s face.

Amy said around a mouthful, "We are going to have so much fun once I get my juices going again."

CHAPTER 6 The House Rules and the Invitation

Nobody answered Amy right away. The faint, sharp smell of ozone she had talked about seemed to linger over the island, and the warm, bright kitchen suddenly felt a good deal smaller than it had a minute before.

Tara stopped wiping down the counter and slowly turned around to face the island.

Tara said carefully, "A-Amy... we like to keep magic use inside the house to a m-minimum."

Amy paused mid-chew. She looked at Tara, then looked away, letting out a soft, dismissive sigh, and rolled her eyes, a small, highly disrespectful, classic high-school-mean-girl eye roll.

Tara saw it, and the soft, nurturing maternal energy vanished from her posture. She stepped away from the counter and closed the distance between them, stepping directly into Amy’s personal space at the island. She didn’t raise her hands, and she didn’t yell, but she stared straight down at the seated witch.

Tara’s face was devoid of its usual gentleness. It was hard, uncompromising, and radiating the quiet, absolute authority of a woman defending her home and her partner.

Amy froze, the last bite of burger hovering near her mouth. She looked up into Tara’s fierce, unblinking eyes, and the feral, chaotic energy drained right out of her as she suddenly remembered who actually held the deed to this house.

Amy said, "Yes, ma’am."

Tara held the stare for one more heavy second to make sure the boundary was permanently set. Then she gave a single, sharp nod and stepped back, returning to the stove as if nothing had happened.

Buffy, who had been watching the entire exchange from the edge of the kitchen, slowly uncrossed her arms. She didn’t drop a witty one-liner to break the tension. She didn’t smile. She just looked at Tara with a flicker of deep respect.

Buffy said, "I’m going for a jog. I’ll be back before Dawn needs to be picked up from school."

Buffy turned on her heel and walked out of the kitchen, and her footsteps faded down the hall until the heavy front door stuck, scraped open in its crooked frame, and was shouldered shut again behind her, leaving the three of them alone in the quiet kitchen.

The only sound in the room was the heavy, methodical tick of the antique clock in the living room, echoing down the hallway. Tara was staring at the skillet with the spatula still in her hand, Willow was leaning against the refrigerator staring at the floor, looking anxious, and Amy was sitting perfectly still on the barstool with her fingers curled around the edge of her empty plate, and for a few long seconds none of them moved.

Finally, Amy broke the silence. She shifted on the stool, pulling Buffy’s oversized hoodie tighter around her shoulders, adopting a perfectly innocent, casual tone.

Amy said, "So... I was thinking. I have literally been trapped in a plastic box for three years. I don’t even know what the Bronze looks like anymore. Would you guys... maybe want to take me out tonight?"

Willow looked up from the floor, surprised.

Amy smiled brightly, laying it on thick.

Amy said, "Just the three of us? A girls’ night? Tara, you could show me around. I just really, really need to be around people who don’t have tails."

Willow’s face softened. She looked over at Tara, silently pleading with her to agree, wanting to help her old friend feel normal again.

Tara looked over her shoulder at Amy, the hard edge from earlier gone and a cautious, reluctant empathy in its place, and gave a slow, hesitant nod.

Tara said, "Yeah. Okay. We can take you out tonight."

Amy’s smile widened into something incredibly bright. But as she lowered her head to wipe her greasy hands on a paper napkin, the innocent facade dropped for a fraction of a second, revealing a calculating, deeply manipulative gleam in her eyes.

Amy slid off the barstool to carry her plate to the sink, and the borrowed clothes followed her about a second late. Buffy’s old grey sweatpants sagged at the waist and bunched at the knees even though they stopped halfway up her shins, and the Sunnydale High hoodie hung off her like a tent, the sleeves swallowing her hands whole. She had to hitch the waistband up with one fist just to walk.

Tara looked her up and down, then pointed the spatula at the whole outfit.

Tara said, "You can’t go to the Bronze wearing those."

Willow said, "She’s right. That’s not an outfit. That’s a hostage situation."

Amy looked down at herself, shoved the sleeves back off her hands, and shrugged.

Amy said, "Fine. But I have no money, no ID, and I’m legally missing. Somebody’s buying."

Early that afternoon, the three of them piled into Joyce’s old car. Willow tossed her bag onto the back seat with her cell phone somewhere at the bottom of it, and neither the bag nor the phone came out again for the rest of the day.

The Sunnydale mall was bright and loud and packed, and Amy stopped dead just inside the doors. She stared at the fountain, the escalators, the crowds, and the glowing signs of the food court with the look of someone whose whole world had been the size of a plastic cage for three years. Every sound seemed to reach her a half-second too loud. Tara quietly took one elbow and Willow took the other, and they steered her toward the first clothing store like two tugboats bringing in a very confused ship.

Inside, Amy was hopeless. She held up a shirt, put it back, picked up another, and forgot which one she had liked. She lost five full minutes to a spinning rack of sunglasses, and she stared at one price tag as if it were written in a dead language. So Willow started making piles, and Tara started holding things up against Amy and tilting her head, and before long the two of them were deep in it. They argued gently over dark denim versus black and passed hangers over the top of the fitting room door, and the quick errand quietly turned into the whole afternoon.

Amy said from behind the curtain, "Pants have so many buttons now. Were there always this many buttons?"

Willow said, "Same number of buttons, Amy."

Amy came out at last in dark jeans, a fitted black long-sleeved top, and a pair of boots Tara had found on the clearance rack, with Buffy’s old Sunnydale High hoodie knotted around her waist by its sleeves, because she flatly refused to let it go into the bag with the sweatpants. She stood in front of the three-way mirror for a long, silent moment, turning one way and then the other. She looked like a person again. For just a second the snark fell away, and she looked much younger than she was, and very lost.

Amy said, "Huh. I have a waist."

Tara smiled and squeezed her arm, and Willow paid without looking at the total. Then Amy caught the smell of the food court and nearly walked into a pillar. She put away two slices of pizza and a soft pretzel with the same frantic focus she had brought to the burgers, while Willow and Tara sat across from her sharing a lemonade and laughing at nothing. For a little while it felt like the normal day Tara had wished for that morning.

Nobody looked at a clock. The mall had no windows, and by the time the shop lights started going down and they walked out into the parking lot with their arms full of bags, the sky was already black. Willow threw the bags into the back seat on top of her own, never once thinking about the phone at the bottom of it, and drove them straight across town to the Bronze.

CHAPTER 7 The Abilene Blueprint

Inside Xander’s apartment that night, the main overhead lights were off, and the cramped, messy room was lit almost entirely by the harsh, flickering blue glow of a CRT computer monitor.

Xander was sitting in his worn desk chair with Anya perched comfortably sideways on his lap in one of his oversized flannel shirts, her bare legs draped over the armrest. Xander had his left arm wrapped securely around her waist, his right hand resting on a clunky computer mouse.

On the screen was a real estate website, with low-resolution photos of sun-baked apartment complexes in Abilene, Texas.

Anya excitedly tapped a perfectly manicured finger against the glass of the monitor.

Anya said, "This one. Right here. The ‘Abilene Vista.’ It explicitly advertises an in-unit washer and dryer, Xander. Do you know what that means? Rack didn’t look up, but his jaw tightened in sheer annoyance, and his voice was cold.

Rack said, "I told you the shop is closed tonight."

Amy Madison stepped through the beads into the back room. Rack had shut the den on her an hour ago, and she had come back anyway. She had untied Buffy’s old Sunnydale High hoodie from around her waist and pulled it on over her new clothes, with the heavy hood up over her messy hair. The innocent, frantic victim act from the kitchen was completely gone. She moved with a dark, calculated, predatory grace.

Amy’s voice came out smooth and unafraid.

Amy said, "You closed the door on the most powerful witch in Sunnydale, Rack. That’s bad for business."

Rack finally looked up. His dead, unblinking eyes narrowed as he took in the former rat.

Rack said, "She isn’t just a witch. She’s a walking void. And whatever is living at the bottom of it is waking up. I don’t deal with ancient gods, Madison."

Amy walked slowly into the center of the room, ignoring the catatonic magic junkies scattered on the Victorian couches behind her.

Amy said, "She’s vulnerable. Her magic is completely ruptured, and she’s terrified of her own shadow."

Amy stopped and looked directly at Rack. Her eyes were entirely clear, sharp, and intensely ambitious. The three years inside the cage hadn’t just broken her; they had made her vicious.

Amy said, "I spent three years on a wheel, listening to the universe hum while I couldn’t touch it. I’m not going back to the bottom of the food chain. If Willow is too scared to play in the deep end... I’m not."

Rack stared at her. For a long moment the only sound in the den was the low hum of the magic in the walls, and then a slow, chilling understanding settled over his face as he recognized the sheer, raw hunger in her.

Rack tilted his head, his voice dropping to a dangerous whisper.

Rack said, "And what exactly are you offering, little rat?"

The dim red light of the den reflected in Amy’s eyes, making them look almost feral.

Amy smiled, wicked and triumphant.

Amy said, "Everything she won’t. I want the power. All of it. And I’ll burn that entire house to the ground to get it."

The low bass hum of the room spiked into a sharp, deafening crescendo.
